## The three texts

This site translates three related grammars of Pāli, the works of one school, into modern English.

| Text | Author and date | Extent | Translation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kaccāyana** (`Kaccāyanabyākaraṇaṃ`) | ascribed to Mahākaccāyana; compiled around the sixth or seventh century CE, probably in India | 673 suttas in eight chapters, each with its `vutti` and examples | [Kaccāyana](/kaccayana/) |
| **Padarūpasiddhi** | Buddhappiya, a Coḻa monk of the thirteenth century who calls himself a pupil of Ānanda | 684 suttas in seven sections, with Buddhappiya's own commentary | [Padarūpasiddhi](/rupasiddhi/) |
| **Bālāvatāra** | Dhammakitti, Sri Lanka, fourteenth century | 269 paragraphs in four sections | [Bālāvatāra](/balavatara/) |

**Kaccāyana** is the earliest Pāli grammar that survives: terse rules (`sutta`) with a short gloss (`vutti`) and examples, arranged by topic. **Buddhappiya's Padarūpasiddhi** takes over practically every sutta of Kaccāyana but replaces the old vutti with his own commentary and rearranges the rules into a teaching order: all the masculine stems in `a` together, with their full paradigm and the derivation of every form, then the other stems, and so on through the seven sections, so that a rule may be quoted more than once. It became the standard textbook of the Kaccāyana school. **Dhammakitti's Bālāvatāra** abridges the two for beginners and follows the Rūpasiddhi's arrangement; it stops at the secondary suffixes and has no chapter on the verb or on primary derivation.

The three translations are made in one style and cross-referenced rule by rule. Every Rūpasiddhi sutta and every Bālāvatāra rule carries a card to the Kaccāyana sutta it is; the Kaccāyana headings give the Rūpasiddhi number of each rule in parentheses; and diagrams on the [Rūpasiddhi](/rupasiddhi/) and [Bālāvatāra](/balavatara/) pages map the later texts onto the Kaccāyana chapters.

## Approach

### Optionality

Kaccāyana hedges its rules with option markers, and a translation stands or falls by how it reads them. Following Aleix Ruiz-Falqués' studies of the question, [Two levels of optionality in the Kaccāyana Vyākaraṇa](/reference/two-levels-of-optionality-in-the-kaccāyana-vyākaraṇa) and [Boundaries and Domains](/reference/boundaries-and-domains---understanding-optionality-in-buddhappiyas-rūpasiddhi), the translation distinguishes two levels of option:

* `vā` marks either an open option (`vikappa`), where both forms are good Pāli, or a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`), where the tradition fixes the domain in which the rule is obligatory (`nicca`), optional (`anicca`) or excluded (`asanta`);
* `kvaci` marks an exception to a wider rule: the form occurs only in the words the examples name;
* `navā` is glossed by the Rūpasiddhi as `kvaci`, and `vibhāsā` as `vā`; `ca`, `api`, `tu` and `niccaṃ` control options too.

In the sutta headings `vā` is rendered "optionally", `kvaci` "sometimes" or "occasionally" and `navā` "rarely"; the Analysis under every sutta then states how the marker is read and, where Buddhappiya or the commentaries fix a domain, what that domain is.

### Terminology

The grammar is described in its own terms rather than through the categories of Latin grammar. An inflection is a `vibhatti`, and its seven forms are numbered ① to ⑦ in the traditional order (`paṭhamā` to `sattamī`), with ⓪ for the vocative, ⨀ for the singular and ⨂ for the plural; the words "case", "declension" and "indeclinable" are not used, and `aliṅga` is glossed as "without gender". In the glosses of the examples gender is marked 🚹 🚺 🚻 and person 👆 🤘 🤟. Stems are `liṅga`, suffixes `paccaya`, roots `dhātu`; Pāli technical terms are kept, set in `backticks`, and glossed on first use.

### Examples, derivations and diagrams

Every example the vutti gives is derived step by step, with `+` for the parts joined, `→` for a substitution and `~~x~~` for an elision, each step naming the rule that makes it; where an example is a quotation from the Tipiṭaka it is cited and linked to the [World Tipiṭaka Edition](https://tipitaka2500.github.io). Counter-examples are explained as well as translated. Diagrams are used for structure only, for the kinds of rule, the map of each text, the sandhi decision charts and the taxonomies of compounds, suffixes and model nouns, never for an individual derivation, which the notation already handles.

### Sources

The Pāli of all three texts is the **Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana Tipiṭaka** edition (CST4) published by the [Vipassana Research Institute](https://www.vridhamma.org) at [tipitaka.org](https://tipitaka.org), also available on [GitHub](https://github.com/fsnow/cst): Kaccāyana with its vutti, Buddhappiya's Padarūpasiddhi and Bālāvatāra, whose complete text is reproduced on the [Bālāvatāra (CSCD4)](/balavatara/cscd4) page. The edition has misprints, and its Bālāvatāra and Rūpasiddhi files have a few paragraphs and numbers out of place; a reading is corrected where the rule itself requires it, every correction is footnoted with the edition's reading, and the suttas are numbered by their position in the text.

The examples and counter-examples are usually taken from the Tipiṭaka and its commentaries. Where they could be identified they are cited from the [World Tipiṭaka Edition](https://tipitaka2500.github.io); some could not be found and may come from texts outside the canon.

## Kaccāyana in context

**Kaccāyana** is the oldest extant Pāli grammar, and was composed around the sixth century CE, most probably in India. It is partly based on **Pāṇini** and **Kātantra**:

* Pāṇini, *Aṣṭādhyāyī* “Eight Lessons” 5th–4th c. B.C.E. Śalātura Northwest India
* Śarvavarman, *Kātantravyākaraṇa* 2nd c. C.E. South India

The author of **Kaccāyana** is attributed to Mahā Kaccāyana, but modern scholars believe it is authored by multiple compilers and gradually evolved over time. According to James D’Alwis in *An introduction to Kachchāyana's grammar of the Pāli language*:

* the grammatical aphorisms (`sutta`), are ascribed to a certain Kaccāyana,
* the "gloss" or explanation (`vutti`) is ascribed to a certain Saṅghanandin
* the examples (`payoga`) is ascribed to a certain Brahmadatta

According to the Thai monk Dhammakitti in *Saddhammasaṅgaha* (Saddhamma-s IX, 18-20, 35):

>`kaccāyano kato yogo saṃghanandi pavuttikā, ṭīkā vimalabodhī ca brahmaputto ca kārakā.`  
>*(SADDHĀNANDA, 1890: 63)*  
>Kaccāyana composed the yoga [“rule”], Saṅghanandin the pavuttikā [“notes”], and Vimalabodhī the ṭīkā [“commentary”], and Brahmaputto the kāraka [“section on factor of action”].

## The structure of Kaccāyana

There are 673 rules (`sutta`), numbered §1–§673 throughout this site, spread across the following chapters (`kappa`) and sections (`kaṇḍa`). The Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana edition counts the Kāraka, Samāsa and Taddhita chapters as the sixth to eighth sections of the noun chapter, and the Uṇādi chapter as the sixth section of the Kibbidhāna chapter.

1. [Sandhikappa](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa) (§1–§51)
   1. Definitions (§1–§11)
   2. Vowel sandhi (§12–§22)
   3. Consonant sandhi (§23–§29)
   4. Niggahīta (`ṃ`) sandhi (§30–§41)
   5. Miscellanea (§42–§51)
2. [Nāmakappa](/kaccayana/2-namakappa) (§52–§270)
   1. Definitions, internal morphology, default inflections (§52–§119)
   2. `amha` and `tumha`, the `ntu` stems, numerals, `rāja`, `puma`, `kamma` (§120–§160)
   3. The pronouns, the `mano` group, `gacchanta`, `brahma`, `atta`, `sakha`, `rāja`, `satthu`, `pitu` (§161–§210)
   4. `atta`, the neuter, the feminine stems, `kiṃ`, `ima` and `eta`, the feminine suffixes, the vocative (§211–§246)
   5. The suffixes that stand for inflections (`to`, `tra`, `dā` …) and the comparatives (§247–§270)
3. [Kārakakappa](/kaccayana/3-karakakappa) (§271–§315): the seven inflection forms and the relations they express
4. [Samāsakappa](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa) (§316–§343): compounds
5. [Taddhitakappa](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa) (§344–§405): nouns made from nouns
6. [Ākhyātakappa](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa) (§406–§523)
   1. The endings and their times (§406–§431)
   2. Derived verbs, the passive, the eight classes of root (§432–§457)
   3. Reduplication, special stems (§458–§481)
   4. Special suffixes and stems (§482–§523)
7. [Kibbidhānakappa](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa) (§524–§623)
   1. The `kicca` and `kita` suffixes (§524–§549)
   2. The suffixes by time: the past, the infinitive and absolutive, the participles (§550–§570)
   3. The shapes of `ta` after each root (§571–§589)
   4. Changes of the root before `ṇ`, `tave` and `tuna` (§590–§606)
   5. Further changes of root and suffix (§607–§623)
8. [Uṇādikappa](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa) (§624–§673): the remaining primary suffixes

## The structure of a sutta

Each `sutta` (rule, or grammatical aphorism) is described in a series of subsections or "fields". The `vutti` is always present, the other fields may be present.

| field | function | tags |
| :-: | --- | --- |
| **vutti** | gloss, or explanation | repeats and expands the words from the sutta |
| **udāharaṇa** | examples | taṃyathā |
| **kimudāharaṇa** | counter-examples | kasmā / kimatthaṃ |
| **payojana** | application/purpose/scope of the rule ("where is it used") | kvattho / kimpayojanaṃ |
| **gahaṇaphala** | result of mentioned words, comments on anuvṛtti, meaning of ca or vā, etc. | (xxx)(sadda)ggahaṇena... eg. rule 35: `casaddaggahaṇena iheva makārassa pakāro hoti`. |

## Types of suttas

The rules can be classified into four categories, and additional subcategories, with examples given below:

* `saññā` (technical term)
  * `anvattha` (meaningful)
    >**8, 10.Aṃiti niggahitaṃ.** <Badge text="anvattha" variant="note" /> \
    >Aṃ iti niggahitaṃ nāma hoti. \
    >Tena kvattho? Aṃ byañjane niggahitaṃ.
  * `rūḷhī` (artificial)
    >**58, 29.Ivaṇṇuvaṇṇā jhalā.** <Badge text="rūḷhī" variant="note" /> \
    >Ivaṇṇuvaṇṇāiccete jhalasaññā honti yathāsaṅkhyaṃ. \
    >Isino , aggino, gahapatino, daṇḍino. Setuno, ketuno, bhikkhuno. Sayambhuno, abhibhuno. \
    >Jhalaiccanena kvattho? Jhalato sassa no vā.
* `adhikāra` (domain)
  * `sīhagatika` (comprehensive) \
    >**52, 60.Jinavacanayuttaṃhi.** <Badge text="sīhagatika" variant="caution" />
    >"Jinavacanayuttaṃ hi" iccetaṃ adhikāratthaṃ veditabbaṃ.
  * `maṇḍūkagatika` (selective)
    >**131, 0.Itthipumanapuṃsakasaṅkhyaṃ.** <Badge text="maṇḍūkagatika" variant="caution" /> \
    >"Itthipumanapuṃsakasaṅkhyaṃ" iccetaṃ adhikāratthaṃ veditabbaṃ.
  * `yathānupubbika` (progressive)
    >**413, 427.Kāle.** <Badge text="yathānupubbika" variant="caution" />
    >"Kāle" iccetaṃ adhikāratthaṃ veditabbaṃ.
* `paribhāsā` (metarule)
  * `saññaṅga` (definition metarule)
    >**9, 11.Parasamaññā payoge.** <Badge text="saññaṅga" variant="tip" /> \
    >Yā ca pana paresu sakkataganthesu samaññā ghosāti vā aghosāti vā, tā payoge sati etthāpi yujjante. \
    >Tattha ghosā nāma-ga gha ṅa, ja jha ña, ḍa ḍha ṇa, da dha na, ba bha ma, ya ra la va ha ḷa, iti ghosā nāma. Aghosā nāma-ka kha, ca cha, ṭa ṭha, ta tha, pa pha, sa, iti aghosā nāma. \
    >Tena kvattho? Vagge ghosāghosānaṃ tatiyapaṭhamā.
  * `vidhyaṅga` (operational metarule)
    >**10, 12.Pubbamadhoṭhita massaraṃ sarena viyojaye.** <Badge text="vidhyaṅga" variant="tip" /> \
    >Tattha sandhiṃ kattukāmo pubbabyañjanaṃ adhoṭhitaṃ assaraṃ katvā sarañca upari katvā sarena viyojaye.
    >Tatrāyamādi.
  * `aññaṅga` (other metarule)
    >**54, 62.Tato ca vibhattiyo.** <Badge text="aññaṅga" variant="tip" /> \
    >Tato jinavacanayuttehi liṅgehi vibhattiyo parā honti.
* `vidhi` (operation)
  * `utsarga` (general rule)
    >**12, 13.Sarā sare lopaṃ.** <Badge text="utsarga" variant="success" /> \
    >Sarā kho sare pare lopaṃ papponti. \
    >Yassindriyāni samathaṅgatāni. No hetaṃ bhante sametāyasmā saṅghena.
  * `apavāda` (exception)
    >**13, 15.Vā paro asarūpā.** <Badge text="apavāda" variant="success" /> \
    >Saramhā asarūpā paro saro lopaṃ pappoti vā. \
    >Cattāro’me bhikkhave dhammā, kinnu’ māvasamaṇiyo. Vāti kasmā? Pañcindriyāni, tayassu dhammā jahitā bhavanti.

According to Aleix Ruiz-Falqués in *A Short Introduction to the Pāli Kaccāyana Grammar*:

>Several `saññā` “technical terms”, \
>within an `adhikāra` “domain”, \
>following `paribhāsā` “metarules”, \
>will be subject to `vidhi` “operations” \
>in order to derive words or sentences.

### Megarules

`mahāsutta` or “megarules” are four rules that are invoked in order to derive forms that cannot be derived otherwise.

* **391, 423.Yadanupapannā nipātanā sijjhanti.** \
  `Ye saddā aniddiṭṭhalakkhaṇā, akkharapadabyañjanato, itthipumanapuṃsakaliṅgato , nāmupasagganipātato, abyayībhāvasamāsataddhitākhyātato, gaṇanasaṅkhyākālakārakappayogasaññāto, sandhipakativuddhilopāgamavikāraviparitato, vibhattivibhajanato ca, te nipātanā sijjhanti.` \
  “Those [words] that are not formed by the suttas, should be formed as irregular forms.”
* **403, 354.Kvacādimajjhuttarānaṃ dīgharassā paccayesu ca.** \
  `Kvaci ādimajjhauttaraiccetesaṃ dīgharassā honti paccayesu ca apaccayesu ca.` \
  “Sometimes, there is lengthening or shortening at the beginning, middle or end [vowels of a word], even before affixes.”
* **404, 370.Tesu vuddhilopāgamavikāraviparītādesā ca.** \
  `Tesu ādimajjhuttaresu yathājinavacanānuparodhena kvaci vuddhi hoti, kvaci lopo hoti, kvaci āgamo hoti, kvaci vikāro hoti, kvaci viparīto hoti, kvaci ādeso hoti.` \
  “And in those cases [cf. rule 405], [sometimes,] there is full grade, elision, augment, modification, change, or replacement.”
* **517, 488.Kvaci dhātuvibhattipaccayānaṃ dīgha viparītādesalopāgamā ca.** \
  `Idha ākhyāte aniddiṭṭhesu sādhanesu kvaci dhātuvibhattipaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamaiccetāni kāriyāni jinavacanānurūpāni kātabbāni.` \
  “Sometimes [for verbal forms that cannot be derived otherwise, there is] lengthening, change, replacement or augment of the verbal root, the personal ending, or the affix(es).”

Rule 52 is halfway between a megarule and a normal rule:

>**52, 60.Jinavacanayuttaṃhi.** \
>"Jinavacanayuttaṃ hi" iccetaṃ adhikāratthaṃ veditabbaṃ.

### Ghost metarules

"Ghost Metarules" are paribhāsās that are consistently invoked in the commentaries, but that you will never see in the sūtra treatise.

Examples:

* `ekamhi vattabbe ekavacanaṃ bahumhi vattabbe bahuvacanan` [e.g. Mmd 28,15–16] \
  “The singular is used when one [object] is to be stated, the plural is used when many [objects] are to be stated.” \
  *[**NOTE**: in Pāṇini, 1.4.21 bahuṣu bahuvacanam (“plural for many [objects]”) & 1.4.22 dvyekayor dvivacanaikavacane (“dual and singular for two or one [objects respectively]”) are considered saṃjñā (“definition”) rules.]*
* `vatticchānupubbikā saddapaṭipatti` [e.g. Mmd 64,17–18] \
  “The meaning of a word is determined by the intention of the speaker.”
* `vuttatthānam appayogo` [23 times in Mmd, e.g. 133,20] \
  “Words whose meaning has already been stated are not used.”
* `yogavibhāgā iṭṭhappasiddhi` [Mmd, e.g. 252,29] \
  “After the splitting up (vibhāgā) of the sutta (yoga) [there is] the expected result [only].”

### Usage of rūḷhī

In a sutta, a `rūḷhī` (artificial technical term) carries the inflection form that matches the role of the term within the rule. For example `jha` (used for masculine/neuter nominal bases ending with `i` or `ī`):

| form | `jha` | usage |
| :-: | :-: | --- |
| ① | `jho` | `i`/`ī` (*replacement) |
| ② | `jhaṃ` | `i`/`ī` (substitute) |
| ③ | `jhena` | *not used* |
| ④ | `jhassa` | *not used* |
| ⑤ | `jhamhā`/`jhato` | “after `i`/`ī`” |
| ⑥ | `jhassa` | “instead of `i`/`ī`” |
| ⑦ | `jhasmiṃ` | “before `i`/`ī`” |

## Types of vidhi (operation)

* ādesa “replacement”
* āgama “insertion”
* vikāra “modification”
* lopa “elision”
* viparīta “metathesis”

## Structure of a vidhi (operation)

An operation converts `from` into `to` surrounded by `before` and `after`, in the scope of `option` and `context`.

| option | before | from | operation | to  | after | context |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | --------- | --- | ----- | ---- |
|        |        |      |           |     |       |      |

As an illustration, the sequence `AXB` is replaced with `AYB`:

| option | before | from | operation | to  | after | context |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | --------- | --- | ----- | ---- |
|        |    A   |   X  | becomes   |  Y  |   B   |      |

Option markers:

* ca
* api
* vā
* kvaci
* na
* niccaṃ
* ...

Context: "in the sense of ..."

Vibhatti endings are used to determine the various roles in the rule

| option | before | from | operation | to  | after | context |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | --------- | --- | ----- | ---- |
|        |    ⑤   |   ⑥  | ādesa   |  ①  |   ⑦   |  ⑦  |
|        |    ⑤   |   ①  | ādesa   |  ①  |   ⑦   |  ⑦  |
|        |    ⑤   |   ①  | ādesa   |  ②  |   ⑦   |  ⑦  |

:::tip[Example]
**§103 `ato n’ ena`**: (After a, nā [becomes] ena):

| option | before | from | operation | to  | after | context |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | --------- | --- | ----- | ---- |
|        |    a   |  nā  |   ādesa   | ena |       |      |
|        |    ⑤   |   ①  | ādesa   |  ①  |       |      |

| | buddha + nā | rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| → | buddha + (nā→ena) | §103 |
| → | **buddhena** | |

:::

:::tip[Example]
**§70: `jhalānam iyuvā sare vā`**: (Optionally, before a vowel: iya replaces i/ī, uva replaces u/ū):

| option | before | from | operation | to  | after | context |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | --------- | --- | ----- | ---- |
|   vā   |        | i/ī  |   ādesa   | iva | sare  |      |
|   vā   |        | u/ū  |   ādesa   | uva | sare  |      |
|   vā   |        |   ⑥  | ādesa   |  ①  |   ⑦   |      |

| | bhikkhu + āsane| rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| → | bhikkh + (u→uva) + (āsane) | §70 |
| → | bhikkh + uv + (~~a~~ + ā) + sane | §12 |
| → | **bhikkhuvāsane** | |

:::

:::tip[Example]
**§12 `sarā sare lopaṃ`**: (Vowel, before vowel, is elided):

| option | before | from | operation | to  | after | context |
| ------ | ------ | ---- | --------- | --- | ----- | ---- |
|        |        | sarā | lopaṃ | ~~sara~~ | sare |      |
|        |        |   ①  | lopaṃ   |     |   ⑦   |      |

| | tena + upasaṅkami | rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| → | ten(~~a~~) + upasaṅkami | §12 |
| → | **tenupasaṅkami** | |

:::

Most vidhi “operational” rules teach paccaya “affixation” or ādesa “replacement” using previous definitions (saññā-s). It is all about adding particles to a base, and then replacing them if/when needed.
To understand vidhi-s, we should keep in mind the following code of inflection forms:

* ⑤ - “after X”
* ⑥ - “instead of X”
* ① - “X”, ādesa
* ⑦ - “before X”
* ⑦ - “in the sense of X”

## Motivation

I set out to translate Kaccāyana anew, and then the two grammars of its school that grew from it, for several reasons:

* I am not entirely satisfied with the existing translations. Some of them seem to lack clarity (primarily due to the writing style). Others don't explain the examples well enough, and yet others are too verbose, inserting explanations not in the original text.
* There are some spelling errors and mistakes in the edition published on [**Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana Tipiṭaka**](https://tipitaka.org), some of which have been incorporated into existing translations. I have taken the opportunity to correct the mistakes, uncovered by scholars such as O. H. Pind and A. Ruiz-Falqués, and to footnote every correction with the edition's reading.
* Unlike some translations, I am honouring the use of option markers in Kaccāyana, and reading them as the tradition read them, after Ruiz-Falqués' two levels of optionality (see [Optionality](#optionality) above).
* I describe the grammar in its own terms, with inflection forms rather than cases, and cross-reference the use of technical terms across the three classical Pāli grammars translated here.
* Translating the Rūpasiddhi and Bālāvatāra alongside Kaccāyana shows how the school itself taught the rules: Buddhappiya's paradigms and derivations make explicit what Kaccāyana leaves to the reader, and Dhammakitti's abridgement shows what a beginner was expected to know.
* It is a good way to further my understanding of Pāli.
* It is also a good opportunity for me to introduce the [Brāhmī script](/reference/brahmi). As we know, there are no surviving Buddhist texts written in Brāhmī, so there is no need to learn this script, however, I like it due to its elegance and simplicity.
* I wanted to augment the translations with diagrams, and incorporate worked through grammatical analysis and rule derivations in the examples.
* In comparison with the other classical Pāli grammars, Kaccāyana is brief and succinct, minimising repetition. It was used as a standard textbook for teaching Pāli in countries such as Burma for many years, and often used in various monasteries as a Pāli reference text.
* I prefer Kaccāyana to most English Pāli textbooks and grammar books, as it describes Pāli without the distorting lens of Western linguistics.
* My translation is presented using a modern responsive web design, featuring Google's Noto Sans font.
