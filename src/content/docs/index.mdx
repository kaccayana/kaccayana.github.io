**Kaccāyana** is the earliest surviving Pāli grammar, traditionally ascribed to the Buddha's disciple Mahākaccāyana and compiled around the sixth or seventh century CE. Two later works of its school are translated alongside it: Buddhappiya's **Padarūpasiddhi** (thirteenth century), which recasts its rules in a teaching order with full paradigms and the derivation of every form, and Dhammakitti's **Bālāvatāra** (fourteenth century), the primer for beginners that abridges both. The three texts are translated in one style and cross-referenced rule by rule, so that a Kaccāyana sutta, Buddhappiya's treatment of it and Dhammakitti's abridgement are always a click apart.

:::note[Status]
The three translations are essentially complete — Kaccāyana §1–§673, the Padarūpasiddhi Rūp 1–684 and Bālāvatāra ¶1–¶269 — and have been validated against a range of reference sources: the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana text itself, whose misprints are corrected and footnoted; the Tipiṭaka, from which every example that could be traced is cited; the five earlier English translations reproduced on this site, each collated sutta by sutta, together with the readings of the Thai, Burmese and Sinhalese editions and of the commentaries (the Nyāsa, the Padarūpasiddhi-ṭīkā, the Saddanīti) that they report; and the studies of Aleix Ruiz-Falqués on optionality, which the analysis of every sutta follows. Where a reading or a construal differs, a footnote says so.
:::

## Features

<CardGrid>
	<Card title="Three related texts" icon="open-book">
		Kaccāyana, its twelfth- or thirteenth-century recast and its fourteenth-century primer, translated in one consistent style, with every later rule linked to the Kaccāyana sutta it is.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Modern terminology" icon="translate">
		The grammar is described in its own terms, in modern natural English: inflection forms ①…⑦ with ⨀ singular and ⨂ plural rather than Latin cases and declensions, and every technical term kept in Pāli and explained.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Optionality as the tradition read it" icon="pen">
		Every `vā`, `kvaci`, `navā` and `vibhāsā` is analysed after Aleix Ruiz-Falqués' account of the two levels of optionality: an open option, or one restricted to a stated domain.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Worked examples" icon="list-format">
		Every example is derived step by step, rule by rule, and its Tipiṭaka source is cited and linked to [tipitaka2500.github.io](https://tipitaka2500.github.io); counter-examples explain what a rule does not do.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Cross-checked" icon="approve-check">
		Validated against a range of reference sources: collated sutta by sutta with the five earlier English translations reproduced here (Nandisena, Malai, D'Alwis, Rachiwong and the 1935 Calcutta Bālāvatāra) and with the editions and commentaries they report; where a reading or a construal differs, a footnote says so.
	</Card>
	<Card title="Structure made visible" icon="document">
		Diagrams of the kinds of rule, maps of each text onto Kaccāyana's chapters, decision charts for sandhi, and taxonomies of compounds, suffixes and model nouns.
	</Card>
</CardGrid>
