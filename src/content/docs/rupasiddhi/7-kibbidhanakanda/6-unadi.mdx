## Uṇādippaccayantanaya (The `uṇādi` suffixes)

> Atha uṇā dayo vuccante.

Now `uṇa` and the rest (`uṇādi`) are stated.

> ‘‘Dhātuyā’’ti adhikāro.

"`Dhātuyā`" (after a root) is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

---

<a name="r651"></a>

### 651 (§650): Kāle vattamānātīte ṇvādayo (`ṇu` and the rest in present and past time) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§650 (651): Kāle vattamānātīte ṇvādayo" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m650" description="`ṇu` and the rest in present and past time" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) “`dhātuyā`” (after a root), announced before this rule, is carried over from [561 (§524): Dhātuyā kammādimhi ṇo](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r561).

:::

> Atīte kāle, vattamāne ca gammamāne dhātūhi ṇuppaccayo hoti. Ādi saddena yu kta mi iccādayo ca honti.

When past time or present time is understood, the suffix `ṇu` is used after roots; and by the word `ādi` ("and the rest") the suffixes `yu`, `kta`, `mi` and the like are used as well.

> Kara karaṇe, akāsi, karotīti vā atthe ṇuppaccayo, ṇa lopo, vuddhi, kāru sippī, kārū kāravo. Vā gatigandhanesu, avāyi, vāyatīti vā vāyu, āyā deso. Sada assādane, assādīyatīti sādu. Rādha, sādha saṃsiddhimhi, sādhīyati anena hitanti sādhu. Bandha bandhane, attani paraṃ bandhatīti bandhu. Cakkha viyattiyaṃ vācāyaṃ, cakkhatīti cakkhu. I gatimhi, enti gacchanti pavattanti sattā etenāti āyu. Dara vidāraṇe, darīyatīti dāru kaṭṭhaṃ. Sanu dāne. Sanotīti sānu pabbatekadeso. Janīyatīti jānu jaṅghāsandhi. Carīyatīti cāru dassanīyo. Raha cāge, rahīyatīti rāhu asurindo. Tara taraṇe, tālu, lo ra ssa.

`kara` is in the sense of doing: in the sense "he made (`akāsi`)" or "he makes (`karoti`)", the suffix `ṇu` is used, the `ṇ` is elided and `vuddhi` applies: `kāru` ("a craftsman", `sippī`); `kārū`, `kāravo` ("craftsmen"). `vā` is in the senses of going and harming: "it blew (`avāyi`)" or "it blows (`vāyati`)" gives `vāyu` ("wind"), with the replacement by `āya`. `sada` is in the sense of relishing: "it is relished (`assādīyati`)" gives `sādu` ("sweet"). `rādha` and `sādha` are in the sense of accomplishing: "welfare is accomplished (`sādhīyati`) by it" gives `sādhu` ("good"). `bandha` is in the sense of binding: "he binds (`bandhati`) another to himself" gives `bandhu` ("a relative"). `cakkha` is in the sense of distinct speech: "it declares (`cakkhati`)" gives `cakkhu` ("the eye"). `i` is in the sense of going: "beings go (`enti`), move and carry on by means of it" gives `āyu` ("life"). `dara` is in the sense of splitting: "it is split (`darīyati`)" gives `dāru` ("wood", `kaṭṭha`). `sanu` is in the sense of giving: "it gives (`sanoti`)" gives `sānu` ("a plateau"), a part of a mountain. "It is produced (`janīyati`)" gives `jānu` ("the knee"), the joint of the lower leg. "It is practised (`carīyati`)" gives `cāru` ("lovely"), lovely to look at. `raha` is in the sense of forsaking: "he is forsaken (`rahīyati`)" gives `rāhu` ("Rāhu"), the lord of the asuras. `tara` is in the sense of crossing: `tālu` ("the palate"), with `l` for the `r`.

> Marā dīnaṃ panettha ṇu mhi ‘‘ghaṭādīnaṃ vā’’ti ettha vā saddena na vuddhi, maru, taru, tanu, dhanu, hanu, manu, asu, vasu, vaṭu, garu iccādi.

Here, however, `mara` and the rest take no `vuddhi` before `ṇu`, by the word `vā` in [§484 (542): Ghaṭādīnaṃ vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m484): `maru` ("a god"), `taru` ("a tree"), `tanu` ("the body"), `dhanu` ("a bow"), `hanu` ("the jaw"), `manu` ("Manu"), `asu` ("breath"), `vasu` ("wealth"), `vaṭu` ("a lad"), `garu` ("a teacher"), and so on.

> Cadi hilādane, yu ppaccayo, ‘‘nudādīhi yuṇvūnamanānanākānanakā sakāritehi cā’’ti anā deso, niggahītāgamo ca, candanaṃ. Bhavati etthāti bhuvanaṃ, ‘‘jhalānamiyuvāsare vā’’ti uvā deso. Kira vikkhepe, kiraṇo. Vicakkhaṇo, kampanaṃ karotīti karuṇā, a kārassu ttaṃ.

`cadi` is in the sense of gladdening: the suffix `yu` is used and replaced by `ana` by [§641 (572): Nudādīhi yuṇvūnamanānanākānanakā sakāritehi ca](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m641), and niggahīta is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)): `candanaṃ` ("sandalwood"). "It comes to be (`bhavati`) here" gives `bhuvanaṃ` ("the world"), with the replacement by `uva` by [§70 (30): Jhalānamiyuvā sare vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m70). `kira` is in the sense of scattering: `kiraṇo` ("a ray"). `vicakkhaṇo` ("discerning"). "It causes trembling (`kampanaṃ karoti`)" gives `karuṇā` ("compassion"), with `u` for the letter `a`.

> Kta ppaccaye – ka lopo, abhavi, bhavatīti vā bhūtaṃ yakkhādi, bhūtāni. Vāyatīti vāto, tāyatīti tāto. Mi mhi – bhavanti etthāti bhūmi, netīti nemi iccādi.

Before the suffix `kta`, the `k` is elided: "it was (`abhavi`)" or "it is (`bhavati`)" gives `bhūtaṃ` ("a being"), a yakkha and the like; `bhūtāni` ("beings"). "It blows (`vāyati`)" gives `vāto` ("wind"); "he protects (`tāyati`)" gives `tāto` ("father"). Before `mi`: "they come to be (`bhavanti`) on it" gives `bhūmi` ("the earth"); "it leads (`neti`)" gives `nemi` ("the rim of a wheel"); and so on.

### 652 (§627): Khyādīhi mana ma ca to vā (`mana` after `khi` and the like; and optionally `t` for its `m`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§627 (652): Khyādīhi mana ma ca to vā" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m627" description="`mana` after `khi` and the like; and optionally `t` for its `m`" />
:::

> Khī bhī su ru hu vā dhū hi lūpī ada iccevamādīhi dhātūhi mana paccayo hoti, ma ssa ca to hoti vā.

After the roots `khī`, `bhī`, `su`, `ru`, `hu`, `vā`, `dhū`, `hi`, `lū`, `pī`, `ada` and the like, the suffix `mana` is used, and its `m` optionally becomes `t`.

> Adadhātuparasseva, makārassa takāratā; \
> Tadaññato na hotāyaṃ, vavatthitavibhāsato.

Only after the root `ada` does the letter `m` become `t`; \
after any other root it does not — by the restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`).

> Khī khaye, khīyanti e ttha upaddavupasaggādayoti atthe mana paccayo, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā na lopo, ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti vuddhi, khemo. Tathā bhī bhaye, bhāyanti etasmāti bhīmo, vā dhikārato na vuddhi. Su abhisave, savatīti somo. Ru gatimhi, romo. Hu dānādanahabyappadānesu, hūyatīti homo. Vā gatigandhanesu, vāmo. Dhū kampane, dhunātīti dhūmo. Hi gatimhi, hinotīti hemo. Lū chedane, lūyatīti lomo. Pī tappane, pīṇanaṃ pemo. Ada bhakkhaṇe, adatīti atthe mana, ma ssa ca vā ta kāro, ‘‘to dassā’’ti ta kāro, attā, ātumā, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ada ssa dīgho, u kārāgamo ca. Yā pāpaṇe, yāmo.

`khī` is in the sense of wearing away: in the sense "misfortunes, troubles and the like wear away (`khīyanti`) here", the suffix `mana` is used; the `na` is elided by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and `vuddhi` applies by [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485): `khemo` ("safety"). Likewise `bhī` is in the sense of fearing: "they fear (`bhāyanti`) because of it" gives `bhīmo` ("terrible"), with no `vuddhi` by the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) “`vā`”. `su` is in the sense of extracting: "it flows (`savati`)" gives `somo` ("the moon"). `ru` is in the sense of going: `romo` ("body hair"). `hu` is in the senses of giving, eating and offering an oblation: "it is offered (`hūyati`)" gives `homo` ("an oblation"). `vā` is in the senses of going and harming: `vāmo` ("left; agreeable"). `dhū` is in the sense of shaking: "it shakes (`dhunāti`)" gives `dhūmo` ("smoke"). `hi` is in the sense of going: "it moves (`hinoti`)" gives `hemo` ("gold"). `lū` is in the sense of cutting: "it is cut (`lūyati`)" gives `lomo` ("hair"). `pī` is in the sense of pleasing: "gladdening (`pīṇanaṃ`)" gives `pemo` ("love"). `ada` is in the sense of eating: in the sense "it eats (`adati`)", `mana` is used and its `m` optionally becomes `t`; by “`to dassa`” (`t` for the `d`) the letter `t` is used: `attā` ("the self"); and `ātumā` ("the self"), with the lengthening of `ada` and the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of `u` by §517. `yā` is in the sense of reaching: `yāmo` ("a watch of the night").

### 653 (§628): Samādihi thamā (`tha` and `ma` after `samu` and the like) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§628 (653): Samādīhi tha mā" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m628" description="`tha` and `ma` after `samu` and the like" />
:::

> Samadamadara raha sapa[^26] vasa yu du hi si dā sā ṭhā bhasa bahausu iccevamādīhi dhātūhi tha ma iccete paccayā honti vā.

After the roots `sama`, `dama`, `dara`, `raha`, `sapa`, `vasa`, `yu`, `du`, `hi`, `si`, `dā`, `sā`, `ṭhā`, `bhasa`, `baha`, `usu` and the like, the suffixes `tha` and `ma` are optionally used.

> Sama upasame, kvaci ggahaṇādhikārā na dhātvantalopo, kilese sametīti samatho samādhi. Evaṃ damanaṃ damatho. Dara dāhe, daraṇaṃ daratho paridāho. Raha upādāne, rahīyatīti ratho, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ha lopo. Sapa akkose, sapanaṃ sapatho. Vasa nivāse, āvasanti etasminti āvasatho. Yu missane, yūtho, dīgho. Du gativuddhimhi, davati vaḍḍhatīti dumo. Hinotīti himo ussāvo. Si bandhane, sīyatīti sīmā, dīgho. Dā avakhaṇḍane, dāmo. Sā sāmatthe, sāmo. Ṭhā gatinivattimhi, thāmo, ṭha ssa tha ttaṃ. Bhasa bhasmīkaraṇe, bhasmā, brahmā dittā ‘‘syā cā’’ti ā ttaṃ. Baha vuddhimhi, brahmā, nipātanato bro ba ssa. Usu dāhe, usmā iccādi.

`sama` is in the sense of calming: by the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) of the mention of `kvaci`, the root's final is not elided; "it calms (`sameti`) the defilements" gives `samatho` ("calm", `samādhi`). Likewise "taming (`damanaṃ`)" gives `damatho` ("taming"). `dara` is in the sense of burning: "burning (`daraṇaṃ`)" gives `daratho` ("distress", `paridāha`). `raha` is in the sense of taking up: "it is taken up (`rahīyati`)" gives `ratho` ("a chariot"), with the `h` elided by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517). `sapa` is in the sense of abusing: "swearing (`sapanaṃ`)" gives `sapatho` ("an oath"). `vasa` is in the sense of dwelling: "they dwell (`āvasanti`) in it" gives `āvasatho` ("a dwelling"). `yu` is in the sense of mixing: `yūtho` ("a herd"), with lengthening. `du` is in the senses of going and growing: "it goes (`davati`), it grows (`vaḍḍhati`)" gives `dumo` ("a tree"). "It moves (`hinoti`)" gives `himo` ("dew", `ussāva`). `si` is in the sense of binding: "it is bound (`sīyati`)" gives `sīmā` ("a boundary"), with lengthening. `dā` is in the sense of cutting off: `dāmo` ("a cord"). `sā` is in the sense of being able: `sāmo` ("dark"). `ṭhā` is in the sense of ceasing to move: `thāmo` ("strength"), with `th` for the `ṭh`. `bhasa` is in the sense of reducing to ashes: `bhasmā` ("ashes"), which takes `ā` by [§189 (113): Syā ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m189) because it belongs to the `brahma` group. `baha` is in the sense of growing: `brahmā` ("Brahmā"), with `bra` for the `ba` by direct statement. `usu` is in the sense of burning: `usmā` ("heat"); and so on.

:::tip[Derivation]

| →   | samatha + (si→o) | 66 (§104) |
| →   | **samatho**      |      |

|     | √raha + tha       | rule |
| --- | ----------------- | ---- |
| →   | rah + ~~a~~ + tha | 425 (§521) |
| →   | ra + ~~h~~ + tha  | 488 (§517) |
| →   | ratha + si        | 334 (§601), 63 (§55) |
| →   | ratha + (si→o)    | 66 (§104) |
| →   | **ratho**         |      |

|     | √baha + ma       | rule |
| --- | ---------------- | ---- |
| →   | bah + ~~a~~ + ma | 425 (§521) |

### 654 (§630): Masussa sussa ccha ra ccherā (`cchara`, `cchera` for the `su` of `masu`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§630 (654): Masussa sussa cchara ccherā" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m630" description="`cchara`, `cchera` for the `su` of `masu`" />

> Masu iccetassa dhātussa su ssa ccharacchera iccete ādesā honti. Masu macchere, kvi ppaccayo, ccharaccherā desā, maccharo, macchero.

### 660 (§638): Vajādīhi pabbajjādayo nipaccante (`pabbajjā` and the like are formed from `vaja` and the like as they stand) :badge[vidhyaṅga]{variant=tip}

From the roots `vaja` and the like, the words `pabbajjā` and the like are formed as they stand (`nipaccante`), by the operation ([`vidhi`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) of suffix, replacement, elision, insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), prohibition, gender and the rest, as they are found expressed.

### 670 (§660): Sūvusānamūvusānamato tho ca (`at` for the `ū`, `u`, `asa` of `sū`, `vu`, `asa`; and `tha`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]

> Hana iccevamādīhi dhātūhi ṇu nu tu iccete paccayā honti. Ṇu ppaccaye hana hiṃsāgatīsu, hanatīti haṇu. Jana janane, jāyatīti jāṇu, dhātvantalopo, dīgho. Bhā dittimhi, bhātīti bhāṇu. After the roots `hana` and the like, the suffixes `ṇu`, `nu` and `tu` are used. ### 684 (§604): Akkharehi kāra (The suffix `kāra` after the names of letters) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

> Akkharehi akkharavācakehi vaṇṇehi kāra ppaccayo hoti. Taddhitā disutte ca ggahaṇena nāmabyapadese syā dyuppatti, akāro, akāraṃ, akārena iccādi, ākāro, okāro, kakāro, yakāro, hakāro, ḷakāro. After the letters, the sounds that name letters, the suffix `kāra` is used. By the mention of `ca` in the rule beginning `Taddhita` ([§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m601)), [the form] is designated a noun and `si` and the rest arise: `akāro`, `akāraṃ`, `akārena` ("the letter `a`"), and so on; `ākāro` ("the letter `ā`"), `okāro` ("the letter `o`"), `kakāro` ("the letter `k`"), `yakāro` ("the letter `y`"), `hakāro` ("the letter `h`"), `ḷakāro` ("the letter `ḷ`"). In `evakāro` and the like, however, the word `kāro` means "it is made, it is uttered" (`karīyati`, `uccārīyati`), and "it is `eva` and it is an utterance (`kāro`)" — `evakāro` ("the word `eva`"). Likewise `dhikāro` ("the word `dhi`"), `huṃkāro` ("the sound `huṃ`"), `sādhukāro` ("the word `sādhu`, applause").

> Bhavantettha gamissādi, hanatyādīti ñāpakā.

The letter `i` in the citation of a root, and [a root] set down with its class sign (`vikaraṇa`) and the ending `ti`: \
the indicators (`ñāpaka`) of these here are `gamissa` and the like, and `hanati` and the like.

---

> _Uṇādippaccayantanayo._

Thus ends the treatment of [words] ending in the `uṇādi` suffixes.

---

> Tabbādī ṇādayo niṭṭhā, tave tunādayo tathā; \
> Mānantādi uṇādīti, chaddhā kitakasaṅgaho.

`tabba` and the rest, `ṇa` and the rest, the `niṭṭhā` [participles], likewise `tave`, `tuna` and the rest, \
`māna`, `anta` and the rest, and the `uṇādi`: thus the `kita` [suffixes] are collected in six groups.

> _Iti padarūpasiddhiyaṃ kibbidhānakaṇḍo sattamo._

Thus [ends] the Kibbidhānakaṇḍa, the seventh section of the Padarūpasiddhi.

---

## Nigamana (Colophon)

> Sandhi nāmaṃ kārakañca, samāso taddhitaṃ tathā; \
> Ākhyātaṃ kitakaṃ kaṇḍā, sattime rūpasiddhiyaṃ.

Sandhi, noun and `kāraka`, compound, likewise `taddhita`, \
verb and `kita`: these are the seven sections in the Rūpasiddhi.

> Tedhā sandhiṃ catuddhā padamapi catudhā pañcadhā nāmikañca, \
> Byāsā chakkārakaṃ chassamasanamapi chabbhedato taddhitañca; \
> Ākhyātaṃ aṭṭhadhā chabbidhamapi kitakaṃ paccayānaṃ pabhedā, \
> Dīpentī rūpasiddhī ciramidha janatābuddhivuḍḍhiṃ karotu.

Showing sandhi in three ways, the word too in four ways, and the noun in four and in five ways, \
the six `kāraka` in detail, the compound too of six kinds, and the `taddhita` in six divisions, \
the verb in eight ways, and the `kita` too of six kinds by the divisions of its suffixes, \
may the Rūpasiddhi long bring growth to the understanding of the people here.

> Vikhyātānandathera vhayavaragurunaṃ tambapaṇṇiddhajānaṃ, \
> Sisso dīpaṅkarā khyaddamiḷavasumatī dīpaladdhappakāso; \
> Bālādiccādivāsadvitayamadhivasaṃ sāsanaṃ jotayī yo, \
> Soyaṃ buddhappiya vho yati imamujukaṃ rūpasiddhiṃ a kāsi.

The pupil of the excellent teacher known as the renowned Elder Ānanda, the banner of Tambapaṇṇi, \
[himself] called Dīpaṅkara, who won renown as the lamp of the Damiḷa land, \
who, dwelling in the two residences, Bālādicca and the other,[^29] made the Teaching shine — \
that monk (`yati`) named Buddhappiya composed this straightforward Rūpasiddhi.

> _Iti padarūpasiddhipakaraṇaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ._

Thus the treatise Padarūpasiddhi is finished.
