## Tekālika (Suffixes of all three times)

## Kiccappaccayantanaya (The `kicca` suffixes)

> Atha dhātūhiyeva bhāvakammakattukaraṇādisādhanasahitaṃ kibbidhānamārabhīyate.

Now begins the `kibbidhāna`, the prescription of the `kita` suffixes, which are added after roots alone, together with the `sādhana`: the action itself (`bhāva`), the object (`kamma`), the agent (`kattu`), the instrument (`karaṇa`) and the rest.

> Tattha kiccakitakavasena duvidhā hi paccayā, tesu kiccasaññāya paṭhamaṃ vuttattā, kiccānamappakattā ca kiccappaccayā tāva vuccante.

Among these the suffixes are indeed of two kinds, `kicca` and `kita`; of the two, the `kicca` suffixes are stated first, because the technical term `kicca` is stated first and because the `kicca` suffixes are few.

> Bhū sattāyaṃ, ‘‘bhūyate, abhavittha, bhavissate vā devadattenā’’ti viggahe –

The root `bhū` in the sense of being: in the analysis (`viggaha`) "there is being, there was being, or there will be being, by Devadatta" (`bhūyate`, `abhavittha`, `bhavissate vā devadattena`) —

> ‘‘Dhātuyā kammādimhi ṇo’’ti ito ‘‘dhātuyā’’ti sabbattha paccayādividhāne vattate, ‘‘parā, paccayā’’ti ca adhikāro.

“`Dhātuyā`” ("after a root") continues from [§524 (561): Dhātuyā kammādimhi ṇo](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m524) into every prescription of suffixes and the rest, and “`parā paccayā`” ("the suffixes come after", from [§432 (362): Dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m432)) is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

### 545 (§540): Bhāvakammesu tabbānīyā (`tabba`, `anīya` in the senses of the action itself and of the object) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§540 (545): Bhāvakammesu tabbānīyā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m540" description="`tabba`, `anīya` in the senses of the action itself and of the object" />
:::

> Bhāvakamma iccetesvatthesu sabbadhātūhi tabba anīya iccete paccayā parā honti. Yogavibhāgena aññatthāpi.

The suffixes `tabba` and `anīya` are used after all roots in the senses of the action itself (`bhāva`) and of the object (`kamma`); by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) they are used in other senses too.

> Tattha –

On this —

> Akammakehi dhātūhi, bhāve kiccā bhavanti te; \
> Sakammakehi kammatthe, arahasakkatthadīpakā.

Those `kicca` suffixes arise after intransitive roots in the sense of the action itself, \
and after transitive roots in the sense of the object; they express the senses of "deserving" (`araha`) and "able" (`sakka`).

> Te ca –

And they —

### 546 (§550): Ṇādayo tekālikā (`ṇa` and the rest belong to all three times) :badge[vidhyaṅga]{variant=tip}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§550 (546): Ṇādayo tekālikā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m550" description="`ṇa` and the rest belong to all three times" />
:::

> Tikāle niyuttā tekālikā, ye idha tatiye dhātvādhikāre vihitā aniddiṭṭhakālā ṇādayo paccayā, te tekālikā hontīti paribhāsato kālattayepi honti.

`Tekālika` means assigned to the three times (`tikāla`); so by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) "the suffixes `ṇa` and the rest that are prescribed here, in this third section, under the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) “`dhātu`” (root), without a time being specified, belong to all three times", they are used in all three times as well.

> Sīhagatiyā ‘‘kvacī’’ti vattate.

“`Kvaci`” ("in some places") continues comprehensively ([`sīhagatiyā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

### 547 (§605): Yathāgamamikāro (The insertion of `i` between root and suffix, as the tradition has it) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§605 (547): Yathāgamamikāro" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m605" description="The insertion of `i` between root and suffix, as the tradition has it" />
:::

> Yathāgamaṃ yathāpayogaṃ jinavacanānuparodhena dhātūhi paro ikārāgamo hoti kvaci byañjanādikesu kiccakitakappaccayesu, ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti vuddhi, ‘‘o ava sare’’ti avādeso, ‘‘naye paraṃ yutte’’ti paraṃ netabbaṃ.

As the tradition has it (`yathāgamaṃ`), that is, according to usage and without conflicting with the word of the Conqueror, the letter `i` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) after roots in some places (`kvaci`), before `kicca` and `kita` suffixes that begin with a consonant; then `vuddhi` by [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485), the replacement `ava` by [§513 (435): O ava sare](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m513), and [the consonant] is joined on to what follows by [§11 (14): Naye paraṃ yutte](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m11).

<a name="r548"></a>

### 548 (§545): Te kiccā (those [suffixes] are called `kicca`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§545 (548): Te kiccā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m545" description="those [suffixes] are called `kicca`" />
:::

> Ye idha vuttā tabbānīyaṇyateyyariccappaccayā, te kiccasaññā hontīti veditabbā. Tato ‘‘aññe kiti’’ti vacanato kiccappaccayānamakitakattā nāmabyapadese asampatte ‘‘taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃvātavetunādīsu cā’’ti ettha caggahaṇena nāmabyapadeso, tato syādyuppatti. Bhāve bhāvassekattā ekavacanameva, ‘‘si’’nti amādeso. Bhavitabbaṃ bhavatā paññena, bhavanīyaṃ.

The suffixes stated here, `tabba`, `anīya`, `ṇya`, `teyya` and `ricca`, are to be understood as having the technical name `kicca`. Then, since by the statement [§546 (562): Aññe kita](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m546) the `kicca` suffixes are not `kita`, the designation of noun (`nāmabyapadesa`) would not apply to them, so it is obtained by the word `ca` in [§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m601), and then `si` and the other inflections arise. In the sense of the action itself only the singular is used, because the action is one, and `aṃ` replaces `si` by [§219 (195): Siṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m219): `bhavitabbaṃ bhavatā paññena` ("you must be wise"), `bhavanīyaṃ`.

> Idha byañjanādittābhāvā anuvattitakvaciggahaṇena ikārāgamābhāvo. Bhāve kiccappaccayantā napuṃsakā. Kamme tiliṅgā.

Here, since [the suffix] does not begin with a consonant, and by the mention of `kvaci` that has continued, the letter `i` is not inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)). In the sense of the action itself, words ending in `kicca` suffixes are neuter; in the sense of the object they have all three genders.

> Kammani abhipubbo, abhibhūyate, abhibhūyittha, abhibhūyissateti abhibhavitabbo kodho paṇḍitena, abhibhavitabbā taṇhā, abhibhavitabbaṃ dukkhaṃ, evaṃ abhibhavanīyo, abhibhavanīyā, abhibhavanīyaṃ, purisakaññācittasaddanayena netabbaṃ, evaṃ sabbattha.

In the sense of the object, with `abhi` before it: "it is overcome, it was overcome, it will be overcome" (`abhibhūyate`, `abhibhūyittha`, `abhibhūyissate`) — `abhibhavitabbo kodho paṇḍitena` ("anger is to be overcome by the wise"), `abhibhavitabbā taṇhā` ("craving is to be overcome"), `abhibhavitabbaṃ dukkhaṃ` ("suffering is to be overcome"); likewise `abhibhavanīyo`, `abhibhavanīyā`, `abhibhavanīyaṃ`. They are to be inflected on the model of the words `purisa`, `kaññā` and `citta`, and so everywhere.

> Ettha hi –

For here —

> Tabbādīheva kammassa, vuttattāva punattanā; \
> Vattabbassa abhāvā na, dutiyā paṭhamā tato.

Since the object is already expressed by `tabba` and the rest themselves, \
and nothing is left for it to state on its own, the second form ② is not used; hence the first ①.

> Āsa upavesane, āsīyittha, āsīyate, āsīyissateti āsitabbaṃ tayā, āsanīyaṃ. Kamme upāsitabbo garu, upāsanīyo.

The root `āsa` in the sense of sitting: "it was sat, it is sat, it will be sat" (`āsīyittha`, `āsīyate`, `āsīyissate`) — `āsitabbaṃ tayā` ("you should sit"), `āsanīyaṃ`. In the sense of the object: `upāsitabbo garu` ("the teacher is to be attended upon"), `upāsanīyo`.

> Sī saye, asīyittha, sīyate, sīyissateti sayitabbaṃ bhavatā, sayanīyaṃ, ‘‘e ayā’’ti ayādeso, atisayitabbo paro, atisayanīyo.

The root `sī` in the sense of lying down: `asīyittha`, `sīyate`, `sīyissate` — `sayitabbaṃ bhavatā` ("you should lie down"), `sayanīyaṃ`, with the replacement `aya` by [§514 (491): E aya](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m514); `atisayitabbo paro` ("the other is to be surpassed"), `atisayanīyo`.

> Pada gatimhi, uppajjittha, uppajjate, uppajjissateti uppajjitabbaṃ tena, uppajjanīyaṃ, ettha ca ‘‘kattarī’’ti adhikāraṃ vinā ‘‘divādito yo’’ti vinādhikārayogavibhāgena yappaccayo, ‘‘tassa cavaggayakāra’’iccādinā cavaggo, ‘‘paradvebhāvo ṭhāne’’ti dvibhāvo, paṭipajjitabbo maggo, paṭipajjanīyo.

The root `pada` in the sense of going: `uppajjittha`, `uppajjate`, `uppajjissate` — `uppajjitabbaṃ tena` ("he should arise"), `uppajjanīyaṃ`. Here the suffix `ya` is added by [§447 (510): Divādito yo](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m447) taken without its governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) “`kattari`” ("in the sense of the agent"), by splitting the rule (`vinādhikārayogavibhāga`); the `c`-group by [§441 (447): Tassa cavaggayakāravakārattaṃ sadhātvantassa](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m441) and the like; and doubling by [§28 (40): Para dvebhāvo ṭhāne](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m28). `paṭipajjitabbo maggo` ("the path is to be followed"), `paṭipajjanīyo`.

> Budha avagamane, abujjhittha, bujjhate, bujjhissateti bujjhitabbo dhammo, bujjhanīyo.

The root `budha` in the sense of understanding: `abujjhittha`, `bujjhate`, `bujjhissate` — `bujjhitabbo dhammo` ("the Dhamma is to be understood"), `bujjhanīyo`.

> Su savaṇe, asūyittha, sūyate, sūyissateti sotabbo dhammo, idha yathāgamaggahaṇena ikārāgamābhāvo, suṇitabbo, ‘‘svādito ṇu ṇā uṇā cā’’ti vinādhikārayogavibhāgena ṇāpaccayo, savaṇīyo.

The root `su` in the sense of hearing: `asūyittha`, `sūyate`, `sūyissate` — `sotabbo dhammo` ("the Dhamma is to be heard"), where by the mention of `yathāgamaṃ` the letter `i` is not inserted; `suṇitabbo`, with the suffix `ṇā` by [§448 (512): Svādito ṇu ṇā uṇā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m448) taken without its governing rule, by splitting the rule; `savaṇīyo`.

> Kara karaṇe, karīyittha, karīyati, karīyissatīti atthe tabbā’nīyā.

The root `kara` in the sense of doing: in the sense "it was done, it is done, it will be done" (`karīyittha`, `karīyati`, `karīyissati`), `tabba` and `anīya` [are added] —

> ‘‘Antassa, karassa, ca, tatta’’nti ca vattate.

“`Antassa`”, “`karassa`”, “`ca`” and “`tattaṃ`” also continue.

### 549 (§620): Tuṃ tu na tabbesu vā (Optionally [`t` for the `r` of `kara`] before `tuṃ`, `tuna`, `tabba`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§620 (549): Tuṃ tuna tabbesu vā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m620" description="Optionally [`t` for the `r` of `kara`] before `tuṃ`, `tuna`, `tabba`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The words `antassa`, `karassa`, `ca` and `tattaṃ` that continue into this rule are those of [573 (§619): Karassa ca tattaṃ tusmiṃ](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r573), and `karotissa` is that of [582 (§594): Purasamupaparīhi karotissa khakharā vā tappaccayesu ca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r582); both come later in the kaṇḍa.

:::

> Kara iccetassa dhātussa antabhūtassa rakārassa takārattaṃ hoti vā tuṃ tu na tabba iccetesu paccayesu paresu. Kattabbo bhavatā dhammo, kattabbā pūjā, kattabbaṃ kusalaṃ, tattābhāve ‘‘karotissā’’ti vattamāne ‘‘tavetunādīsu kā’’ti ettha ādisaddena tabbepi kādeso, kātabbaṃ hitaṃ.

The final letter `r` of the root `kara` optionally becomes the letter `t` when the suffixes `tuṃ`, `tuna` and `tabba` follow: `kattabbo bhavatā dhammo` ("the Dhamma is to be practised by you"), `kattabbā pūjā` ("worship is to be performed"), `kattabbaṃ kusalaṃ` ("good is to be done"). When the change to `t` is absent, while “`karotissa`” continues, the replacement `kā` is made before `tabba` as well, by the word `ādi` in [§595 (637): Tave tunādīsu kā](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m595): `kātabbaṃ hitaṃ` ("what is beneficial is to be done").

### 550 (§549): Rahādito ṇa (`ṇ` for the `n` [of `ana`] after roots ending in `r`, `h` and the like) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§549 (550): Rahādito ṇa" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m549" description="`ṇ` for the `n` [of `ana`] after roots ending in `r`, `h` and the like" />
:::

> Rakāra hakārādyantehi[^1] dhātūhi parassa anānīyādinakārassa ṇakāro hoti. Ādisaddena ramu apa ñā tāditopi.

After roots ending in the letters `r`, `h` and the like, the `n` of a following `ana`, `anīya` or the like becomes the letter `ṇ`; by the word `ādi` it does so after `ramu`, `apa`, `ñā`, `tā` and the like as well.

> Rahādito parassettha, nakārassa asambhavā; \
> Anānīyādinasseva, sāmathyāyaṃ ṇakāratā.

Since here a following letter `n` is impossible after `ra`, `ha` and the rest, \
by the force of the context (`sāmatthiya`) this change to `ṇ` is of the `n` of `ana`, `anīya` and the like alone.

> Karaṇīyo dhammo, karaṇārahoti attho, karaṇīyā, karaṇīyaṃ.

`karaṇīyo dhammo` ("the Dhamma is to be practised"), the meaning being "deserving to be done" (`karaṇāraha`); `karaṇīyā`, `karaṇīyaṃ`.

> Bhara bharaṇe, bharīyatīti bharitabbo, bharaṇīyo.

The root `bhara` in the sense of supporting: "he is supported" (`bharīyati`) — `bharitabbo`, `bharaṇīyo` ("to be supported").

> Gaha upādāne, agayhittha, gayhati, gayhissatīti gahetabbo, ‘‘tesu vuddhī’’tiādinā ikārassekāro, saṅgaṇhitabbo, ‘‘gahādito ppaṇhā’’ti vinādhikārayogavibhāgena ṇhāpaccayo, halopasaralopādi, saṅgaṇhaṇīyo, gahaṇīyo.

The root `gaha` in the sense of taking: "it was taken, it is taken, it will be taken" (`agayhittha`, `gayhati`, `gayhissati`) — `gahetabbo` ("to be taken"), the `i` becoming `e` by [§404 (370): Tesu vuddhilopāgamavikāraviparītādesā ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m404) and the like; `saṅgaṇhitabbo` ("to be treated kindly"), with the suffix `ṇhā` by [§450 (517): Gahādito ppa ṇhā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m450) taken without its governing rule, by splitting the rule, and the elision of the `h`, the elision of the vowel and the rest; `saṅgaṇhaṇīyo`, `gahaṇīyo`.

> Ādiggahaṇena ramu kīḷāyaṃ, ramīyittha, ramīyati, ramīyissatīti ramitabbo, ramaṇīyo vihāro.

By the mention of `ādi`: the root `ramu` in the sense of playing: `ramīyittha`, `ramīyati`, `ramīyissati` — `ramitabbo` ("to be delighted in"), `ramaṇīyo vihāro` ("a delightful dwelling").

> Apa pāpuṇane, uṇāpaccayo, pāpīyatīti pāpuṇitabbo, ‘‘gupādīnañcā’’ti dhātvantassa lopo, dvittañca, pattabbo, patteyyo, pāpuṇaṇīyo, pāpaṇīyo.

The root `apa` in the sense of reaching, with the suffix `uṇā`: "it is reached" (`pāpīyati`) — `pāpuṇitabbo` ("to be reached"); with the elision of the root's final by [§580 (630): Gupādīnañca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m580) and doubling, `pattabbo`, `patteyyo`; `pāpuṇaṇīyo`, `pāpaṇīyo`.

> ‘‘Antassa, vā’’ti ca vattate.

“`Antassa`” and “`vā`” also continue.

### 551 (§596): Gama khana hanādīnaṃ tuṃtabbādīsu na (Optionally `n` for the final of `gama`, `khana`, `hana` etc. before `tuṃ`, `tabba` and the like) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§596 (551): Gama khana hanādīnaṃ tuṃ tabbādīsu na" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m596" description="Optionally `n` for the final of `gama`, `khana`, `hana` etc. before `tuṃ`, `tabba` and the like" />
:::

> Gama khana hana iccevamādīnaṃ makāranakārantānaṃ dhātūnamantassa nakāro hoti vā tuṃ tabba tave tuna tvāna tvā iccevamādīsu takārādippaccayesu paresu. Agacchīyittha, gacchīyati, gacchīyissatīti gantabbo maggo, gamitabbaṃ, gamanīyaṃ.

The final of the roots `gama`, `khana`, `hana` and the like, which end in the letters `m` and `n`, optionally becomes the letter `n` when suffixes beginning with the letter `t`, such as `tuṃ`, `tabba`, `tave`, `tuna`, `tvāna` and `tvā`, follow: "it was gone, it is gone, it will be gone" (`agacchīyittha`, `gacchīyati`, `gacchīyissati`) — `gantabbo maggo` ("the path is to be travelled"), `gamitabbaṃ`, `gamanīyaṃ` ("to be gone").

> Khanu avadāraṇe, akhaññittha, khaññati, khaññissatīti khantabbaṃ āvāṭaṃ, khanitabbaṃ, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā khanantassa ṇattañca, khaṇitabbaṃ, khaṇaṇīyaṃ, khananīyaṃ vā.

The root `khanu` in the sense of digging: "it was dug, it is dug, it will be dug" (`akhaññittha`, `khaññati`, `khaññissati`) — `khantabbaṃ āvāṭaṃ` ("a pit is to be dug"), `khanitabbaṃ`; and with the final of `khana` becoming `ṇ` by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the like, `khaṇitabbaṃ`, `khaṇaṇīyaṃ`, or `khananīyaṃ` ("to be dug").

> Hana hiṃsāgatīsu, ahaññittha, haññate, haññissateti hantabbaṃ, hanitabbaṃ, hananīyaṃ.

The root `hana` in the senses of injuring and going: "it was struck, it is struck, it will be struck" (`ahaññittha`, `haññate`, `haññissate`) — `hantabbaṃ`, `hanitabbaṃ`, `hananīyaṃ` ("to be struck").

> Mana ñāṇe, amaññittha, maññate, maññissateti mantabbo, manitabbo, yappaccaye ca vaggādi, maññitabbaṃ, maññanīyaṃ.

The root `mana` in the sense of knowing: "it was thought, it is thought, it will be thought" (`amaññittha`, `maññate`, `maññissate`) — `mantabbo`, `manitabbo` ("to be thought"); and with the suffix `ya`, the [`c`-]group and the rest: `maññitabbaṃ`, `maññanīyaṃ`.

> Pūja pūjāyaṃ, apūjīyittha, pūjīyati, pūjīyissatīti atthe tabbānīyā. ‘‘Curādito ṇe ṇayā’’ti akattaripi ṇeṇayā, ikārāgamānīyesu ‘‘saralopo’’tiādinā kāritasarassa lopo, pūjetabbo, pūjayitabbo, pūjanīyo bhagavā.

The root `pūja` in the sense of honouring: in the sense "he was honoured, he is honoured, he will be honoured" (`apūjīyittha`, `pūjīyati`, `pūjīyissati`), `tabba` and `anīya`. By [§452 (525): Curādito, ṇe ṇayā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m452), `ṇe` and `ṇaya` are added even outside the sense of the agent, and before the inserted `i` and before `anīya` the vowel of the causative [suffix] is elided by [§83 (67): Saralopo’ mādesa paccayādimhi saralope tu pakati](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m83) and the like: `pūjetabbo`, `pūjayitabbo` ("to be honoured"), `pūjanīyo bhagavā` ("the Blessed One is to be honoured").

> ‘‘Tabbānīyā’’ti yogavibhāgena kattukaraṇesupi, yā pāpuṇane, niyyātīti niyyānīko maggo. Gacchantīti gamanīyā bhogā. Naha soce, nahāyati etenāti nahānīyaṃ cuṇṇaṃ.

By splitting the rule at “`tabbānīyā`”, [the suffixes are used] in the senses of the agent and of the instrument as well: the root `yā` in the sense of reaching — "it leads out" (`niyyāti`), `niyyānīko maggo` ("the path that leads out"); "they go" (`gacchanti`), `gamanīyā bhogā` ("possessions that go away"); the root `naha` in the sense of cleansing — "one bathes with it" (`nahāyati etena`), `nahānīyaṃ cuṇṇaṃ` ("bathing powder").

> ‘‘Bhāvakammesū’’ti adhikāro.

“`Bhāvakammesu`” ("in the senses of the action itself and of the object") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

### 553 (§621): Kāritaṃ viya ṇānubandho (a suffix marked with `ṇ` counts as a causative) :badge[vidhyaṅga]{variant=tip}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§621 (553): Kāritaṃ viya ṇānubandho" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m621" description="a suffix marked with `ṇ` counts as a causative" />
:::

> Anubandho appayogī, ṇakārānubandho paccayo kāritaṃ viya daṭṭhabboti kāritabyapadeso, ‘‘kāritānaṃ ṇo lopa’’nti ṇalopo, ‘‘asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite’’ti vuddhi, ikārāgamo, kattabbaṃ kāriyaṃ.

An indicatory letter (`anubandha`) is one that is not employed. A suffix with the indicatory letter `ṇ` is to be regarded like a causative (`kārita`), so it has the designation of causative: the `ṇ` is elided by [§523 (526): Kāritānaṃ ṇo lopaṃ](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m523), `vuddhi` by [§483 (527): Asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m483), and the letter `i` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)): `kattabbaṃ`, `kāriyaṃ` ("to be done").

> Hara haraṇe, aharīyittha, harīyati, harīyissatīti vā haritabbaṃ hāriyaṃ.

The root `hara` in the sense of carrying: "it was carried, it is carried, or it will be carried" (`aharīyittha`, `harīyati`, `harīyissati`) — `haritabbaṃ`, `hāriyaṃ` ("to be carried").

> Bhara bharaṇe, bharitabbaṃ bhāriyaṃ.

The root `bhara` in the sense of supporting: `bharitabbaṃ`, `bhāriyaṃ` ("to be supported").

> Labha lābhe, labhitabbaṃ labbhaṃ, ‘‘yavataṃ talana’’iccādisutte kāraggahaṇena yavato bhakārassa bhakāro, dvittaṃ.

The root `labha` in the sense of getting: `labhitabbaṃ`, `labbhaṃ` ("obtainable"); by the mention of `kāra` in the sutta [§269 (41): Yavataṃ ta la ṇa dakārānaṃ byañjanāni ca la ña jakārattaṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/5-pancamakanda#m269) and the like, the letter `bh` with `y` after it becomes `bh`, and it is doubled.

> Sāsa anusiṭṭhimhi, sāsitabbo sisso, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ākārassikāro.

The root `sāsa` in the sense of instructing: `sāsitabbo`, `sisso` ("a pupil, one to be instructed"), the letter `ā` becoming `i` by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the like.

> Vaca viyattiyaṃ vācāyaṃ, ṇyappaccayādimhi kate ‘‘antānaṃ, ṇānubandhe’’ti ca vattate.

### 554 (§623): Kagā cajānaṃ (`k`, `g` for root-final `c`, `j` before a `ṇ`-marked suffix) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§623 (554): Ka gā ca jānaṃ" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m623" description="`k`, `g` for root-final `c`, `j` before a `ṇ`-marked suffix" />

> Caja iccetesaṃ dhātvantānaṃ kakāragakārādesā honti ṇakārānubandhe paccaye pareti cassa kādeso. Vacanīyaṃ vākyaṃ.

The root-finals `c` and `j` are replaced by the letters `k` and `g` when a suffix with the indicatory letter `ṇ` follows; so the `c` is replaced by `k`: `vacanīyaṃ`, `vākyaṃ` ("a sentence", what is to be said).

> Bhaja sevāyaṃ, bhajanīyaṃ bhāgyaṃ, jassa gādeso.

> Ci caye, acīyittha, cīyati, cīyissatīti cetabbaṃ ceyyaṃ, ikārassekāro vuddhi, yakārassa dvittaṃ, vinipubbo ‘‘do dhassa cā’’ti sutte caggahaṇena cakārassa chakāro, viniccheyyaṃ, vinicchitabbaṃ, vinicchanīyaṃ. ‘‘Kiyādito nā’’ti vinādhikārayogavibhāgena tabbānīyatuṃtunādīsu ca nāpaccayo, vinicchinitabbaṃ, vinicchinanīyaṃ.

### 556 (§544): Vada mada gamu yuja garahākārādīhi jjammaggayheyyāgāro vā (`jja`, `mma`, `gga`, `yha`, `eyya` for `ṇya` with the root's final, after `vada` etc.; `gāra` for `gara`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§544 (556): Vada mada gamu yuja garahākārādīhi jja mma gga yheyyā gāro vā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m544" description="`jja`, `mma`, `gga`, `yha`, `eyya` for `ṇya` with the root's final, after `vada` etc.; `gāra` for `gara`" />
:::

> Vada mada gamu yuja garaha iccevamādīhi dhātūhi, ākārantehi ca parassa ṇyappaccayassa dhātvantena saha yathākkamaṃ jja mma gga yha eyya iccete ādesā honti vā, garassa ca gārādeso, garahassa garassevāyaṃ gāro. Vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ vāsaddo.

After the roots `vada`, `mada`, `gamu`, `yuja`, `garaha` and the like, and after roots ending in the letter `ā`, the following suffix `ṇya` is optionally replaced, together with the root's final and in that order, by `jja`, `mma`, `gga`, `yha` and `eyya`, and `gara` is replaced by `gāra` — this `gāra` is for the `gara` of `garaha` alone. This word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`).

> Vada viyattiyaṃ vācāyaṃ, avajjittha, vajjati, vajjissatīti vā vajjaṃ vadanīyaṃ, rassattaṃ. Vajjaṃ doso.

The root `vada` in the sense of articulate speech: "it was said, it is said, or it will be said" (`avajjittha`, `vajjati`, `vajjissati`) — `vajjaṃ`, `vadanīyaṃ` ("what is to be said"), with shortening; `vajjaṃ` is also a fault (`dosa`).

> Mada ummāde, amajjittha, majjate, majjissati etenāti majjaṃ madanīyaṃ. Madaggahaṇena karaṇepi ṇyappaccayo. Gamu sappagatimhi, gantabbaṃ gammaṃ.

The root `mada` in the sense of madness: "one was intoxicated, is intoxicated, will be intoxicated by it" (`amajjittha`, `majjate`, `majjissati etena`) — `majjaṃ` ("an intoxicant"), `madanīyaṃ` ("intoxicating"); by the mention of `mada`, the suffix `ṇya` is used in the sense of the instrument (`karaṇa`) too. The roots `gamu` and `sappa` in the sense of going: `gantabbaṃ`, `gammaṃ` ("to be gone to").

> Yuja yoge, ayujjittha, yujjate, yujjissatīti yoggaṃ, niyojjo vā.

The root `yuja` in the sense of joining: "it was yoked, it is yoked, it will be yoked" (`ayujjittha`, `yujjate`, `yujjissati`) — `yoggaṃ` ("to be yoked"), or `niyojjo` ("to be employed").

> Garaha nindāyaṃ, agarayhittha, garahīyati, garahīyissatīti atthe ṇyappaccayo, tassiminā dhātvantena saha yhādeso, garassa gārādeso ca, gārayho, gārayhā, gārayhaṃ, garahaṇīyaṃ.

The root `garaha` in the sense of blaming: in the sense "he was blamed, he is blamed, he will be blamed" (`agarayhittha`, `garahīyati`, `garahīyissati`), the suffix `ṇya`; by this rule it is replaced, together with the root's final, by `yha`, and `gara` by `gāra`: `gārayho`, `gārayhā`, `gārayhaṃ`, `garahaṇīyaṃ` ("blameworthy").

> Ādisaddena aññepi damajahantā gayhante. Gada viyattiyaṃ vācāyaṃ, gajjate, gadanīyaṃ vā gajjaṃ. Pada gatimhi, pajjanīyaṃ pajjaṃ gāthā. Khāda bhakkhaṇe, khajjateti khajjaṃ khādanīyaṃ. Damu damane, adammittha, dammate, damīyissatīti dammo damanīyo. Bhuja pālanabyavaharaṇesu, abhujjittha, bhujjati, bhujjissatīti bhoggaṃ, bhojjaṃ vā, kāraggahaṇena yassa jakāro. Gahetabbaṃ gayhamiccādi.

By the word `ādi`, other roots ending in `d`, `m`, `j` and `h` are included as well. The root `gada` in the sense of articulate speech: "it is uttered" (`gajjate`) — `gadanīyaṃ`, or `gajjaṃ` ("what is to be uttered"). The root `pada` in the sense of going: `pajjanīyaṃ`, `pajjaṃ` ("a verse", `gāthā`). The root `khāda` in the sense of eating: "it is eaten" (`khajjate`) — `khajjaṃ`, `khādanīyaṃ` ("food to be eaten"). The root `damu` in the sense of taming: "he was tamed, he is tamed, he will be tamed" (`adammittha`, `dammate`, `damīyissati`) — `dammo`, `damanīyo` ("to be tamed"). The root `bhuja` in the senses of protecting and of using: "it was enjoyed, it is enjoyed, it will be enjoyed" (`abhujjittha`, `bhujjati`, `bhujjissati`) — `bhoggaṃ`, or `bhojjaṃ` ("to be enjoyed"), the `y` becoming the letter `j` by the mention of `kāra`; `gahetabbaṃ`, `gayhaṃ` ("to be taken"), and so on.

> Ākārantato pana dā dāne, adīyittha, dīyati, dīyissatīti atthe ṇyappaccayo, tassiminā dhātvantena ākārena saha eyyādeso, deyyaṃ, dātabbaṃ. Anīye ‘‘saralopo’’tiādinā pubbasarassa lope sampatte tattheva tuggahaṇena nisedhetvā ‘‘sarā sare lopa’’nti ākāre lutte parasarassa dīgho, dānīyaṃ.

But after a root ending in `ā`: the root `dā` in the sense of giving; in the sense "it was given, it is given, it will be given" (`adīyittha`, `dīyati`, `dīyissati`), the suffix `ṇya`, which by this rule is replaced, together with the root's final `ā`, by `eyya`: `deyyaṃ`, `dātabbaṃ` ("to be given"). Before `anīya`, when the elision of the preceding vowel would apply by [§83 (67): Saralopo’ mādesa paccayādimhi saralope tu pakati](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m83) and the like, it is blocked by the mention of `tu` in that very rule; then, the `ā` having been elided by [§12 (13): Sarā sare lopaṃ](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m12), the following vowel is lengthened: `dānīyaṃ`.

> Pā pāne, apīyittha, pīyati, pīyissatīti peyyaṃ, pātabbaṃ, pānīyaṃ. Hā cāge, ahīyittha, hīyati, hīyissatīti heyyaṃ, hātabbaṃ, hānīyaṃ. Mā māne, amīyittha, mīyati, mīyissatīti meyyaṃ, mātabbaṃ, minitabbaṃ, metabbaṃ vā. Ñā avabodhane, aññāyittha, ñāyati, ñāyissatīti ñeyyaṃ, ñātabbaṃ, ñāteyyaṃ. ‘‘Ñāssa jā jaṃ nā’’ti jādese ‘‘kiyādito nā’’ti vinādhikārayogavibhāgena nāpaccayo, ikārāgamo ca, jānitabbaṃ, vijānanīyaṃ. Khyā pakathane, saṅkhyātabbaṃ, saṅkhyeyyaṃ iccādi.

The root `pā` in the sense of drinking: "it was drunk, it is drunk, it will be drunk" (`apīyittha`, `pīyati`, `pīyissati`) — `peyyaṃ`, `pātabbaṃ`, `pānīyaṃ` ("to be drunk"). The root `hā` in the sense of giving up: `ahīyittha`, `hīyati`, `hīyissati` — `heyyaṃ`, `hātabbaṃ`, `hānīyaṃ` ("to be given up"). The root `mā` in the sense of measuring: `amīyittha`, `mīyati`, `mīyissati` — `meyyaṃ`, `mātabbaṃ`, `minitabbaṃ`, or `metabbaṃ` ("to be measured"). The root `ñā` in the sense of understanding: `aññāyittha`, `ñāyati`, `ñāyissati` — `ñeyyaṃ`, `ñātabbaṃ`, `ñāteyyaṃ` ("to be known"); when `jā` has replaced it by [§470 (514): Ñāssa jā jaṃ nā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m470), the suffix `nā` is added by [§449 (513): Kiyādito nā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m449) taken without its governing rule, by splitting the rule, and the letter `i` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) as well: `jānitabbaṃ`, `vijānanīyaṃ` ("to be understood"). The root `khyā` in the sense of telling: `saṅkhyātabbaṃ`, `saṅkhyeyyaṃ` ("to be counted"), and so on.

### 558 (§539): Ramhi ranto rādi no (before an `r`-suffix the root's final, with the `r`, is elided) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§539 (558): Ramhi ranto rādino" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m539" description="before an `r`-suffix the root's final, with the `r`, is elided" />

> Rakārānubandhe paccaye pare sabbo dhātvanto rādipaccayarakāramariyādo no hoti, lopamāpajjateti attho. Rantoti ettha rakāro sandhijo, kattabbaṃ kiccaṃ. ‘‘Riccā’’ti yogavibhāgena bharāditopi riccappaccayo, yathā, bharīyatīti bhacco, saralopo. I gatimhi, pati etabbo paṭicco.

When a suffix with the indicatory letter `r` follows, the whole root-final, up to and including the letter `r` of the `r`-initial suffix, "is not" (`no`), that is, it is elided. By splitting the rule at “`ricca`”, the suffix `ricca` is used after `bhara` and the like too; for example, "he is supported" (`bharīyati`) — `bhacco` ("a dependant"), with the elision of the vowel; the root `i` in the sense of going: "it is to be gone back to" (`pati etabbo`) — `paṭicco`.

### 559 (§635): Pesātisaggapattakālesu kiccā (the `kicca` suffixes in the senses of command, permission and due time) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§635 (559): Pesātisaggapattakālesukiccā" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m635" description="the `kicca` suffixes in the senses of command, permission and due time" />
:::

> Pesa atisagga pattakāla iccetesvatthesu kiccappaccayā honti. Pesanaṃ nāma ‘‘kattabbamidaṃ bhavatā’’ti āṇāpanaṃ, ajjhesanañca. Atisaggo nāma ‘‘kimidaṃ mayā kattabba’’nti puṭṭhassa vā ‘‘pāṇo na hantabbo’’tiādinā paṭipattidassanamukhena vā kattabbassa anuññā. Pattakālo nāma sampattasamayo yo attano kiccakaraṇasamayamanupaparikkhitvā na karoti, tassa samayārocanaṃ, na tattha ajjhesanamatthīti. Te ca ‘‘bhāvakammesu kiccattakkhatthā’’ti vuttattā bhāvakammesveva bhavanti.

The `kicca` suffixes are used in the senses of command (`pesa`), permission (`atisagga`) and due time (`pattakāla`). Command (`pesana`) is ordering, "this is to be done by you", and also requesting (`ajjhesanā`). Permission (`atisagga`) is the sanctioning (`anuññā`) of what is to be done, either for one who has asked "what is to be done by me?" or by way of showing the practice, as in "a living being is not to be killed" (`pāṇo na hantabbo`) and the like. Due time (`pattakāla`) is the time having arrived: it is announcing the time to one who, not having examined the time for doing his own duty, does not do it, and no request lies in it. And these suffixes occur only in the senses of the action itself and of the object, since it is stated in [§625 (605): Bhāvakammesu kiccattakkhatthā](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m625) that "the `kicca` suffixes, `ta` and the suffixes with the sense of `kha` are used in the senses of the action itself and of the object".

> Pesane tāva – karīyatu bhavatā kammanti atthe iminā tabbānīyā, sesaṃ vuttanayameva, kattabbaṃ kammaṃ bhavatā, karaṇīyaṃ kiccaṃ bhavatā.

First, in command: in the sense "let the work be done by you" (`karīyatu bhavatā kammaṃ`), `tabba` and `anīya` are used by this rule, the rest being as already stated: `kattabbaṃ kammaṃ bhavatā` ("the work is to be done by you"), `karaṇīyaṃ kiccaṃ bhavatā` ("the duty is to be done by you").

> Atisagge bhujjatu bhavatāti atthe tabbādi, ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti vuddhi.

In permission: in the sense "let it be eaten by you" (`bhujjatu bhavatā`), `tabba` and the rest, with `vuddhi` by [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485) —

> ‘‘Tassā’’ti vattate.

“`Tassa`” ("of that") continues.

### 560 (§578): Bhujādīnamanto no dvi ca (After `bhuja` etc. the final is dropped and the `ta` doubled) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§578 (560): Bhujādīnamanto no dvi ca" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m578" description="After `bhuja` etc. the final is dropped and the `ta` doubled" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The `tassa` that 559 says continues into this rule is the `ta` of [625 (§572): Sāsadisato tassa riṭṭho ca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/3-atita#r625), which comes later in the kaṇḍa.

:::

> Bhuja iccevamādīnaṃ jakārādiantānaṃ dhātūnamanto no hoti, parassa kiccakitakappaccayatakārassa ca dvebhāvo hoti. Bhottabbaṃ bhojanaṃ bhavatā, bhojanīyaṃ bhojjaṃ bhavatā.

The final of the roots `bhuja` and the like, which end in the letter `j` and other letters, "is not", that is, it is elided, and the letter `t` of a following `kicca` or `kita` suffix is doubled: `bhottabbaṃ bhojanaṃ bhavatā` ("the food may be eaten by you"), `bhojanīyaṃ`, `bhojjaṃ bhavatā`.

> Ikārāgamayuttatakāre pana – ‘‘namakarānamantānaṃ niyuttatamhī’’ti ettha ‘‘antānaṃ niyuttatamhī’’ti yogavibhāgena dhātvantalopādinisedho, ‘‘rudhādito niggahītapubba’’nti vinādhikārayogavibhāgena, ‘‘niggahītañcā’’ti vā niggahītāgamo, bhuñjitabbaṃ tayā, yuñjitabbaṃ.

But when the letter `t` is joined with the inserted `i`: by splitting [§617 (633): Na ma ka rānamantānaṃ niyuttatamhi](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m617) at `antānaṃ niyuttatamhi`, the elision of the root's final and the rest are prohibited, and by [§446 (509): Rudhādito niggahitapubbañca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m446) taken without its governing rule, by splitting the rule, or else by [§37 (57): Niggahitañca](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m37), the niggahīta is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)): `bhuñjitabbaṃ tayā` ("it is to be eaten by you"), `yuñjitabbaṃ` ("to be yoked").

> Samayārocane pana – i ajjhayane adhipubbo, adhīyataṃ bhavatāti atthe tabbānīyādi, ikārāgamavuddhiayādesaajjhādesā ca, ṇyappaccaye tu vuddhi, yakārassa dvittañca, ajjhayitabbaṃ, ajjheyyaṃ bhavatā, ajjhayanīyaṃ bhavatā, avassaṃ kattabbanti vākye pana ‘‘kiccā’’ti adhikicca ‘‘avassakādhamiṇesu ṇī cā’’ti avassakādhamiṇatthe ca tabbādayo, kattabbaṃ me bhavatā gehaṃ, karaṇīyaṃ, kāriyaṃ. Evaṃ dātabbaṃ me bhavatā sataṃ, dānīyaṃ, deyyaṃ.

But in announcing the time: the root `i` in the sense of studying, with `adhi` before it; in the sense "let it be studied by you" (`adhīyataṃ bhavatā`), `tabba`, `anīya` and the rest, with the insertion of `i`, `vuddhi`, the replacement `aya` and the replacement `ajjha`, but with the suffix `ṇya` `vuddhi` and doubling of the letter `y`: `ajjhayitabbaṃ`, `ajjheyyaṃ bhavatā`, `ajjhayanīyaṃ bhavatā` ("it is to be studied by you"). But in a sentence meaning "it must certainly be done" (`avassaṃ kattabbaṃ`), with “`kiccā`” governing, `tabba` and the rest are used by [§636 (659): Avassakā’dhamiṇesu ṇī ca](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m636) in the senses of necessity (`avassaka`) and of a debtor (`adhamiṇa`) as well: `kattabbaṃ me bhavatā gehaṃ` ("you must build me a house"), `karaṇīyaṃ`, `kāriyaṃ`; likewise `dātabbaṃ me bhavatā sataṃ` ("you must pay me a hundred"), `dānīyaṃ`, `deyyaṃ`.

> Dhara dhāraṇe, curādittā ṇeṇayā, vuddhi, ikārāgamo ca, dhāretabbaṃ, dhārayitabbaṃ iccādi.

The root `dhara` in the sense of holding: since it belongs to the `cura` class, `ṇe` and `ṇaya`, `vuddhi` and the insertion of `i`: `dhāretabbaṃ`, `dhārayitabbaṃ` ("to be held"), and so on.

> ‘‘Nudādīhi yuṇvūnamanānanākānanakā sakāritehi cā’’ti sakāritehipi yuṇvūnamādesavidhānatoyeva dhātuppaccayantatopi kiccakitakappaccayā bhavantīti daṭṭhabbo. Tena titikkhāpīyatīti titikkhāpetabbo. Evaṃ tikicchāpetabbo tikicchāpanīyo. Abhāvīyittha, bhāvīyati, bhāvīyissatīti bhāvetabbo maggo. Bhāvayitabbo, bhāvanīyo, bhāvanīyaṃ, bhāvanīyā, akārīyittha, kārīyati, kārīyissatīti kāretabbaṃ, kārayitabbaṃ, kārāpetabbaṃ, kārāpayitabbaṃ, kārāpanīyamiccādi ca siddhaṃ bhavati.

Precisely because [§641 (572): Nudādīhi yuṇvūnamanānanākānanakā sakāritehi ca](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m641) prescribes the replacements of `yu` and `ṇvu` after [roots] with causative suffixes as well, it is to be understood that the `kicca` and `kita` suffixes are used also after [a lemma] ending in a root-suffix (`dhātuppaccaya`). Hence "he is made to endure" (`titikkhāpīyati`) — `titikkhāpetabbo` ("to be made to endure"); likewise `tikicchāpetabbo`, `tikicchāpanīyo` ("to be made to treat"). "It was developed, it is developed, it will be developed" (`abhāvīyittha`, `bhāvīyati`, `bhāvīyissati`) — `bhāvetabbo maggo` ("the path is to be developed"), `bhāvayitabbo`, `bhāvanīyo`, `bhāvanīyaṃ`, `bhāvanīyā`; and "it was caused to be done, it is caused to be done, it will be caused to be done" (`akārīyittha`, `kārīyati`, `kārīyissati`) — `kāretabbaṃ`, `kārayitabbaṃ`, `kārāpetabbaṃ`, `kārāpayitabbaṃ`, `kārāpanīyaṃ` ("to be caused to be done") and so on are established.

> Kattabbaṃ karaṇīyañca, kāriyaṃ kiccamiccapi; \
> Kāretabbaṃ tathā kārā-petabbaṃ kiccasaṅgaho.

`kattabbaṃ` and `karaṇīyaṃ`, `kāriyaṃ` and `kiccaṃ` too, \
`kāretabbaṃ` and likewise `kārāpetabbaṃ` — this is the summary of the `kicca` forms.
