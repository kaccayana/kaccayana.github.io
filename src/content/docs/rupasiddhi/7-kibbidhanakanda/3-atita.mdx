## Atītappaccayantanaya (Suffixes of the past)

---

<a name="r612"></a>

### 612 (§555): Atīte ta tavantu tāvī (`ta`, `tavantu`, `tāvī` in the past) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§555 (612): Atīte tatavantutāvī" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m555" description="`ta`, `tavantu`, `tāvī` in the past" />
:::

> Atīte kāle sabbehi dhātūhi ta tavantu tāvī iccete paccayā honti. Ete eva parasamaññāya niṭṭha saññakāpi, te ca kita saññattā kattari bhavanti. Abhavīti bhūto, bhūtā, bhūtaṃ, ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti etthānuvattitavā ggahaṇena ta tavantutāvī su vuddhi na hoti. Hu dānādanahabyappadānesu, ahavīti huto aggiṃ.

In past time (`atīte kāle`), the suffixes `ta`, `tavantu` and `tāvī` are used after all roots. These same suffixes are also named `niṭṭha` in the terminology of others (`parasamaññā`), and since they are named `kita` they are used in the sense of the agent (`kattā`). `abhavi` ("he was") — `bhūto` 🚹 ("one who has been"), `bhūtā` 🚺, `bhūtaṃ` 🚻; by the mention of `vā` carried over into [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485), there is no strengthening (`vuddhi`) before `ta`, `tavantu` and `tāvī`. `hu` in the senses of giving, eating and offering the oblation (`dāna`, `adana`, `habyappadāna`): `ahavi` ("he offered") — `huto aggiṃ` ("he has made an offering to the fire").

> Tavantu ppaccaye – ‘‘ā simhī’’ti ā kāro, aggiṃ hutavā, hutavanto iccādi guṇavantusamaṃ. Tāvīmhi – aggiṃ hutāvī, aggiṃ hutāvino iccādi daṇḍīsamaṃ. Itthiyaṃ inī paccayo – hutāvinī, napuṃsake – rassattaṃ hutāvi.

With the suffix `tavantu`, the letter `ā` by [§124 (98): Ā simhi](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m124): `aggiṃ hutavā` ("he who has made an offering to the fire"), `hutavanto` and so on, like `guṇavantu`. With `tāvī`: `aggiṃ hutāvī`, `aggiṃ hutāvino` and so on, like `daṇḍī`. In the feminine, the suffix `inī`: `hutāvinī` ("she who has offered"); in the neuter, shortening: `hutāvi`.

> Vasa nivāse, vassaṃ avasīti atthe ta ppaccayo, sa kārantattā ‘‘sādisanta’’iccādinā ṭhā dese sampatte –

`vasa` in the sense of dwelling (`nivāsa`): the suffix `ta` in the sense `vassaṃ avasi` ("he spent the rains"); and since the root ends in the letter `s`, when the replacement `ṭṭha` by [§573 (626): Sādisanta puccha bhanja hansādīhiṭṭho](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m573) and the rest would apply —

> ‘‘Tassā’’ti adhikāro, ‘‘sādī’’ti ca.

“`tassa`” ("of it") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), and so is “`sādi`” ("together with the consonant before it").

### 613 (§574): Vasato uttha (`uttha` for `ta` with the root's final, after `vasa`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§574 (613): Vasato uttha" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m574" description="`uttha` for `ta` with the root's final, after `vasa`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The words `tassa` and `sādi` belong to [625 (§572): Sāsadisato tassa riṭṭho ca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/3-atita#r625) and [626 (§573): Sādi, santapucchabhanjahansādīhi ṭṭho](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/3-atita#r626), which are stated later on this page.

:::

> Vasa iccetasmā dhātumhā parassa ta kārassa sahādibyañjanena utthā deso hoti, saralopādi. Vassaṃ vuttho, vutthā sā, ‘‘saralopo’’tiādisutte tu ggahaṇato pubbalopābhāve ‘‘adhivatthā devatā, vatthabba’’ntiādīsu paralopo.

After the root `vasa`, the following letter `ta`, together with the consonant before it, is replaced by `uttha`; then the vowel elision and the rest: `vassaṃ vuttho` ("he has spent the rains"), `vutthā sā` ("she has dwelt"). Because of the mention of `tu` in the sutta [§83 (67): Saralopo’ mādesa paccayādimhi saralope tu pakati](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m83), the preceding vowel is not elided, and so it is the following vowel that is elided, as in `adhivatthā devatā` ("the deity dwelling [in it]"), `vatthabbaṃ` ("to be dwelt in") and the like.

| →   | vas + ~~a~~ + ta                 | 425 (§521) |

| →   | v + (~~a~~ + u)ttha              | 67 (§83)  |
| →   | vuttha + si                      | 334 (§601), 63 (§55) |
| →   | vuttha + (si→o)                  | 66 (§104) |
| →   | **vuttho**                       |      |

| --- | -------------------------------- | ---- |

| →   | adhi + va + (s + ta → uttha)     | 613 (§574) |
| →   | adhi + va + (~~u~~)ttha          | 67 (§83) (`tu`), 15 (§13) |
| →   | adhivatth + (~~a~~ + ā)          | 176 (§237), 67 (§83) |

“`vasassa`” ("of `vasa`") continues, with a change of inflection (`vipariṇāma`).

<a name="r614"></a>

### 614 (§575): Vassa vā vu (Optionally `vu`, that is `u`, for the `va` of `vasa`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§575 (614): Vassa vā vu" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m575" description="Optionally `vu`, that is `u`, for the `va` of `vasa`" />
:::

> Vasa iccetassa dhātussa va kārassa ta kāre pare u kāro hoti, tattha va kārāgamo ca vā hoti. Niṭṭhata kāre evāyaṃ. Atha vā ‘‘vū’’ti ettha va kāro sandhijo, tantañāyena dutiyañcettha vā ggahaṇamicchitabbaṃ, tena a kārassapi u kāro siddho bhavati, usito brahmacariyaṃ, vusito, tathā vusitavā, vusitāvī, i kārāgamena byavahitattā utthādeso na bhavati.

When the letter `ta` follows, the letter `va` of the root `vasa` becomes the letter `u`, and there the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of the letter `v` is optional (`vā`). This [applies] only before the `niṭṭha` letter `ta`. Or else the letter `v` in `vu` arises from sandhi, and by the method of the treatise (`tantañāya`) a second `vā` is to be understood here too, whereby `u` for the letter `a` as well is established: `usito brahmacariyaṃ` ("he has lived the holy life"), `vusito` ("he has lived"); likewise `vusitavā`, `vusitāvī` ("he who has lived"). The replacement `uttha` does not take place, because the insertion of the letter `i` intervenes.

> Bhuja pālanabyavaharaṇesu, odanaṃ abhuñjīti atthe tatavantutāvī, ‘‘bhujādīnamanto no dvi cā’’ti dhātvantalopo, ta kārassa dvittañca, bhutto, bhuttavā, bhuttāvī. Tathā ranja rāge, arañjīti ratto, rattā, rattaṃ. Yuja yoge, ayuñjīti yutto, yuttā, yuttaṃ. Vica vivecane vi pubbo, viviccīti vivitto, vivittā, vivittaṃ. Muca mocane, amuccīti bandhanā mutto. Tathā ti ppaccayepi iminā dhātvantalopadvittāni, āsajjanaṃ āsatti, vimuccanaṃ, vimuccati etāyāti vā vimutti.

`bhuja` in the senses of protecting and consuming (`pālana`, `byavaharaṇa`): the suffixes `ta`, `tavantu` and `tāvī` in the sense `odanaṃ abhuñji` ("he ate the rice"); by [§578 (560): Bhujādīnamanto no dvi ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m578) the root's final is elided and the letter `t` is doubled: `bhutto` ("one who has eaten"), `bhuttavā`, `bhuttāvī`. Likewise `ranja` in the sense of passion (`rāga`): `arañji` ("he took delight") — `ratto` 🚹 ("impassioned"), `rattā` 🚺, `rattaṃ` 🚻. `yuja` in the sense of joining (`yoga`): `ayuñji` ("he engaged") — `yutto` 🚹 ("yoked"), `yuttā` 🚺, `yuttaṃ` 🚻. `vica` in the sense of separating (`vivecana`), with `vi` before it: `vivicci` ("he withdrew") — `vivitto` 🚹 ("secluded"), `vivittā` 🚺, `vivittaṃ` 🚻. `muca` in the sense of releasing (`mocana`): `amucci` ("he was freed") — `bandhanā mutto` ("freed from bondage"). Likewise before the suffix `ti` too, by this rule the root's final is elided and the doubling is made: `āsajjanaṃ` ("clinging") — `āsatti` ("attachment"); `vimuccanaṃ` ("being released"), or `vimuccati etāya` ("by this one is released") — `vimutti` ("release").

> Kudha kope, akujjhīti atthe ta ppaccayo, tassa ‘‘dhaḍhabhahehi dhaḍhā cā’’ti dha ttaṃ, ‘‘hacatutthānamantānaṃ dodhe’’ti dha kārassa da kāro, kuddho. Yudha sampahāre, ayujjhīti yuddho, yuddhaṃ. Sidha saṃsiddhimhi, asijjhīti siddho. Ā pubbo rabha rābhasse, ārabhīti āraddho gantuṃ. Naha bandhane saṃ pubbo, sannayhīti sannaddho, ‘‘dhaḍhabhahehi dhaḍhā cā’’ti nahā dito ta kārassa dha kāro.

`kudha` in the sense of anger (`kopa`): the suffix `ta` in the sense `akujjhi` ("he was angry"); it becomes `dha` by [§576 (607): Dha ḍha bha he hi dhaḍḍhā ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m576), and the letter `dh` [of the root] becomes the letter `d` by [§611 (608): Hacatutthānamantānaṃ do dhe](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m611): `kuddho` ("angered"). `yudha` in the sense of fighting (`sampahāra`): `ayujjhi` ("he fought") — `yuddho` ("one who has fought"), `yuddhaṃ` ("a battle"). `sidha` in the sense of accomplishment (`saṃsiddhi`): `asijjhi` ("it succeeded") — `siddho` ("accomplished"). `rabha` in the sense of undertaking (`rābhassa`), with `ā` before it: `ārabhi` ("he began") — `āraddho gantuṃ` ("he has begun to go"). `naha` in the sense of binding (`bandhana`), with `saṃ` before it: `sannayhi` ("he girded himself") — `sannaddho` ("girded"); by [§576 (607): Dha ḍha bha he hi dhaḍḍhā ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m576) the letter `t` of `ta` becomes the letter `dh` after `naha` and the like.

> Vaḍḍha vaḍḍhane, avaḍḍhīti atthe ta ppaccayo, ta ssa ḍhattaṃ, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā dhātvākārassuttaṃ, ḍalopo ca.

`vaḍḍha` in the sense of growing (`vaḍḍhana`): the suffix `ta` in the sense `avaḍḍhi` ("he grew"); it becomes `ḍha`, by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) the letter `a` of the root becomes `u`, and the `ḍ` is elided.

> ‘‘Hacatutthānamantāna’’nti vattate.

“`hacatutthānamantānaṃ`” ("of root-final `h` and the fourth letters") continues.

### 615 (§612): Ḍo ḍhakāre (and `ḍ` before `ḍh`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§612 (615): Ḍo ḍhakāre" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m612" description="and `ḍ` before `ḍh`" />
:::

> Hacatutthānaṃ dhātvantānaṃ ḍakārādeso hoti ḍha kāre pare. Vuḍḍho, vuḍḍhā, ‘‘bo vassā’’ti batte buḍḍho. Ti ppaccaye – bujjhanaṃ, bujjhati vā etāyāti buddhi. Evaṃ siddhi, vaḍḍhi. Tabba ppaccaye – boddhabbamiccādi.

Root-final `h` and the fourth letters [of the groups] are replaced by the letter `ḍ` when the letter `ḍh` follows: `vuḍḍho` 🚹 ("grown; aged"), `vuḍḍhā` 🚺; with `b` for `v` by “`bo vassa`”, `buḍḍho`. Before the suffix `ti`: `bujjhanaṃ` ("understanding"), or `bujjhati etāya` ("by this one understands") — `buddhi` ("intelligence"); likewise `siddhi` ("accomplishment"), `vaḍḍhi` ("growth"). Before the suffix `tabba`: `boddhabbaṃ` ("to be understood"), and so on.

Guha saṃvaraṇe, aguhīti gūḷhaṃ. Vaha pāpuṇane, upavahīti upavuḷho, ‘‘vaca vasa vahādīnamukāro vassā’’ti yogavibhāgena u ttaṃ.

After roots ending in the letter `h`, the following suffix `ta`, and `ti` too, is replaced by the letter `ha`, and the root-final `h` optionally (`vā`) becomes the letter `l` — leaving aside `daha` and `naha`. This is an exception ([`apavāda`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) to the change to `ḍha`. `ruha` in the sense of growing (`janana`): `aruhi` ("he climbed") — `āruḷho rukkhaṃ` ("he has climbed the tree"), or, since there is no distinction between `l` and `ḷ`, `ārulho`; `ruhanaṃ` ("growing") — `ruḷhī` ("growth"). `gāhu` in the sense of stirring (`viloḷana`): `agāhi` ("he plunged") — `gāḷho` ("plunged in"); `ajjhogāḷho mahaṇṇavaṃ` ("plunged into the great ocean"). `baha` in the sense of growth (`vuddhi`): `abahi` ("it grew") — `bāḷho` ("intense"), with lengthening by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517). `muha` in the sense of confusion (`vecitta`): `amuyhi` ("he was confused") — `mūḷho` ("deluded"). `guha` in the sense of concealing (`saṃvaraṇa`): `aguhi` ("it was hidden") — `gūḷhaṃ` ("hidden"). `vaha` in the sense of reaching (`pāpuṇana`): `upavahi` ("it was carried up") — `upavuḷho` ("carried up"), with `u` [for `va`] by splitting the rule [§487 (478): Vaca vasa vahādīnamukāro vassa ye](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m487).

<a name="r626"></a>

### 626 (§573): Sādi, santapucchabhanjahansādīhi ṭṭho (`ṭṭha` for `ta` with the root's final, after roots in `s`, `puccha`, `bhanja`, `hansa`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§573 (626): Sādisanta puccha bhanja hansādīhiṭṭho" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m573" description="`ṭṭha` for `ta` with the root's final, after roots in `s`, `puccha`, `bhanja`, `hansa`" />
:::

> Ādinā saha vattatīti sādi. Sa kārantehi, puccha bhanjahansa iccevamādīhi ca dhātūhi parassa anantarikassa ta kārassa sahādibyañjanena dhātvantenaṭṭhā deso hoti. Hansa ssa satipi santatte punaggahaṇaṃ[^13] kvaci ṭṭhā desassa aniccatādīpanatthaṃ, tena ‘‘viddhasto utrasto’’tiādīsu na hoti. Tuṭṭho, santusito. Bhasa bhassane, abhassīti bhaṭṭho, bhassito. Nasa adassane, nassīti naṭṭho. Daṃsa daṃsane, adaṃsīyitthāti daṭṭho sappena, ḍaṃsito vā, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā da ssa ḍattaṃ. Phusa phassane, aphusīyitthāti phuṭṭho rogena, phussito vā. Isu icchāyaṃ, esīyitthāti iṭṭho, icchito, esito. Masa āmasane, āmasīyitthāti āmaṭṭho[^14]. Vasa secane, avassīti vuṭṭho devo, pavisīyitthāti paviṭṭho, uddisīyitthāti uddiṭṭho. Puccha pucchane, apucchīyitthāti puṭṭho pañhaṃ, pucchito. Bhanja avamaddane, abhañjīyitthāti bhaṭṭhaṃ dhaññaṃ. Hansa pītimhi, ahaṃsīti haṭṭho, pahaṭṭho, pahaṃsito.

`sādi` means "what occurs together with the initial [consonant]" (`ādinā saha vattati`). After roots ending in the letter `s`, and after `puccha`, `bhanja`, `hansa` and the like, the following letter `ta`, when nothing intervenes, is replaced by `ṭṭha` together with the initial consonant, the root's final. Although `hansa` already ends in `s`, it is mentioned again to show that in some places the replacement `ṭṭha` is not invariable; hence in `viddhasto` ("destroyed"), `utrasto` ("terrified") and the like it does not take place. So `tuṭṭho` ("pleased"), `santusito` ("contented"). `bhasa` in the sense of falling (`bhassana`): `abhassi` ("it fell") — `bhaṭṭho` ("fallen"), `bhassito`. `nasa` in the sense of disappearing (`adassana`): `nassi` ("it perished") — `naṭṭho` ("perished"). `daṃsa` in the sense of biting (`daṃsana`): `adaṃsīyittha` ("he was bitten") — `daṭṭho sappena` ("bitten by a snake"), or `ḍaṃsito`, with `ḍ` for `d` by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517). `phusa` in the sense of touching (`phassana`): `aphusīyittha` ("he was touched") — `phuṭṭho rogena` ("afflicted by disease"), or `phussito`. `isu` in the sense of wishing (`icchā`): `esīyittha` ("it was wished for") — `iṭṭho` ("desired"), `icchito` ("wished for"), `esito` ("sought"). `masa` in the sense of touching (`āmasana`): `āmasīyittha` ("it was touched") — `āmaṭṭho` ("touched"). `vasa` in the sense of sprinkling (`secana`): `avassi` ("it rained") — `vuṭṭho devo` ("the sky has rained"); `pavisīyittha` ("it was entered") — `paviṭṭho` ("entered"); `uddisīyittha` ("it was pointed out") — `uddiṭṭho` ("pointed out"). `puccha` in the sense of asking (`pucchana`): `apucchīyittha` ("he was asked") — `puṭṭho pañhaṃ` ("asked a question"), `pucchito`. `bhanja` in the sense of crushing (`avamaddana`): `abhañjīyittha` ("it was crushed") — `bhaṭṭhaṃ dhaññaṃ` ("threshed grain"). `hansa` in the sense of gladness (`pīti`): `ahaṃsi` ("he rejoiced") — `haṭṭho` ("glad"), `pahaṭṭho`, `pahaṃsito` ("delighted").

### 628 (§577): Bhanjato ggo ca (And `gga` for `ta` with the root's final, after `bhanja`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§577 (628): Bhanjato ggo ca" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m577" description="And `gga` for `ta` with the root's final, after `bhanja`" />

> Bha njato dhātumhā ta ppaccayassa sahādibyañjanena ggo ādeso hoti. Bhaggo rāgo anena. Vasa nivāse, parivasīyitthāti parivuttho[^15] parivāso, vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ, uttha u ādesā. Vasa acchādane, nivasīyitthāti nivatthaṃ vatthaṃ, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā sta kārasaṃyogassa ttha ttaṃ, evaṃ nivatthabbaṃ. Saṃsa pasaṃsane, pasaṃsīyitthāti pasattho pasaṃsito, pasaṃsanaṃ pasatthi. Badha bandhane, abajjhitthāti baddho raññā, alabhīyitthāti laddhaṃ me dhanaṃ, dha ttada ttāni. Rabha rābhasse, ārabhīyitthāti āraddhaṃ vīriyaṃ. Daha bhasmīkaraṇe, adayhitthāti[^16] daḍḍhaṃ vanaṃ, abhujjitthāti bhutto odano, bhujādittā dhātvantalopo, dvittañca. Caja hānimhi, pariccajīyitthāti pariccattaṃ dhanaṃ, amuccitthāti mutto saro.

After the root `bhanja`, the suffix `ta` together with the consonant before it is replaced by `gga`: `bhaggo rāgo anena` ("passion has been broken by him"). The root `vasa`, in the sense of dwelling: `parivasīyittha` ("it was served"), hence `parivuttho parivāso` ("the probation has been served"); `vusitaṃ brahmacariyaṃ` ("the holy life has been lived"), with the replacements `uttha` and `u`. The root `vasa`, in the sense of clothing: `nivasīyittha` ("it was put on"), hence `nivatthaṃ vatthaṃ` ("the garment that was put on"), the cluster `s` + `t` becoming `tth` by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517); likewise `nivatthabbaṃ` ("to be worn"). The root `saṃsa`, in the sense of praising: `pasaṃsīyittha` ("he was praised"), hence `pasattho` or `pasaṃsito` ("praised"); `pasaṃsanaṃ` ("praising"), hence `pasatthi` ("praise"). The root `badha`, in the sense of binding: `abajjhittha` ("he was bound"), hence `baddho raññā` ("bound by the king"); `alabhīyittha` ("it was obtained"), hence `laddhaṃ me dhanaṃ` ("wealth has been obtained by me"), with the changes to `dha` and to `d`. The root `rabha`, in the sense of undertaking: `ārabhīyittha` ("it was undertaken"), hence `āraddhaṃ vīriyaṃ` ("energy has been aroused"). The root `daha`, in the sense of reducing to ashes: `adayhittha` ("it was burnt"), hence `daḍḍhaṃ vanaṃ` ("the forest that was burnt"); `abhujjittha` ("it was eaten"), hence `bhutto odano` ("the rice that was eaten"), with the elision of the root's final and the doubling, since `bhuja` belongs to the `bhuja` group. The root `caja`, in the sense of giving up: `pariccajīyittha` ("it was given up"), hence `pariccattaṃ dhanaṃ` ("wealth that was given up"); `amuccittha` ("it was released"), hence `mutto saro` ("the arrow that was released").

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§579 (629): Vaca vāvu" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m579" description="`u` for the `va` of `vaca`, the final dropped and the `ta` doubled" />

:::note[Analysis]

### 631 (§582): Bhidādito innaannaīṇā vā (Optionally `inna`, `anna`, `īṇa` for `ta` after `bhida` etc., the final dropped) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§582 (631): Bhidādito inna anna īṇāvā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m582" description="Optionally `inna`, `anna`, `īṇa` for `ta` after `bhida` etc., the final dropped" />
:::

> Bhida iccevamādīhi dhātūhi parassa ta ppaccayassa inna annaīṇa iccete ādesā honti vā, anto ca no hoti. Vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ vā saddo, saralopādi.

After the roots `bhida` and the like, the following suffix `ta` is optionally replaced by `inna`, `anna` and `īṇa`, and the final is not [there]. This word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`), and the vowel elision and the rest follow.

> Bhidi vidāraṇe, abhijjitthāti bhinno ghaṭo bhavatā, bhijjīti vā bhinno devadatto. Chidi dvidhākaraṇe, achijjitthāti chinno rukkho, acchinnaṃ cīvaraṃ, ucchijjīti ucchinno. Adīyitthāti[^17] dinno suṅko. Sada visaraṇagatyāvasānesu, nisīdīti nisinno. Khida uttāsane, khida dīnabhāve vā, akhijjīti khinno.

The root `bhidi`, in the sense of splitting: `abhijjittha` ("it was broken"), hence `bhinno ghaṭo bhavatā` ("the pot has been broken by you"); or `bhijji` ("he was broken"), hence `bhinno devadatto` ("Devadatta is broken"). The root `chidi`, in the sense of cutting in two: `achijjittha` ("it was cut"), hence `chinno rukkho` ("the tree that was cut down"); `acchinnaṃ cīvaraṃ` ("the robe that was snatched away"); `ucchijji` ("it was cut off"), hence `ucchinno` ("cut off"). `adīyittha` ("it was given"), hence `dinno suṅko` ("the toll has been paid"). The root `sada`, in the senses of dispersing, going and ending: `nisīdi` ("he sat down"), hence `nisinno` ("seated"). The root `khida`, in the sense of impaling, or `khida` in the sense of dejection: `akhijji` ("he was dejected"), hence `khinno` ("dejected").

> ‘‘Kattari kiti’’ti ito maṇḍūkagatiyā ‘‘kattarī’’ti vattate.

### 634 (§626): Kammani dutiyāyaṃ kto (`tta` denotes the agent when the object stands in the second form) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§626 (634): Kammani dutiyāya tto" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m626" description="`tta` denotes the agent when the object stands in the second form" />
:::

> Kammatthe dutiyāyaṃ vibhattiyaṃ vijjamānāyaṃ dhātūhi kattari kta ppaccayo hoti. Idameva vacanaṃ ñāpakaṃ abhihite kammādimhi dutiyādīnamabhāvassa. Dānaṃ adāsīti atthe kta ppaccayo, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā paccayaka kārassa lopo, ta ssa innā deso, dānaṃ dinno devadatto. Rakkha pālane, sīlaṃ arakkhīti sīlaṃ rakkhito, bhattaṃ abhuñjīti bhattaṃ bhutto, garuṃ upāsīti garumupāsito iccādi.

When the second inflection ② in the sense of the object is present, the suffix `kta` is used after roots in the sense of the agent. This very statement is the indicator (`ñāpaka`) that the second and the other inflections are absent when the object and the rest are already expressed. In the sense of `dānaṃ adāsi` ("he gave a gift") the suffix `kta` is added; the `k` of the suffix is elided by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517), and `inna` replaces the `ta`: `dānaṃ dinno devadatto` ("Devadatta has given a gift"). The root `rakkha`, in the sense of protecting: `sīlaṃ arakkhi` ("he guarded virtue"), hence `sīlaṃ rakkhito` ("he has guarded virtue"); `bhattaṃ abhuñji` ("he ate the meal"), hence `bhattaṃ bhutto` ("he has eaten the meal"); `garuṃ upāsi` ("he attended on the teacher"), hence `garumupāsito` ("he has attended on the teacher"), and so on.

<a name="r635"></a>

### 635 (§643): Bhyādīhi matibudhipūjādīhi ca kto (`tta` after `bhī` etc., and after roots of thinking, knowing and honouring) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§643 (635): Bhyādīhi mati budhi pūjādīhi ca tto" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m643" description="`tta` after `bhī` etc., and after roots of thinking, knowing and honouring" />
:::

> Bhī iccevamādīhi dhātūhi, mati budhi pūjā dīhi ca kta ppaccayo hoti. So ca ‘‘bhāvakammesu kiccattakkhatthā’’ti vuttattā bhāvakammesveva bhavati.

The suffix `kta` is used after the roots `bhī` and the like, and after the roots of the `mati`, `budhi` and `pūjā` groups. And since it is said in [§625 (605): Bhāvakammesu kiccattakkhatthā](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m625) that the `kicca` suffixes, `kta` and the suffixes with the sense of `kha` are used in the senses of the action (`bhāva`) and the object (`kamma`), it occurs only in the senses of the action and the object.

> Bhī bhaye, abhāyitthāti bhītaṃ bhavatā. Supa saye, asupīyitthāti suttaṃ bhavatā. Evaṃ sayitaṃ bhavatā. Asa bhojane, asitaṃ bhavatā, pacito odano bhavatā.

The root `bhī`, in the sense of fearing: `abhāyittha` ("fearing was done"), hence `bhītaṃ bhavatā` ("fearing was done by you"). The root `supa`, in the sense of sleeping: `asupīyittha` ("sleeping was done"), hence `suttaṃ bhavatā` ("sleeping was done by you"); likewise `sayitaṃ bhavatā` ("lying down was done by you"). The root `asa`, in the sense of eating: `asitaṃ bhavatā` ("eating was done by you"); `pacito odano bhavatā` ("the rice was cooked by you").

> Idha matyā dayo icchatthā, budhi ādayo ñāṇatthā.

Here `mati` and the rest have the sense of wishing (`icchā`), and `budhi` and the rest the sense of knowing (`ñāṇa`).

> Mana ñāṇe saṃ pubbo, ‘‘budhagamāditthe kattarī’’ti ta ppaccaye sampatte iminā kammani kta ppaccayo, ‘‘gama khanā’’tiādinā dhātvantalopo, raññā sammato. Kappa takkane, saṅkappito. Dhara dhāraṇe, curādittā ṇe, vuddhi, ikārāgamo, saralopādi, avadhārito.

The root `mana`, in the sense of knowing, with `saṃ` before it: where the suffix `ta` would apply by [§557 (606): Budhagamāditthe kattari](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m557), by this rule the suffix `kta` is used in the sense of the object, and the root's final is elided by [§586 (600): Gama khana hana ramādīnamanto](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m586): `raññā sammato` ("authorized by the king"). The root `kappa`, in the sense of reasoning: `saṅkappito` ("thought about"). The root `dhara`, in the sense of holding: `ṇe`, since it belongs to the `cura` class, `vuddhi`, the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of `i`, the vowel elision and the rest: `avadhārito` ("affirmed").

> Budha avagamane, avabujjhitthāti buddho bhagavā mahesakkhehi devamanussehi. I ajjhayane, adhīyitthāti adhīto.

The root `budha`, in the sense of understanding: `avabujjhittha` ("he was understood"), hence `buddho bhagavā mahesakkhehi devamanussehi` ("the Blessed One is understood by gods and men of great influence"). The root `i`, in the sense of studying: `adhīyittha` ("it was studied"), hence `adhīto` ("studied").

> I gatimhi, abhisamito. Vida ñāṇe, avedīyitthāti vidito. Ñā avabodhane, aññāyitthāti ñāto. Vidha vedhane, paṭivijjhitthāti paṭividdho dhammo. Takka vitakke, takkito.

The root `i`, in the sense of going: `abhisamito` ("realized"). The root `vida`, in the sense of knowing: `avedīyittha` ("it was known"), hence `vidito` ("known"). The root `ñā`, in the sense of understanding: `aññāyittha` ("it was understood"), hence `ñāto` ("known"). The root `vidha`, in the sense of piercing: `paṭivijjhittha` ("it was penetrated"), hence `paṭividdho dhammo` ("the Dhamma has been penetrated"). The root `takka`, in the sense of reasoning: `takkito` ("reasoned").

> Pūjanatthesu –

Among those with the sense of honouring —

> Pūja pūjāyaṃ, apūjīyitthāti pūjito bhagavā. Cāya santānapūjanesu apa pubbo, apacāyito. Māna pūjāyaṃ, mānito. Ci caye, apacito. Vanda abhivandane, vandito. Kara karaṇe, sakkato. Sakkāra pūjāyaṃ, sakkārito iccādi.

The root `pūja`, in the sense of honouring: `apūjīyittha` ("he was honoured"), hence `pūjito bhagavā` ("the Blessed One is honoured").
