## Vattamānakālikamānantappaccayantanaya (Present participles)

---

<a name="r646"></a>

### 646 (§565): Vattamāne mānantā (`māna`, `anta` in the present) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§565 (646): Vattamāne mānantā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m565" description="`māna`, `anta` in the present" />
:::

> Āraddho aparisamatto attho vattamāno, tasmiṃ vattamāne kāle gammamāne sabbadhātūhi mānaanta iccete paccayā honti. Te ca kita saññattā ‘‘kattari kita’’ti kattari bhavanti.

A matter (`attha`) that has begun but is not yet finished is present (`vattamāna`). When that present time is understood, the suffixes `māna` and `anta` are used after all roots; and since they are termed `kita`, they denote the agent by [§624 (563): Kattari kita](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m624).

> Antamāna ppaccayānañcettha ‘‘parasamaññāpayoge’’ti parasamaññāvasena parassapadattanopadasaññattā tyā dīsu viya antamāne su ca vikaraṇappaccayā bhavanti.

And here, since by [§9 (11): Parasamaññā payoge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m9) the suffixes `anta` and `māna` bear the names `parassapada` and `attanopada` by the terminology of the other grammars (`parasamaññā`), the class suffixes (`vikaraṇa`) are used before `anta` and `māna` too, just as before `ti` and the rest.

> Teneva māna ppaccayo ‘‘attanopadāni bhāve ca kammanī’’ti bhāvakammesupi hoti, tassa ca ‘‘attanopadāni parassapadatta’’nti kvaci anta ppaccayādeso ca.

For that very reason the suffix `māna` is used in the impersonal (`bhāva`) and the passive (`kamma`) as well, by [§453 (444): Attanopadāni bhāve ca kammani](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m453), and in some places it is replaced by the suffix `anta`, by [§518 (446): Attanopadāni parassapadattaṃ](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m518).

> Gamu, sappa gatimhi, gacchatīti atthe anta ppaccayo, ‘‘bhūvādito a’’iti a ppaccayo, ‘‘gamissanto ccho vā sabbāsū’’ti dhātvantassa cchā deso, saralopādi, nāmabyapadese syā dyuppatti. Gacchanta si itīdha ‘‘vā’’ti vattamāne ‘‘simhi gacchantādīnaṃ ntasaddo aṃ’’ iti nta ssa a mādeso.

The roots `gamu` and `sappa` are in the sense of going. In the sense "he goes" (`gacchati`) the suffix `anta` is used, then the suffix `a` by [§445 (433): Bhūvādito a](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m445), the replacement `ccha` for the final of the root by [§476 (442): Gamissanto ccho vā sabbāsu](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m476), and the elision of the vowel and the rest; once the word is designated a noun (`nāmabyapadesa`), `si` and the other inflections arise. Here, at `gacchanta` + `si`, while “`vā`” continues, `nta` is replaced by `aṃ` by [§186 (107): Simhigacchantādīnaṃ ntasaddo aṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m186).

> Vā saddassa vavatthitavibhāsattā[^24] e kāro kāraparassa na bhavati, saralopādi, so puriso gacchaṃ, gacchanto gaṇhāti, sesaṃ guṇavantusamaṃ.

Since the word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`), the replacement does not take place after the letters `e` and `o`. With the elision of the vowel and the rest: `so puriso gacchaṃ, gacchanto gaṇhāti` ("that man takes while going"). The rest is the same as `guṇavantu`.

> Itthiyaṃ ‘‘nadādito vā ī’’ti ī paccayo, ‘‘sesesu ntuvā’’ti ntu byapadese ‘‘vā’’ti adhikicca ‘‘ntussa tamīkāre’’ti ta kāre saralopasilopā, sā kaññā gacchatī, gacchantī iccādi itthisamaṃ.

In the feminine the suffix `ī` is used by [§238 (187): Nadādito vā ī](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m238); once the `nta` is designated `ntu` by [§187 (108): Sesesu ntuva](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m187), with “`vā`” taken as governing, `ntu` becomes `ta` before the `ī` by [§241 (191): Ntussa tamīkāre](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m241), and the vowel and `si` are elided: `sā kaññā gacchatī, gacchantī` ("that girl, going"), and so on, the same as `itthī`.

> Napuṃsake pure viya nta ssa a mādeso, taṃ cittaṃ gacchaṃ, gacchantaṃ, gacchantāni iccādi pulliṅgasamaṃ.

In the neuter, `nta` is replaced by `aṃ` as before: `taṃ cittaṃ gacchaṃ, gacchantaṃ` ("that mind, going"), `gacchantāni`, and so on, the same as the masculine.

> Tathā gacchatīti atthe māna ppaccayo, cchā desādi ca, so gacchamāno gaṇhāti, te gacchamānā iccādi purisa saddasamaṃ. Sā gacchamānā, tā gacchamānāyo iccādi kaññā saddasamaṃ. Taṃ gacchamānaṃ, tāni gacchamānāni iccādi citta saddasamaṃ.

Likewise, in the sense "he goes" the suffix `māna` is used, with the replacement `ccha` and the rest: `so gacchamāno gaṇhāti` ("he takes while going"), `te gacchamānā` ("they, going"), and so on, the same as the word `purisa`; `sā gacchamānā` ("she, going"), `tā gacchamānāyo` ("they, going"), and so on, the same as the word `kaññā`; `taṃ gacchamānaṃ` ("it, going"), `tāni gacchamānāni` ("they, going"), and so on, the same as the word `citta`.

> Gacchīyatīti atthe ‘‘attanopadāni bhāve ca kammanī’’ti kammani māna ppaccayo, ‘‘bhāvakammesu yo’’ti ya ppaccayo, ‘‘ivaṇṇāgamo vā’’ti i kārāgamo, cchā deso, so tena gacchiyamāno, sā gacchiyamānā, taṃ gacchiyamānaṃ.

In the sense "it is gone to" (`gacchīyati`), the suffix `māna` is used in the passive by [§453 (444): Attanopadāni bhāve ca kammani](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m453), the suffix `ya` by [§440 (445): Bhāvakammesu yo](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m440), the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of the letter `i` by [§442 (448): Ivaṇṇāgamo vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m442), and the replacement `ccha`: `so tena gacchiyamāno` ("he, being gone to by him"), `sā gacchiyamānā`, `taṃ gacchiyamānaṃ`.

> Cchā desābhāve ‘‘pubbarūpañcā’’ti ya kārassa ma kāro, dhammo adhigammamāno hitāya bhavati, adhigammamānā, adhigammamānaṃ.

When the replacement `ccha` is absent, the letter `y` becomes the letter `m` by [§443 (449): Pubbarūpañca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m443): `dhammo adhigammamāno hitāya bhavati` ("the Dhamma, being attained, makes for welfare"), `adhigammamānā`, `adhigammamānaṃ`.

> Tathā maha pūjāyaṃ, mahatīti mahaṃ, mahanto, mahatī, mahantī, mahaṃ, mahantaṃ, mahamāno, mahamānā, mahamānaṃ. Kammani ‘‘yamhi dādhāmāṭhāhāpā maha mathādīnamī’’iti dhātvantassa a kārassa īkāro, mahīyamāno, mahīyamānā, mahīyamānaṃ.

Likewise the root `maha` is in the sense of honouring (`pūjā`): "he honours" (`mahati`) — `mahaṃ`, `mahanto` ("honouring"); `mahatī`, `mahantī`; `mahaṃ`, `mahantaṃ`; `mahamāno`, `mahamānā`, `mahamānaṃ`. In the passive, the letter `a` at the end of the root becomes the letter `ī` by [§502 (493): Yamhi dā dhā mā ṭhā hā pā mahamathādīnamī](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m502): `mahīyamāno`, `mahīyamānā`, `mahīyamānaṃ` ("being honoured").

> Evaṃ caratīti caraṃ, caratī, carantī, carantaṃ, caramāno, cariyamāno, pacatīti pacaṃ, pacatī, pacantī, pacantaṃ, pacamāno, paccamāno, ‘‘tassa cavagga’’iccādinā ca vaggattaṃ, dvittañca.

So too "he walks" (`carati`) — `caraṃ`, `caratī`, `carantī`, `carantaṃ`, `caramāno` ("walking"), `cariyamāno` ("being walked"); and "he cooks" (`pacati`) — `pacaṃ`, `pacatī`, `pacantī`, `pacantaṃ`, `pacamāno` ("cooking"), `paccamāno` ("being cooked"), where the `ya` becomes a letter of the `c`-group by [§441 (447): Tassa cavaggayakāravakārattaṃ sadhātvantassa](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m441) and the rest, and is doubled.

:::tip[Example]

> `mahīyamāno vajirāvudhoriva` \
> [(19Vv 2.1.14: Mahārathavimānavatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/19Vv/2/2.1/2.1.14#880) \
🔵🚹①⨀(√maha + ya + māna) 🚹①⨀(vajirāvudha) 🔼(iva) \
being honoured | the wielder of the thunderbolt | like

:::

> Bhū sattāyaṃ, bhavatīti atthe anta ppaccayo, a ppaccayavuddhiavādesādi, so bhavaṃ, bhavanto. Itthiyaṃ īpaccayo, ‘‘bhavato bhoto’’ti bhotā deso, bhotī, bhotī, bhotiyo. Napuṃsake bhavaṃ, bhavantaṃ, bhavantāni, abhibhavamāno. Bhāve bhūyamānaṃ. Kammani abhibhūyamāno.

The root `bhū` is in the sense of being (`sattā`). In the sense "he is" (`bhavati`) the suffix `anta` is used, with the suffix `a`, `vuddhi`, the replacement `ava` and the rest: `so bhavaṃ`, `bhavanto` ("he, being"). In the feminine, the suffix `ī` and the replacement `bhota` by [§242 (192): Bhavato bhoto](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m242): `bhotī`; `bhotī`, `bhotiyo`. In the neuter, `bhavaṃ`, `bhavantaṃ`, `bhavantāni`; and `abhibhavamāno` ("overcoming"). In the impersonal, `bhūyamānaṃ` ("being"); in the passive, `abhibhūyamāno` ("being overcome").

> Jara vayohānimhi, ‘‘jara marāna’’ntiādinā jīra jīyyā desā, jīratīti jīraṃ, jīrantī, jīrantaṃ, jīramāno, jīrīyamāno, jīyaṃ, jīyantī, jīyantaṃ, jīyamāno, jīyyamāno.

The root `jara` is in the sense of the loss of youth (`vayohāni`), with the replacements `jīra` and `jīyya` by [§505 (482): Jaramarānaṃ jīra jīyya mīyyā vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m505) and the rest: "he ages" (`jīrati`) — `jīraṃ`, `jīrantī`, `jīrantaṃ`, `jīramāno` ("ageing"), `jīrīyamāno` ("being aged"); `jīyaṃ`, `jīyantī`, `jīyantaṃ`, `jīyamāno`, `jīyyamāno`.

> Mara pāṇacāge, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ekassa ya kārassa lopo, maratīti mīyaṃ, mīyantī, mīyantaṃ, mīyamāno, mīyyamāno, maraṃ, marantī, marantaṃ, maramāno, marīyamāno. Labhaṃ, labhantī, labhantaṃ, labhamāno, labbhamāno. Vahaṃ, vahantī, vahantaṃ, vahamāno, vuyhamāno. ‘‘Isuyamūnamanto ccho vā’’ti cchā deso, icchatīti icchaṃ, icchantī, icchantaṃ, icchamāno, icchīyamāno, issamāno.

The root `mara` is in the sense of giving up life (`pāṇacāga`), with one of the two letters `y` elided by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest: "he dies" (`marati`) — `mīyaṃ`, `mīyantī`, `mīyantaṃ`, `mīyamāno` ("dying"), `mīyyamāno`; `maraṃ`, `marantī`, `marantaṃ`, `maramāno`, `marīyamāno` ("being killed"). `Labhaṃ`, `labhantī`, `labhantaṃ`, `labhamāno` ("obtaining"), `labbhamāno` ("being obtained"). `Vahaṃ`, `vahantī`, `vahantaṃ`, `vahamāno` ("carrying"), `vuyhamāno` ("being carried"). With the replacement `ccha` by [§522 (476): Isuyamūnamanto ccho vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m522): "he wishes" (`icchati`) — `icchaṃ`, `icchantī`, `icchantaṃ`, `icchamāno` ("wishing"), `icchīyamāno`, `issamāno` ("being wished for").

> ‘‘Disassa passadissadakkhā vā’’ti passa dissa dakkhā desā, passatīti passaṃ, passantī, passantaṃ, passamāno, vipassīyamāno, dissamāno, dissanto, māna ssa antā deso, dissaṃ, dissantī, dissantaṃ, dakkhaṃ, dakkhantī, dakkhantaṃ, dakkhamāno dakkhiyamāno iccādi.

With the replacements `passa`, `dissa` and `dakkha` by [§471 (483): Disassa passa dissa dakkhā vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m471): "he sees" (`passati`) — `passaṃ`, `passantī`, `passantaṃ`, `passamāno` ("seeing"), `vipassīyamāno` ("being seen clearly"); `dissamāno` ("being seen"), and `dissanto` with `māna` replaced by `anta`; `dissaṃ`, `dissantī`, `dissantaṃ`; `dakkhaṃ`, `dakkhantī`, `dakkhantaṃ`, `dakkhamāno`, `dakkhiyamāno`, and so on.

> Tuda byathane, tudatīti tudaṃ, tudantī, tudantaṃ, tudamāno, tujjamāno. Pavisatīti pavisaṃ, pavisantī, pavisantaṃ, pavisamāno, pavisīyamāno iccādi.

The root `tuda` is in the sense of piercing (`byathana`): "he pierces" (`tudati`) — `tudaṃ`, `tudantī`, `tudantaṃ`, `tudamāno` ("piercing"), `tujjamāno` ("being pierced"). "He enters" (`pavisati`) — `pavisaṃ`, `pavisantī`, `pavisantaṃ`, `pavisamāno` ("entering"), `pavisīyamāno` ("being entered"), and so on.

:::tip[Example]

> `khuppipāsāya mīyamāno` \
> [(9M 2.3: Mahādukkhakkhandhasutta)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/9M/2/2.3#542) \
🚺③⨀(khuppipāsā) 🔵🚹①⨀(√mara + ya + māna) \
by hunger and thirst | being killed

:::

> Hū, bhū sattāyaṃ, a ppaccayalopo, pahotīti pahonto, pahontī, pahontaṃ, pahūyamānaṃ tena. Setīti sento, sentī, sentaṃ, semāno, sayaṃ, sayantī, sayantaṃ, sayamāno, sayāno vā, māna ssa ānā deso, atisīyamāno.

The roots `hū` and `bhū` are in the sense of being, with the suffix `a` elided: "he is able" (`pahoti`) — `pahonto`, `pahontī`, `pahontaṃ` ("being able"); `pahūyamānaṃ tena` ("being able, on his part"). "He lies" (`seti`) — `sento`, `sentī`, `sentaṃ`, `semāno` ("lying"); `sayaṃ`, `sayantī`, `sayantaṃ`, `sayamāno`, or `sayāno` with `māna` replaced by `āna`; `atisīyamāno` ("being surpassed").

> Asa sabbhāve, ‘‘sabbatthāsassādilopo cā’’ti a kārassa lopo, atthīti saṃ, santo, satī, santī, santaṃ, samāno, samānā, samānaṃ.

The root `asa` is in the sense of existing (`sabbhāva`), with the letter `a` elided by [§506 (496): Sabbatthā’sassādilopo ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m506): "he is" (`atthi`) — `saṃ`, `santo` ("being"); `satī`, `santī`; `santaṃ`; `samāno`, `samānā`, `samānaṃ`.

> Ṭhā gatinivattimhi, ‘‘vā’’ti vattamāne ‘‘ṭhā tiṭṭho’’ti tiṭṭhā deso, tiṭṭhaṃ, tiṭṭhantī, tiṭṭhantaṃ, tiṭṭhamāno. Tiṭṭhā bhāve ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ṭhā to ha kārāgamo, rassattañca, upaṭṭhahaṃ, upaṭṭhahantī, upaṭṭhahantaṃ, upaṭṭhahamāno. Ṭhīyamānaṃ tena, upaṭṭhīyamāno, upaṭṭhahīyamāno.

The root `ṭhā` is in the sense of the stopping of motion (`gatinivatti`): while “`vā`” continues, the replacement `tiṭṭha` by [§468 (492): Ṭhā tiṭṭho](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m468): `tiṭṭhaṃ`, `tiṭṭhantī`, `tiṭṭhantaṃ`, `tiṭṭhamāno` ("standing"). When `tiṭṭha` is absent, the insertion of the letter `h` after `ṭhā`, and shortening, by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest: `upaṭṭhahaṃ`, `upaṭṭhahantī`, `upaṭṭhahantaṃ`, `upaṭṭhahamāno` ("attending on"). `Ṭhīyamānaṃ tena` ("standing, on his part"); `upaṭṭhīyamāno`, `upaṭṭhahīyamāno` ("being attended on").

> Pā pāne, ‘‘pā pibo’’ti pibā deso, pibatīti pibaṃ, pibantī, pibantaṃ, pibamāno, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ba kārassa va ttaṃ, pivaṃ, pivantī, pivantaṃ, pivamāno, pīyamāno, pīyamānā, pīyamānaṃ iccādi.

The root `pā` is in the sense of drinking (`pāna`), with the replacement `piba` by [§469 (494): Pā pivo](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m469): "he drinks" (`pibati`) — `pibaṃ`, `pibantī`, `pibantaṃ`, `pibamāno` ("drinking"); and with the letter `b` becoming `v` by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest, `pivaṃ`, `pivantī`, `pivantaṃ`, `pivamāno`; `pīyamāno`, `pīyamānā`, `pīyamānaṃ` ("being drunk"), and so on.

> Hu dānādanahabyappadānesu, a ppaccaye pure viya dvibhāvādi, juhotīti juhaṃ, juhantī, juhantaṃ, juhamāno, hūyamāno. Evaṃ jahaṃ, jahantī, jahantaṃ, jahamāno, jahīyamāno. Dadātīti dadaṃ, dadantī, dadantaṃ, dadamāno, dvittā bhāve dānaṃ dento, dentī, dentaṃ, dīyamāno.

The root `hu` is in the senses of giving, eating and offering oblations; before the suffix `a`, the doubling and the rest as before: "he offers" (`juhoti`) — `juhaṃ`, `juhantī`, `juhantaṃ`, `juhamāno` ("offering"), `hūyamāno` ("being offered"). Likewise `jahaṃ`, `jahantī`, `jahantaṃ`, `jahamāno` ("abandoning"), `jahīyamāno` ("being abandoned"). "He gives" (`dadāti`) — `dadaṃ`, `dadantī`, `dadantaṃ`, `dadamāno` ("giving"); when there is no doubling, `dānaṃ dento` ("giving a gift"), `dentī`, `dentaṃ`; `dīyamāno` ("being given").

The root `janī` is in the sense of appearing (`pātubhāva`), with the change to `ā` by splitting [§585 (619): Janādīnamā timhi ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m585) at “`janādīnamā`”: "he is born" (`jāyati`) — `jāyaṃ`, `jāyamāno` ("being born"), `jaññamāno`.

### 647 (§651): Bhavissati gamādīhi ṇī ghiṇa (`ṇī`, `ghiṇ` after `gamu` and the like, in the future sense) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§651 (647): Bhavissati gamādīhi ṇī ghiṇa" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m651" description="`ṇī`, `ghiṇ` after `gamu` and the like, in the future sense" />
:::

> Bhavissati kāle gammamāne gamā dīhi dhātūhi ṇī ghiṇa iccete paccayā honti. Ṇa kārā vuddhatthā. Āyati gamanaṃ sīlamassāti atthe ṇī, vuddhiṇa lopā. Gāmī, gāmino, āgāmī kālo. Ghiṇa paccaye – ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā gha lopo, gāmaṃ gāmi, gāmī, gāmayo.

When future time is understood, the suffixes `ṇī` and `ghiṇ` are used after the roots `gamu` and the like. The letters `ṇ` serve for `vuddhi`. In the sense "going in the future is his habit" (`āyati gamanaṃ sīlamassa`), `ṇī`, with `vuddhi` and the elision of the `ṇ`: `gāmī` ("one who will go"), `gāmino` ("those who will go"); `āgāmī kālo` ("the time to come"). Before the suffix `ghiṇ`, the `gh` is elided by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest: `gāmaṃ gāmi` ("one who will go to the village"), `gāmī`, `gāmayo` ("those who will go").

> Kiriyāyaṃ kiriyatthāyaṃ gammamānāyaṃ dhātūhi ṇvutu iccete paccayā honti bhavissati kāle. Ṇvu mhi – ṇa lopavuddhiakā desā, karissaṃ vajatīti kārako vajati.

When an action (`kiriyā`) is understood, that is, when the purpose is an action (`kiriyattha`), the suffixes `ṇvu` and `tu` are used after roots in the sense of future time. With `ṇvu`: the elision of the `ṇ`, `vuddhi` and the replacement `aka`: "he goes, about to do" (`karissaṃ vajati`) — `kārako vajati` ("the doer goes").

### 650 (§655): Sese ssaṃ ntu mānānā (`ssaṃ`, `ntu`, `māna`, `āna` in the remaining [future] sense) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§655 (650): Sese ssaṃ ntumānānā" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m655" description="`ssaṃ`, `ntu`, `māna`, `āna` in the remaining [future] sense" />
:::

> Kammasmiṃ upapade sese aparisamattatthe dhātūhi ssaṃntu māna āna iccete paccayā honti bhavissati kāle gammamāne, te ca kitakattā kattari bhavanti. Kammaṃ karissatīti atthe ssaṃ paccayo, i kārāgamo, si lopo, kammaṃ karissaṃ vajati, sāpekkhattā na samāso. Ntu paccaye ‘‘tanādito oyirā’’ti o, ‘‘simhi vā’’ti ntva’ntassa a ttaṃ, kammaṃ karissatīti kammaṃ karonto vajati iccādi guṇavantu samaṃ.

When the object is the accompanying word, in the remaining sense, the sense of what is not yet finished, the suffixes `ssaṃ`, `ntu`, `māna` and `āna` are used after roots when future time is understood; and being `kita` suffixes, they denote the agent. In the sense "he will do the work" (`kammaṃ karissati`), the suffix `ssaṃ` is used, with the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of the letter `i` and the elision of `si`: `kammaṃ karissaṃ vajati` ("he goes, about to do the work"); since the word is dependent (`sāpekkha`), there is no compound. Before the suffix `ntu`, `o` by [§451 (520): Tanādito oyirā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m451), and the final of `ntu` becomes `a` by [§94 (105): Simhi vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m94): "he will do the work" — `kammaṃ karonto vajati` ("he goes, doing the work"), and so on, the same as `guṇavantu`.

> Khādanaṃ khādissatīti khādanaṃ khādissaṃ vajati, khādanaṃ khādanto, khādanaṃ khādamāno, khādanaṃ khādāno vajati. "He will eat the food" (`khādanaṃ khādissati`) — `khādanaṃ khādissaṃ vajati` ("he goes, about to eat the food"); `khādanaṃ khādanto`, `khādanaṃ khādamāno`, `khādanaṃ khādāno vajati` ("he goes, eating the food"). "He will walk the road" (`maggaṃ carissati`) — `maggaṃ carissaṃ` ("about to walk the road"), `maggaṃ caranto`, `maggaṃ caramāno`, `maggaṃ carāno vajati` ("he goes, walking the road"). The root `bhikkha` is in the sense of begging (`āyācana`): "he will beg alms" (`bhikkhaṃ bhikkhissati`) — `bhikkhaṃ bhikkhissaṃ carati` ("he goes about, about to beg alms"); `bhikkhaṃ bhikkhanto`, `bhikkhaṃ bhikkhamāno`, `bhikkhaṃ bhikkhāno carati` ("he goes about, begging alms"); and so on.
