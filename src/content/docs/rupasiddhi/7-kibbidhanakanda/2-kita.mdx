## Tekālika (Suffixes of all three times)

## Kitakappaccayantanaya (The `kita` suffixes)

> Idāni kitakappaccayā vuccante.

Now the `kita` suffixes are stated.

> Kara karaṇe, pure viya dhātusaññādi.

The root `kara` in the sense of doing; the designation `dhātu` (root) and the rest as before.

> Kumbhaiccupapadaṃ, tato dutiyā.

`kumbha` ("a pot") is the accompanying word (`upapada`); after it the second form ②.

> ‘‘Kumbhaṃ karoti, akāsi, karissatī’’ti vā viggahe –

In the analysis "he makes, made, or will make a pot" (`kumbhaṃ karoti, akāsi, karissati`) —

> ‘‘Parā, paccayā’’ti ca vattate.

And the words `parā` ("after") and `paccayā` ("suffixes") continue.

### 561 (§524): Dhātuyā kammādimhi ṇo (`ṇa` after a root preceded by its object and the like) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§524 (561): Dhātuyā kammādimhi ṇo" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m524" description="`ṇa` after a root preceded by its object and the like" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The `parā` and `paccayā` that are said to continue into this rule are those of the gloss of [545 (§540): Bhāvakammesu tabbānīyā](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r545), `paccayā parā honti`.

:::

> Kammasmiṃ ādimhi sati dhātuyā paro ṇappaccayo hoti.

When an object stands first, the suffix `ṇa` is used after the root.

> So ca –

And it [the suffix `ṇa`] —

<a name="r562"></a>

### 562 (§546): Aññe kita (the other [suffixes] are called `kita`) :badge[rūḷhī]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§546 (562): Aññe kita" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m546" description="the other [suffixes] are called `kita`" />
:::

> Tatiye dhātvādhikāre vihitā kiccehi aññe paccayā kiticceva saññā hontīti kitasaññā katā.

The suffixes prescribed in the third section governed by the word `dhātu`, other than the `kicca` suffixes, have simply the technical term ([`saññā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) `kita`; so the technical term `kita` is made.

### 563 (§624): Kattari kita (the `kita` suffixes denote the agent) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§624 (563): Kattari kita" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m624" description="the `kita` suffixes denote the agent" />
:::

> Kattari kārake kitapaccayo hotīti niyamato kattari bhavati, so ca ‘‘ṇādayo tekālikā’’ti vuttattā kālattaye ca hoti. Pure viya kāritabyapadesaṇalopavuddhiyo, paccayantassāliṅgattā syādimhi asampatte ‘‘taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃvātavetunādīsu cā’’ti kitakantattā nāmaṃva kate syādyuppatti, tato kumbhaṃ karotīti atthe ‘‘amādayo parapadebhī’’ti dutiyātappurisasamāso, ‘‘nāmāna’’ntiādinā samāsasaññā, ‘‘tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā cā’’ti vibhattilopo, ‘‘pakati cassa sarantassā’’ti pakatibhāvo, puna samāsattā nāmamiva kate syādyuppatti.

Since a `kita` suffix is used in the `kāraka` of the agent (`kattā`), by this restriction [the suffix `ṇa`] is used in the sense of the agent; and because it is so stated by [546 (§550): Ṇādayo tekālikā](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r546), it is used in all three times as well. As before, there follow the designation as a causative (`kāritabyapadesa`), elision of the `ṇ` and `vuddhi`. Since what ends in a suffix is without gender (`aliṅga`), `si` and the rest would not apply; but because it ends in a `kita` suffix it is treated as a noun by [§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m601), and so `si` and the rest arise. After that, in the sense "he makes a pot" (`kumbhaṃ karoti`), a `tappurisa` compound of the second form ② is formed by [§327 (351): Amādayo parapadebhi](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa#m327); it receives the technical term ([`saññā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) `samāsa` by [§316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa#m316) and the rest; its inflections are elided by [§317 (332): Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa#m317); it keeps its natural form (`pakatibhāva`) by [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa#m318); and, because it is now a compound, it is again treated as a noun and `si` and the rest arise.

> So kumbhakāro, te kumbhakārā iccādi. Itthiyaṃ kumbhakārī, kumbhakāriyo iccādi, tathā kammaṃ karotīti kammakāro. Evaṃ mālākāro, kaṭṭhakāro, rathakāro, suvaṇṇakāro, suttakāro, vuttikāro, ṭīkākāro.

`so kumbhakāro` ("he is a potter"), `te kumbhakārā` ("they are potters"), and so on; in the feminine `kumbhakārī` ("a woman potter"), `kumbhakāriyo` ("women potters"), and so on. Likewise "he does work" (`kammaṃ karoti`) — `kammakāro` ("a workman"). So `mālākāro` ("a garland-maker"), `kaṭṭhakāro` ("a woodworker"), `rathakāro` ("a chariot-maker"), `suvaṇṇakāro` ("a goldsmith"), `suttakāro` ("the author of a sutta"), `vuttikāro` ("the author of a vutti"), `ṭīkākāro` ("the author of a ṭīkā").

> Gaha upādāne, pattaṃ agaṇhi, gaṇhāti, gaṇhissatīti vā pattaggāho. Evaṃ rasmiggāho, rajjuggāho.

The root `gaha` in the sense of taking: "he took, takes or will take the bowl" (`pattaṃ agaṇhi, gaṇhāti, gaṇhissati`) — `pattaggāho` ("one who takes the bowl"). So `rasmiggāho` ("one who holds the reins"), `rajjuggāho` ("one who holds the rope").

> Ve tantasantāne, tantaṃ avāyi, vāyati, vāyissatīti vā tantavāyo, ‘‘te āvāyā kārite’’ti āyādeso, vākye panettha ‘‘te āvāyā’’ti yogavibhāgena āyādeso. Evaṃ tunnavāyo.

The root `ve` in the sense of weaving: "he wove, weaves or will weave thread" (`tantaṃ avāyi, vāyati, vāyissati`) — `tantavāyo` ("a weaver"), with the replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) `āya` by [§515 (541): Te āvāyā kārite](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m515); in the sentence here, however, the replacement `āya` comes by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) at `te āvāyā`. So `tunnavāyo` ("a tailor").

> Mā parimāṇe, dhaññaṃ amini, mināti, minissatīti vā atthe ṇappaccaye kate –

The root `mā` in the sense of measuring: once the suffix `ṇa` has been added in the sense "he measured, measures or will measure grain" (`dhaññaṃ amini, mināti, minissati`) —

> ‘‘Ṇamhī’’ti vattate.

“`Ṇamhi`” ("before a `ṇ` [suffix]") continues.

### 564 (§593): Ākārantānamāyo (`āya` for the final `ā` of a root before a `ṇ` suffix) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§593 (564): Ākārantānamāyo" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m593" description="`āya` for the final `ā` of a root before a `ṇ` suffix" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The `ṇamhi` that 563 says continues into this rule is that of [579 (§590): Ṇamhi ranjassa jo bhāvakaraṇesu](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r579), which stands later on the page.

:::

> Ākārantānaṃ dhātūnaṃ antassa āyādeso hoti ṇakārānubandhe paccaye pare, saralopādi. Dhaññamāyo. Evaṃ dānaṃ dadātīti dānadāyo.

The final of a root ending in `ā` is replaced ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) by `āya` when a suffix carrying the indicatory letter `ṇ` (`ṇakārānubandha`) follows; then elision of the vowel and the rest: `dhaññamāyo` ("a grain-measurer"). So "he gives gifts" (`dānaṃ dadāti`) — `dānadāyo` ("a giver of gifts").

> Kamu kantimhi, dhammaṃ akāmayi, kāmayati, kāmayissatīti vā dhammakāmo puriso, dhammakāmā kaññā, dhammakāmaṃ cittaṃ. Evaṃ atthakāmo, hitakāmo, sukhakāmo, dhammaṃ pāletīti dhammapālo iccādi.

The root `kamu` in the sense of desiring: "he desired, desires or will desire the Dhamma" (`dhammaṃ akāmayi, kāmayati, kāmayissati`) — `dhammakāmo puriso` ("a man who loves the Dhamma"), `dhammakāmā kaññā` ("a girl who loves the Dhamma"), `dhammakāmaṃ cittaṃ` ("a mind that loves the Dhamma"). So `atthakāmo` ("one who desires [another's] good"), `hitakāmo` ("one who desires [another's] welfare"), `sukhakāmo` ("one who desires happiness"); "he protects the Dhamma" (`dhammaṃ pāleti`) — `dhammapālo` ("a protector of the Dhamma"), and so on.

> Damu damane, ‘‘ariṃ adami, dameti, damissatī’’ti viggahe ‘‘dhātuyā’’ti adhikāro, ‘‘kammādimhī’’ti ca vattate.

The root `damu` in the sense of taming: in the analysis "he tamed, tames or will tame the enemy" (`ariṃ adami, dameti, damissati`), “`dhātuyā`” ("after a root") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), and “`kammādimhi`” ("when an object or the like stands first") also continues.

<a name="r565"></a>

### 565 (§525): Saññāyamanu (in a name, `a` [after the root] and the insertion of `nu` [in the word before]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§525 (565): Saññāyama nu" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m525" description="in a name, `a` [after the root] and the insertion of `nu` [in the word before]" />
:::

> Kammūpapade ādimhi sati saññāyaṃ gamyamānāyaṃ dhātuyā appaccayo hoti, upapadante nukārāgamo ca. Ettha ca ‘‘nu niggahītaṃ padante’’ti sutte ‘‘padante’’ti vacanato upapadanteyeva nukārāgamo hotīti daṭṭhabbaṃ. ‘‘Tesu vuddhī’’tiādinā ukāralopo. Ayaṃ pana nvāgamo samāsaṃ katvā upapadavibhattilope kateyeva hotīti veditabbaṃ.

When an object stands first as the accompanying word (`upapada`) and a name (`saññā`) is understood, the suffix `a` is used after the root, and the letter `nu` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) at the end of the accompanying word. And here, since the sutta [566 (§537): Nu niggahītaṃ padante](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r566) says `padante` ("at the end of a word"), it should be understood that `nu` is inserted only at the end of the accompanying word. Its `u` is elided by [§404 (370): Tesu vuddhilopāgamavikāraviparītādesā ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m404) and the rest. It should be understood, however, that this insertion of `nu` is made only once the compound has been formed and the inflection of the accompanying word elided.

### 566 (§537): Nu niggahītaṃ padante (the insertion of `nu` becomes niggahīta at the end of a word) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§537 (566): Nu niggahitaṃ padante" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m537" description="the insertion of `nu` becomes niggahīta at the end of a word" />
:::

> Upapadabhūtanāmapadante vattamāno nukārāgamo niggahītamāpajjate, niggahītassa vaggantattaṃ, sesaṃ samaṃ, vuddhābhāvova viseso, arindamo rājā.

The inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) `nu` occurring at the end of the noun that is the accompanying word becomes niggahīta, and the niggahīta becomes the nasal of its group (`vagganta`); the rest is the same, the only difference being that there is no `vuddhi`: `arindamo rājā` ("King Arindama, the tamer of enemies").

> Tathā tara taraṇe, vessaṃ taratīti vessantaro, taṇhaṃ karoti hiṃsatīti taṇhaṅkaro bhagavā. Evaṃ medhaṅkaro, saraṇaṅkaro, dīpaṅkaro.

Likewise the root `tara` in the sense of crossing: "he crosses everything" (`vessaṃ tarati`) — `vessantaro` ("Vessantara"); "he makes, destroys, craving" (`taṇhaṃ karoti hiṃsati`) — `taṇhaṅkaro bhagavā` ("the Blessed One Taṇhaṅkara"). So `medhaṅkaro` ("Medhaṅkara, the maker of wisdom"), `saraṇaṅkaro` ("Saraṇaṅkara, the maker of a refuge"), `dīpaṅkaro` ("Dīpaṅkara, the maker of light").

> ‘‘Ādimhi, a’’iti ca vattate.

“`Ādimhi`” and “`a`” also continue.

### 567 (§526): Pure dadā ca iṃ (`a` after `dadā` with `pura` before it, and `iṃ` for the `a` of `pura`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§526 (567): Pure dadā ca iṃ" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m526" description="`a` after `dadā` with `pura` before it, and `iṃ` for the `a` of `pura`" />
:::

> Purasadde ādimhi sati ‘‘dada dāne’’iccetāya dhātuyā appaccayo hoti, purasadde akārassa iñca hoti. Ettha ca ‘‘tadanuparodhenā’’ti paribhāsato purasaddantasseva iṃ hotīti daṭṭhabbaṃ. Ṇādīnaṃ tekālikattepi upapadatthavisesena atīteyevāyamappaccayo hotīti daṭṭhabbaṃ. Pure dānaṃ adadīti purindado sakko. Idhāpi vibhattilope kateyeva iṃ ādeso.

When the word `pura` stands first, the suffix `a` is used after the root `dada` in the sense of giving, and the `a` of the word `pura` becomes `iṃ`. And here, by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) [§56 (64): Tadanuparodhena](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m56), it should be understood that only the final [`a`] of the word `pura` becomes `iṃ`. Although `ṇa` and the rest belong to all three times, it should be understood that this suffix `a`, because of the particular sense of its accompanying word, is used in the past only: "he gave gifts formerly" (`pure dānaṃ adadi`) — `purindado sakko` ("Sakka the Purindada, the giver of old"). Here too the replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) `iṃ` is made only once the inflection has been elided.

> ‘‘Kammādimhi, a’’iti ca vattate.

“`Kammādimhi`” and “`a`” also continue.

### 568 (§527): Sabbato ṇvu tvāvī vā (`ṇvu`, `tu`, `āvī` [and `a`] after every root, with or without a preceding object) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§527 (568): Sabbato ṇvu tvāvī vā" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m527" description="`ṇvu`, `tu`, `āvī` [and `a`] after every root, with or without a preceding object" />
:::

> Sabbato dhātuto kammādimhi vā akammādimhi vā sati a ṇvu tu āvī iccete cattāro paccayā honti. Vāggahaṇaṃ ‘‘akammādimhi vā’’ti vikappanatthaṃ.

After every root, whether or not an object or the like stands first, these four suffixes are used: `a`, `ṇvu`, `tu` and `āvī`. The mention of `vā` is there to make the option (`vikappana`) "or when no object or the like stands first" (`akammādimhi vā`).

> Appaccaye tāva – dhara dhāraṇe, dhammaṃ adhari, dharati, dharissatīti vā dhammadharo. Evaṃ vinayadharo. Tathā taṃ karotīti takkaro, dvittaṃ. Evaṃ hitakaro, divasakaro, dinakaro, divākaro, nisākaro, dhanuṃ gaṇhātīti dhanuggaho. Evaṃ kaṭaggaho, sabbakāmaṃ dadātīti sabbakāmadado, sabbadado.

First, with the suffix `a`: the root `dhara` in the sense of holding: "he held, holds or will hold the Dhamma" (`dhammaṃ adhari, dharati, dharissati`) — `dhammadharo` ("one who knows the Dhamma by heart"). So `vinayadharo` ("one who knows the Vinaya by heart"). Likewise "he does that" (`taṃ karoti`) — `takkaro` ("one who does that"), with doubling. So `hitakaro` ("one who does good"), `divasakaro`, `dinakaro`, `divākaro` ("the day-maker, the sun"), `nisākaro` ("the night-maker, the moon"); "he holds a bow" (`dhanuṃ gaṇhāti`) — `dhanuggaho` ("an archer"). So `kaṭaggaho` ("victory, taking the winning throw"); "he gives every wish" (`sabbakāmaṃ dadāti`) — `sabbakāmadado` ("the giver of every wish"), `sabbadado` ("the giver of everything").

> Āto pana – annaṃ adāsi, dadāti, dadissatīti annado. Evaṃ dhanado, saccaṃ sandahatīti saccasandho. Pā pāne, majjaṃ pivatīti majjapo. Tā pālane, gavaṃ saddaṃ tāyatīti gottaṃ. Evaṃ kattari.

But after [a root ending in] `ā`: "he gave, gives or will give food" (`annaṃ adāsi, dadāti, dadissati`) — `annado` ("a giver of food"). So `dhanado` ("a giver of wealth"); "he keeps his word" (`saccaṃ sandahati`) — `saccasandho` ("true to his word"). The root `pā` in the sense of drinking: "he drinks liquor" (`majjaṃ pivati`) — `majjapo` ("a drinker of liquor"). The root `tā` in the sense of protecting: "it guards the name" (`gavaṃ saddaṃ tāyati`) — `gottaṃ` ("a clan"). Thus in the sense of the agent.

> Akammādimhi pana ‘‘yasmā dapetī’’ti sutte bhayaggahaṇena sesasādhanepi appaccayo.

When no object stands first, however, the suffix `a` is used in the other `kāraka` senses (`sādhana`) as well, by the mention of `bhaya` in the sutta [§271 (88, 308): Yasmā dapeti bhayamādatte vā tadapādānaṃ](/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m271).

> Nī pāpuṇane vipubbo, vinesi, vineti, vinessati etena, etthāti vā vinayo, ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti vuddhi, ayādeso ca, nayanaṃ nayo. Si sevāyaṃ nipubbo, nissīyittha, nissīyati, nissīyissatīti vā nissayo. Si saye, anusayi, anuseti, anusessatīti vā anusayo.

The root `nī` in the sense of reaching, with the prefix `vi`: "one trained, trains or will train by it, or in it" (`vinesi, vineti, vinessati etena, ettha`) — `vinayo` ("discipline"), with `vuddhi` by [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485) and the replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) `aya`; "leading" (`nayanaṃ`) — `nayo` ("a method"). The root `si` in the sense of resorting to, with the prefix `ni`: "it was, is or will be relied on" (`nissīyittha, nissīyati, nissīyissati`) — `nissayo` ("a support"). The root `si` in the sense of lying: "it lay, lies or will lie dormant" (`anusayi, anuseti, anusessati`) — `anusayo` ("an underlying tendency").

> I gatimhi patipubbo, paṭicca ekasmā phalametīti paccayo, samudayo. Ci caye, vinicchīyate anena, vinicchayanaṃ vā vinicchayo, uccayanaṃ uccayo, sañcayo, dhammaṃ vicinātīti dhammavicayo. Khī khaye, khayanaṃ khayo. Ji jaye, vijayanaṃ vijayo, jayo. Kī dabbavinimaye, vikkayanaṃ vikkayo, kayo. Lī silesane, allīyati etthāti ālayo, layo. Evaṃ ivaṇṇantato.

The root `i` in the sense of going, with the prefix `pati`: "depending on it, the fruit comes from a single [cause]" (`paṭicca ekasmā phalameti`) — `paccayo` ("a condition"); `samudayo` ("arising"). The root `ci` in the sense of heaping up: "it is decided by this", or "deciding" (`vinicchīyate anena, vinicchayanaṃ`) — `vinicchayo` ("decision"); "heaping up" (`uccayanaṃ`) — `uccayo` ("accumulation"), `sañcayo` ("accumulation"); "he investigates the Dhamma" (`dhammaṃ vicināti`) — `dhammavicayo` ("investigation of the Dhamma"). The root `khī` in the sense of wasting away: "wasting away" (`khayanaṃ`) — `khayo` ("destruction"). The root `ji` in the sense of conquering: "conquering" (`vijayanaṃ`) — `vijayo` ("victory"), `jayo` ("victory"). The root `kī` in the sense of exchanging goods: "selling" (`vikkayanaṃ`) — `vikkayo` ("sale"), `kayo` ("buying"). The root `lī` in the sense of clinging: "one clings to it" (`allīyati ettha`) — `ālayo` ("attachment"), `layo` ("clinging"). Thus after roots ending in an `i`-vowel.

> Āsuṇantīti assavā, avādeso, paṭissavanaṃ paṭissavo. Su gatimhi, ābhavaggā savantīti āsavā. Ru sadde, ravatīti ravo. Bhavatīti bhavo. Pabhavati etasmāti pabhavo. Lū chedane, lavanaṃ lavo. Evaṃ uvaṇṇantato.

"They listen" (`āsuṇanti`) — `assavā` ("obedient"), with the replacement `ava`; "assenting" (`paṭissavanaṃ`) — `paṭissavo` ("assent"). The root `su` in the sense of going: "they flow as far as the peak of existence" (`ābhavaggā savanti`) — `āsavā` ("the defilements"). The root `ru` in the sense of sounding: "it cries" (`ravati`) — `ravo` ("a cry"). "It comes to be" (`bhavati`) — `bhavo` ("existence"). "[Something] arises from it" (`pabhavati etasmā`) — `pabhavo` ("a source"). The root `lū` in the sense of cutting: "reaping" (`lavanaṃ`) — `lavo` ("reaping"). Thus after roots ending in a `u`-vowel.

> Niggaṇhāti, niggahaṇaṃ vā niggaho, paggaho, saṅgaṇhāti tena, saṅgahaṇaṃ vā saṅgaho. Vara varaṇe, saṃvaraṇaṃ saṃvaro. Dara ādare, ādaraṇaṃ ādaro. Āgacchati, āgamananti vā āgamo, āgamīyanti ettha, etena vā atthāti āgamo pariyatti. Sappatīti sappo. Dibbatīti devo. Kamu padavikkhepe, pakkamanaṃ, pakkamatīti vā pakkamo. Evaṃ vikkamo.

"He restrains", or "restraining" (`niggaṇhāti, niggahaṇaṃ`) — `niggaho` ("restraint"); `paggaho` ("exertion"); "he treats kindly by it", or "kind treatment" (`saṅgaṇhāti tena, saṅgahaṇaṃ`) — `saṅgaho` ("support"). The root `vara` in the sense of restraining: "restraining" (`saṃvaraṇaṃ`) — `saṃvaro` ("restraint"). The root `dara` in the sense of regard: "regarding" (`ādaraṇaṃ`) — `ādaro` ("respect"). "It comes", or "coming" (`āgacchati, āgamanaṃ`) — `āgamo` ("a coming"); "the meanings are come at in it, or by it" (`āgamīyanti ettha, etena vā atthā`) — `āgamo`, the scriptures (`pariyatti`). "It creeps" (`sappati`) — `sappo` ("a snake"). "He shines" (`dibbati`) — `devo` ("a god"). The root `kamu` in the sense of stepping: "setting out", or "he sets out" (`pakkamanaṃ, pakkamati`) — `pakkamo` ("setting out"). So `vikkamo` ("valour").

> Cara caraṇe, vane caratīti vanacaro, kāmo avacarati etthāti kāmāvacaro loko, kāmāvacarā saññā, kāmāvacaraṃ cittaṃ. Gāvo caranti etthāti gocaro, chaṭṭhītappuriso.

The root `cara` in the sense of moving: "he roams in the forest" (`vane carati`) — `vanacaro` ("a forester"); "sensual desire ranges in it" (`kāmo avacarati ettha`) — `kāmāvacaro loko` ("the sense-sphere world"), `kāmāvacarā saññā` ("sense-sphere perception"), `kāmāvacaraṃ cittaṃ` ("a sense-sphere mind"). "Cattle roam in it" (`gāvo caranti ettha`) — `gocaro` ("pasture"), a `tappurisa` of the sixth form ⑥.

> Pādena pivatīti pādapo. Evaṃ kacchapo, tatiyātappuriso.

"It drinks with its foot" (`pādena pivati`) — `pādapo` ("a tree"). So `kacchapo` ("a tortoise"), a `tappurisa` of the third form ③.

> Ruha janane, sirasmiṃ ruhatīti siroruho, guhāyaṃ sayatīti guhāsayaṃ cittaṃ. Evaṃ kucchisayā vātā. Ṭhā gatinivattimhi, pabbate aṭṭhāsi, tiṭṭhati, ṭhassatīti vā pabbataṭṭho puriso, pabbataṭṭhā nadī, pabbataṭṭhaṃ osadhaṃ. Evaṃ thalaṭṭhaṃ, jalaṭṭhaṃ, sattamītappuriso.

The root `ruha` in the sense of being born: "it grows on the head" (`sirasmiṃ ruhati`) — `siroruho` ("hair"); "it lies in the cave" (`guhāyaṃ sayati`) — `guhāsayaṃ cittaṃ` ("the mind that lies in the cave"). So `kucchisayā vātā` ("the winds that lie in the belly"). The root `ṭhā` in the sense of ceasing to move: "he stood, stands or will stand on the mountain" (`pabbate aṭṭhāsi, tiṭṭhati, ṭhassati`) — `pabbataṭṭho puriso` ("a man standing on the mountain"), `pabbataṭṭhā nadī` ("a river on the mountain"), `pabbataṭṭhaṃ osadhaṃ` ("a herb on the mountain"). So `thalaṭṭhaṃ` ("standing on dry land"), `jalaṭṭhaṃ` ("standing in the water"), a `tappurisa` of the seventh form ⑦.

### 570 (§622): Anakā yuṇvūnaṃ (`ana`, `aka` for `yu`, `ṇvu`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§622 (570): Anakā yu ṇvūnaṃ" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m622" description="`ana`, `aka` for `yu`, `ṇvu`" />
:::

> Yuṇvu iccetesaṃ paccayānaṃ ana aka iccete ādesā hontīti akādeso. Sesaṃ kumbhakārasaddasamaṃ, rathakārako. Tathā annaṃ dadātīti annadāyako, ‘‘ākārantānamāyo’’ti āyādeso, ‘‘itthiyamato āpaccayo’’ti āpaccayo, ‘‘tesu vuddhī’’tiādinā akārassa ikāro, annadāyikā kaññā, annadāyakaṃ kulaṃ. Lokaṃ netīti lokanāyako, vineti satteti vināyako, ‘‘te āvāyā kārite’’ti āyādeso.

The suffixes `yu` and `ṇvu` are replaced ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) by `ana` and `aka` respectively; so [`ṇvu` here] is replaced by `aka`. The rest is the same as for the word `kumbhakāra`: `rathakārako` ("a chariot-maker"). Likewise "he gives food" (`annaṃ dadāti`) — `annadāyako` ("a giver of food"), with the replacement `āya` by [564 (§593): Ākārantānamāyo](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r564); with the suffix `ā` by [§237 (176): Itthīyamato āpaccayo](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m237) and `i` for the `a` by [§404 (370): Tesu vuddhilopāgamavikāraviparītādesā ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m404) and the rest, `annadāyikā kaññā` ("a girl who gives food"); `annadāyakaṃ kulaṃ` ("a family that gives food"). "He leads the world" (`lokaṃ neti`) — `lokanāyako` ("the leader of the world"); "he trains beings" (`vineti satte`) — `vināyako` ("a guide"), with the replacement `āya` by [§515 (541): Te āvāyā kārite](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m515).

> Akammūpapade karotīti kārako, kārikā, kārakaṃ. Dadātīti dāyako, dāyikā, dāyakaṃ. Netīti nāyako, nāyikā, nāyakaṃ. Bhagavato ovādānusāsaniṃ asuṇi, suṇāti, suṇissatīti vā sāvako, sāvikā, āvādeso. Lunātīti lāvako. Pu pavane, punātīti pāvako, bhavatīti bhāvako, upāsatīti upāsako, upāsikā. Gaṇhātīti gāhako.

With no object as the accompanying word: "he does" (`karoti`) — `kārako` ("a doer"), `kārikā`, `kārakaṃ`; "he gives" (`dadāti`) — `dāyako` ("a giver"), `dāyikā`, `dāyakaṃ`; "he leads" (`neti`) — `nāyako` ("a leader"), `nāyikā`, `nāyakaṃ`. "He heard, hears or will hear the Blessed One's advice and instruction" (`bhagavato ovādānusāsaniṃ asuṇi, suṇāti, suṇissati`) — `sāvako` ("a disciple"), `sāvikā`, with the replacement `āva`. "He reaps" (`lunāti`) — `lāvako` ("a reaper"). The root `pu` in the sense of purifying: "it purifies" (`punāti`) — `pāvako` ("fire"); "he becomes" (`bhavati`) — `bhāvako` ("one who becomes"); "he attends" (`upāsati`) — `upāsako` ("a lay follower"), `upāsikā`. "He takes" (`gaṇhāti`) — `gāhako` ("one who takes").

> Pacatīti pācako. Ayaji, yajati, yajissatīti vā yājako. Ettha hi ‘‘kagā cajāna’’nti cajānaṃ kagatte sampatte –

"He cooks" (`pacati`) — `pācako` ("a cook"). "He sacrificed, sacrifices or will sacrifice" (`ayaji, yajati, yajissati`) — `yājako` ("a sacrificer"). For here, when the change of `c` and `j` to `k` and `g` would apply by [554 (§623): Kagā cajānaṃ](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r554) —

<a name="r571"></a>

### 571 (§618): Na kagattaṃ cajā ṇvumhi (`c`, `j` do not become `k`, `g` before `ṇvu`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§618 (571): Na ka gattaṃ ca jāṇvumhi" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m618" description="`c`, `j` do not become `k`, `g` before `ṇvu`" />
:::

> Dhātvantabhūtā cakārajakārā kakāragakārattaṃ nāpajjante ṇvuppaccaye pareti paṭisiddhattā na bhavati.

The letters `c` and `j` at the end of a root do not become the letters `k` and `g` when the suffix `ṇvu` follows; so, being prohibited, [the change] does not take place.

> Jana janane, janetīti janako, janikā, ‘‘ghaṭādīnaṃ vā’’ti ettha vāggahaṇena vuddhi na hoti. Evaṃ khanatīti khanako, sametīti samako, gametīti gamako, dametīti damako, ahani, hanti, hanissatīti vā vadhako, ‘‘vadho vā sabbatthā’’ti hanassa vadhādeso, hantīti ghātako[^2], ‘‘hanassa ghāto’’ti ṇvumhi ghātādeso, gāvo hanatīti goghātako, rundhatīti rundhako, niggahītāgamo, saṃyogantattā na vuddhi hoti. Evaṃ bhuñjatīti bhuñjako, kiṇātīti kāyako, pāletīti pālako, pūjetīti pūjako.

The root `jana` in the sense of producing: "he begets" (`janeti`) — `janako` ("a father"), `janikā` ("a mother"); by the mention of `vā` in [§484 (542): Ghaṭādīnaṃ vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m484) there is no `vuddhi`. So "he digs" (`khanati`) — `khanako` ("a digger"); "he pacifies" (`sameti`) — `samako` ("one who pacifies"); "he sends" (`gameti`) — `gamako` ("one who sends"); "he tames" (`dameti`) — `damako` ("a tamer"); "he killed, kills or will kill" (`ahani, hanti, hanissati`) — `vadhako` ("a killer"), with the replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) `vadha` for `hana` by [§592 (503): Vadho vā sabbattha](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m592); "he kills" (`hanti`) — `ghātako` ("a killer"), with the replacement `ghāta` before `ṇvu` by [§591 (544): Hanassa ghāto](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m591); "he kills cattle" (`gāvo hanati`) — `goghātako` ("a butcher"); "he obstructs" (`rundhati`) — `rundhako` ("one who obstructs"), with the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of niggahīta and no `vuddhi`, since [the root] then ends in a conjunct. So "he eats" (`bhuñjati`) — `bhuñjako` ("an eater"); "he buys" (`kiṇāti`) — `kāyako` ("a buyer"); "he protects" (`pāleti`) — `pālako` ("a protector"); "he honours" (`pūjeti`) — `pūjako` ("one who honours").

### 572 (§641): Nudādīhi yuṇvūnamanānanākānanakā sakāritehi ca (`ana`, `ānana`, `aka`, `ānanaka` for `yu` and `ṇvu` after `nuda` etc., and after causatives) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§641 (572): Nudādīhi yuṇvūnamanānanākānanakā sakāritehi ca" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m641" description="`ana`, `ānana`, `aka`, `ānanaka` for `yu` and `ṇvu` after `nuda` etc., and after causatives" />
:::

> Nudādīhi dhātūhi, sakāritehi ca dhātūhi paresaṃ yuṇvuppaccayānaṃ yathākkamaṃ ana ānana aka ānanaka iccete ādesā honti.

After the roots `nuda` and the like, and after roots with causative [suffixes], the suffixes `yu` and `ṇvu` that follow are replaced ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), in order, by `ana`, `ānana`, `aka` and `ānanaka`.

> Ettha hi –

For here —

> Sakāritehi yuṇvūnaṃ, kāriyassa vidhānato; \
> Kiccakitthambhavo dhātu-ppaccayehipi vediyo.

Since an operation on `yu` and `ṇvu` is prescribed after [roots] with causatives, \
it should be understood that `kicca` and `kita` [suffixes] arise after root suffixes (`dhātuppaccaya`) as well.

> Nuda khepe papubbo, panudi, panudati, panudissatīti vā atthe ṇvuppaccayo, tassiminā akādeso, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā nudissa dīgho, panūdako.

The root `nuda` in the sense of throwing, with the prefix `pa`: in the sense "he dispelled, dispels or will dispel" (`panudi, panudati, panudissati`), the suffix `ṇvu` is used and is replaced by `aka` by this rule; the [`u` of] `nuda` is lengthened by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest: `panūdako` ("one who dispels").

> Sūda paggharaṇe, sūdatīti sūdako. Ñā avabodhane, aññāsi, jānāti, jānissatīti vā atthe ṇvuppaccayo, tassānena ānanakādeso, ‘‘ñāssa jājaṃnā’’ti jādeso, saralopādi, jānanako.

The root `sūda` in the sense of oozing: "it oozes" (`sūdati`) — `sūdako` ("a cook"). The root `ñā` in the sense of understanding: in the sense "he knew, knows or will know" (`aññāsi, jānāti, jānissati`), the suffix `ṇvu` is used and is replaced by `ānanaka` by this rule; `jā` replaces `ñā` by [§470 (514): Ñāssa jā jaṃ nā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m470); elision of the vowel and the rest: `jānanako` ("one who knows").

> Sakāritehi pana āṇa pesane, āṇāpesi, āṇāpeti, āṇāpessatīti vā atthe ‘‘sabbato ṇvutvāvī vā’’ti ṇvuppaccayo, tassiminā akādeso, saralopādi, āṇāpako, saññāpetīti saññāpako, sañjānanako, ettha ānanakādeso, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā kāritalopo. Tathā dāpetīti dāpako, ‘‘anakā yuṇvūna’’nti akādeso, patiṭṭhāpetīti patiṭṭhāpako, nibbānaṃ sampāpetīti nibbānasampāpako, kārāpetīti kārāpako, kārāpikā iccādi.

After roots with causatives, however: the root `āṇa` in the sense of sending out: in the sense "he commanded, commands or will command" (`āṇāpesi, āṇāpeti, āṇāpessati`), the suffix `ṇvu` is used by [568 (§527): Sabbato ṇvu tvāvī vā](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r568) and is replaced by `aka` by this rule; elision of the vowel and the rest: `āṇāpako` ("one who commands"); "he makes known" (`saññāpeti`) — `saññāpako` ("one who makes known"), `sañjānanako` ("one who makes known"), here with the replacement `ānanaka` and elision of the causative by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest. Likewise "he causes to give" (`dāpeti`) — `dāpako` ("one who causes to give"), with the replacement `aka` by [570 (§622): Anakā yuṇvūnaṃ](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r570); "he establishes" (`patiṭṭhāpeti`) — `patiṭṭhāpako` ("one who establishes"); "he brings to nibbāna" (`nibbānaṃ sampāpeti`) — `nibbānasampāpako` ("one who brings to nibbāna"); "he causes to be done" (`kārāpeti`) — `kārāpako` ("one who causes to be done"), `kārāpikā`, and so on.

> Tuppaccaye akāsi, karoti, karissatīti vā atthe ‘‘sabbato ṇvutvāvī vā’’ti tuppaccayo, so ca kitasaññattā ṇvuppaccayo viya sabbattha kattariyeva bhavati.

With the suffix `tu`: in the sense "he did, does or will do" (`akāsi, karoti, karissati`), the suffix `tu` is used by [568 (§527): Sabbato ṇvu tvāvī vā](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r568); and since it has the technical term `kita`, it is used, like the suffix `ṇvu`, in the sense of the agent only, everywhere.

> ‘‘Antassā’’ti vattate.

“`Antassa`” ("of the final") continues.

### 573 (§619): Karassa ca tattaṃ tusmiṃ (and the `r` of `kara` becomes `t` before `tu`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§619 (573): Karassa ca tattaṃ tusmiṃ" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m619" description="and the `r` of `kara` becomes `t` before `tu`" />
:::

> Kara iccetassa dhātussa antassa rakārassa takārattaṃ hoti tuppaccaye pare. Casaddena bharādīnañca, tato nāmamiva kate syādyuppatti, ‘‘satthupitādīnamā sismiṃ silopo cā’’ti āttaṃ, silopo, tassa kattā takkattā, chaṭṭhīsamāso. Tathā bharatīti bhattā.

The final letter `r` of the root `kara` becomes the letter `t` when the suffix `tu` follows; by the word `ca`, so does [the final] of `bhara` and the like. Then, once [the word] is treated as a noun, `si` and the rest arise; [the `u`] becomes `ā` and `si` is elided by [§199 (158): Satthupitādīnamā sismiṃ silopo ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m199): `kattā` ("a doer"). "The doer of that" (`tassa kattā`) — `takkattā`, a compound of the sixth form ⑥. Likewise "he supports" (`bharati`) — `bhattā` ("a husband, a master").

> Hara haraṇe, haratīti hattā, bhindatīti bhettā, bheditā vā, chindatīti chettā, dadātīti dātā, bhojanassa dātā[^3] bhojanadātā, sandahatīti sandhātā, avaci, vacati, vakkhatīti vā vattā, ‘‘bhujādīnamanto no dvi cā’’ti dhātvantalopo, dvittañca, bhuñjatīti bhottā, abujjhi, bujjhati, bujjhissatīti vā bujjhitā, yakārikārāgamā, jānātīti ñātā, jinātīti jetā, suṇātīti sotā, gaṇhātīti gahetā, bhavatīti bhavitā, saratīti saritā, gacchatīti gantā. ‘‘Gama khana hanādīnaṃ tuṃ tabbādīsu na’’iti dhātvantassa nattaṃ. Evaṃ khanatīti khantā, hanatīti hantā, maññatīti mantā, pāletīti pāletā, pālayitā.

The root `hara` in the sense of carrying: "he carries" (`harati`) — `hattā` ("a carrier"); "he breaks" (`bhindati`) — `bhettā` ("a breaker"), or `bheditā`; "he cuts" (`chindati`) — `chettā` ("a cutter"); "he gives" (`dadāti`) — `dātā` ("a giver"); "the giver of food" (`bhojanassa dātā`) — `bhojanadātā`; "he joins together" (`sandahati`) — `sandhātā` ("a reconciler"); "he spoke, speaks or will speak" (`avaci, vacati, vakkhati`) — `vattā` ("a speaker"), with elision of the root's final and doubling by [560 (§578): Bhujādīnamanto no dvi ca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r560); "he eats" (`bhuñjati`) — `bhottā` ("an eater"); "he understood, understands or will understand" (`abujjhi, bujjhati, bujjhissati`) — `bujjhitā` ("one who understands"), with the insertions ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of `y` and `i`; "he knows" (`jānāti`) — `ñātā` ("a knower"); "he conquers" (`jināti`) — `jetā` ("a conqueror"); "he hears" (`suṇāti`) — `sotā` ("a hearer"); "he takes" (`gaṇhāti`) — `gahetā` ("a taker"); "he becomes" (`bhavati`) — `bhavitā` ("one who becomes"); "he remembers" (`sarati`) — `saritā` ("one who remembers"); "he goes" (`gacchati`) — `gantā` ("one who goes"), the root's final becoming `n` by [551 (§596): Gama khana hanādīnaṃ tuṃtabbādīsu na](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r551). So "he digs" (`khanati`) — `khantā` ("a digger"); "he kills" (`hanati`) — `hantā` ("a killer"); "he thinks" (`maññati`) — `mantā` ("a thinker"); "he protects" (`pāleti`) — `pāletā`, `pālayitā` ("a protector").

> Kārite bhāvetīti bhāvetā, bhāvayitā. Evaṃ sāretā, sārayitā, dāpetā, dāpayitā, hāpetā, hāpayitā, nirodhetā, nirodhayitā, bodhetā, bodhayitā, ñāpetā, ñāpayitā, sāvetā, sāvayitā, gāhetā[^4], gāhayitā, kāretā, kārayitā, kārāpetā, kārāpayitā iccādi.

With a causative: "he develops" (`bhāveti`) — `bhāvetā`, `bhāvayitā` ("one who develops"). So `sāretā`, `sārayitā` ("one who reminds"); `dāpetā`, `dāpayitā` ("one who causes to give"); `hāpetā`, `hāpayitā` ("one who neglects"); `nirodhetā`, `nirodhayitā` ("one who makes cease"); `bodhetā`, `bodhayitā` ("one who awakens"); `ñāpetā`, `ñāpayitā` ("one who makes known"); `sāvetā`, `sāvayitā` ("one who announces"); `gāhetā`, `gāhayitā` ("one who makes take"); `kāretā`, `kārayitā` ("one who causes to do"); `kārāpetā`, `kārāpayitā` ("one who causes to be done"), and so on.

> Āvīpaccaye disa pekkhane, bhayaṃ apassi, passati, passissatīti vā atthe āvīpaccayo, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā disassa dassādeso, bhayadassāvī, bhayadassāvino iccādi daṇḍīva neyyaṃ. Itthiyaṃ bhayadassāvinī. Napuṃsake bhayadassāvi cittaṃ.

With the suffix `āvī`: the root `disa` in the sense of seeing: in the sense "he saw, sees or will see danger" (`bhayaṃ apassi, passati, passissati`), the suffix `āvī` is used; `dassa` replaces `disa` by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest: `bhayadassāvī` ("one who sees danger"), `bhayadassāvino`, and so on, to be inflected like `daṇḍī`. In the feminine `bhayadassāvinī`; in the neuter `bhayadassāvi cittaṃ` ("a mind that sees danger").

> Sāsa anusiṭṭhimhi, sadevakaṃ lokaṃ diṭṭhadhammikādivasena sāsatīti atthe –

The root `sāsa` in the sense of instructing: in the sense "he instructs the world with its gods in what concerns the present life and the rest" (`sadevakaṃ lokaṃ diṭṭhadhammikādivasena sāsati`) —

<LinkCard title="§566 (574): Sāsādīhi ratthu" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m566" description="`ratthu` after `sāsa` and the like" />

### 577 (§528): Visa ruja padādito ṇa (`ṇa` after `visa`, `ruja`, `pada` and the like [without a preceding object]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§528 (577): Visa ruja padādito ṇa" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m528" description="`ṇa` after `visa`, `ruja`, `pada` and the like [without a preceding object]" />

> Visa ruja pada iccevamādīhi dhātūhi paro ṇappaccayo hotīti ṇappaccayo. So ca kitasaññattā kattari bhavati, kāritabyapadesaṇalopavuddhiyo, paveso.

After the roots `visa`, `ruja`, `pada` and the like, the suffix `ṇa` is used: hence the suffix `ṇa`. And since it has the technical term `kita`, it is used in the sense of the agent; the designation as a causative, elision of the `ṇ` and `vuddhi`: `paveso` ("entering").

> Tathā ruja roge, aruji, rujati, rujissatīti vā rogo, ‘‘kagācajāna’’nti jakārassa gakāro, uppajjatīti uppādo. Phusa phusane, aphusi, phusati, phusissati, phusanti vā tena sampayuttāti phasso, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā phusassa phasso, saṃyogantattā na vuddhi. Bhavatīti bhāvo. Uca samavāye, ucatīti oko, cakārassa kakāro. Aya gatimhi, ayi, ayati, ayissati, ayati vā itoti āyo. Budha avagamane, sammā bujjhatīti sambodho, āharatīti āhāro, upahanatīti upaghāto, ‘‘hanassa ghāto’’ti ghātādeso.

Likewise the root `ruja` in the sense of disease: "it hurt, hurts or will hurt" (`aruji, rujati, rujissati`) — `rogo` ("disease"), the letter `j` becoming the letter `g` by [554 (§623): Kagā cajānaṃ](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r554); "it arises" (`uppajjati`) — `uppādo` ("arising"). "It comes to be" (`bhavati`) — `bhāvo` ("a state"). The root `uca` in the sense of gathering: "it gathers" (`ucati`) — `oko` ("an abode"), the letter `c` becoming the letter `k`. The root `aya` in the sense of going: "it went, goes, will go", or "it comes from here" (`ayi, ayati, ayissati, ayati vā ito`) — `āyo` ("income"). The root `budha` in the sense of understanding: "he fully understands" (`sammā bujjhati`) — `sambodho` ("full awakening"); "it brings" (`āharati`) — `āhāro` ("food"); "it strikes" (`upahanati`) — `upaghāto` ("injury"), with the replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) `ghāta` by [§591 (544): Hanassa ghāto](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m591).

### 579 (§590): Ṇamhi ranjassa jo bhāvakaraṇesu (`nj` of `ranja` becomes `j` before a `ṇ` suffix, in the senses of action and instrument) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§590 (579): Ṇamhiranjassa jo bhāvakaraṇesu" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m590" description="`nj` of `ranja` becomes `j` before a `ṇ` suffix, in the senses of action and instrument" />
:::

> Ranja iccetassa dhātussa antabhūtassa njassa jakārādeso hoti bhāvakaraṇa iccetesvatthesu vihite ṇakāravatippaccaye pare.

The final `nj` of the root `ranja` is replaced by the letter `j` when a suffix carrying the letter `ṇ` follows, one prescribed in the senses of the action itself (`bhāva`) and the instrument (`karaṇa`).

> Ettha hi –

For here —

> Ṇamhi ranjassa karaṇe, jādesassa vidhānato; \
> Akattaripi viññeyyo, kārake ṇassa sambhavoti.

Since the replacement `j` is prescribed for `ranja` before `ṇ` in the sense of the instrument, \
it should be understood that the suffix `ṇa` arises in a `kāraka` other than the agent as well.

> Ranjanti anenāti rāgo, rañjīyati anenāti vā rāgo, sayaṃ rañjatītipi rāgo. ‘‘Ṇamhi ranjassa jo’’ti yogavibhāgena jakāro. Pajjate anenāti pādo, patujjate anenāti patodo, jarīyati anenāti jāro. Evaṃ dāro. Tathā kammādīsu, bhujjatīti bhogo. Evaṃ bhāgo, bhāro, labbhatīti lābho, voharīyatīti vohāro, dīyatīti dāyo, vihaññati etasmāti vighāto, viharanti etthāti vihāro, āramanti etasminti ārāmo. Evaṃ papāto iccādi.

`rāgo` ("passion") is that by which they are impassioned (`ranjanti anena`), or that by which one is impassioned (`rañjīyati anena`), or also that which is itself impassioned (`sayaṃ rañjati`), the letter `j` coming by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) at “`Ṇamhi ranjassa jo`”. `pādo` ("a foot") is that by which one goes (`pajjate anena`); `patodo` ("a goad") is that by which one is goaded (`patujjate anena`); `jāro` ("a paramour") is the one by whom one is worn out (`jarīyati anena`); so too `dāro` ("a wife"). Likewise in the senses of the object and the rest: `bhogo` ("enjoyment, property") is what is enjoyed (`bhujjati`); so too `bhāgo` ("a share") and `bhāro` ("a burden"); `lābho` ("gain") is what is obtained (`labbhati`); `vohāro` ("an expression") is what is expressed (`voharīyati`); `dāyo` ("a gift") is what is given (`dīyati`); `vighāto` ("affliction") is that because of which one suffers hardship (`vihaññati etasmā`); `vihāro` ("a dwelling") is that in which they dwell (`viharanti ettha`); `ārāmo` ("a park") is that in which they delight (`āramanti etasmiṃ`); so too `papāto` ("a precipice"), and so on.

> ‘‘Ṇa’’iti vattate.

### 580 (§529): Bhāve ca (and `ṇa` in the sense of the action itself) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

The suffix `ṇa` is used after roots in the sense of the action itself (`bhāva`), when the action itself is what is to be expressed: `bhāvo` ("being") is "there is being" (`bhūyate`) or "being" (`bhavanaṃ`); `pāko` ("cooking") is "it is cooked" (`paccate`) or "cooking" (`pacanaṃ`), with the replacement `k` by [554 (§623): Kagā cajānaṃ](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r554).

<a name="r588"></a>

### 588 (§642): Iyatamakiesānamantassaro dīghaṃ kvaci disassa guṇaṃ do raṃ sakkhī ca (lengthened pronoun + `disa`, `risa`, `dikkha`, `dī`: `īdiso`, `tādiso`, `kīdiso`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§642 (588): I ya ta ma ki e sāna’mantassaro dīghaṃ kvaci du sa ssa guṇaṃ do raṃ sakkhī ca" href="/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m642" description="lengthened pronoun + `disa`, `risa`, `dikkha`, `dī`: `īdiso`, `tādiso`, `kīdiso`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

Kaccāyana's sutta reads `du sa ssa guṇaṃ`, the `u` of `dusa` taking `guṇa`; Buddhappiya reads `disassa guṇaṃ`, the pronouns being the subordinate member (`guṇa`) of `disa`.

:::

> Ima ya ta amha kiṃ eta samāna iccetesaṃ sabbanāmānaṃ upamānupapadabhāvena disa ssa dhātussa guṇabhūtānaṃ anto saro dīgha māpajjate, disa iccetassa dhātussa antassa sa kkha ī iccete ādesā ca honti. Disa ssa da kāro ra kāramāpajjateti kvimhi dhātvantassa sa saddādesaṃ katvā kvilopādimhi ca kate i iti nipātanena ima saddassi kāre, tassiminā dīghe ca kate syā dyuppatti.

When the pronouns `ima`, `ya`, `ta`, `amha`, `kiṃ`, `eta` and `samāna` have become the subordinate member (`guṇa`) of the root `disa` by standing before it as the standard of comparison (`upamāna`), its accompanying word (`upapada`), their final vowel is lengthened; the final of the root `disa` is also replaced by `sa`, `kkha` and `ī`; and the `d` of `disa` becomes `r`. Accordingly, before `kvi` the root's final is replaced by the sound `sa`, the elision of `kvi` and the rest are done, the word `ima` becomes `i` by the direct statement “`i`”, and that `i` is lengthened by this rule; then the inflections `si` and the rest are added.

> Īdiso puriso, īdisā kaññā, īdisī vā, īdisaṃ cittaṃ. Tathā yamiva naṃ passati, yo viya dissatīti vā yādiso, yādisā, yādisī, yādisaṃ. Tamiva naṃ passati, so viya dissatīti vā tādiso, tādisā, tādisī, tādisaṃ. Mamiva naṃ passati, ahaṃ viya so dissatīti vā mādiso, mādisā, mādisī, mādisaṃ, ma iti nipātanena amha saddassa ma saddādeso. Kimiva naṃ passati, ko viya dissatīti vā kīdiso, kīdisā, kīdisī, kīdisaṃ. Etamiva naṃ passati, eso viya dissatīti vā ediso, etādiso vā, edisā, edisī, edisaṃ, e iti nipātanena eta saddassa e kāro. Samānaṃ katvā naṃ passati, samāno viya dissatīti sādiso, sadiso, sa iti nipātanena samāna ssa sā deso, tadantassa vā dīgho, sādisā, sādisī, sadisā, sadisī, sādisaṃ, sadisaṃ.

`īdiso puriso` ("such a man"), `īdisā kaññā` or `īdisī` ("such a girl"), `īdisaṃ cittaṃ` ("such a mind"). Likewise `yādiso`, `yādisā`, `yādisī`, `yādisaṃ` ("of whatever kind") is one whom one sees like whichever one (`yaṃ iva naṃ passati`), or who is seen like whichever one (`yo viya dissati`). `tādiso`, `tādisā`, `tādisī`, `tādisaṃ` ("of that kind") is one whom one sees like that one (`taṃ iva naṃ passati`), or who is seen like that one (`so viya dissati`). `mādiso`, `mādisā`, `mādisī`, `mādisaṃ` ("like me") is one whom one sees like me (`maṃ iva naṃ passati`), or who is seen like me (`ahaṃ viya so dissati`), the word `amha` being replaced by the sound `ma` by the direct statement “`ma`”. `kīdiso`, `kīdisā`, `kīdisī`, `kīdisaṃ` ("of what kind?") is one whom one sees like whom (`kiṃ iva naṃ passati`), or who is seen like whom (`ko viya dissati`). `ediso` or `etādiso`, `edisā`, `edisī`, `edisaṃ` ("such as this") is one whom one sees like this one (`etaṃ iva naṃ passati`), or who is seen like this one (`eso viya dissati`), the word `eta` becoming the letter `e` by the direct statement “`e`”. `sādiso`, `sadiso` ("similar") is one whom one sees having made him the same (`samānaṃ katvā naṃ passati`), or who is seen like one who is the same (`samāno viya dissati`); `samāna` is replaced by `sa` by the direct statement “`sa`”, and the final of that `sa` is optionally lengthened: `sādisā`, `sādisī`, `sadisā`, `sadisī`, `sādisaṃ`, `sadisaṃ`.

### 590 (§532): Tassīlādīsu ṇītvāvī ca (`ṇī`, `tu`, `āvī` in the sense of habit and the like) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]

> Sīlaṃ pakati, tassīla taddhamma tassādhukārīsvatthesu gamyamānesu sabbadhātūhi ṇī tu āvī iccete paccayā hontīti kattari ṇī paccayo, saṃyogantattāna vuddhi. Sesaṃ neyyaṃ.

`sīla` is one's nature (`pakati`). When the senses "that is his habit" (`tassīla`), "that is his nature" (`taddhamma`) and "he does that well" (`tassādhukārī`) are understood, the suffixes `ṇī`, `tu` and `āvī` are used after all roots; so the suffix `ṇī` is added here in the sense of the agent, and because the root ends in a conjunct there is no `vuddhi`. > Piyapasaṃsī rājā. Atha vā piyaṃ pasaṃsi, pasaṃsati, pasaṃsissati vā sīlena vā dhammena vā sādhu vāti piyapasaṃsī, piyapasaṃsinī, piyapasaṃsi kulaṃ. ### 591 (§533): Sadda ku dha ca la ma ṇḍa ttha rucādīhi yu (`yu` after roots meaning sound, anger, motion, adorning, and after `ruca` etc.) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§533 (591): Sadda kudha cala maṇḍattha rucādīhi yu" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m533" description="`yu` after roots meaning sound, anger, motion, adorning, and after `ruca` etc." />

> Sadda ku dha ca la maṇḍatthe hi dhātūhi, rucā dīhi ca yu ppaccayo hoti tassīlādīsvatthesu.

After roots with the meanings sound (`sadda`), anger (`kudha`), motion (`cala`) and adorning (`maṇḍa`), and after `ruca` and the like, the suffix `yu` is used in the senses of habit and the like.

> Ghusa sadde, ghosituṃ sīlaṃ assāti vā ghosanasīloti vā aghosayi, ghosayati, ghosayissati sīlena, dhammena, sādhu vāti atthe iminā yu ppaccayo, tassa ‘‘anakā yuṇvūna’’nti anā deso, ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti vuddhi, so ghosano, sā ghosanā. Bhāsa viyattiyaṃ vācāyaṃ, bhāsituṃ sīlamassāti vā bhāsanasīlo, bhāsanadhammo, bhāsane sādhukārīti vā bhāsano.

The root `ghusa`, in the sense of sound: in the sense "one whose habit is to shout" (`ghosituṃ sīlaṃ assa`), that is, "one of a habit of shouting", or "he shouted, shouts or will shout by habit, by nature, or well" (`aghosayi, ghosayati, ghosayissati sīlena, dhammena, sādhu`), the suffix `yu` is added by this rule; it is replaced by `ana` by [570 (§622): Anakā yuṇvūnaṃ](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r570), and there is `vuddhi` by [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485): `so ghosano` ("he is given to shouting"), `sā ghosanā` ("she is given to shouting"). The root `bhāsa`, in the sense of articulate speech: `bhāsano` ("one given to talking") is one whose habit is to talk (`bhāsituṃ sīlaṃ assa`), that is, one of a habit of talking, one of a nature of talking, or one who does well in talking.

> Kudha kope, kujjhituṃ sīlamassāti vā kujjhanasīloti vā kodhano, kodhanā, kodhanaṃ.

The root `kudha`, in the sense of anger: `kodhano`, `kodhanā`, `kodhanaṃ` ("irascible") is one whose habit is to be angry (`kujjhituṃ sīlaṃ assa`), that is, one of a habit of being angry.

> Rusa rose, rosituṃ sīlamassāti vā rosanasīloti vā rosano.

The root `rusa`, in the sense of rage: `rosano` ("given to rage") is one whose habit is to rage (`rosituṃ sīlaṃ assa`), that is, one of a habit of raging.

> Cala kampane, calituṃ sīlaṃ yassāti vā calati sīlenāti vā calano. Kapi calane, kampituṃ sīlaṃ yassāti vā akampi, kampati, kampissati sīlenāti vā kampano, i kārānubandhidhātusarato ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā, ‘‘niggahītañcā’’ti vā niggahītāgamo. Phadi kiñcicalane, phandituṃ sīlaṃ yassāti vā phandati sīlenāti vā phandano.

The root `cala`, in the sense of shaking: `calano` ("given to shaking") is one whose habit is to shake (`calituṃ sīlaṃ yassa`), or one who shakes by habit (`calati sīlena`). The root `kapi`, in the sense of moving: `kampano` ("given to trembling") is one whose habit is to tremble, or one who trembled, trembles or will tremble by habit (`akampi, kampati, kampissati sīlena`); after the vowel of a root marked with the letter `i`, a niggahīta is inserted by [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) and the rest, or by [§466 (489): Niggahitañca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m466). The root `phadi`, in the sense of slight motion: `phandano` ("given to quivering") is one whose habit is to quiver, or one who quivers by habit.

> Maḍi bhūsāyaṃ, maṇḍayituṃ sīlaṃ yassāti vā maṇḍayati sīlenāti vā maṇḍano. Bhūsa alaṅkāre, bhūsanasīloti vā abhūsayi, bhūsayati, bhūsayissati[^8] sīlenāti vā bhūsano, bhūsanā, bhūsanaṃ.

The root `maḍi`, in the sense of adorning: `maṇḍano` ("given to adorning") is one whose habit is to adorn, or one who adorns by habit. The root `bhūsa`, in the sense of decorating: `bhūsano`, `bhūsanā`, `bhūsanaṃ` ("given to decorating") is one of a habit of decorating, or one who decorated, decorates or will decorate by habit (`abhūsayi, bhūsayati, bhūsayissati sīlena`).

> Ruca dittimhi, arucci, ruccati, ruccissati sīlenāti vā rocano. Juta dittimhi, ajoti, jotati, jotissati sīlenāti vā jotano. Vaḍḍha vaḍḍhane, vaḍḍhituṃ sīlamassāti vaḍḍhano iccādi.

The root `ruca`, in the sense of shining: `rocano` ("given to shining") is one who shone, shines or will shine by habit (`arucci, ruccati, ruccissati sīlena`). The root `juta`, in the sense of shining: `jotano` ("given to shining") is one who shone, shines or will shine by habit (`ajoti, jotati, jotissati sīlena`). The root `vaḍḍha`, in the sense of growing: `vaḍḍhano` ("given to growing") is one whose habit is to grow (`vaḍḍhituṃ sīlaṃ assa`); and so on.

### 592 (§534): Pārādigamimhā rū (`rū` after `gamu` preceded by `pāra` and the like) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§534 (592): Pārādigamimhā rū" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/1-pathamakanda#m534" description="`rū` after `gamu` preceded by `pāra` and the like" />

> Pārā diupapadehi parasmā gami iccetasmā dhātumhā paro rū paccayo hoti tassīlādīsvatthesu kattariyeva. Pāro ādi yesaṃ te pārādayo, pārādīhi gami pārādigami. Rā dilopo, bhavapāraṃ gantuṃ sīlaṃ yassāti vā bhavapāraṃ gamanasīlo, bhavapāraṃ gamanadhammo, bhavapāraṃ gamane sādhukārīti vā bhavapāragū, bhavapāraguno. Antaṃ gamanasīlo antagū. The suffix `rū` is used, in the senses of habit and the like and only in the sense of the agent, after the root `gami` (`gamu`) when it follows `pāra` and the like as its accompanying words (`upapada`). Bhikkha yācane, bhikkhituṃ sīlaṃ yassāti vā abhikkhi, bhikkhati, bhikkhissati sīlenāti vā bhikkhanadhammoti vā bhikkhane sādhukārīti vā bhikkhu, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā rassattaṃ. ### 600 (§586): Gamakhanahanaramādīnamanto (The final of `gama`, `khana`, `hana`, `ramu` etc. [is optionally dropped before `ta`, `ti`]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

Buddhappiya's gloss has no `vā`; Kaccāyana's vutti reads `anto byañjano no hoti vā`, with the elision optional. > Gama khana hana rama iccevamādīnaṃ ma kārana kārantānaṃ dhātūnaṃ anto byañjano no hoti ta ppaccaye, ti mhi cāti dhātvantalopo. Gamanaṃ, gantabbāti vā gati, upahananaṃ upahati, ramanti tāya, ramaṇaṃ vā rati. Tanu vitthāre, tananaṃ tati. Yamu uparame, niyamanaṃ niyati. ‘‘Ramato, ramatī’’tiādīsu pana akārabyavahitattā na dhātvantalopo, bhuñjanaṃ bhutti, yuñjanaṃ yutti, ‘‘bhujādīnamanto no dvi cā’’ti dhātvantalopo, dvittañca. But in `ramato` ("of one who delights"), `ramati` ("he delights") and the like the root's final is not elided, because the letter `a` intervenes. "Eating (`bhuñjanaṃ`)", `bhutti`, eating; "joining (`yuñjanaṃ`)", `yutti`, fitness, with the elision of the root's final and the doubling by [560 (§578): Bhujādīnamanto no dvi ca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r560). "Attaining (`samāpajjanaṃ`)", or "one attains (`samāpajjate`)", `samāpatti`, attainment, and `sampatti`, success, with the elision of the root's final and the doubling by [630 (§580): Gupādīnañca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/3-atita#r630). > ‘‘Ta’’iti vattate.

“`ta`” continues.

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§557 (606): Budhagamāditthe kattari" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m557" description="`ta` in the agent sense after `budha`, `gamu` and the like, in all times" />

<a name="r607"></a>

### 607 (§576): Dhaḍhabhahehi dha ḍhā ca (`dha`, `ḍha` for `ta` after roots in `dh`, `ḍh`, `bh`, `h`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§576 (607): Dha ḍha bha he hi dhaḍḍhā ca" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m576" description="`dha`, `ḍha` for `ta` after roots in `dh`, `ḍh`, `bh`, `h`" />
:::

> Dhaḍha nta bhaha ntehi dhātūhi parassa paccayata kārassa yathākkamaṃ dha kāraḍha kārādesā hontīti dhabha to ta kārassa dha kāro, ‘‘hacatutthāna’’nti ettha ha kāraggahaṇato ha kāratopi kvaci dha ttaṃ, abyavadhāne cāyaṃ, tena ‘‘rundhati, ārādhito, vaḍḍhito, labhitvā, gahito’’tiādīsu paccayāgamabyavahitattā na bhavati.

After roots ending in `dh` and `ḍh`, and in `bh` and `h`, the letter `t` of the suffix that follows is replaced by the letters `dh` and `ḍh` respectively; so after `dh` and `bh` the letter `t` becomes `dh`, and since the letter `h` is mentioned in [608 (§611): Hacatutthānamantānaṃ do dhe](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r608), after `h` too it becomes `dh` in some places (`kvaci`). And this holds where nothing intervenes (`abyavadhāna`); hence it does not happen in `rundhati` ("he obstructs"), `ārādhito` ("won over"), `vaḍḍhito` ("increased"), `labhitvā` ("having obtained"), `gahito` ("taken") and the like, because a suffix or an insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) stands between.

### 608 (§611): Hacatutthānamantānaṃ do dhe (root-final `h` and fourth letters become `d` before `dh`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§611 (608): Hacatutthānamantānaṃ do dhe" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m611" description="root-final `h` and fourth letters become `d` before `dh`" />
:::

> Ha kāravaggacatutthānaṃ dhātvantabhūtānaṃ da kārādeso hoti dha kāre pare. Buddho bhagavā. Saraṇaṃ agacchi, gacchati, gacchissatīti vā saraṇaṅgato upāsako, ‘‘gamakhanahanaramādīnamanto’’ti dhātvantalopo. Evaṃ jānātīti ñāto. I gatimhi, upetīti upeto. Cinta cintāyaṃ, cintetīti cittaṃ, ‘‘gupādīnañcā’’ti dhātvantalopadvittāni. Sanja saṅge, rūpādīsu asajji, sajjati, sajjissatīti vā satto, ‘‘bhujādīnamanto no dvi cā’’ti dhātvantalopo, dvittañca.

The letter `h` and the fourth letters of the classes, when they are a root's final, are replaced by the letter `d` when `dh` follows: `buddho bhagavā`, "the Awakened One, the Blessed One". "He went, goes or will go (`agacchi, gacchati, gacchissati`) for refuge (`saraṇaṃ`)", `saraṇaṅgato upāsako`, "a lay follower gone for refuge", with the elision of the root's final by [600 (§586): Gamakhanahanaramādīnamanto](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/2-kita#r600). In the same way "he knows (`jānāti`)", `ñāto`, one who knows. `i`, in the sense of going: "he approaches (`upeti`)", `upeto`, one who has approached. `cinta`, in the sense of thinking: "it thinks (`cinteti`)", `cittaṃ`, the mind, with the elision of the root's final and the doubling by [630 (§580): Gupādīnañca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/3-atita#r630). `sanja`, in the sense of clinging: "it clung, clings or will cling (`asajji, sajjati, sajjissati`) to forms and the rest", `satto`, a being, with the elision of the root's final and the doubling by [560 (§578): Bhujādīnamanto no dvi ca](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/1-kicca#r560).
