## Pulliṅga (Masculine nouns)

> Tattha paṭhamaṃ a kārantamhā pulliṅgā jātinimittā purisa saddā syā divibhattiyo parā yojīyante.

Among these, first, the inflections `si` and the rest are attached after the masculine word `purisa` (a man), which ends in `a` and is applied on the ground of a genus (`jātinimitta`).

---

<a name="r60"></a>

### 60 (§52): Jinavacanayuttañhi (Suitable for the teachings of the Conqueror) :badge[sīhagatika]{variant=caution}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§52 (60): Jinavacanayuttaṃ hi" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m52" description="Suitable for the teachings of the Conqueror" />
:::

> Adhikāro yaṃ. Tattha pañca māre jitavāti jino, buddho. Jinassa vacanaṃ jinavacanaṃ, tassa jinavacanassa yuttaṃ jinavacanayuttaṃ, tepiṭakassa buddhavacanassa māgadhikāya sabhāvaniruttiyā yuttaṃ anurūpamevāti idaṃ adhikāratthaṃ veditabbaṃ.

This is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)). Here the `jina` (conqueror) is the one who conquered (`jitavā`) the five Māras: the Buddha. The Conqueror's word is `jinavacana`, and what is suited to that word of the Conqueror is `jinavacanayutta`: suited to, which is to say simply in conformity with, the Māgadhan natural language (`sabhāvanirutti`) of the Buddha's word in the three Piṭakas. This is how the sense of the governing rule should be understood.

> Sā māgadhī mūlabhāsā, narā yāyādikappikā; \
> Brahmāno ca’ssutālāpā, sambuddhā cāpi bhāsare.

That Māgadhī is the root language, the one spoken by the people of the first aeon, \
by the Brahmās, by those who have never heard speech, and by the Fully Awakened Ones too.

> Adhikāro pana tividho sīhagatikamaṇḍūkagatikayathānupubbikavasena, ayaṃ pana sīhagatiko pubbāparavilokanato, yathānupubbikoyeva vā.

Now, a governing rule is of three kinds: comprehensive ([`sīhagatika`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), selective ([`maṇḍūkagatika`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) and progressive ([`yathānupubbika`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)). This one, for its part, is comprehensive ([`sīhagatika`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), since it looks backward and forward; or else it is simply progressive ([`yathānupubbika`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

> Sakkatavisadisaṃ katvā jinavacanānurūpavasena pakatiṭṭhapanatthaṃ paribhāsamāha.

Departing from Sanskrit, he states a metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) so as to set up the lemmas (`pakati`) in conformity with the Conqueror's word:

<a name="r61"></a>

### 61 (§53): Liṅgañca nipaccate (And lemmas are derived) :badge[sīhagatika]{variant=caution}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§53 (61): Liṅgañca nippajjate" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m53" description="And lemmas are derived" />
:::

> Liṅgaṃ pāṭipadikaṃ, yathā yathā jinavacanayuttañhi liṅgaṃ, tathā tathā idha liṅgaṃ nipaccate ṭhapīyati. Ca saddena dhātavo cāti jinavacanānurūpato ‘‘purisa’’iti liṅge ṭhapite tato tassa dhātuppaccayavibhattivajjitassa atthavato saddassa ‘‘parasamaññā payoge’’ti paribhāsato liṅgasaññāyaṃ –

A lemma (`liṅga`) is a nominal base (`pāṭipadika`). In whatever way a lemma is suited to the Conqueror's word, in that way it is here derived (`nipaccate`), that is, set up (`ṭhapīyati`); and by the word `ca` so are the roots (`dhātu`). Once the lemma `purisa` has been set up in conformity with the Conqueror's word, and that meaningful word, free of root, suffix and inflection, has then received the name `liṅga` by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) [§9 (11): Parasamaññā payoge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m9) —

> Ito paraṃ vibhattippaccayādividhāne sabbattha liṅga ggahaṇamanuvattate.

from here on, the word `liṅga` is carried over (`anuvattate`) into every prescription of inflections, suffixes and the like.

<a name="r62"></a>

### 62 (§54): Tato ca vibhattiyo (And their inflections) :badge[sīhagatika]{variant=caution}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§54 (62): Tato ca vibhattiyo" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m54" description="And their inflections" />
:::

> Tato jinavacanayuttehi liṅgehi parā vibhattiyo honti. Ca saddaggahaṇena tavetunādipaccayantanipātatopi. Kammādivasena, ekattādivasena ca liṅgatthaṃ vibhajantīti vibhattiyo.

The inflections (`vibhatti`) come after those lemmas that are suited to the Conqueror's word, and by the mention of the word `ca` they also come after particles (`nipāta`) ending in the suffixes `tave`, `tuna`[^1] and the like. They are called `vibhatti` because they divide (`vibhajanti`) the lemma's meaning by way of object and the rest, and by way of singular and the rest.

> Kā ca pana tā vibhattiyo? ‘‘Vibhattiyo’’ti adhikāro.

And what, then, are those inflections? “`vibhattiyo`” ("inflections") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

<a name="r63"></a>

### 63 (§55): Siyo, aṃyo, nāhi, sanaṃ, smāhi, sanaṃ, smiṃsu (inflection forms) :badge[rūḷhī]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§55 (63): Si yo, aṃ yo, nā hi, sa naṃ, smā hi, sa naṃ, smiṃ su" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m55" description="inflection forms" />
:::

> Syādayo dvisatta vibhattiyo nāma honti. Tattha si, yo iti paṭhamā, aṃ, yo iti dutiyā, nā, hi iti tatiyā, sa, naṃ iti catutthī, smā, hi iti pañcamī, sa, naṃ iti chaṭṭhī, smiṃ, su iti sattamī.

`si` and the rest, twice seven of them, are called inflections (`vibhatti`). Of these, `si` and `yo` are the first form ①, `aṃ` and `yo` the second ②, `nā` and `hi` the third ③, `sa` and `naṃ` the fourth ④, `smā` and `hi` the fifth ⑤, `sa` and `naṃ` the sixth ⑥, and `smiṃ` and `su` the seventh ⑦.

| symbol | ⨀ | ⨂ | inflection form |
| :-: | :-: | :-: | --- |
| ① | `si` | `yo` | `paṭhamā` ("first") |
| ② | `aṃ` | `yo` | `dutiyā` ("second") |
| ③ | `nā` | `hi` | `tatiyā` ("third") |
| ④ | `sa` | `naṃ` | `catutthī` ("fourth") |
| ⑤ | `smā` | `hi` | `pañcamī` ("fifth") |
| ⑥ | `sa` | `naṃ` | `chaṭṭhī` ("sixth") |
| ⑦ | `smiṃ` | `su` | `sattamī` ("seventh") |

> Idaṃ pana saññādhikāraparibhāsāvidhisuttesu saññāsutta nti daṭṭhabbaṃ, vuttañhi vuttiyaṃ ‘‘vibhattiiccanena kvattho, amhassa mamaṃ savibhattissa se’’ti, itarathā purimasuttena ekayogo kattabboti. Ettha ca paṭhamādivohāro, ekavacanādivohāro ca anvatthavasena, parasamaññāvasena vā siddhoti veditabbo.

Now, of the suttas that are technical terms ([`saññā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), governing rules ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), metarules ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) and operations ([`vidhi`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), this one should be understood as a technical-term sutta, for the vutti says: "What is the use of the term `vibhatti`? [§120 (243): Amhassa mamaṃ savibhattissa se](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m120)." Otherwise it would have to be made one rule (`ekayoga`) with the preceding sutta.[^2] And here the usage `paṭhamā` ("first") and the rest, and the usage `ekavacana` ("singular") and the rest, should be understood to be established either as meaningful ([`anvattha`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) or by the terminology of others (`parasamaññā`).

> Ekassa vacanaṃ ekavacanaṃ, bahūnaṃ vacanaṃ bahuvacanaṃ, dvinnaṃ pūraṇī dutiyātiādi, itarathā purimasutte ca saddena saññākaraṇe appakataniratthakavidhippasaṅgo siyā.

The expression of one is the singular (`ekavacana`), the expression of many is the plural (`bahuvacana`), `dutiyā` ("second") is the ordinal of two, and so on. Otherwise, if the naming were done by the word `ca` of the preceding sutta, the result would be a prescription that is beside the point and serves no purpose.

> ‘‘Jinavacanayuttañhi, liṅgañca nipaccate’’ti ca vattate. Idha pana padanipphādanampi jinavacanassāvirodhenāti ñāpetuṃ paribhāsantaramāha.

[60 (§52): Jinavacanayuttañhi](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r60) and [61 (§53): Liṅgañca nipaccate](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r61) also continue. Now, to make it known that here the formation of words (`pada`) too does not conflict with the Conqueror's word, he states a further metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)):

### 64 (§56): Tadanuparodhena (those word endings) :badge[sīhagatika]{variant=caution}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§56 (64): Tadanuparodhena" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m56" description="those word endings" />
:::

> Yathā yathā tesaṃ jinavacanānaṃ uparodho na hoti, tathā tathā idha liṅgaṃ, ca saddenākhyātañca nipaccate, nipphādīyatīti attho. Teneva idha ca ākhyāte ca dvivacanāggahaṇaṃ, sakkatavisadisato vibhattippaccayādividhānañca katanti daṭṭhabbaṃ.

Here the lemma, and by the word `ca` the verb (`ākhyāta`) as well, is derived (`nipaccate`), meaning formed (`nipphādīyati`), in whatever way leaves those words of the Conqueror unobstructed. That is exactly why, it should be understood, the dual number (`dvivacana`) is not admitted either here or in the verbs, and the inflections, suffixes and the rest are prescribed in a way unlike Sanskrit.

> Tattha avisesena sabbasyādivibhattippasaṅge ‘‘vatticchānupubbikā saddappavattī’’ti vatticchāvasā –

There, since all the inflections `si` and the rest would apply without distinction, and "the occurrence of words follows the speaker's wish", it is by way of the speaker's wish (`vatticchā`) that —

### 65 (§284): Liṅgatthe paṭhamā (the first form ① for the bare meaning of the lemma) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§284 (283): Liṅgatthe paṭhamā" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m284" description="the first form ① for the bare meaning of the lemma" />
:::

> Liṅgatthābhidhānamatte paṭhamāvibhatti hotīti paṭhamā. Tatthāpi aniyamenekavacanabahuvacanappasaṅge ‘‘ekamhī vattabbe ekavacana’’nti paribhāsato liṅgatthassekattavacanicchāyaṃ paṭhamekavacanaṃ si.

The first inflection ① is used merely to express the lemma's meaning (`liṅgattha`): hence `paṭhamā` ("first"). There too, singular and plural would apply without restriction; so, by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) "when one is to be expressed, the singular", the first-form singular is `si` when the lemma's meaning is to be expressed as one.

> ‘‘Ato nenā’’ti ito ‘‘ato’’ti vattate, liṅga ggahaṇañca.

The word `ato` ("after `a`") continues from [79 (§103): Ato nena](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r79), and so does the word `liṅga`.

<a name="r66"></a>

### 66 (§104): So (`si` to `o`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§104 (66): So" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m104" description="`si` to `o`" />
:::

> Si, oiti dvipadamidaṃ. Liṅgassa a kārato parassa si vacanassa o kāro hoti.

This is two words: `si` and `o`. After the letter `a` of a lemma, the inflection `si` is replaced by the letter `o`.

> Suttesu hi paṭhamāniddiṭṭhassa kāriyino chaṭṭhīvipariṇāmena vivaraṇaṃ ādesāpekkhanti daṭṭhabbaṃ.

For it should be understood that, where the suttas state in the first form ① the item that undergoes the operation (`kāriyī`), the explanation that converts it into the sixth form ⑥ does so with the replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) in view.

> Ettha ca sī ti vibhatti gayhate vibhattikāriyavidhippakaraṇato, ‘‘tato ca vibhattiyo’’ti ito vibhatti ggahaṇānuvattanato vā, evaṃ sabbattha syā dīnaṃ kāriyavidhāne vibhattiyevāti daṭṭhabbaṃ.

And here `si` is taken as the inflection, because this section prescribes operations on inflections, or because the word `vibhatti` is carried over from [62 (§54): Tato ca vibhattiyo](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r62); and in the same way it should be understood that wherever an operation is prescribed for `si` and the rest, only the inflection is meant.

> ‘‘Vā paro asarūpā’’ti paralope sampatte tadapavādena pubbalopamāha.

When elision of what follows would apply by [§13 (15): Vā paro asarūpā](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m13), he states elision of what precedes as an exception ([`apavāda`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) to it:

<a name="r67"></a>

### 67 (§83): Saralopo’mādesappaccayādimhi saralope tu pakati (the vowel is elided before `aṃ`, a replacement or a suffix; but after the elision, the natural state) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§83 (67): Saralopo’ mādesa paccayādimhi saralope tu pakati" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m83" description="the vowel is elided before `aṃ`, a replacement or a suffix; but after the elision, the natural state" />
:::

> Pubbassarassa lopo hoti aṃ vacane, ādesappaccayādibhūte ca sare pare, saralope kate tu parasarassa pakatibhāvo hoti. Ettha ca ‘‘saralope’’ti punaggahaṇaṃ imināva katasaralopanimitteyeva parassa vikāre sampatte pakatibhāvatthaṃ. Parasarassa pakatibhāvavidhānasāmatthiyato amādesappaccayādibhūte sare paretipi siddhaṃ.

The preceding vowel is elided when the inflection `aṃ` follows, or when a vowel that is a replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), a suffix (`paccaya`) or the like follows; but once the vowel has been elided, the following vowel keeps its natural form (`pakatibhāva`).[^3] And here `saralope` ("when the vowel is elided") is repeated so that the natural form is kept only where a modification ([`vikāra`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of what follows would apply because of a vowel elision made by this rule itself. The force of prescribing the natural form for the following vowel also establishes the reading "when the vowel that follows is `aṃ`, a replacement, a suffix or the like".

> Tyādivibhattiyo cettha, paccayattena gayhare; \
> Ādiggahaṇamākhyāta-kitakesvāgamatthidaṃ.

And here the inflections `ti` and the rest are taken as suffixes; \
this mention of `ādi` is for the insertions ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) in verbs and in `kita` words.

> Paccayasāhacariyā, cādeso pakatīparo; \
> Padantassaralopo na, tena’bbhāhādike pare.

And by association with `paccaya`, the replacement is one that follows a lemma (`pakati`); \
hence a word-final vowel is not elided before `abbha`, `āha` and the like.

> Tu ggahaṇaṃ bhikkhunī ādīsu saralopanivattanatthaṃ, ‘‘naye paraṃ yutte’’ti paraṃ netabbaṃ. Puriso tiṭṭhati.

The mention of `tu` is there to block the vowel elision in `bhikkhunī` and the like. What follows is to be joined on by [§11 (14): Naye paraṃ yutte](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m11): `puriso tiṭṭhati` ("the man stands").

> Puriso ca puriso cāti purisa purisaiti vattabbe –

When `puriso ca puriso ca` ("a man and a man"), that is, `purisa purisa`, is to be said —

### 68 (§388): Sarūpānamekasesvasakiṃ (of like forms occurring more than once, one alone remains) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§388 (68): Sarūpānamekasesvasakiṃ" href="/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m388" description="of like forms occurring more than once, one alone remains" />
:::

> Sarūpānaṃ samānarūpānaṃ padabyañjanānaṃ majjhe ekova sissate, aññe lopamāpajjante asakinti ekaseso. Ettha ca ‘‘sarūpāna’’nti vuttattāva siddhe asakimpayoge punāsakiṃ gahaṇaṃ e kavibhattivisayānamevāsakimpayoge evāyanti dassanatthaṃ, na ca vicchāpayoge’tippasaṅgo. ‘‘Vaggā pañcapañcaso mantā’’ti ettha ‘‘pañcapañcaso’’ti niddeseneva vicchāpayogasiddhiyā ñāpitattā, atha vā sahavacanicchāya’maya’mekaseso.

Among words and letters of the same form (`sarūpa`), that is, of like form, one alone remains and the others are elided when they occur more than once (`asakiṃ`): this is `ekasesa`, the remaining of one. And here, although occurrence more than once is established just by saying `sarūpānaṃ`, `asakiṃ` is mentioned again to show that this applies only to a repeated occurrence of what falls within the scope of a single inflection. Nor is there over-application to a distributive use (`vicchā`), because the very wording `pañcapañcaso` in [§7 (9): Vaggā pañcapañcaso mantā](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m7) has made it known that the distributive use is established. Or else this `ekasesa` takes place when they are to be expressed together (`sahavacanicchā`).

> Yogavibhāgato cettha, ekasesvasakiṃ iti; \
> Virūpekaseso hoti, vā ‘‘pitūna’’ntiādisu.

And here, by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) into `ekasesvasakiṃ`, \
there is optionally an `ekasesa` of unlike forms, as in `pitūnaṃ` and the like.

> Tattheva liṅgatthassa ba huttavacanicchāyaṃ ‘‘bahumhi vattabbe bahuvacana’’nti paṭhamābahuvacanaṃ yo, purisa yo itīdha ‘‘ato, vā’’ti ca vattate.

In the same [lemma],[^4] when the lemma's meaning is to be expressed as many, the first-form plural is `yo`, since "when many are to be expressed, the plural"; and here, in `purisa` + `yo`, “`ato`” and “`vā`” also continue.

<a name="r69"></a>

### 69 (§107): Sabbayonīnamāe (For both `yo`: `ā`, `e`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§107 (69): Sabbayonīnamāe" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m107" description="For both `yo`: `ā`, `e`" />
:::

> A kārantato liṅgamhā paresaṃ sabbesaṃ paṭhamāyonīnaṃ, dutiyāyonīnañca yayākkamaṃ ākārekārādesā honti vāti ā kāro, sabba ggahaṇaṃ sabbādesatthaṃ, saralopādi purimasadisameva, purisā tiṭṭhanti.

After a lemma ending in `a`, all the following `yo` inflections, those of the first form ① and those of the second form ②, are optionally replaced by `ā` and `e` respectively; so here the letter `ā`. The mention of `sabba` ("all") is there so that the whole of `yo` is replaced. The vowel elision and the rest are just as before: `purisā tiṭṭhanti` ("the men stand").

> Vā icceva rūpā rūpāni, aggayo, munayo.

It is precisely because of `vā` that there are `rūpā` and `rūpāni`, and `aggayo` and `munayo`.

> Vā saddoyaṃ vavatthitavibhāsattho[^5], tena cettha –

This word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`); and so here —

> Niccameva ca pulliṅge, aniccañca napuṃsake; \
> Asantaṃ jhe katatte tu, vidhiṃ dīpeti vā suti.

obligatory indeed in the masculine, and optional in the neuter, \
but inapplicable where a `jha` [final] has been made [`a`]: such is the operation the word `vā` shows.

> Tatthevālapanavacanicchāyaṃ ‘‘liṅgatthe paṭhamā’’ti vattate.

In the same [lemma], when the vocative is to be expressed, [65 (§284): Liṅgatthe paṭhamā](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r65) continues.

<a name="r70"></a>

### 70 (§285): Ālapane ca (and in the vocative ⓪) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§285 (70): Ālapane ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m285" description="and in the vocative ⓪" />
:::

### 74 (§220): Sesato lopaṃ gasipi (after the rest, `ga` and `si` too are elided) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§220 (74): Sesato lopaṃ gasipi" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m220" description="after the rest, `ga` and `si` too are elided" />
:::

> ‘‘Siṃ, so, syā ca, sakhato gasse vā, ghate cā’’ti evamādīhi niddiṭṭhehi añño seso nāma, tato sesato liṅgamhā gasi iccete lopamāpajjante.

“The rest” (`sesa`) is the name for whatever is other than what is specified by [§219 (195): Siṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m219), [§104 (66): So](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m104), [§189 (113): Syā ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m189), [§113 (132): Sakhato gasse vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m113), [§114 (178): Ghate ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m114) and the like; after that remaining lemma, the following are elided: `ga` and `si`.

> Api ggahaṇaṃ dutiyatthasampiṇḍanatthaṃ, ettha ca satipi si ggahaṇe ga[^6] iti vacanameva ñāpakamaññatthāpi si ggahaṇe ālapanāggahaṇassa. Keci ālapanābhibyattiyā bhavanta saddaṃ vā he saddaṃ vā payujjante. Bho purisa tiṭṭha, he purisā vā.

The mention of `api` is there to include (`sampiṇḍana`) a second item. And here, although `si` is mentioned, the very use of the word `ga` is the indicator (`ñāpaka`) that elsewhere too, where `si` is mentioned, the vocative is not included. Some, to make the vocative explicit, use the word `bhavanta` or the word `he`: `bho purisa tiṭṭha` ("stand, man!") or `he purisā` ("O man!").

> Bahuvacane na viseso, bhavanto purisā tiṭṭhatha.

In the plural there is no difference: `bhavanto purisā tiṭṭhatha` ("stand, men!").

> Tattheva kammatthavacanicchāyaṃ ‘‘vā’’ti[^7] vattate.

In the same [lemma], when the sense of the object (`kamma`) is to be expressed, “`vā`” continues.

### 105 (§94): Simhī vā (Optionally in `si`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§94 (105): Simhi vā" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m94" description="Optionally in `si`" />

> Ntu paccayassa anto a ttaṃ hoti vā si mhi vibhattimhi. Himavanto, himavā, sesaṃ samaṃ.

> Tattha satimantu bandhumantu saddānaṃ aṃse su rūpabhedo. ‘‘Attaṃ, ntussā’’ti ca vattate.

### 155 (§119): Lato vokāro ca (And after `la`, [`yo`] becomes the letter `vo`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§119 (155): Lato vokāro ca" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m119" description="And from `la`: becomes `vo`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

`attaṃ` continues from [252 (§90): Pañcādīnamattaṃ](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/5-alinganama#r252), which Buddhappiya states later.

:::

> La saññato paresaṃ yo vacanānaṃ vo kārādeso hoti vā. Kāra ggahaṇena yo naṃ no ca hoti. Ca saddaggahaṇaṃ katthaci nivattanatthaṃ. Atha vā ca ggahaṇaṃ noggahaṇānuvattanatthaṃ, tena jantusabbaññū ādito yonaṃ no ca hoti. Vā saddo vavatthitavibhāsattho. Tena –

After a [final] named `la`, the `yo` inflections are optionally replaced by the letter `vo`. By the mention of `kāra`, the `yo`s also become `no`. The word `ca` is mentioned to stop the rule from applying in some places; or else `ca` is mentioned to carry on the mention of `no`, and so the `yo`s after `jantu`, `sabbaññū` and the like also become `no`. The word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`). Hence —

> Bhikkhuppabhutito niccaṃ, vo yonaṃ hetuādito; \
> Vibhāsā na ca vo no ca, amuppabhutito bhave.

After `bhikkhu` and the like, `vo` always replaces the `yo`s; after `hetu` and the like \
it does so optionally; and after `amu` and the like neither `vo` nor `no` occurs.

> ‘‘Attaṃ, akatarasso’’ti ca vattate.

And "`attaṃ`" (becoming `a`) and "`akatarasso`" (unshortened) continue.

> E kāranto appasiddho.

[A masculine] ending in `e` is not well attested.

> O kāranto pulliṅgo go saddo.

A masculine ending in `o` is the word `go` (cow, ox).

> Tato syā dyuppatti, si lopo, go gacchati.

After it, `si` and the rest arise, and `si` is elided: `go gacchati` (the ox goes).

> ‘‘Gāva se’’ti ito ‘‘go’’ti adhikāro, ‘‘āvā’’ti ca vattate.

From [§73 (174): Gāva se](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m73) "`go`" (the `o` of `go`) is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), and "`āva`" continues.

### 175 (§77): Tato namaṃ patimhālutte ca samāse (From that, `naṃ` becomes `aṃ` before `pati` when inflections are not elided in a compound) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

<LinkCard title="§77 (175): Tato namaṃ patimhā lutte ca samāse" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m77" description="From that, `naṃ` becomes `aṃ` before `pati` when inflections are not elided in a compound" />

> Tato go saddato parassa naṃ vacanassa aṃ ādeso hoti, go saddokārassa avā deso ca pati mhi pare alutte ca samāse. Ca saddena asamāsepi aṃ avā desā. Gavaṃpatissa therassa, gavaṃ.

By the word `ca`, the replacements `aṃ` and `ava` are made outside a compound as well: `Gavaṃpatissa therassa` (of the Elder Gavampati); `gavaṃ` (of cows).

> ‘‘Suhināsu cā’’ti ettha ca saddena naṃmhi gu ādeso. ‘‘No ca dvādito naṃmhī’’ti sutte ca saddena na kārāgamo ca. Gunnaṃgonaṃ vā.

> Gāvā gāvamhā gāvasmā gavā gavamhā gavasmā, gohi gobhi, gāvassa gavassa, gavaṃ gunnaṃ gonaṃ, gāve gāvamhi gāvasmiṃ gave gavamhi gavasmiṃ, gāvesu gavesu gosu.

| ③ | gāvena, gavena | gohi, gobhi |
| ④ | gāvassa, gavassa | gavaṃ, gunnaṃ, gonaṃ |
