## Sabbanāma (Pronouns)

> Atha sabbanāmāni vuccante.

Now the pronouns (`sabbanāma`) are stated.

> Sabba, katara, katama, ubhaya, itara, añña, aññatara, aññatama, pubba, para, apara, dakkhiṇa, uttara, adhara, ya, ta, eta, ima, amu, kiṃ, eka, ubha, dvi, ti, catu, tumha, amha iti sattavīsati sabbanāmāni, tāni sabbanāmattā tiliṅgāni.

`sabba` (all), `katara` (which of two), `katama` (which of many), `ubhaya` (both), `itara` (the other), `añña` (another), `aññatara` (a certain one), `aññatama` (one or other), `pubba` (former; east), `para` (other; further), `apara` (another; west), `dakkhiṇa` (southern; right), `uttara` (northern; upper), `adhara` (lower), `ya` (which), `ta` (that), `eta` (this), `ima` (this), `amu` (that yonder), `kiṃ` (what), `eka` (one), `ubha` (both), `dvi` (two), `ti` (three), `catu` (four), `tumha` (you) and `amha` (I): these twenty-seven are the pronouns; being pronouns, they are of three genders.

> Tattha sabba saddo niravasesattho, so yadā pulliṅgavisiṭṭhatthābhidhāyī, tadā rūpanayo. Pure viya syā dyuppatti, ‘‘so’’ti si ssa o kāro, saralopaparanayanāni. Sabbo jano.

Among them the word `sabba` means "without remainder". When it expresses a meaning qualified as masculine, the course of its forms is as follows. As before, the inflections `si` and the rest are added; by [66 (§104): So](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r66) the `si` becomes `o`; elision of the vowel and carrying over of what follows: `sabbo jano` ("all the people").

> Bahuvacane ‘‘sabba yo’’ itīdha ‘‘parasamaññā payoge’’ti sabbādīnaṃ sabbanāmasaññā.

In the plural, when we have `sabba` + `yo`, by [§9 (11): Parasamaññā payoge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m9) `sabba` and the rest receive the technical name `sabbanāma` ("pronoun").

> ‘‘Yo’’ti vattate.

"`yo`" continues.

### 200 (§164): Sabbanāmakārate paṭhamo (`e` for the first `yo` after a pronoun in `a`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§164 (200): Sabbanāmakārate paṭhamo" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m164" description="`e` for the first `yo` after a pronoun in `a`" />
:::

> Sabbesaṃ itthipumanapuṃsakānaṃ nāmāni sabbanāmāni, tesaṃ sabbesaṃ sabbanāmasaññānaṃ liṅgānaṃ a kārato paro paṭhamo yo e ttamāpajjate. Sabbe purisā.

Names that apply to everything, feminine, masculine and neuter alike, are `sabbanāma` ("pronouns"). After the letter `a` of all those lemmas that bear the name `sabbanāma`, the first `yo` (①⨂) that follows becomes `e`: `sabbe purisā` ("all men").

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | sabba + yo (🚹①⨂)     | rule |
| --- | --------------------- | ---- |
| →   | sabba + (yo→e)        | 200 (§164) |
| →   | sabb + (~~a~~ + e)    | 67 (§83)  |
| →   | **sabbe**             |      |

:::

> Akārato ti kiṃ? Sabbā amū.

By saying `akārato` (after `a`) we exclude a lemma that ends in another vowel: `sabbā amū` ("all those [women] yonder").

> He sabba sabbā, he sabbe, sabbaṃ, sabbe, sabbena, sabbehi sabbebhi.

`he sabba`, `he sabbā`; `he sabbe`; `sabbaṃ`; `sabbe`; `sabbena`; `sabbehi`, `sabbebhi`.

> Catutthekavacane āyā dese sampatte –

In the fourth-form singular ④⨀, when the replacement by `āya` would apply —

> ‘‘Ato, ā e, smāsmiṃnaṃ, āya catutthekavacanassā’’ti ca vattate.

“`ato`” (after `a`), “`ā e`”, “`smāsmiṃnaṃ`” and “`āya catutthekavacanassa`” also continue.

### 201 (§110): Tayo neva ca sabbanāmehi (And not the three [replacements] after pronouns in `a`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§110 (201): Tayo neva ca sabbanāmehi" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m110" description="And not the 3 [replacements] with pronouns ending in `a`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The three replacements are those of [69 (§107): Sabbayonīnamāe](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r69), [90 (§108): Smāsmiṃna vā](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r90) and [304 (§109): Āya catutthekavacanassa tu](/rupasiddhi/3-karakakanda#r304); `ato` continues from [79 (§103): Ato nena](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r79).

:::

> A kārantehi sabbanāmehi paresaṃ smā smiṃ iccetesaṃ, catutthekavacanassa ca ā e āya iccete ādesā neva hontīti āyā desābhāvo. Ca ggahaṇaṃ katthaci paṭisedhanivattanatthaṃ, tena pubbādīhi smā smiṃnaṃ ā e ca honti. Sabbassa.

After pronouns ending in `a`, the replacements `ā` and `e` for the `smā` and `smiṃ` that follow, and `āya` for the fourth-form singular, do not occur at all; hence there is no replacement by `āya`. The mention of `ca` is there to stop the prohibition in some places, so that after `pubba` and the like `ā` and `e` do occur for `smā` and `smiṃ`: `sabbassa` ("to all").

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | sabba + sa (🚹④⨀)     | rule |
| --- | --------------------- | ---- |
| →   | sabba + (s) + sa      | 86 (§61) (the `āya` of 304 (§109) denied by 201 (§110)) |
| →   | **sabbassa**          |      |

:::

> ‘‘Akāro, e’’ti ca vattate.

“`akāro`” (the letter `a`) and “`e`” also continue.

### 203 (§168): Sabbato naṃ saṃsānaṃ (`saṃ`, `sānaṃ` for `naṃ` after any pronoun) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§168 (203): Sabbato naṃ saṃ sānaṃ" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m168" description="`saṃ`, `sānaṃ` for `naṃ` after any pronoun" />
:::

> Sabbato sabbanāmato parassa naṃ vacanassa saṃ sānaṃ iccete ādesā honti.

After every pronoun, the inflection `naṃ` (④⨂, ⑥⨂) that follows is replaced by `saṃ` and `sānaṃ`.

> Sabbesaṃ sabbesānaṃ, sabbasmā sabbamhā, sabbehi sabbebhi, sabbassa, sabbesaṃ sabbesānaṃ, sabbasmiṃ sabbamhi, sabbesu.

`sabbesaṃ`, `sabbesānaṃ`; `sabbasmā`, `sabbamhā`; `sabbehi`, `sabbebhi`; `sabbassa`; `sabbesaṃ`, `sabbesānaṃ`; `sabbasmiṃ`, `sabbamhi`; `sabbesu`.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | sabba + naṃ (🚹④⨂)      | rule |
| --- | ----------------------- | ---- |
| →   | sabb + (a→e) + naṃ      | 202 (§102) |
| →   | sabbe + (naṃ→saṃ)       | 203 (§168) |
| →   | **sabbesaṃ**            |      |

|     | sabba + naṃ (🚹④⨂)      | rule |
| --- | ----------------------- | ---- |
| →   | sabb + (a→e) + naṃ      | 202 (§102) |
| →   | sabbe + (naṃ→sānaṃ)     | 203 (§168) |
| →   | **sabbesānaṃ**          |      |

:::

> Itthiyaṃ ‘‘itthiyamato āppaccayo’’ti ā ppaccayo. Aññaṃ kaññā saddasamaṃ aññatra sa naṃ smiṃ vacanehi. Sabbā pajā, sabbā sabbāyo, he sabbe, he sabbā sabbāyo, sabbaṃ, sabbā sabbāyo, sabbāya, sabbāhi sabbābhi.

In the feminine, the suffix `ā` is added by [§237 (176): Itthīyamato āpaccayo](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m237). Except before the inflections `sa`, `naṃ` and `smiṃ`, everything else is the same as the word `kaññā`: `sabbā pajā` ("all people"); `sabbā`, `sabbāyo`; `he sabbe`; `he sabbā`, `he sabbāyo`; `sabbaṃ`; `sabbā`, `sabbāyo`; `sabbāya`; `sabbāhi`, `sabbābhi`.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | sabba + si (🚺①⨀)        | rule |
| --- | ------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | sabba + (ā) + si         | 176 (§237) |
| →   | sabb + (~~a~~ + ā) + si  | 67 (§83)  |
| →   | sabbā + ~~si~~           | 74 (§220) |
| →   | **sabbā**                |      |

:::

> Catutthekavacane ‘‘sabbanāmato vā’’, sabbato ko ti ito ‘‘sabbato’’ti ca vattate.

For the fourth-form singular ④⨀, “`sabbanāmato vā`” continues, and so does “`sabbato`” from [224 (§178): Sabbato ko](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/4-sabbanama#r224).

### 204 (§179): Ghapato smiṃsānaṃ saṃsā (`saṃ`, `sā` for `smiṃ`, `sa` after `gha`, `pa` pronouns) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

### 207 (§180): Netāhi smimāyayā (No `āya`, `yā` for `smiṃ` after these) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§180 (207): Netāhi smimāya yā" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m180" description="No `āya`, `yā` for `smiṃ` after these" />
:::

> Etehi sabbanāmehi ghapa saññehi parassa smiṃ vacanassa neva āya yā desā hontīti āyā bhāvo. Vā dhikārato kvaci hoti dakkhiṇāya uttarāyāti ādi.

After these pronouns that bear the names `gha` and `pa`, the inflection `smiṃ` (⑦⨀) that follows is not replaced by `āya` or `yā` at all; hence there is no `āya`. By the governing `vā`, it does occur in some places: `dakkhiṇāya` ("in the south"), `uttarāya` ("in the north") and so on.

> Saṃya mādesā, sabbassaṃ sabbāyaṃ, sabbāsu.

The replacements `saṃ` and `yaṃ`: `sabbassaṃ`, `sabbāyaṃ`; `sabbāsu`.[^58]

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | sabbā + smiṃ (🚺⑦⨀)    | rule |
| --- | ---------------------- | ---- |
| →   | sabbā + (smiṃ→saṃ)     | 204 (§179) |

| →   | sabba + (s) + saṃ      | 206 (§62)  |
| →   | **sabbassaṃ**          |      |

|     | sabbā + smiṃ (🚺⑦⨀)    | rule |
| --- | ---------------------- | ---- |
| →   | sabbā + (smiṃ→yaṃ)     | 180 (§216) |
| →   | **sabbāyaṃ**           |      |

|     | dakkhiṇā + smiṃ (🚺⑦⨀)   | rule |
| --- | ------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | dakkhiṇā + (smiṃ→āya)    | 179 (§111) (the `āya` admitted `kvaci` by the governing `vā`) |
| →   | dakkhiṇ + (~~ā~~ + āya)  | 67 (§83)  |
| →   | **dakkhiṇāya**           |      |

:::

> Napuṃsake sabbaṃ cittaṃ, sabbāni, he sabba, he sabbāni, sabbaṃ, sabbāni. Sesaṃ pulliṅge viya ñeyyaṃ.

In the neuter: `sabbaṃ cittaṃ` ("every mind"); `sabbāni`; `he sabba`; `he sabbāni`; `sabbaṃ`; `sabbāni`. The rest should be understood as in the masculine.

> Evaṃ katarā dīnaṃ aññatama saddapariyantānaṃ tīsupi liṅgesu rūpanayo.

The forms of `katara` and the rest, down to the word `aññatama`, run the same way in all three genders.

> Tattha katarakatama saddā pucchanatthā.

Among them, the words `katara` ("which of two") and `katama` ("which of many") have the sense of a question.

> Ubhaya saddo dviavayavasamudāyavacano.

The word `ubhaya` ("both") expresses a whole made up of two parts.

> Itara saddo vuttappaṭiyogavacano.

The word `itara` ("the other") expresses the counterpart of what has been mentioned.

> Añña saddo adhikatāparavacano.

The word `añña` ("another") expresses what is other than the matter in hand.[^59]

> Aññatara aññatama saddā aniyamatthā.

The words `aññatara` ("a certain one") and `aññatama` ("one or other") have an indefinite sense.

> ‘‘Yo, sabbanāmakārate paṭhamo’’ti ca vattate.

“`yo`” and “`Sabbanāmakārate paṭhamo`” ([200 (§164): Sabbanāmakārate paṭhamo](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/4-sabbanama#r200)) also continue.

### 208 (§165): Dvandaṭṭhā vā (Optionally [`e`] after [a pronoun] standing in a `dvanda`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§165 (208): Dvandaṭṭhā vā" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m165" description="Optionally [`e`] after [a pronoun] standing in a `dvanda`" />

> Dvandasamāsaṭṭhā sabbanāmakārato paro paṭhamo yo e ttamāpajjate vā. Kataro ca katamo cāti katarakatame, katarakatamā vā iccādi.

After the letter `a` of a pronoun standing in a `dvanda` compound, the first `yo` (①⨂) that follows optionally becomes `e`: `kataro ca katamo ca` ("which of two and which of many") gives `katarakatame`, or `katarakatamā`, and so on.

> Pubbā dayo disādivavatthānavacanā.

`pubba` and the rest express the fixing of a direction and the like.

> Pubbo kālo. Bahuvacane ‘‘dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā’’ti ettha parā ti niddesato pubbādīhi yo vacanassa vikappenekāro.

`pubbo kālo` ("the former time"). In the plural, because [§432 (362): Dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m432) uses the wording `parā`, the inflection `yo` after `pubba` and the rest optionally becomes the letter `e`.

> Pubbe pubbā, he pubba, he pubbe he pubbā, pubbaṃ, pubbe, pubbena, pubbehi pubbebhi, pubbassa, pubbesaṃ pubbesānaṃ. ‘‘Smāsmiṃnaṃ vā’’ti vikappenākārekārā. Pubbā pubbasmā pubbamhā, pubbehi pubbebhi, pubbassa, pubbesaṃ pubbesānaṃ, pubbe pubbasmiṃ pubbamhi, pubbesu.

### 210 (§167): Bahubbīhimhi ca (And in a `bahubbīhi`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§167 (210): Bahubbīhimhi ca" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m167" description="And in a `bahubbīhi`" />

> Bahubbīhimhi ca samāse sabbanāmikavidhānaṃ nāññaṃ hoti. Piyapubbāya, piyapubbānaṃ, piyapubbe.

In a `bahubbīhi` compound too, no other prescription for pronouns applies: `piyapubbāya` ("of the woman whose former [husband] was dear"), `piyapubbānaṃ`, `piyapubbe`.

> Ca saddaggahaṇena disatthasabbanāmānaṃ bahubbīhimhi sabbanāmikavidhānaṃva hoti.

By the mention of the word `ca`, pronouns that mean a direction do take the prescription for pronouns in a `bahubbīhi`.

> Dakkhiṇassā ca pubbassā ca yadantarāḷanti atthe bahubbīhi, dakkhiṇapubbassaṃ, dakkhiṇapubbassā, evaṃ uttarapubbassaṃ, uttarapubbassā iccādi.

A `bahubbīhi` in the sense "the [direction] that lies between the south and the east": `dakkhiṇapubbassaṃ` ("in the south-east"), `dakkhiṇapubbassā` ("of the south-east"); likewise `uttarapubbassaṃ`, `uttarapubbassā` ("north-east") and so on.

For the words `ya`, `ta`, `eta` and the rest, no form occurs in the vocative. The word `ya` ("which", "whoever") has an indefinite sense.

> Yo puriso, ye purisā, yaṃ, ye. Yā kaññā, yā yāyo, yaṃ, yā yāyo. Yaṃ cittaṃ, yāni, yaṃ, yāni. Sesaṃ sabbattha sabba saddasamaṃ.

The first ① and second ② forms of `ya` in the three genders — `yo puriso` ("which man"), `yā kaññā` ("which girl"), `yaṃ cittaṃ` ("which mind"); everywhere else it is the same as the word `sabba`:

| | 🚹 ⨀ | 🚹 ⨂ | 🚺 ⨀ | 🚺 ⨂ | 🚻 ⨀ | 🚻 ⨂ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

| ② | yaṃ | ye | yaṃ | yā, yāyo | yaṃ | yāni |

`ta` ("that"), `eta` ("this"), `ima` ("this here"), `amu` ("that yonder") and `kiṃ` ("who?", "what?") express, in that order, what is out of sight, what is near, what is very near, what is far, and a question.

> Ta saddassa bhedo. ‘‘Ta si’’ itīdha –

The word `ta` differs. Here, in `ta` + `si` —

> ‘‘Anapuṃsakassāyaṃ simhīti, sa’’miti ca vattate.

“`Anapuṃsakassāyaṃ simhi`” ([218 (§172): Anapuṃsakassāyaṃ simhi](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/4-sabbanama#r218)) and “`saṃ`” also continue.

### 226 (§229): Sesesu ca (and `ka` before the remaining [inflections]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§229 (226): Sesesu ca" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m229" description="and `ka` before the remaining [inflections]" />
:::

> Ki miccetassa ka saddo ādeso hoti va ppaccayato sesesu vibhattibhedesu. Ettha ca ‘‘kissa ka ve cā’’ti sutte ca saddena va ppaccayāvasiṭṭha tha mādippaccayānaṃ gahitattā sesa ggahaṇena vibhattiyova gayhante. Ca ggahaṇaṃ katthaci nivattanatthaṃ, tena ‘‘kissa, kismi’’ntiādi ca sijjhati. ‘‘So’’ti si ssa o, saralopādi.

`kiṃ` is replaced by the word `ka` before the remaining kinds of inflection, other than the suffix `va`.[^66] And here, since the word `ca` in the sutta [§227 (270): Kissa ka ve ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m227) already takes in the suffixes other than `va`, `thaṃ` and the rest, the mention of `sesa` ("the remaining") takes in the inflections alone. The mention of `ca` is for stopping [the replacement] in some places; hence `kissa`, `kismiṃ` and the like are established too. By [66 (§104): So](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r66) the `si` becomes `o`, with elision of the vowel and the rest.

> Ko eso, ke, kaṃ, ke, kena, kehi kebhi, kassa kissa, niggahītalopādi, kesaṃ kesānaṃ, kasmā kamhā, kehi kebhi, kassa kissa, kesaṃ kesānaṃ, kasmiṃ kismiṃ kamhi kimhi, kesu.

The inflection table of `kiṃ` (who, what), 🚹(kiṃ); in `kissa`, the elision of the niggahīta and the rest:

| | ⨀ | ⨂ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ① | ko eso ("who is this?") | ke |
| ② | kaṃ | ke |
| ③ | kena | kehi, kebhi |
| ④ | kassa, kissa | kesaṃ, kesānaṃ |
| ⑤ | kasmā, kamhā | kehi, kebhi |
| ⑥ | kassa, kissa | kesaṃ, kesānaṃ |
| ⑦ | kasmiṃ, kismiṃ, kamhi, kimhi | kesu |

> Itthiyaṃ ‘‘kiṃ si’’itīdha ‘‘sesesu cā’’ti vibhattiyaṃ parāyaṃ kā dese kate ‘‘itthiyamato āppaccayo’’ti majjhe ā ppaccayo, si lopo.

In the feminine, in `kiṃ` + `si`, once the replacement `ka` has been made by “`Sesesu ca`” with the inflection following, the suffix `ā` comes in the middle by [§237 (176): Itthīyamato āpaccayo](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m237), and `si` is elided.

> Kā esā kaññā, kā kāyo, kaṃ, kā kāyo iccādi sabbā saddasamaṃ.

`kā esā kaññā` ("who is this girl?"); `kā`, `kāyo`; `kaṃ`; `kā`, `kāyo`, and so on, the same as the word `sabbā`.

> Napuṃsake kiṃ si, lopavidhissa balavatarattā paṭhamaṃ si lope kate puna vibhattiparattābhāvā, ‘‘tadanuparodhenā’’ti paribhāsato vā kā desābhāvo. Kiṃ etaṃ, kāni.

In the neuter, `kiṃ` + `si`: because the rule of elision is the stronger, the `si` is elided first, and then, since no inflection follows any more, there is no replacement by `ka` — or there is none by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) [§56 (64): Tadanuparodhena](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m56): `kiṃ etaṃ` ("what is this?"); `kāni`.

> Dutiyekavacane ‘‘kvaci lopa’’nti niggahītalope kate ‘‘aṃmo niggahītaṃ jhalapehī’’ti niggahītaṃ. Kiṃ, kāni iccādi pulliṅgasamaṃ.

The word `eka` expresses a number ("one"), sameness ("the same")[^67], one without a companion ("alone") and another ("some"). When it expresses a number it takes singular inflections only, throughout; elsewhere it takes plural inflections too. `eko`, `ekā`, `ekaṃ`, and so on, are everywhere the same as the word `sabba`; the only difference is before `saṃ` and `sā`.

> Ese’so etamiti ca, \
> Pasiddhi atthesu yesu lokassa; \
> Thīpunnapuṃsakāniti, \
> Vuccante tāni nāmāni.

as "`esā`", "`eso`" or "`etaṃ`" [she, he, it] — \
in whichever meanings the world's usage so stands, \
feminine, masculine and neuter: \
so are those nouns called.

---

> _Tiliṅgaṃ niṭṭhitaṃ._

The [nouns of] three genders are finished.
