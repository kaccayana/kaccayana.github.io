:::note[Source]
Phramaha Sriporn Rachiwong, *Rūpasiddhi: A Study of Some Aspects*, thesis submitted to the University of Pune for the degree of Doctor of Philosophy in Pāli, 1995, under the guidance of Dr. J. R. Joshi. Copyright remains with the author. Reproduced from a scan of the typescript, [available here as a PDF (46 MB)](/reference/rupasiddhi-1995-rachiwong.pdf), as a reading edition: the text was recognised from the page images and proofread against them page by page. The author's spelling, punctuation and terminology are kept as printed, including slips of the typescript; the diacritics of the Pāli and Sanskrit are restored from the page; quotation marks are normalised to straight quotes; footnotes keep the thesis's own numbers. The printed page numbers are kept as hidden comments of the form `<!-- p. 127 (PDF p. 143) -->`. A word that could not be read is followed by `[?]`. One line is missing at most page turns of the translation: the scan was made with a window that stops one line above the foot of the typescript, so the last line of nearly every full page is absent from the PDF and from its text layer, and the typescript itself occasionally drops a line at a page break; nothing could be recovered, and each such gap is marked `[…]` where the text resumes.

The thesis translates three sections of Buddhappiya's Padarūpasiddhi — the Nāmakaṇḍa, the Samāsakaṇḍa and the Ākhyātakaṇḍa — rule by rule, each rule headed by its two numbers in the form `(K-N)`: K is the Kaccāyana sutta and N the Rūpasiddhi rule in the edition the thesis follows — the Thai-script Padarūpasiddhi edited by Siri Petchai (Bangkok 1984), which, like the Sinhalese Mahārūpasiddhi (Colombo 1964), counts 668 rules because it does not number the sixteen rules that the Burmese text states twice (§183, and the fifteen `kāraka` rules first given with the `purisa` paradigm and again in the Kārakakaṇḍa). In the Nāmakaṇḍa N agrees with the numbering of this site's [Padarūpasiddhi](/rupasiddhi/) pages, so that rule `(244-72)` is [§244](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/4-catutthakanda#m244) of the Kaccāyana pages and [72](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r72) of the Rūpasiddhi pages; in the Samāsakaṇḍa and the Ākhyātakaṇḍa its N runs fifteen, then sixteen, below this site's (its `(334-330)` is this site's 345, its `(481-508)` is 524), so the Kaccāyana number K is the surer guide, allowing for the thesis's own occasional misprints of it. Not reproduced is the Pāli text of the three sections that the thesis prints in Devanagari before the translation (printed pp. 30–119, PDF pp. 46–135); the Padarūpasiddhi pages of this site carry the same text from the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana edition. The Explanatory Notes collate that Thai edition ("Rūp T.") with the Burmese Buddhasāsanasamiti edition ("B.", the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana text these pages print) and the Sinhalese edition ("S."), and cite the Burmese Padarūpasiddhi-ṭīkā ("PDṬ") and a Thai handbook, the *Padarūpasiddhi Plae lae Athibai* ("PDP"). This site's [Nāmakaṇḍa](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda), [Samāsakaṇḍa](/rupasiddhi/4-samasakanda) and [Ākhyātakaṇḍa](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda) pages have been collated with the thesis rule by rule; their footnotes record where its Thai text or its construal differs from what they print, and the corrections it prompted.

## Acknowledgement

"YO NIRUTTIṀ NA SIKKHEYYA SIKKHANTO PIṬAKATTAYAṀ\
PADE PADE VIKAṄKHEYYA VANE ANDHAGAJO YATHĀ."

"One who would not study Grammar (Nirutti) while\
studying the 'Tipiṭaka', would obtain uncertainty in every\
step like a blind elephant (roaming about) in a jungle."

The present thesis deals with the study of 'Rūpasiddhi' a Pali grammatical work. It is divided into four parts i. e. Text, Translation, Notes and Bibliography. I have attempted to present the text in Devanāgarī script along with an English translation and notes. It is expected that this modest attempt would lead to the production of complete edition of 'Rūpasiddhi'' in future.

I wish to take this opportunity to express my sincere thanks to my guide Dr. J. R. Joshi, Reader in Pali, Department of Sanskrit and Prakrit Languages, University of Pune, without whose sincere guidance and co-operation the work would not have been accomplished.
