## Vikaraṇavidhāna (The class suffixes)

> Isu icchākantīsu, pure viya dhātvantalopo, tyā dyuppatti, a ppaccayo ca.

`isu` in [the senses of] wishing and desiring. As before, the root's final is elided, the inflections `ti` and the rest arise, and the suffix `a` [is added].

> ‘‘Dhātūna’’nti vattamāne –

While `dhātūnaṃ` ("of the roots") continues —

### 476 (§522): Isuyamūnamanto ccho vā (optionally `ccha` for the final of `isu`, `yamu`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§522 (476): Isuyamūnamanto ccho vā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m522" description="optionally `ccha` for the final of `isu`, `yamu`" />
:::

> Isuyamu iccetesaṃ dhātūnaṃ anto ccho hoti vā. Vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ vā saddo, ‘‘anto ccho vā’’ti yogavibhāgena āsa ssapi. So saggaṃ icchati, icchanti. Icchasi, icchatha. Icchāmi, icchāma. Cchā desābhāve asaṃyogantattā ‘‘aññesu cā’’ti vuddhi, esati, esanti iccādi.

The final of the roots `isu` and `yamu` optionally becomes `ccha`. This word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`), and by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) into `anto ccho vā` ("the final optionally becomes `ccha`") the replacement applies to `āsa` as well: `so saggaṃ icchati` ("he wishes for heaven"), `icchanti`; `icchasi`, `icchatha`; `icchāmi`, `icchāma`. Where `ccha` is not substituted, the root does not end in a conjunct, so `vuddhi` applies by [§485 (434): Aññesu ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m485): `esati`, `esanti`, and so on.

> Kamme attanopadassa yebhuyyena parassapadattameva payojīyati, tena cettha attanopade rūpāni saṅkhipissāma. So icchīyati, esīyati, issate, issati, ya kārassa pubbarūpattaṃ. Tathā icchatu, esatu. Iccheyya, eseyya. Parokkhāhiyyattanīsu pana rūpāni sabbattha payogamanugamma payojetabbāni, icchi, esi. Icchissati, esissati. Icchissā, esissā iccādi[^21].

In the passive, the `attanopada` is for the most part employed only in the shape of the `parassapada`, and so here we shall abridge the `attanopada` forms: `so icchīyati` ("he is wished for"), `esīyati`; `issate`, `issati`, where the letter `ya` takes the form of the preceding letter (`pubbarūpa`). Likewise `icchatu`, `esatu`; `iccheyya`, `eseyya`. In the `parokkhā` and the `hiyyattanī`, however, the forms are to be employed everywhere by following usage. `icchi`, `esi`; `icchissati`, `esissati`; `icchissā`, `esissā`, and so on.

> Yamu uparame, ni pubbo, cchā deso ca. Niyacchati, niyacchanti. Niyamati, niyamanti. Saṃ pubbo ‘‘saye cā’’ti ña ttaṃ, dvittañca. Saññamati, saññamanti.

`yamu` is in the sense of restraining. With the prefix `ni` and the replacement by `ccha`: `niyacchati`, `niyacchanti`; `niyamati`, `niyamanti`. With the prefix `saṃ`, the `niggahīta` becomes `ñ` by [§33 (51): Sa ye ca](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m33) and is doubled: `saññamati`, `saññamanti`.

> Kamme niyacchīyati, niyamīyati, niyammati, saññamīyati vā. Tathā niyacchatu, saññamatu. Niyaccheyya, saññameyya. Niyacchī, saññamī. Niyacchissati, saññamissati. Niyacchissa, saññamissa iccādi.

In the passive: `niyacchīyati`, `niyamīyati`, `niyammati`, or `saññamīyati`. Likewise `niyacchatu`, `saññamatu`; `niyaccheyya`, `saññameyya`; `niyacchī`, `saññamī`; `niyacchissati`, `saññamissati`; `niyacchissa`, `saññamissa`, and so on.

> Āsa upavesane, yogavibhāgena cchā deso, rassattaṃ. Acchati, acchanti. Acchasi, acchatha. Acchāmi, acchāma. Aññatra upa pubbo upāsati, upāsanti. Acchīyati, upāsīyati. Acchatu, upāsatu. Accheyya, upāseyya. Acchī, upāsī. Acchissati, upāsissati. Acchissa, upāsissa iccādi.

`āsa` is in the sense of sitting down. By the split rule it takes the replacement `ccha`, with shortening: `acchati`, `acchanti`; `acchasi`, `acchatha`; `acchāmi`, `acchāma`. Elsewhere, with the prefix `upa`: `upāsati`, `upāsanti`. `acchīyati`, `upāsīyati`; `acchatu`, `upāsatu`; `accheyya`, `upāseyya`; `acchī`, `upāsī`; `acchissati`, `upāsissati`; `acchissa`, `upāsissa`, and so on.

> Labha lābhe, labhati, labhanti. Labhasi, labhatha. Labhāmi, labhāma. Labhate, labhante. Labhase, labhavhe. Labhe, labhāmhe.

`labha` is in the sense of getting: `labhati`, `labhanti`; `labhasi`, `labhatha`; `labhāmi`, `labhāma`; `labhate`, `labhante`; `labhase`, `labhavhe`; `labhe`, `labhāmhe`.

> Kamme ya kārassa pubbarūpatte kate ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā purimabha kārassa ba kāro, labbhate, labbhante. Labbhati, labbhanti. Labbhataṃ, labbhatu. Labbhe, labbheyya.

> Ajjatanimhi ‘‘vā, antalopo’’ti ca vattamāne –

### 478 (§487): Vaca vasa vahādīnamukāro vassa ye (`u` for the `va` of `vaca`, `vasa`, `vaha` etc. before `ya`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§487 (478): Vaca vasa vahādīnamukāro vassa ye" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m487" description="`u` for the `va` of `vaca`, `vasa`, `vaha` etc. before `ya`" />
:::

> Vaca vasa vaha iccevamādīnaṃ dhātūnaṃ va kārassa u kāro hoti ya ppaccaye pare, ādi saddena vaḍḍha ssa ca. ‘‘Vassa a va’’iti samāsena dutiyañcettha va ggahaṇaṃ icchitabbaṃ, tena a kārassapi u kāro hoti, purimapakkhe paralopo. ‘‘Tassa cavagga’’iccādinā sadhātvantassa ya kārassa ca kāro, dvittaṃ. Uccate, uccante. Vuccate, vuccante. Vuccati, vuccanti vā iccādi. Tathā vacatu, vuccatu. Vaceyya, vucceyya. Avacā, avaccā, avacū, avaccū. Avaca, avaco, avacuttha. Avaca, avacaṃ, avacamhā. Avacuttha iccādi.

The letter `va` of the roots `vaca`, `vasa`, `vaha` and the like becomes the letter `u` when the suffix `ya` follows, and by the word `ādi` so does that of `vaḍḍha`. Since `vassa` is the compound `va` + `a`, a second mention of `va` is wanted here as well, so that the letter `a` too becomes `u`; on the former alternative, what follows is elided. By the rule beginning `Tassa cavagga` [§441] the letter `ya`, together with the final of the root, becomes the letter `c` and is doubled: `uccate`, `uccante`; `vuccate`, `vuccante`; or `vuccati`, `vuccanti`, and so on. ### 488 (§517): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca (sometimes lengthening, change, replacement, elision and insertion of roots, endings and suffixes) :badge[vidhyaṅga]{variant=tip}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517" description="sometimes lengthening, change, replacement, elision and insertion of roots, endings and suffixes" />
:::

> Idha dhātvādhikāre ākhyāte, kitake ca avihitalakkhaṇesu payogesu kvaci dhātūnaṃ, tyādivibhattīnaṃ, dhātuvihitappaccayānañca dīghatabbiparītaādesalopāgamaiccetāni kāriyāni jinavacanānurūpato bhavanti. Tattha –

Here, while “`dhātu`” (root) is the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), in those usages of the verb and of the `kita` for which no rule has been laid down, the following operations sometimes take place, in conformity with the Conqueror's word: lengthening and its reverse, replacement ([`ādesa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), elision ([`lopa`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) and insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) — of roots, of the endings `ti` and the rest, and of the suffixes prescribed after roots. Among these —

> Nāmhi rasso kiyādīnaṃ, saṃyoge caññadhātunaṃ; \
> Āyūnaṃ vā vibhattīnaṃ, mhā, ssāntassa ca rassatā.

Before `nā` the `kī` class is shortened, and so are other roots before a conjunct; \
the endings `ā` and `ū` are optionally shortened, and so is the final of `mhā` and `ssā`.

> Gamito cchassa ñcho vāssa, gamissajjatanimhi gā; \
> Ucāgamo vā tthamhesu, dhātūnaṃ yamhi dīghatā.

After `gamu`, its `ccha` optionally becomes `ñcha`, and in the `ajjatanī` `gamu` becomes `gā`; \
`u` is optionally inserted before `ttha` and `mha`, and roots are lengthened before `ya`.

> Eyyeyyāseyyāmettañca, vā ssessettañca pāpuṇe; \
> Okārā attamittañca, ātthā papponti vā tthathe.

`eyya`, `eyyāsi` and `eyyāmi` optionally take `e`, and so does `ssa`; \
`o` takes `a` and `i`, and `ā` and `ttha` optionally become `ttha` and `tha`.

> Tathā brūtoti antīnaṃ, au vāha ca dhātuyā; \
> Parokkhāya vibhattimhi, anabbhāsassa dīghatā.

Likewise after `brū`, `ti` and `anti` optionally become `a` and `u`, and the root becomes `āha`; \
before a `parokkhā` ending, what is not the `abbhāsa` is lengthened.

> Saṃyoganto akārettha, vibhattippaccayādi tu; \
> Lopa māpajjate nicca-mekārokārato paroti.

Here, after a conjunct, the letter `a` [remains]; but the initial of an ending or a suffix \
is always elided after the letter `e` or `o`.

> E kārato parassa antia kārassa lopo, vajjenti, vadenti, vadanti. Vajjesi, vadesi, vadasi, vajjetha, vadetha, vadatha. Vajjemi, vajjāmi, vademi, vadāmi, vajjema, vadema, vajjāma, vadāma.

After `e`, the `a` of the following `anti` is elided: `vajjenti`, `vadenti`, `vadanti` ("they say"). `vajjesi`, `vadesi`, `vadasi` ("you say"), `vajjetha`, `vadetha`, `vadatha`; `vajjemi`, `vajjāmi`, `vademi`, `vadāmi` ("I say"), `vajjema`, `vadema`, `vajjāma`, `vadāma` ("we say").

:::tip[Derivation]

> Kammani vajjīyati, vajjīyanti. Vajjati, vajjanti. Vadīyati vā. Vajjetu, vadetu, vadatu. Vajje, vajjeyya, vade, vadeyya, vajjeyyuṃ, vadeyyuṃ. Vajjeyyāsi, vajjesi, vadeyyāsi. Avadi, vadi, vadiṃsu. Vadissati, vadissanti. Avadissa iccādi.

### 490 (§480): Hotissare’hohe bhavissantimhi ssassa ca (`eha`, `oha`, `e` for the vowel of `hū` in the `bhavissantī`; `ssa` elided) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§480 (490): Hotissarehohe bhavissantimhissassa ca" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m480" description="`eha`, `oha`, `e` for the vowel of `hū` in the `bhavissantī`; `ssa` elided" />

> Hū iccetassa dhātussa saro eha oha etta māpajjate bhavissantimhi vibhattimhi, ssa ssa ca lopo hoti vā. I kārāgamo, saralopādi.

When a `bhavissantī` ending follows, the vowel of the root `hū` becomes `eha`, `oha` or `e`, and the `ssa` is optionally elided. The letter `i` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) [§516], the vowel is elided, and so on.

> Hehiti, hehinti. Hehisi, hehitha. Hehāmi, hehāma. Lopābhāve – hehissati, hehissanti. Hehissasi, hehissatha. Hehissāmi, hehissāma. Ohādese – hohiti, hohinti. Hohisi, hohitha. Hohāmi, hohāma. Tathā hohissati, hohissanti. Hohissasi, hohissatha. Hohissāmi, hohissāma. Ekārādese – heti, henti. Hesi, hetha. Hemi, hema. Hessati, hessanti. Hessasi, hessatha. Hessāmi, hessāma. Bhāve hūyissate.

`hehiti` ("he will be"), `hehinti`; `hehisi`, `hehitha`; `hehāmi`, `hehāma`. When the `ssa` is not elided — `hehissati`, `hehissanti`; `hehissasi`, `hehissatha`; `hehissāmi`, `hehissāma`. With the replacement by `oha` — `hohiti`, `hohinti`; `hohisi`, `hohitha`; `hohāmi`, `hohāma`; and likewise `hohissati`, `hohissanti`; `hohissasi`, `hohissatha`; `hohissāmi`, `hohissāma`. With the replacement by `e` — `heti`, `henti`; `hesi`, `hetha`; `hemi`, `hema`; `hessati`, `hessanti`; `hessasi`, `hessatha`; `hessāmi`, `hessāma`. In the impersonal, `hūyissate`.

> Kālātipattiyaṃ ahavissa, ahavissaṃsu. Ahuyissatha iccādi.

In the `kālātipatti`, `ahavissa` ("he would have been"), `ahavissaṃsu`; `ahuyissatha`,[^28] and so on.

> Hū, bhū sattāyaṃ, bhū itīdha anu pubbo, tyā dyuppatti a ppaccayassa lopavuddhiyo, anubhoti, anubhonti. Anubhosi, anubhotha. Anubhomi, anubhoma.

Of `hū` and `bhū`, "in the sense of being", here `bhū` is taken with the prefix `anu`: the endings `ti` and the rest arise, the suffix `a` is elided, and `vuddhi` applies: `anubhoti` ("he experiences"), `anubhonti`; `anubhosi`, `anubhotha`; `anubhomi`, `anubhoma`.

> Kamme anubhūyati, anubhūyanti. Tathā anubhotu, anubhontu. Anubhohi, anubhotha. Anubhomi, anubhoma. Anubhūyatu, anubhūyantu. Anubhave, anubhaveyya. Anubhūyeyya. Anubhosi, anubhavi. Anubhossati, anubhossanti. Anubhossasi, anubhossatha. Anubhossāmi, anubhossāma. Anubhavissati vā. Anubhossa, anubhavissa vā iccādi.

> Sī saye a ppaccayalopo, vuddhi ca, seti, senti. Sesi, setha. Semi, sema. Sete, sente iccādi.

### 491 (§514): E aya (a root-final `e` becomes `aya` [before a vowel]) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§514 (491): E aya" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m514" description="a root-final `e` becomes `aya` [before a vowel]" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

`sare` and the mention of `dhātu` continue from [435 (§513): O ava sare](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/1-bhuvadigana-vibhatti#r435).

:::

> E kārassa dhātvantassa sare pare ayā deso hoti, saralopādi. Sayati, sayanti. Sayasi, sayatha. Sayāmi, sayāma.

A root-final letter `e` is replaced by `aya` when a vowel follows; the vowel is elided, and so on: `sayati` ("he lies down"), `sayanti`; `sayasi`, `sayatha`; `sayāmi`, `sayāma`.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √sī + a + ti                  | rule |
| --- | ----------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | s(ī→e) + a + ti               | 434 (§485) |
| →   | s(e→aya) + a + ti             | 491 (§514) |
| →   | say + (~~a~~ + a) + ti        | 67 (§83) |
| →   | **sayati**                    |      |

:::

> Kamme ati pubbo, ‘‘kvaci dhātvā’’dinā yamhi rassasarassa dhātvantassa dīgho, atisīyate, atisīyante. Atisīyati, atisīyanti. Bhāve sīyate.

In the passive, with the prefix `ati`, the root's short final vowel is lengthened before `ya` by the rule beginning “`Kvaci dhātu`” [§517]: `atisīyate` ("it is lain upon"), `atisīyante`; `atisīyati`, `atisīyanti`. In the impersonal, `sīyate`.

> Tathā setu, sentu. Sehi, setha. Semi, sema. Sayatu, sayantu. Saya, sayāhi, sayatha. Sayāmi, sayāma. Sayataṃ, sayantaṃ. Sayassu, sayavho. Saye, sayāmase. Atisīyataṃ, atisīyantaṃ. Atisīyatu, atisīyantu. Bhāve sīyataṃ.

Likewise `setu` ("let him lie down"), `sentu`; `sehi`, `setha`; `semi`, `sema`; `sayatu`, `sayantu`; `saya`, `sayāhi`, `sayatha`; `sayāmi`, `sayāma`; `sayataṃ`, `sayantaṃ`; `sayassu`, `sayavho`; `saye`, `sayāmase`; `atisīyataṃ`, `atisīyantaṃ` ("let it be lain upon"); `atisīyatu`, `atisīyantu`. In the impersonal, `sīyataṃ`.

> Saye, sayeyya, sayeyyuṃ. Atisīyeyya. Bhāve sīyetha.

`saye`, `sayeyya` ("he would lie down"), `sayeyyuṃ` ("they would lie down"); `atisīyeyya`; in the impersonal, `sīyetha`.

> Asayi, sayi, asayiṃsu, sayiṃsu, asayuṃ. Sāgame atisesi, atisesuṃ. Kamme accasīyittha, accasīyi, atisīyi. Bhāve sīyittha.

`asayi`, `sayi` ("he lay down"), `asayiṃsu`, `sayiṃsu`, `asayuṃ` ("they lay down"). With the insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of `s`, `atisesi` ("he lay upon"), `atisesuṃ`. In the passive, `accasīyittha`, `accasīyi`, `atisīyi` ("it was lain upon"). In the impersonal, `sīyittha`.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | ati + sī + ī (ajjatanī 🤟⨀)   | rule |
| --- | ----------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | ati + sī + ~~a~~ + ī          | 487 (§510) |
| →   | ati + s(ī→e) + (s) + ī        | 434 (§485), 523 (§491) |
| →   | atises + (ī→i)                | 488 (§517) |
| →   | **atisesi**                   |      |

:::

> Sayissati, sayissanti. I kārāgamābhāve sessati, sessanti. Kamme atisīyissate[^29], atisīyissati. Bhāve sīyissate.

`sayissati` ("he will lie down"), `sayissanti`; without the insertion of the letter `i`, `sessati`, `sessanti`. In the passive, `atisīyissate`, `atisīyissati`. In the impersonal, `sīyissate`.

> Asayissā, asayissaṃsu. Kamme accasīyissatha iccādi.

`asayissā` ("he would have lain down"), `asayissaṃsu`; in the passive, `accasīyissatha`, and so on.

> Nī pāpuṇane, dvikammako yaṃ, ajaṃ gāmaṃ neti, nenti. Nesi, netha. Nemi, nema. Lopābhāve nayati, nayanti iccādi. Kamme nīyate gāmaṃ ajo devadattena, nīyare, nīyante. Nīyati, nīyanti.

`nī` is in the sense of reaching; this root takes two objects (`dvikammaka`): `ajaṃ gāmaṃ neti` ("he leads the goat to the village"), `nenti`; `nesi`, `netha`; `nemi`, `nema`. When the suffix is not elided, `nayati`, `nayanti`, and so on. In the passive, `nīyate gāmaṃ ajo devadattena` ("the goat is led to the village by Devadatta"), `nīyare`, `nīyante`; `nīyati`, `nīyanti`.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √nī + a + ti           | rule |
| --- | ---------------------- | ---- |
| →   | n(ī→e) + a + ti        | 434 (§485) |

:::

> Tathā netu, nayatu. Nīyataṃ, nīyantaṃ. Naye, nayeyya. Nīyetha, nīyeyya. Anayi, nayi, anayiṃsu, nayiṃsu. Vinesi, vinesuṃ. Anīyittha, nīyittha. Nayissati, nessati. Nayissate, nīyissate, nīyissati. Anayissa, anīyissa iccādi.

> Ṭhā gatinivattimhi, ‘‘vā’’ti vattate.

`ṭhā` is in the sense of the stopping of motion; “`vā`” continues.

### 493 (§502): Yamhi dādhā mā ṭhā hā pā maha mathādīnamī (before `ya`, `ī` for the final of `dā`, `dhā`, `mā`, `ṭhā`, `hā`, `pā`, `maha`, `matha` etc.) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§502 (493): Yamhi dā dhā mā ṭhā hā pā mahamathādīnamī" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m502" description="before `ya`, `ī` for the final of `dā`, `dhā`, `mā`, `ṭhā`, `hā`, `pā`, `maha`, `matha` etc." />

### 507 (§499): Dādhātussa dajjaṃ (optionally `dajja` for the whole root `dā`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§499 (507): Dādhātussa dajjaṃ" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m499" description="optionally `dajja` for the whole root `dā`" />

> Dā iccetassa dhātussa sabbassa dajjā deso hoti vā, vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ vā saddo, a ppaccayalopo. Dajjati, dajjanti. Dajjasi, dajjatha. Dajjāmi, dajjāma. Dajjā desābhāve ‘‘lopañcettamakāro’’ti a ppaccayassa e kāro, dānaṃ deti, denti. Desi, detha.

The whole root `dā` is optionally replaced by `dajja`; this word `vā` has the sense of a `vavatthitavibhāsā` (a restricted option). The suffix `a` is elided: `dajjati` ("he gives"), `dajjanti`; `dajjasi`, `dajjatha`; `dajjāmi`, `dajjāma`. When the replacement by `dajja` is not made, the suffix `a` becomes the letter `e` by [§510 (487): Lopañcettamakāro](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m510): `dānaṃ deti` ("he gives a gift"), `denti`; `desi`, `detha`.

### 508 (§482): Dādantassaṃ mimesu (the final of `dā` becomes `aṃ` before `mi`, `ma`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§482 (508): Dādantassaṃ mi mesu" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m482" description="the final of `dā` becomes `aṃ` before `mi`, `ma`" />

> `brāhmaṇānaṃ adā dānaṃ, sivīnaṃ raṭṭhavaḍḍhano` \

to the brahmins | gave | a gift | of the Sivis | the increaser of the realm

> Kamme ‘‘yamhi dādhā’’iccādinā ī kāro, dīyate, dīyante. Dīyati, dīyanti. Dīyyate, dīyyante. Dīyyati, dīyyanti vā iccādi.

In the passive, the final becomes the letter `ī` by the rule beginning [§502 (493): Yamhi dā dhā mā ṭhā hā pā mahamathādīnamī](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m502): `dīyate` ("it is given"), `dīyante`; `dīyati`, `dīyanti`; or `dīyyate`, `dīyyante`, `dīyyati`, `dīyyanti`, and so on.[^34]

> Dadātu, dadantu. Dadāhi, dadātha. Dadāmi, dadāma. Dadataṃ, dadantaṃ. Dadassu, dadavho. Dade, dadāmase. Dajjatu, dajjantu iccādi. Detu, dentu. Dehi, detha. Demi, dema. Kamme dīyataṃ, dīyantaṃ. Dīyatu, dīyantu.

[`pañcamī`] `dadātu` ("let him give"), `dadantu`; `dadāhi`, `dadātha`; `dadāmi`, `dadāma`; [`attanopada`] `dadataṃ`, `dadantaṃ`; `dadassu`, `dadavho`; `dade`, `dadāmase`. [With `dajja`] `dajjatu`, `dajjantu`, and so on. `detu`, `dentu`; `dehi`, `detha`; `demi`, `dema`. In the passive, `dīyataṃ`, `dīyantaṃ`; `dīyatu`, `dīyantu`.

> Sattamiyaṃ dade, dadeyya, dadeyyuṃ. Dadeyyāsi, dadeyyātha. Dadeyyāmi, dadeyyāma. Dadetha, daderaṃ. Dadetho, dadeyyāvho. Dadeyyaṃ, dadeyyāmhe. Dajje, dajjeyya.

In the `sattamī`, `dade`, `dadeyya` ("he would give"), `dadeyyuṃ`; `dadeyyāsi`, `dadeyyātha`; `dadeyyāmi`, `dadeyyāma`; [`attanopada`] `dadetha`, `daderaṃ`; `dadetho`, `dadeyyāvho`; `dadeyyaṃ`, `dadeyyāmhe`. [With `dajja`] `dajje`, `dajjeyya`.

> ‘‘Kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā eyyassāttañca, dajjā, dajjuṃ, dajjeyyuṃ. Dajjeyyāsi, dajjeyyātha. Dajjaṃ, eyyāmissa amādeso ca, dajjeyyāmi, dajjeyyāma. Dvittābhāve deyya, deyyuṃ. Deyyāsi, deyyātha. Dīyetha, dīyeyya.

And by the rule beginning [§517 (488): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m517) `eyya` becomes `ā`: `dajjā`; `dajjuṃ`, `dajjeyyuṃ`; `dajjeyyāsi`, `dajjeyyātha`; `dajjaṃ`, with `eyyāmi` replaced by `aṃ`, and `dajjeyyāmi`; `dajjeyyāma`. When there is no doubling, `deyya`, `deyyuṃ`; `deyyāsi`, `deyyātha`. [In the passive] `dīyetha`, `dīyeyya`.

> Hiyyattaniyaṃ adadā, adadū. Adado, adadattha. Adadaṃ, adadamha. Adadattha, adadamhase. Kamme adīyittha.

In the `hiyyattanī`, `adadā` ("he gave"), `adadū`; `adado`, `adadattha`; `adadaṃ`, `adadamha`; [`attanopada`] `adadattha`, `adadamhase`. In the passive, `adīyittha`.

> Ajjatanimhi adadi, adadiṃsu, adaduṃ. Adajji, adajjiṃsu. Adāsi, adaṃsu. Adāsi, ado, adittha. Adāsiṃ, adāsimha, adamha. Adādānaṃ purindado. Kamme adīyittha, adīyyi.

In the `ajjatanī`, `adadi` ("he gave"), `adadiṃsu`, `adaduṃ`; `adajji`, `adajjiṃsu`; `adāsi`, `adaṃsu`; `adāsi`, `ado`, `adittha`; `adāsiṃ`, `adāsimha`, `adamha`; [`attanopada`] `adā dānaṃ purindado` ("Purindada gave a gift"). In the passive, `adīyittha`, `adīyyi`.

> Bhavissantiyaṃ ikārāgamo, saralopādi, dadissati, dadissanti. Dajjissati, dajjissanti. Rassattaṃ, dassati, dassanti. Dassasi, dassatha. Dassāmi, dassāma. Dassate. Dīyissate, dīyissati.

In the `bhavissantī`, the letter `i` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), with elision of the vowel and the rest: `dadissati` ("he will give"), `dadissanti`; `dajjissati`, `dajjissanti`. With shortening, `dassati`, `dassanti`; `dassasi`, `dassatha`; `dassāmi`, `dassāma`; [`attanopada`] `dassate`. [In the passive] `dīyissate`, `dīyissati`.

> Kālātipattiyaṃ adadissa, adajjissa, adajjissā, adassa, adassā, adassaṃsu. Adīyissatha, adīyissa iccādi.

In the `kālātipatti`, `adadissa` ("he would have given"), `adajjissa`, `adajjissā`, `adassa`, `adassā`, `adassaṃsu`. [In the passive] `adīyissatha`, `adīyissa`, and so on.

> Dhā dhāraṇe, pure viya vibhattuppatti, dvittarassattāni, appaccayalopo ca, ‘‘dutiyacatutthānaṃ paṭhamatatiyā’’ti dha kārassa da kāro, dadhāti, dadhanti. Apipubbo tassa ‘‘tesu vuddhī’’tiādinā akāralopo, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā dha kārassa ha kāro, rassattañca, dvāraṃ pidahati, pidahanti. Dvebhāvābhāve nidhiṃ nidheti, nidhenti.

`dhā` is in the sense of holding. As before, the endings arise, with doubling, shortening and the elision of the suffix `a`, and by [§461 (464): Dutiyacatutthānaṃ paṭhamatatiyā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m461) the letter `dh` becomes `d`: `dadhāti` ("he holds"), `dadhanti`. When `api` precedes, its letter `a` is elided by the rule beginning [§404 (370): Tesu vuddhilopāgamavikāraviparītādesā ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m404), the letter `dh` becomes `h` by the rule beginning `Kvaci dhātu` [§517], and the vowel is shortened: `dvāraṃ pidahati` ("he shuts the door"), `pidahanti`. When there is no doubling, `nidhiṃ nidheti` ("he deposits a treasure"), `nidhenti`.

> Kamme dhīyate, dhīyati, pidhīyate, pidhīyati.

In the passive, `dhīyate` ("it is held"), `dhīyati`; `pidhīyate`, `pidhīyati`.

> Tathā dadhātu, pidahatu, nidhetu, nidhentu. Dadhe, dadheyya, pidahe, pidaheyya, nidhe, nidheyya. Dadhāsi, pidahi. Dhassati, pidahissati, paridahessati. Adhassa, pidahissa iccādi.

Likewise [`pañcamī`] `dadhātu`, `pidahatu`, `nidhetu`, `nidhentu`; [`sattamī`] `dadhe`, `dadheyya`, `pidahe`, `pidaheyya`, `nidhe`, `nidheyya`; [`ajjatanī`] `dadhāsi`, `pidahi`; [`bhavissantī`] `dhassati`, `pidahissati`, `paridahessati`; [`kālātipatti`] `adhassa`, `pidahissa`, and so on.
