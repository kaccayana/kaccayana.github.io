## Dhātuppaccayantanaya (Roots with derivative suffixes)

> Atha dhātuppaccayantā vuccante.

And now the [verbs] ending in a root-suffix (`dhātuppaccayanta`) are stated.

> Tattha dhātvatthe niddiṭṭhā khādikārita ntā paccayā dhātuppaccayā nāma.

Among these, the suffixes prescribed in the sense of a root, from `kha` to the `kārita` (causative) suffixes, are called root-suffixes (`dhātuppaccaya`).

> Tija nisāna bandhanakhamāsu, dhātusaññādi.

`Tija` is [the root] in the senses of sharpening (`nisāna`), binding (`bandhana`) and enduring (`khamā`); it is named a root (`dhātu`), and the rest [follows].

> ‘‘Dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā’’ti ito dhātu ggahaṇaṃ anuvattate, ‘‘parā, paccayā’’ti ca adhikāro.

The word `dhātu` (root) continues from [§432 (362): Dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m432), and “`parā paccayā`” ("the suffixes come after") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

<a name="r528"></a>

### 528 (§433): Tija gupa kita mānehi kha cha sā vā (optionally `kha`, `cha`, `sa` after `tija`, `gupa`, `kita`, `māna`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§433 (528): Tija gupa kita mānehi kha cha sā vā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m433" description="optionally `kha`, `cha`, `sa` after `tija`, `gupa`, `kita`, `māna`" />
:::

> Tija gupa kita māna iccetehi dhātūhi kha cha sa iccete paccayā parā honti vā.

After the roots `tija`, `gupa`, `kita` and `māna`, the suffixes `kha`, `cha` and `sa` are optionally used.

> Tijato khantiyaṃ khova, nindāyaṃ gupato tu cho; \
> Kitā cho sova mānamhā, vavatthitavibhāsato.

After `tija` only `kha`, in the sense of enduring; after `gupa` `cha`, but in the sense of blame (`nindā`); \
after `kita` `cha`, and after `māna` only `sa`: such is the restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`).

> ‘‘Kvacādivaṇṇānamekassarānaṃ dvebhāvo’’ti dhātvādissa dvibhāvo.

By [§458 (461): Kvacādivaṇṇānamekassarānaṃ dvebhāvo](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m458) the first [syllable] of the root is doubled.

> ‘‘Byañjanantassā’’ti vattamāne –

While “`byañjanantassa`” ("of [a root] ending in a consonant") continues —

### 529 (§473): Ko khe ca (And `k` before `kha`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§473 (529): Ko khe ca" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m473" description="And `k` before `kha`" />

:::note[Analysis]

`Byañjanantassa`, carried into this rule by the lead-in of 528, is the word of [531 (§472): Byañjanantassa co chappaccayesu ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/4-dhatuppaccayanta#r531), which is stated only after this one.

> Dhātvantassa byañjanassa ka kārādeso hoti kha ppaccaye pare.

When the suffix `kha` follows, the final consonant of the root is replaced by the letter `k`.

> Titikkha iti ṭhite –

When `titikkha` stands [thus formed] —

Since the endings `ti` and the rest are prescribed after a root and so would not occur after what is not a root, [the next rule] is stated:

<a name="r530"></a>

### 530 (§455): Dhātuppaccayehi vibhattiyo (the endings come after the root-suffixes) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§455 (530): Dhātuppaccayehi vibhattiyo" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m455" description="the endings come after the root-suffixes" />
:::

> Dhātvatthe niddiṭṭhehi khā dikārita ntehi paccayehi tyā dayo vibhattiyo hontīti pure viya vattamānādayo yojetabbā.

The endings `ti` and the rest are used after the suffixes prescribed in the sense of a root, from `kha` to the `kārita` suffixes; so the `vattamānā` and the other [tenses] are to be applied as before.

> Ativākyaṃ titikkhati, titikkhanti. Kamme titikkhīyati. Tathā titikkhatu, titikkhantu. Titikkheyya, titikkheyyuṃ. Atitikkhi, atitikkhiṃsu. Titikkhissati. Atitikkhissa iccādi.

`Ativākyaṃ titikkhati` ("he endures abuse"), `titikkhanti`; in the passive, `titikkhīyati`. Likewise in the other tenses, and so on:

| 🤟 | ⨀ | ⨂ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `vattamānā` | titikkhati | titikkhanti |
| `vattamānā`, passive | titikkhīyati | — |
| `pañcamī` | titikkhatu | titikkhantu |
| `sattamī` | titikkheyya | titikkheyyuṃ |
| `ajjatanī` | atitikkhi | atitikkhiṃsu |
| `bhavissantī` | titikkhissati | — |
| `kālātipatti` | atitikkhissa | — |

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √tija + kha + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | tij + ~~a~~ + kha + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (ti) + tij + kha + ti            | 461 (§458), 462 (§459) |
| →   | ti + ti(j→k) + kha + ti          | 529 (§473) |
| →   | **titikkhati**                   |      |

|     | √tija + kha + ya + ti (vattamānā, passive 🤟⨀) | rule             |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- | ---------------- |
| →   | titikkha + ya + ti                            | 511 (§521), 461 (§458), 529 (§473) |
| →   | titikkha + (ī) + ya + ti                      | 448 (§442) |
| →   | titikkh + ~~a~~ + ī + ya + ti                 | 67 (§83) |
| →   | **titikkhīyati**                              |                  |

|     | √tija + a + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | tij + ~~a~~ + a + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | t + (i→e) + j + a + ti         | 434 (§485) |
| →   | tej + (a→e) + ti               | 487 (§510) |
| →   | **tejeti**                     |      |

:::

:::tip[Example]

The Dhammapada has the verb in the future:

> `ativākyaṃ titikkhissaṃ, dussīlo hi bahujjano` \
> [(18Dh 23.1: Attadantavatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/23/23.1#345) \
🚻②⨀(ativākya) ⏯👆⨀(√tija + kha + ssaṃ) 🚹①⨀(dussīla) 🔼(hi) 🚹①⨀(bahujjana) \
abuse | I shall endure | of bad conduct | for | the many

:::

> Kha ppaccayābhāve a ppaccayassa e kāro, tejeti, tejati vā, tejanti iccādi.

Without the suffix `kha`, the suffix `a` becomes `e`: `tejeti` ("he sharpens"), or `tejati`; `tejanti`[^42], and so on.

> Gupa gopane, cha ppaccaye dvibhāvo, ‘‘pubbobbhāso’’ti abbhāsasaññā, ‘‘abbhāsassā’’ti vattamāne ‘‘antassivaṇṇākāro vā’’ti abbhāsantassikāro, ‘‘kavaggassa cavaggo’’ti abbhāsaga kārassa ja kāro ca.

`Gupa` in the sense of guarding (`gopana`): when the suffix `cha` is added there is doubling,[^43] by [§459 (462): Pubbo’bbhāso](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m459) [the first syllable] is named `abbhāsa`, and while “`abbhāsassa`” ("of the `abbhāsa`") continues, the final [vowel] of the `abbhāsa` becomes `i` by [§465 (463): Antassivaṇṇākāro vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m465) and the `g` of the `abbhāsa` becomes `j` by [§462 (467): Kavaggassa cavaggo](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m462).

### 531 (§472): Byañjanantassa co chappaccayesu ca (And a final consonant becomes `c` before `cha`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§472 (531): Byañjanantassa co chappaccayesu ca" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m472" description="And a final consonant becomes `c` before `cha`" />
:::

> Dhātvantassa[^44] byañjanassa ca kārādeso hoti cha ppaccayesu paresu. Tato vibhattiyo, kāyaṃ jigucchati, jigucchanti. Sesaṃ purimasamaṃ. Chā bhāve gopeti, gopenti iccādi.

When the suffixes `cha` follow, the final consonant of the root is replaced by the letter `c`. Then the endings: `kāyaṃ jigucchati` ("he is disgusted with the body"), `jigucchanti`; the rest is as before. Without `cha`: `gopeti` ("he guards"), `gopenti`, and so on.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √gupa + cha + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | gup + ~~a~~ + cha + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (gu) + gup + cha + ti            | 461 (§458), 462 (§459) |
| →   | g(u→i) + gup + cha + ti          | 463 (§465) |
| →   | (g→j)i + gup + cha + ti          | 467 (§462) |
| →   | ji + gu(p→c) + cha + ti          | 531 (§472) |
| →   | **jigucchati**                   |      |

|     | √gupa + a + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | gup + ~~a~~ + a + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | g + (u→o) + p + a + ti         | 434 (§485) |
| →   | gop + (a→e) + ti               | 487 (§510) |
| →   | **gopeti**                     |      |

:::

> Kita rogāpanayane, chappaccayo, dvittañca.

`Kita` in the sense of removing disease (`rogāpanayana`): the suffix `cha`, and the doubling.

> Abbhāsa ggahaṇamanuvattate.

The word `abbhāsa` continues.

### 532 (§463): Mānakitānaṃ vatattaṃ vā (Optionally `v`, `t` for [the `abbhāsa` of] `māna`, `kita`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§463 (532): Mānakitānaṃ vatattaṃ vā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m463" description="Optionally `v`, `t` for [the `abbhāsa` of] `māna`, `kita`" />
:::

> Abbhāsagatānaṃ māna kita iccetesaṃ dhātūnaṃ va kārata kārattaṃ hoti vā yathākkamanti ta kāro, dhātvantassa ca kāro, sesaṃ samaṃ. Rogaṃ tikicchati, tikicchanti iccādi. Ta kārābhāve ‘‘kavaggassa cavaggo’’ti ca kāro, vicikicchati, vicikicchanti iccādi.

[The initial] of the roots `māna` and `kita`, when they stand in the `abbhāsa`, optionally becomes `v` and `t` respectively; so here the letter `t`. The final of the root becomes `c`, and the rest is the same: `rogaṃ tikicchati` ("he treats a disease"), `tikicchanti`, and so on. Without the `t`, there is `c` by [§462 (467): Kavaggassa cavaggo](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m462): `vicikicchati` ("he doubts"), `vicikicchanti`, and so on.

| →   | kit + ~~a~~ + cha + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (ki) + kit + cha + ti            | 461 (§458), 462 (§459) |
| →   | (k→t)i + kit + cha + ti          | 532 (§463) |
| →   | ti + ki(t→c) + cha + ti          | 531 (§472) |

|     | vi + √kita + cha + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |

| →   | vi + (k→c)i + kit + cha + ti          | 467 (§462) |
| →   | vici + ki(t→c) + cha + ti             | 531 (§472) |
| →   | **vicikicchati**                      |      |

:::

> Māna vīmaṃsapūjāsu, sappaccayadvibhāvaī kārava kārā.

`Māna` in the senses of investigating (`vīmaṃsā`) and honouring (`pūjā`): the suffix `sa`, the doubling, the `ī` and the `v`.

### 533 (§467): Tato pāmānānaṃ vā maṃ sesu (After it, `vā`, `maṃ` for `pā`, `māna` before `sa`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§467 (533): Tato pāmānānaṃ vā maṃ sesu" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m467" description="After it, `vā`, `maṃ` for `pā`, `māna` before `sa`" />
:::

> Tato abbhāsato parāsaṃ pāmānānaṃ dhātūnaṃ vāmaṃiccete ādesā honti yathākkamaṃ sa ppaccaye pare. Sesū ti bahuvacananiddeso payogepi vacanavipallāsañāpanatthaṃ. Atthaṃ vīmaṃsati, vīmaṃsanti iccādi.

When the suffix `sa` follows, the roots `pā` and `māna` standing after it, the `abbhāsa`, are replaced by `vā` and `maṃ` respectively. The plural statement `sesu` is there to make known that in usage too the number is interchanged (`vacanavipallāsa`). `Atthaṃ vīmaṃsati` ("he investigates the matter"), `vīmaṃsanti`, and so on.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √māna + sa + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | mān + ~~a~~ + sa + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (mā) + mān + sa + ti            | 461 (§458), 462 (§459) |
| →   | m(ā→ī) + mān + sa + ti          | 463 (§465) |
| →   | (m→v)ī + mān + sa + ti          | 532 (§463) |
| →   | vī + (mān→maṃ) + sa + ti        | 533 (§467) |
| →   | **vīmaṃsati**                   |      |

:::

> Aññattha ‘‘lopañcettamakāro’’ti a ppaccayasse kāro, māneti, mānenti.

Elsewhere the suffix `a` becomes `e` by [§510 (487): Lopañcettamakāro](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m510): `māneti` ("he honours"), `mānenti`.

> Bhuja pālanabyavaharaṇesu, bhottumicchatīti atthe –

`Bhuja` in the senses of protecting (`pālana`) and enjoying (`byavaharaṇa`): in the sense `bhottum icchati` ("he wishes to eat") —

> ‘‘Kha cha sā, vā’’ti ca vattate.

“`Kha cha sā`” and “`vā`” also continue.

### 534 (§434): Bhuja ghasa hara su pādīhi tumicchatthesu ([`kha`, `cha`, `sa`] after `bhuja`, `ghasa`, `hara`, `su`, `pā` and the like, in the sense of wishing to) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§434 (534): Bhuja ghasa hara su pādīhi tumicchatthesu" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m434" description="[`kha`, `cha`, `sa`] after `bhuja`, `ghasa`, `hara`, `su`, `pā` and the like, in the sense of wishing to" />
:::

> Bhuja ghasa hara su pā iccevamādīhi dhātūhi tu micchatthesu ca kha cha sa iccete paccayā honti vā. Tumicchānaṃ, tumantayuttaicchāya vā atthā tumicchatthā, tena tumantarahitesu ‘‘bhojanamicchatī’’tiādīsu na honti, ‘‘vuttatthānamappayogo’’ti vākyassa appayogo, dhātvādissa dvebhāve kate ‘‘dutiyacatutthānaṃ paṭhamatatiyā’’ti abbhāsabha kārassa ba kāro, dhātvantassa ‘‘ko khe cā’’ti ka kāro, bubhukkhati, bubhukkhanti iccādi.

After the roots `bhuja`, `ghasa`, `hara`, `su`, `pā` and the like, the suffixes `kha`, `cha` and `sa` are optionally used in the senses of wishing to [do] (`tumicchattha`) as well. The `tumicchattha` are the senses (`attha`) of `tuṃ` and of wishing (`icchā`), or the senses of a wish joined to a form in `tuṃ`; so where there is no form in `tuṃ`, as in `bhojanam icchati` ("he wants food"), the suffixes are not used. By [the maxim] `vuttatthānam appayogo` ("words whose meaning has been expressed are not used") the sentence [`bhottuṃ icchati`] is not used. Once the first [syllable] of the root has been doubled, the `bh` of the `abbhāsa` becomes `b` by [§461 (464): Dutiyacatutthānaṃ paṭhamatatiyā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m461) and the final of the root becomes `k` by [529 (§473): Ko khe ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/4-dhatuppaccayanta#r529): `bubhukkhati` ("he wishes to eat"), `bubhukkhanti`, and so on.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √bhuja + kha + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | bhuj + ~~a~~ + kha + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (bhu) + bhuj + kha + ti           | 461 (§458), 462 (§459) |
| →   | (bh→b)u + bhuj + kha + ti         | 464 (§461) |
| →   | bu + bhu(j→k) + kha + ti          | 529 (§473) |
| →   | **bubhukkhati**                   |      |

|     | √ghasa + cha + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | ghas + ~~a~~ + cha + ti           | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (gha) + ghas + cha + ti           | 461 (§458), 462 (§459) |
| →   | (gh→g)a + ghas + cha + ti         | 464 (§461) |
| →   | (g→j)a + ghas + cha + ti          | 467 (§462) |
| →   | j(a→i) + ghas + cha + ti          | 463 (§465) |
| →   | ji + gha(s→c) + cha + ti          | 531 (§472) |
| →   | **jighacchati**                   |      |

:::

> Vā ti kimatthaṃ? Bhottumicchati, icchatthesū ti kimatthaṃ? Bhottuṃ gacchati.

What is `vā` for? So that `bhottum icchati` ("he wishes to eat") [stands as well]. What is `icchatthesu` ("in the senses of wishing") for? To exclude `bhottuṃ gacchati` ("he goes to eat").

> Ghasa adane, ghasitumicchatīti atthe chappaccayo, dvittaṃ, tatiya ca vagga ikāra ca kārādesā, jighacchati, jighacchanti.

`Ghasa` in the sense of eating (`adana`): in the sense `ghasitum icchati` ("he wishes to eat") the suffix `cha`, the doubling, and the replacements by the third [letter of the group], by the `c` group, by `i` and by `c`: `jighacchati` ("he is hungry"), `jighacchanti`.

> Hara haraṇe, haritumicchatīti atthe sa ppaccayo.

`Hara` in the sense of taking (`haraṇa`): in the sense `haritum icchati` ("he wishes to take"), the suffix `sa`.

### 535 (§474): Harassa gī se (`gī` for `hara` before `sa`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§474 (535): Harassa gīse" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m474" description="`gī` for `hara` before `sa`" />

> Hara iccetassa dhātussa sabbassa gī hoti se paccaye pare. ‘‘Gīse’’ti yogavibhāgena jissapi, ṭhānūpacārenādesassāpi dhātuvohārattā dvittaṃ, bhikkhaṃ jigīsati, jigīsanti.

When the suffix `sa` follows, the whole of the root `hara` becomes `gī`.[^45] By splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) to read `gī se` [on its own], [`gī`] replaces `ji` as well. Since a replacement too is treated as the root, by transfer of its original's status (`ṭhānūpacāra`), the doubling [applies to `gī`]: `bhikkhaṃ jigīsati` ("he wishes to get alms"), `jigīsanti`.

`Su` in the sense of hearing (`savaṇa`): `sotum icchati` ("he wishes to hear") is `sussūsati`, `sussūsanti`; the lengthening is by [§517] `Kvaci dhātu…` and the rest.

> Pā pāne, pātumicchatīti atthe sa ppaccayadvittarassattai kārādesā, ‘‘tato pāmānānaṃ vāmaṃ sesū’’ti vā deso, pivāsati, pivāsanti iccādi.

`Ji` in the sense of conquering (`jaya`): `vijetum icchati` ("he wishes to conquer") is `vijigīsati`, and so on.

> Saṅgho pabbatamiva attānamācarati, pabbato iva ācaratīti vā atthe –

In the sense `saṅgho pabbatam iva attānam ācarati` ("the Saṅgha conducts itself like a mountain"), or `pabbato iva ācarati` ("it behaves like a mountain") —

### 536 (§435): Āya nāmato kattupamānādācāre (`āya` after a noun to which the agent is likened, in the sense of behaving) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§435 (536): Āya nāmato kattūpamānādācāre" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m435" description="`āya` after a noun to which the agent is likened, in the sense of behaving" />
:::

> Ācaraṇakriyāya kattuno upamānabhūtamhā nāmato āya ppaccayo hoti ācāratthe. Upamīyati etenāti upamānaṃ, kattuno upamānaṃ kattupamānaṃ, ‘‘vuttatthānamappayogo’’ti iva saddanivatti, dhātuppaccayantattā ‘‘tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā cā’’ti sutte tesaṃ gahaṇena vibhattilopo, ‘‘pakati cassa sarantassā’’ti pakatibhāvo, saralopādi, ‘‘dhātuppaccayehi vibhattiyo’’ti vibhattuppatti, pabbatāyati saṅgho, evaṃ samuddamiva attānamācarati samuddāyati, cicciṭamiva attānamācarati cicciṭāyati saddo. Evaṃ dhūmāyati.

The suffix `āya` is used in the sense of behaving (`ācāra`) after a noun that is the standard of comparison (`upamāna`) for the agent of the action of behaving. An `upamāna` is that by which [something] is compared (`upamīyati`), and the `upamāna` of the agent is the `kattupamāna`. By [the maxim] `vuttatthānam appayogo` the word `iva` is dropped. Because [the lemma] ends in a root-suffix, these [lemmas] are included in the word `tesaṃ` of the rule [§317 (332): Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa#m317), and so the inflection [of the noun] is elided; by [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](/kaccayana/4-samasakappa#m318) [the lemma] keeps its natural form; the elision of the vowel and the rest [follow]; and by [530 (§455): Dhātuppaccayehi vibhattiyo](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/4-dhatuppaccayanta#r530) the endings arise: `pabbatāyati saṅgho` ("the Saṅgha behaves like a mountain"). Likewise `samuddam iva attānam ācarati` ("it conducts itself like the ocean") is `samuddāyati`, and `cicciṭam iva attānam ācarati` ("it behaves like [the sound] cicciṭa") is `cicciṭāyati saddo` ("the sound sizzles"). Likewise `dhūmāyati` ("it smokes").

| --- | ---------------------------------------- | ---- |

> ‘‘Nāmato, ācāre’’ti ca vattate.

“`Nāmato`” ("after a noun") and “`ācāre`” ("in the sense of behaving") also continue.

### 537 (§436): Īyūpamānā ca (and `īya` after a noun that is a standard of comparison) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§436 (537): Īyūpamānā ca" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m436" description="and `īya` after a noun that is a standard of comparison" />

> Upamānabhūtā nāmato īya ppaccayo hoti ācāratthe. Puna upamāna ggahaṇaṃ kattu ggahaṇanivattanatthaṃ, tena kammatopi sijjhati, sesaṃ samaṃ. Achattaṃ chattamivācarati chattīyati, aputtaṃ puttamivācarati puttīyati sissamācariyo.

The suffix `īya` is used in the sense of behaving after a noun that is a standard of comparison. The word `upamāna` is mentioned again to stop the word `kattu` (agent) [from continuing]; hence [the suffix] is obtained after [a noun to which] the object [is compared] as well. The rest is the same. `Achattaṃ chattam iva ācarati` ("he treats what is not an umbrella as an umbrella") is `chattīyati`, and `aputtaṃ puttam iva ācarati` ("he treats one who is not a son as a son") is `puttīyati sissam ācariyo` ("the teacher treats the pupil as a son").

| →   | chatt + (~~a~~ + ī)ya + ti              | 67 (§83) |

By saying `upamānā` ("after a standard of comparison") we exclude `dhammam ācarati` ("he practises the Dhamma"); by saying `ācāre` ("in the sense of behaving") we exclude `achattaṃ chattam iva rakkhati` ("he guards what is not an umbrella as an umbrella").

### 540 (§438): Dhātūhi ṇe ṇaya ṇāpe ṇāpayā kāritāni hetvatthe (the causative suffixes `ṇe`, `ṇaya`, `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` after roots, named `kārita`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§438 (540): Dhātūhi ṇe ṇaya ṇāpe ṇāpayā kāritāni hetvatthe" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m438" description="the causative suffixes `ṇe`, `ṇaya`, `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` after roots, named `kārita`" />
:::

> Sabbehi dhātūhi hetvatthe abhidheyye ṇe ṇayaṇāpe ṇāpaya iccete paccayā parā honti, te kāritasaññā ca honti. Hetuyeva attho hetvattho, so ca ‘‘yo kāreti sa hetū’’ti laddhahetusañño suddhakattuno payojako hetukattā, atthato pesanajjhesanādiko payojakabyāpāro idha hetu nāma.

After all roots, when the sense of the causative agent (`hetu`) is to be expressed, the suffixes `ṇe`, `ṇaya`, `ṇāpe` and `ṇāpaya` are used, and they are named `kārita` (causative). The `hetvattha` is the sense (`attha`) that is the `hetu` itself, and that [`hetu`] is the causative agent (`hetukattā`), the one who sets the plain agent (`suddhakattā`) to work and who has received the name `hetu` by [§282 (295): Yo kāreti sa hetu](/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m282); in substance, what is here called `hetu` is the employer's activity — commanding, requesting and the like.

> Ettha ca –

And on this [point] —

> Ṇe ṇayāva uvaṇṇantā, āto dve pacchimā siyuṃ; \
> Sesato caturo dve vā, vāsaddassānuvattito.

After [roots] ending in `u` or `ū` only `ṇe` and `ṇaya`; after [roots ending in] `ā` the last two; \
after the rest all four, or two, because the word `vā` continues.

> Akammā dhātavo honti, kārite tu sakammakā; \
> Sakammakā dvikammāssu, dvikammā tu tikammakā.

Roots without an object become, in the causative, roots with an object; \
those with one object take two, and those with two objects three.

> Tasmā kattari kamme ca, kāritākhyātasambhavo; \
> Na bhāve suddhakattā ca, kārite kammasaññito.

So a causative verb can be formed in the active and in the passive, \
but not in the impersonal; and in the causative the plain agent is termed the object.

> Niyādīnaṃ padhānañca, appadhānaṃ duhādinaṃ; \
> Kārite suddhakattā ca, kammamākhyātagocaranti.

For `nī` and the like the principal [object], for `duha` and the like the secondary one, \
and in the causative the plain agent, is the object within the verb's range.[^46]

> Tattha yo koci bhavati, tamañño ‘‘bhavāhi bhavāhi’’ iccevaṃ bravīti, atha vā bhavantaṃ bhavituṃ samatthaṃ payojayati, bhavituṃ payojetīti vā atthe iminā ṇeṇaya ppaccayā, kāritasaññā ca, ‘‘vuttatthānamappayogo’’ti vākyassa appayogo, ‘‘kāritānaṃ ṇo lopa’’nti ṇa lopo, ‘‘asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite’’ti ū kārasso kāro vuddhi.

Here: someone is (`bhavati`), and another says to him "be! be!" (`bhavāhi bhavāhi`), or else sets to work one who is and is able to be, or sets [him] to be — in this sense, by this rule the suffixes `ṇe` and `ṇaya` [are added to `bhū`] and named `kārita`; by [the maxim] `vuttatthānam appayogo` the sentence is not used; the `ṇ` is elided by [526 (§523): Kāritānaṃ ṇo lopaṃ](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r526); and by [527 (§483): Asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r527) the `ū` becomes `o`, which is the `vuddhi`.

> ‘‘O, e’’ti ca vattate, dhātu ggahaṇañca.

“`O`” and “`e`” also continue, and so does the word `dhātu` (root).

### 543 (§486): Guha dusānaṃ dīghaṃ (lengthening for `guha`, `dusa` before the causative) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§486 (543): Guha dusānaṃ dīghaṃ" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m486" description="lengthening for `guha`, `dusa` before the causative" />
:::

> Guhadusa iccetesaṃ dhātūnaṃ saro dīgha māpajjate kārite pare, vuddhāpavādoyaṃ.

When a `kārita` suffix follows, the vowel of the roots `guha` and `dusa` is lengthened; this is an exception ([`apavāda`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) to the `vuddhi`.

> Guha saṃvaraṇe, guhituṃ payojayati gūhayati, gūhayanti. Dusa appītimhi, dussantaṃ payojayati dūsayati, dūsayanti iccādi.

`Guha` in the sense of concealing[^50] (`saṃvaraṇa`): "he sets [someone] to hide" is `gūhayati`, `gūhayanti`. `Dusa` in the sense of displeasure (`appīti`): "he sets to work one who is offending" is `dūsayati` ("he corrupts"), `dūsayanti`, and so on.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √guha + ṇaya + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | guh + ~~a~~ + ṇaya + ti          | 511 (§521) |
| →   | guh + ~~ṇ~~aya + ti              | 526 (§523) |
| →   | g + (u→ū) + h + aya + ti         | 543 (§486) |
| →   | **gūhayati**                     |      |

|     | √dusa + ṇaya + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | dus + ~~a~~ + ṇaya + ti          | 511 (§521) |
| →   | dus + ~~ṇ~~aya + ti              | 526 (§523) |
| →   | d + (u→ū) + s + aya + ti         | 543 (§486) |
| →   | **dūsayati**                     |      |

:::

> Tathā icchantaṃ payojayati icchāpeti, icchāpayati, eseti, esayati. Niyacchantaṃ payojayati niyāmeti, niyāmayati. Āsantaṃ payojayati āseti, āsayati, acchāpeti, acchāpayati. Labhantaṃ payojayati lābheti, lābhayati. Vacantaṃ payojayati vāceti, vācayati, vācāpeti, vācāpayati. Evaṃ vāseti, vāsayati, vāsāpeti, vāsāpayati. Vāheti, vāhayati, vāhāpeti, vāhāpayati. Jīreti, jīrayati, jīrāpeti, jīrāpayati. Māreti, mārayati, mārāpeti, mārāpayati. Dasseti, dassayati iccādi.

Likewise:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `isu` (`icchati`) | `icchantaṃ` — one wishing | eseti, esayati | icchāpeti, icchāpayati |
| `yamu` with `ni` (`niyacchati`) | `niyacchantaṃ` — one restraining | niyāmeti, niyāmayati | — |
| `āsa` (`acchati`) | `āsantaṃ` — one sitting | āseti[^51], āsayati | acchāpeti, acchāpayati |
| `labha` | `labhantaṃ` — one obtaining | lābheti, lābhayati | — |
| `vaca` | `vacantaṃ` — one speaking | vāceti, vācayati | vācāpeti, vācāpayati |
| `vasa` | likewise | vāseti, vāsayati | vāsāpeti, vāsāpayati |
| `vaha` | | vāheti, vāhayati | vāhāpeti, vāhāpayati |
| `jara` (`jīrati`) | | jīreti, jīrayati | jīrāpeti, jīrāpayati |
| `mara` | | māreti, mārayati | mārāpeti, mārāpayati |
| `disa` | | dasseti, dassayati | — |

> Tathā tudantaṃ payojayati todeti, todayati, todāpeti, todāpayati. Pavisantaṃ payojayati, pavisituṃ vā paveseti, pavesayati, pavesāpeti, pavesāpayati. Uddisantaṃ payojayati uddisāpeti, uddisāpayati. Pahontaṃ payojayati pahāveti, pahāvayati. Sayantaṃ payojayati sāyeti, sāyayati, sāyāpeti, sāyāpayati. Ettha e kārassa āyā deso, sayāpeti, sayāpayati, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ṇāpeṇāpayesu āyā desassa rassattaṃ. Nayantaṃ payojayati nayāpeti, nayāpayati. Patiṭṭhantaṃ payojayati patiṭṭhāpeti, patiṭṭhāpayati, patiṭṭhapeti vā.

Likewise:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tuda` | `tudantaṃ` — one striking | todeti, todayati | todāpeti, todāpayati |
| `visa` with `pa` | `pavisantaṃ`, or `pavisituṃ` — one entering, or to enter | paveseti, pavesayati | pavesāpeti, pavesāpayati |
| `disa` with `u` (`uddisati`) | `uddisantaṃ` — one reciting | — | uddisāpeti, uddisāpayati |
| `hū` with `pa` (`pahoti`) | `pahontaṃ` — one who is able | pahāveti[^52], pahāvayati | — |
| `sī` (`sayati`) | `sayantaṃ` — one lying down | sāyeti, sāyayati | sāyāpeti, sāyāpayati; sayāpeti, sayāpayati |
| `nī` (`nayati`) | `nayantaṃ` — one leading | — | nayāpeti, nayāpayati |
| `ṭhā` with `pati` | `patiṭṭhantaṃ` — one standing firm | — | patiṭṭhāpeti, patiṭṭhāpayati, or patiṭṭhapeti |

Here the `e` is replaced by `āya`; in `sayāpeti`, `sayāpayati` the replacement `āya` is shortened before `ṇāpe` and `ṇāpaya` by [§517] `Kvaci dhātu…` and the rest.

> Hanantaṃ payojayatīti atthe ṇeṇayā dayo.

In the sense "he sets to work one who is striking", `ṇe`, `ṇaya` and the rest [are added to `hana`].[^53]

> ‘‘Ṇamhī’’ti vattate.

“`Ṇamhi`” ("before a suffix marked with `ṇ`") continues.

### 544 (§591): Hanassa ghāto (`ghāta` for `hana` before a `ṇ` suffix) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§591 (544): Hanassa ghāto" href="/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m591" description="`ghāta` for `hana` before a `ṇ` suffix" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

This rule stands in Kaccāyana's Kibbidhāna, [§591 (544): Hanassa ghāto](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m591), with `ṇamhi` carried from [§590 (579): Ṇamhiranjassa jo bhāvakaraṇesu](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m590); Buddhappiya reads that `ṇamhi` of the `kārita` suffixes and places the rule here.

:::

> Hana iccetassa dhātussa ghātā deso hoti ṇa kāravati kāritappaccaye pare. Ghāteti, ghātayati, ghātāpeti, ghātāpayati, ‘‘vadho vā sabbatthā’’ti vadhā dese vadheti, vadhāpeti.

When a `kārita` suffix carrying `ṇ` follows, the root `hana` is replaced by `ghāta`: `ghāteti` ("he has [someone] killed"), `ghātayati`, `ghātāpeti`, `ghātāpayati`; and with the replacement `vadha` by [§592 (503): Vadho vā sabbattha](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m592), `vadheti`, `vadhāpeti`.

:::tip[Derivation]

|     | √hana + ṇe + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | han + ~~a~~ + ṇe + ti          | 511 (§521) |
| →   | (han→ghāta) + ṇe + ti          | 544 (§591) |
| →   | ghāta + ~~ṇ~~e + ti            | 526 (§523) |
| →   | ghāt + (~~a~~ + e) + ti        | 67 (§83) |
| →   | **ghāteti**                    |      |

|     | √hana + ṇāpe + ti (vattamānā 🤟⨀) | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | (han→vadh) + ṇāpe + ti            | 511 (§521), 503 (§592) |
| →   | vadh + ~~ṇ~~āpe + ti              | 526 (§523) |
| →   | **vadhāpeti**                     |      |

:::

> Juhontaṃ payojayati juhāveti, juhāvayati. Jahantaṃ payojayati jahāpeti, jahāpayati, hāpeti, hāpayati. Dadantaṃ payojayati dāpeti, dāpayati. Pidahantaṃ payojayati pidhāpeti, pidhāpayati, pidahāpeti, pidahāpayati.

The `juhoti` group:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `hu` (`juhoti`) | `juhontaṃ` — one offering | juhāveti[^54], juhāvayati | — |
| `hā` (`jahāti`) | `jahantaṃ` — one abandoning | — | jahāpeti, jahāpayati; hāpeti, hāpayati |
| `dā` (`dadāti`) | `dadantaṃ` — one giving | — | dāpeti, dāpayati |
| `dhā` with `pi` (`pidahati`) | `pidahantaṃ` — one closing | — | pidhāpeti, pidhāpayati; pidahāpeti, pidahāpayati |

> Rundhantaṃ payojayati rodheti, rodhayati, rodhāpeti, rodhāpayati. Chindantaṃ payojayati chedeti, chedayati, chedāpeti, chedāpayati. Yuñjantaṃ payojayati yojeti, yojayati, yojāpeti, yojāpayati. Bhuñjantaṃ payojayati bhojeti, bhojayati, bhojāpeti, bhojāpayati. Muñcantaṃ payojayati moceti, mocayati, mocāpeti, mocāpayati.

The `rudha` class:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `rudha` | `rundhantaṃ` — one obstructing | rodheti, rodhayati | rodhāpeti, rodhāpayati |
| `chidi` | `chindantaṃ` — one cutting | chedeti, chedayati | chedāpeti, chedāpayati |
| `yuja` | `yuñjantaṃ` — one yoking | yojeti, yojayati | yojāpeti, yojāpayati |
| `bhuja` | `bhuñjantaṃ` — one eating | bhojeti, bhojayati | bhojāpeti, bhojāpayati |
| `muca` | `muñcantaṃ` — one releasing | moceti, mocayati | mocāpeti, mocāpayati |

> Dibbantaṃ payojayati deveti, devayati. Uppajjantaṃ payojayati uppādeti, uppādayati. Bujjhantaṃ payojayati bodheti, bodhayati. ‘‘Dādhāntato yo kvacī’’ti ya kārāgamo, bujjhāpeti, bujjhāpayati. Tussantaṃ payojayati toseti, tosayati, tosāpeti, tosāpayati. Sammantaṃ payojayati sameti, samayati, ghaṭādittā na vuddhi. Kuppantaṃ payojayati kopeti, kopayati. Jāyantaṃ payojayati janeti, janayati, ghaṭādittā na vuddhi.

The `diva` class:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `divu` | `dibbantaṃ` — one playing | deveti, devayati | — |
| `pada` with `u` | `uppajjantaṃ` — one arising | uppādeti, uppādayati | — |
| `budha` | `bujjhantaṃ` — one understanding | bodheti, bodhayati | bujjhāpeti, bujjhāpayati |
| `tusa` | `tussantaṃ` — one rejoicing | toseti, tosayati | tosāpeti, tosāpayati |
| `samu` | `sammantaṃ` — one becoming calm | sameti, samayati | — |
| `kupa` | `kuppantaṃ` — one becoming angry | kopeti, kopayati | — |
| `jana` | `jāyantaṃ` — one being born | janeti, janayati | — |

In `bujjhāpeti`, `bujjhāpayati` the `y` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) by [§606 (642): Dadhantato yo kvaci](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/4-catutthakanda#m606). `Sameti`, `samayati` and `janeti`, `janayati` have no `vuddhi`, because the roots belong to the `ghaṭādi` [group].

> Suṇantaṃ payojayati dhammaṃ sāveti, sāvayati. Pāpuṇantaṃ payojayati pāpeti, pāpayati.

The `su` class:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `su` | `suṇantaṃ` — one hearing: `dhammaṃ sāveti`, he makes the Dhamma heard | sāveti, sāvayati |
| `apa` with `pa` (`pāpuṇāti`) | `pāpuṇantaṃ` — one attaining | pāpeti, pāpayati |

> Vikkiṇantaṃ payojayati vikkāyāpeti, vikkāyāpayati. Jinantaṃ payojayati jayāpeti, jayāpayati. Jānantaṃ payojayati ñāpeti, ñāpayati. Gaṇhantaṃ payojayati gāheti, gāhayati, gāhāpeti, gāhāpayati, gaṇhāpeti, gaṇhāpayati.

The `kī` class:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `kī` with `vi` | `vikkiṇantaṃ` — one selling | — | vikkāyāpeti, vikkāyāpayati |
| `ji` | `jinantaṃ` — one conquering | — | jayāpeti, jayāpayati |
| `ñā` | `jānantaṃ` — one knowing | — | ñāpeti, ñāpayati |
| `gaha` | `gaṇhantaṃ` — one taking | gāheti, gāhayati | gāhāpeti, gāhāpayati; gaṇhāpeti, gaṇhāpayati |

> Vitanantaṃ payojayati vitāneti, vitānayati. Yo koci karoti, tamañño ‘‘karohi karohi’’iccevaṃ bravīti, karontaṃ payojayati, kātuṃ vā kāreti, kārayati, kārāpeti, kārāpayati iccādi.

The `tanu` class:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `tanu` with `vi` | `vitanantaṃ` — one spreading out | vitāneti, vitānayati | — |
| `kara` | someone does, and another says to him "do! do!" (`karohi karohi`), or sets to work one who is doing, or [sets him] to do | kāreti, kārayati | kārāpeti, kārāpayati |

> Corentaṃ payojayati corāpeti, corāpayati. Cintentaṃ payojayati cintāpeti, cintāpayati, pūjentaṃ payojayati pūjāpeti, pūjāpayati iccādi. Sabbattha subodhaṃ.

The `cura` class:

| root | `… payojayati` | `ṇāpe`, `ṇāpaya` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cura` | `corentaṃ` — one stealing | corāpeti, corāpayati |
| `cinta` | `cintentaṃ` — one thinking | cintāpeti, cintāpayati |
| `pūja` | `pūjentaṃ` — one honouring | pūjāpeti, pūjāpayati |

Everywhere [the forms are] easily understood.

> Dhātuppaccayato cāpi, kāritappaccayā siyuṃ; \
> Sakāritehi yuṇvūnaṃ, dassanañcettha ñāpakaṃ.

The `kārita` suffixes may come after a root-suffix as well; \
and the indication (`ñāpaka`) of this here is that `yu` and `ṇvu` are seen [prescribed] "together with the causatives".

> Tena titikkhantaṃ payojayati titikkheti, titikkhāpeti. Tikicchantaṃ payojayati tikiccheti, tikicchayati, tikicchāpeti, tikicchāpayati. Evaṃ bubhukkheti, bubhukkhayati, bubhukkhāpeti, bubhukkhāpayati, pabbatāyantaṃ payojayati pabbatāyayati. Puttīyayati iccādipi siddhaṃ bhavati.

Hence "he sets to work one who is enduring" is `titikkheti`, `titikkhāpeti`; "he sets to work one who is treating" is `tikiccheti`, `tikicchayati`, `tikicchāpeti`, `tikicchāpayati`; likewise `bubhukkheti`, `bubhukkhayati`, `bubhukkhāpeti`, `bubhukkhāpayati`; "he sets to work one who behaves like a mountain" is `pabbatāyayati`; and `puttīyayati` and the like are established as well.

---

> _Dhātuppaccayantanayo._

The section on [verbs] ending in a root-suffix [is finished].

---

> Sāsanatthaṃ samuddiṭṭhaṃ, ākhyātaṃ sakabuddhiyā; \
> Bāhusaccabalenīdaṃ, cintayantu vicakkhaṇā.

For the sake of the Teaching, the verb has been set out according to my own understanding; \
let the discerning think it through by the strength of their wide learning.

> Bhavati tiṭṭhati seti, ahosi evamādayo; \
> Akammakāti viññeyyā, kammalakkhaṇaviññunā.

`Bhavati`, `tiṭṭhati`, `seti`, `ahosi` and the like \
are to be known as [verbs] without an object by one who knows the mark of the object.

> Akammakāpi hetvattha-ppaccayantā sakammakā; \
> Taṃ yathā bhikkhu bhāveti, maggaṃ rāgādidūsakanti.

Even [roots] without an object take an object when they end in a suffix with the causative sense; \
for example, "the bhikkhu develops the path that destroys passion and the rest".

---

> _Iti padarūpasiddhiyaṃ ākhyātakaṇḍo chaṭṭho._

Thus ends the sixth section of the Padarūpasiddhi, the Ākhyātakaṇḍa (the verb section).
