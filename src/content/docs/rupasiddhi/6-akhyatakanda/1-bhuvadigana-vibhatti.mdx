## Bhūvādigaṇa (The `bhū` class)

## Vibhattividhāna (The endings)

> Atha ākhyātavibhattiyo kriyāvācīhi dhātūhi parā vuccante.

Now the verbal inflections (`ākhyātavibhatti`), which come after the roots that express action, are stated.

> Tattha kriyaṃ ācikkhatīti ākhyātaṃ, kriyāpadaṃ. Vuttañhi ‘‘kālakārakapurisaparidīpakaṃ kriyālakkhaṇamākhyātika’’nti. Tattha kālo ti atītādayo, kāraka miti kammakattubhāvā, purisā ti paṭhamamajjhimuttamā, kriyā ti gamanapacanādiko dhātvattho, kriyālakkhaṇaṃ saññāṇaṃ etassāti kriyālakkhaṇaṃ, atiliṅgañca.

Here the `ākhyāta` (verb) is so called because it declares (`ācikkhati`) an action: it is the action-word (`kriyāpada`). For it is said: "The verbal word is that which has action as its mark and which brings to light time, `kāraka` and person." There "time" means the past and the rest; "`kāraka`" means the object, the agent and the bare action (`kamma`, `kattā`, `bhāva`); "persons" means the first, the middle and the highest; "action" means the meaning of a root, such as going or cooking; "having action as its mark" means that action is its distinguishing sign (`saññāṇa`); and it is beyond gender (`atiliṅga`).

> Vuttampi cetaṃ –

And this too has been said:

> ‘‘Yaṃ tikālaṃ tipurisaṃ, kriyāvāci tikārakaṃ; \
> Atiliṅgaṃ dvivacanaṃ, tadākhyātanti vuccatī’’ti.

That which has three times and three persons, expresses action and has three `kāraka`; \
which is beyond gender and has two numbers — that is called `ākhyāta`.

> Kālādivasena dhātvatthaṃ vibhajantīti vibhattiyo, tyādayo, tā pana vattamānā pañcamī sattamī parokkhāhiyyattanī ajjatanī bhavissantī kālātipatti cāti aṭṭhavidhā bhavanti.

They are called `vibhatti` (inflections) because they divide up (`vibhajanti`) the meaning of the root according to time and the rest: `ti` and the others. And they are of eight kinds: `vattamānā`, `pañcamī`, `sattamī`, `parokkhā`, `hiyyattanī`, `ajjatanī`, `bhavissantī` and `kālātipatti`.

> Kriyaṃ dhārentīti dhātavo, bhūvādayo, khādidhātuppaccayantā ca, te pana atthavasā dvidhā bhavanti sakammakā, akammakā cāti. Tatra sakammakā ye dhātavokammāpekkhaṃ kriyaṃ vadanti, yathā – kaṭaṃ karoti, gāmaṃ gacchati, odanaṃ pacatītiādayo, akammakā ye kammanirapekkhaṃ kriyaṃ vadanti, yathā – acchati, seti, tiṭṭhatītiādayo.

They are called `dhātu` (roots) because they bear (`dhārenti`) an action: `bhū` and the rest, and also those that end in the root-suffixes `kha` and the like. And by their meaning they are of two kinds: transitive (`sakammaka`, "having an object") and intransitive (`akammaka`). Of these the transitive are the roots that express an action which looks to an object, as `kaṭaṃ karoti` (he makes a mat), `gāmaṃ gacchati` (he goes to the village), `odanaṃ pacati` (he cooks rice) and the like; the intransitive are those that express an action independent of an object, as `acchati` (he sits), `seti` (he lies), `tiṭṭhati` (he stands) and the like.

> Te pana sattavidhā bhavanti vikaraṇappaccayabhedena, kathaṃ? A vikaraṇā bhūvādayo. Niggahītapubbakaa vikaraṇā rudhādayo, ya vikaraṇā divādayo, ṇuṇā uṇā vikaraṇā svādayo, nāppaṇhā vikaraṇā kiyādayo, oyira vikaraṇā tanādayo, sakatthe ṇe ṇaya ntā curādayoti.

They are further of seven kinds, by the difference of their class suffix (`vikaraṇapaccaya`). How? `bhū` and the rest have the class suffix `a`; `rudha` and the rest have `a` preceded by a niggahīta; `divu` and the rest have `ya`; `su` and the rest have `ṇu`, `ṇā` and `uṇā`; `kī` and the rest have `nā`, `ppa` and `ṇhā`; `tanu` and the rest have `o` and `yira`; `cura` and the rest end in `ṇe` and `ṇaya` in the root's own sense.

| class | class suffix | rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bhūvādi` | `a` | [433 (§445): Bhūvādito a](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/1-bhuvadigana-vibhatti#r433) |
| `rudhādi` | `a`, with a niggahīta before the root's final | [509 (§446): Rudhādito niggahītapubbañca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r509) |
| `divādi` | `ya` | [510 (§447): Divādito yo](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r510) |
| `svādi` | `ṇu`, `ṇā`, `uṇā` | [512 (§448): Svādito ṇuṇāuṇā ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r512) |
| `kiyādi` | `nā`, `ppa`, `ṇhā` | [513 (§449): Kiyādito nā](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r513), [517 (§450): Gahāditoppaṇhā](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r517) |
| `tanādi` | `o`, `yira` | [520 (§451): Tanādito oyirā](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r520) |
| `curādi` | `ṇe`, `ṇaya` | [525 (§452): Curādito ṇeṇayā](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r525) |

Kaccāyana counts eight classes ([§445 (433): Bhūvādito a](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m445) to [§452 (525): Curādito, ṇe ṇayā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m452)); Buddhappiya folds the `gaha` class, whose suffixes are `ppa` and `ṇhā`, into the `kī` class, and so has seven. The order of his chapter follows this list: the endings and the whole conjugation on the `bhū` class first (424–508), then each of the other classes with its model root.

> Tattha paṭhamaṃ a vikaraṇesu bhūvādīsu dhātūsu paṭhamabhūtā akammakā bhū iccetasmā dhātuto tyā dayo parā yojīyante.

Among these, first, the inflections `ti` and the rest are added after the intransitive root `bhū`, which stands first among the roots of the `bhū` class, whose class suffix is `a`.

> Bhū sattāyaṃ, ‘‘bhū’’iccayaṃ dhātu sattāyamatthe vattate, kriyāsāmaññabhūte bhavane vattateti attho.

"`bhū` in the sense of being" [says the root-list]: the root `bhū` occurs in the sense of being (`sattā`), that is, it occurs in being (`bhavana`), which is action in general.

> ‘‘Bhū’’iti ṭhite –

When `bhū` stands [ready for derivation] —

<a name="r424"></a>

### 424 (§457): Bhūvādayo dhātavo (`bhū` and the rest are `dhātu`, roots) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§457 (424): Bhūvādayo dhātavo" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m457" description="`bhū` and the rest are `dhātu`, roots" />
:::

> Bhū iccevamādayo ye kriyāvācino saddagaṇā, te dhātu saññā honti. Bhū ādi yesaṃ te bhūvādayo, atha vā bhūvā ādī pakārā yesaṃ te bhūvādayo.

The groups of words that express action and begin with `bhū` are named `dhātu` (roots). `bhūvādayo` means those of which `bhū` is the first, or else those whose types (`pakāra`) are `bhū`, `vā` and the like.

> Bhūvādīsu vakāroyaṃ, ñeyyo āgamasandhijo; \
> Bhūvāppakārā vā dhātū, sakammākammakatthato.

In `bhūvādi` this letter `v` should be understood as born of sandhi by insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)); \
or else the roots are of the types `bhū` and `vā`, by way of their transitive and intransitive meanings.

> ‘‘Kvaci dhātū’’tiādito ‘‘kvacī’’ti vattate.

“`kvaci`” ("in some places") continues from [488 (§517): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/2-vikaranavidhana#r488).

### 425 (§521): Dhātussanto loponekassarassa (the final of a polysyllabic root is elided) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§521 (425): Dhātussanto lopo’nekasarassa" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m521" description="the final of a polysyllabic root is elided" />
:::

> Anekassarassa dhātussa anto kvaci lopo hoti.

The final of a root that has more than one vowel is elided in some places (`kvaci`).

> Kvaci ggahaṇaṃ ‘‘mahīyati samatho’’tiādīsu nivattanatthaṃ, iti anekassarattābhāvā idha dhātvantalopo na hoti.

The mention of `kvaci` is there to stop [the elision] in `mahīyati` ("he is honoured"), `samatho` ("calm") and the like; so here, since [`bhū`] does not have more than one vowel, the root's final is not elided.

> Tato dhātvādhikāravihitānekappaccayappasaṅge ‘‘vatticchānupubbikā saddappaṭipattī’’ti katvā vattamānavacanicchāyaṃ –

After that, since the many suffixes prescribed under the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) `dhātu` would apply, it is on the principle that "the formation of words follows the speaker's wish" (`vatticchā`) that, when the present is to be expressed —

<a name="r426"></a>

### 426 (§423): Vattamānā tianti, sitha, mima, teante, sevhe, emhe (`vattamānā`: the twelve endings of the present) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§423 (426): Vattamānā ti anti, si tha, mi ma, te ante, se vhe, e mhe" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m423" description="`vattamānā`: the twelve endings of the present" />
:::

> Tyā dayo dvādasa vattamānāsaññā hontīti tyādīnaṃ vattamānatthavisayattā vattamānā saññā.

The twelve [endings] `ti` and the rest are named `vattamānā`, and they are so named because their domain (`visaya`) is the sense of the present (`vattamāna`).

### 428 (§414): Vattamānā paccuppanne (The `vattamānā` in the present) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§414 (428): Vattamānā paccuppanne" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m414" description="The `vattamānā` in the present" />
:::

> Paccuppanne kāle gamyamāne vattamānāvibhatti hoti, kālo ti cettha kriyā, karaṇaṃ kāro, ra kārassa la kāro, kālo.

The `vattamānā` inflection is used when present time is understood. And here `kāla` (time) means action (`kriyā`): `kāra` is the doing (`karaṇa`), and with the letter `r` replaced by the letter `l` it becomes `kāla`.

> Tasmā –

Therefore:

> Kriyāya gamyamānāya, vibhattīnaṃ vidhānato; \
> Dhātūheva bhavantīti, siddhaṃ tyādivibhattiyo.

Because the inflections are prescribed when an action is understood, \
it is established that the inflections `ti` and the rest come from roots alone.

> Idha pana kālassa atītānāgatapaccuppannāṇattiparikappakālātipattivasena[^1] chadhā bhinnattā ‘‘paccuppanne’’ti viseseti. Taṃ taṃ kāraṇaṃ paṭicca uppanno paccuppanno, paṭiladdhasabhāvo, na tāva atītoti attho.

But here, since time is divided six ways — past, future, present, command, supposition and lapse of time — the rule specifies "in the present" (`paccuppanne`). The present (`paccuppanna`) is what has arisen (`uppanna`) in dependence on (`paṭicca`) this or that cause; the meaning is that it has obtained its own nature and is not yet past.

> Paccuppannasamīpepi, tabbohārūpacārato; \
> Vattamānā atītepi, taṃkālavacanicchayāti.

For what is near the present too, by figurative extension (`upacāra`) of that usage, \
and for the past too, the `vattamānā` is used, when one wishes to speak of it as of that time.

> Tasmiṃ paccuppanne vattamānāvibhattiṃ katvā, tassā ṭhānāniyame ‘‘dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā’’ti paribhāsato dhātuto paraṃ vattamānappaccaye katvā, tesamaniyamappasaṅgesati ‘‘vatticchānupubbikā saddappaṭipattī’’ti parassapadavacanicchāyaṃ –

The `vattamānā` inflection is applied in that present [sense]; since its position is not fixed, the `vattamānā` suffix is placed after the root by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) [362 (§432): Dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā](/rupasiddhi/5-taddhitakanda#r362) ("suffixes come after roots and lemmas"); and since these would apply without restriction, it is on the principle that "the formation of words follows the speaker's wish" that, when the `parassapada` is to be expressed —

### 429 (§406): Atha pubbāni vibhattīnaṃ cha parassapadāni (Now, the first six of the inflections are `parassapada`) :badge[rūḷhī]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§406 (429): Atha pubbāni vibhattīnaṃ cha parassapadāni" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m406" description="Now, the first six of the inflections are `parassapada`" />
:::

> Atha taddhitānantaraṃ vuccamānānaṃ sabbāsaṃ vattamānādīnaṃ aṭṭhavidhānaṃ vibhattīnaṃ yāni yāni pubbakāni cha padāni, tāni tāni atthato aṭṭhacattālīsamattāni parassapada saññāni hontītiādimhi channaṃ parassapadasaññā, parassatthāni padāni parassapadāni, tabbāhullato tabbohāro.

Now (`atha`), of all the eight kinds of inflection, `vattamānā` and the rest, which are stated immediately after the `taddhita`, whichever six words come first [in each set] — forty-eight in all — are named `parassapada`; so in the first [set] the six are named `parassapada`. `parassapada` means "words whose meaning is for another" (`parassa atthāni padāni`), and that usage (`vohāra`) comes from what predominates (`bāhulla`).

> ‘‘Dhātūhi ṇe ṇaya’’iccādito ‘‘dhātūhī’’ti vattamāne –

While “`dhātūhi`” ("after roots") continues from [540 (§438): Dhātūhi ṇe ṇaya ṇāpe ṇāpayā kāritāni hetvatthe](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/4-dhatuppaccayanta#r540) —

### 430 (§456): Kattari parassapadaṃ (the `parassapada` endings in the active) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§456 (430): Kattari parassapadaṃ" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m456" description="the `parassapada` endings in the active" />
:::

> Kattari kārake abhidheyye sabbadhātūhi parassapadaṃ hotīti parassapadaṃ katvā, tassāpyaniyamappasaṅge vatticchāvasā –

When the `kāraka` to be expressed is the agent (`kattā`), the `parassapada` [endings] are used after all roots. The `parassapada` is applied accordingly, and since it too would apply without restriction, it is by way of the speaker's wish (`vatticchā`) that —

> Vipariṇāmena ‘‘parassapadānaṃ, attanopadāna’’nti ca vattate.

With a change of inflection (`vipariṇāma`), “`parassapadānaṃ`” and “`attanopadānaṃ`” ("of the `parassapada`", "of the `attanopada`") also continue.

### 431 (§408): Dve dve paṭhamamajjhimuttamapurisā (Two by two: first, middle and highest person) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§408 (431): Dve dve paṭhama majjhimuttamapurisā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m408" description="Two by two: first, middle and highest person" />
:::

> Tāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ parassapadāna’mattanopadānañca dve dve vacanāni yathākkamaṃ paṭhamamajjhimuttamapurisa saññāni honti. Taṃ yathā? Ti anti iti paṭhamapurisā, si tha iti majjhimapurisā, mi ma iti uttamapurisā. Attanopadesupi te ante iti paṭhamapurisā, se vhe iti majjhimapurisā, e mhe iti uttamapurisā. Evaṃ sesāsu sattasu vibhattīsupi yojetabbanti. Evaṃ aṭṭhavibhattivasena channavutividhe ākhyātapade dvattiṃsa dvattiṃsa paṭhamamajjhimauttamapurisā hontīti vattamānaparassapadādimhi dvinnaṃ paṭhamapurisasaññā.

Of those inflections, both `parassapada` and `attanopada`, the words two by two are named, in order, first person (`paṭhamapurisa`), middle person (`majjhimapurisa`) and highest person (`uttamapurisa`). How so? `ti` and `anti` are the first person, `si` and `tha` the middle person, `mi` and `ma` the highest person. Among the `attanopada` too, `te` and `ante` are the first person, `se` and `vhe` the middle person, `e` and `mhe` the highest person. The same should be applied in the remaining seven inflections too. Thus the verbal word, which is of ninety-six kinds by way of the eight inflections, has thirty-two first persons, thirty-two middle persons and thirty-two highest persons; and so in the first [set], the `vattamānā` `parassapada`, the two are named first person.

### 434 (§485): Aññesu ca (and before the other suffixes) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

“`asaṃyogantassa`” and “`vuddhi`” continue into this rule from [527 (§483): Asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r527), which Buddhappiya states later.

> Kāritato aññesu paccayesu asaṃyogantānaṃ dhātūnaṃ vuddhi hoti. Ca ggahaṇena ṇu ppaccayassāpi vuddhi hoti. Ettha ca ‘‘ghaṭādīnaṃ vā’’ti ito vā saddo anuvattetabbo, so ca vavatthitavibhāsattho. Tena –

> Ivaṇṇuvaṇṇantānañca, lahūpantāna dhātunaṃ; \
> Ivaṇṇuvaṇṇānameva, vuddhi hoti parassa na.

For roots ending in an `i`- or `u`-vowel, and for roots whose penult is light, \
`vuddhi` is of the `i`- and `u`-vowels only, not of any other [vowel].

> Yuvaṇṇānampi ya ṇu ṇā-nāniṭṭhādīsu vuddhi na; \
> Tudādissāvikaraṇe, na chetvādīsu vā siyā.

nor does the `tuda` group before the class suffix `a`; and in `chetvā` and the like it is optional (`vā`).

And since this [strengthening] too would apply without restriction, by the metarule ([`paribhāsā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) [365 (§405): Ayuvaṇṇānañcāyo vuddhī](/rupasiddhi/5-taddhitakanda#r365) the `vuddhi` of the letter `ū` is the letter `o`.

> Vipariṇāmena ‘‘dhātūna’’nti vattate.

### 441 (§409): Sabbesamekābhidhāne paro puriso (When all [persons] are expressed together, the later person) :badge[vidhyaṅga]{variant=tip}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§409 (441): Sabbesamekābhidhāne paro puriso" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m409" description="When all [persons] are expressed together, the later person" />
:::

> Sabbesaṃ paṭhamamajjhimānaṃ, paṭhamuttamānaṃ, majjhimuttamānaṃ tiṇṇaṃ vā purisānaṃ ekatobhidhāne kātabbe paro puriso yojetabbo. Ekakālānamevābhidhāne cāyaṃ. So ca pacati, tvañca pacasīti pariyāyappasaṅge tumhe pacathā ti bhavati. Evaṃ so ca pacati, ahañca pacāmīti mayaṃ pacāma, tathā tvañca pacasi, ahañca pacāmi, mayaṃ pacāma, so ca pacati, tvañca pacasi, ahañca pacāmi, mayaṃ pacāma. Evaṃ sabbattha yojetabbaṃ.

When all the persons are to be expressed together — the first and the middle, the first and the highest, the middle and the highest, or all three — the later person is used. And this applies only when persons of one time are expressed. Where the sequence `so ca pacati, tvañca pacasi` ("he cooks and you cook") presents itself, it becomes `tumhe pacatha` ("you [all] cook"). Likewise `so ca pacati, ahañca pacāmi` ("he cooks and I cook") becomes `mayaṃ pacāma` ("we cook"); so too `tvañca pacasi, ahañca pacāmi` ("you cook and I cook") becomes `mayaṃ pacāma`, and `so ca pacati, tvañca pacasi, ahañca pacāmi` ("he cooks, you cook and I cook") becomes `mayaṃ pacāma`. It is to be applied in this way everywhere.

> Ekābhidhāne ti kimatthaṃ? ‘‘So ca pacati, tvañca pacissasi, ahaṃ paciṃ’’ ettha bhinnakālattā ‘‘mayaṃ pacimhā’’ti na bhavati.

By saying `ekābhidhāne` (expressed together) we exclude `so ca pacati, tvañca pacissasi, ahaṃ paciṃ` ("he cooks, you will cook, I cooked"): here, because the times differ, it does not become `mayaṃ pacimhā` ("we cooked").

> Gamu sappa gatimhi, pure viya dhātusaññāyaṃ dhātvantalopo.

“`gamu` and `sappa` in the sense of going” [says the root-list]. As before, once it has been named a root, the root's final is elided.

> Kattari tyā dyuppatti.

In the active, the inflections `ti` and the rest arise.

### 442 (§476): Gamissanto ccho vā sabbāsu (Optionally `ccha` for the final of `gamu` in all [suffixes and inflections]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§476 (442): Gamissanto ccho vā sabbāsu" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m476" description="Optionally `ccha` for the final of `gamu` in all [suffixes and inflections]" />
:::

> Gamu iccetassa dhātussanto ma kāro ccho hoti vā sabbāsu vibhattīsu, sabba ggahaṇena mānanta ya kāritappaccayesu ca. Vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ vā saddo. Tenāyaṃ –

The final of the root `gamu`, the letter `m`, optionally becomes `ccha` before all the inflections, and by the mention of `sabba` ("all") before the suffixes `māna` and `anta`, `ya` and the causatives as well. This word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`); hence this [verse]:

> Vidhiṃ niccañca vāsaddo, māna’ntesu tu kattari; \
> Dīpetāniccamaññattha, parokkhāyamasantakaṃ.

The word `vā` shows the operation to be obligatory before `māna` and `anta` in the active, \
optional elsewhere, and absent in the `parokkhā`.

> Appaccayaparanayanāni, so puriso gāmaṃ gacchati, te gacchanti, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā garupubbarassato parassa paṭhamapurisabahuvacanassa re vā hoti, gacchare. Tvaṃ gacchasi, tumhe gacchatha. Ahaṃ gacchāmi, mayaṃ gacchāma.

Then come the suffix `a` and the joining on of what follows: `so puriso gāmaṃ gacchati` ("that man goes to the village"), `te gacchanti` ("they go"); and by [488 (§517): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/2-vikaranavidhana#r488) the first person plural ending, when it follows a short vowel preceded by a heavy syllable, optionally becomes `re`: `gacchare`. `tvaṃ gacchasi` ("you go"), `tumhe gacchatha` ("you [all] go"); `ahaṃ gacchāmi` ("I go"), `mayaṃ gacchāma` ("we go").

### 444 (§453): Attanopadāni bhāve ca kammani (the `attanopada` endings in the impersonal and the passive) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§453 (444): Attanopadāni bhāve ca kammani" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m453" description="the `attanopada` endings in the impersonal and the passive" />

> Bhāve ca kammani ca kārake abhidheyye attanopadāni honti, ca saddena kammakattaripi. Bhavanaṃ bhāvo, so ca kārakantarena asaṃsaṭṭho kevalo bhavanalavanādiko dhātvattho. Karīyatīti kammaṃ. When the `kāraka` to be expressed is the bare action (`bhāva`) or the object (`kamma`), the `attanopada` endings are used, and by the word `ca` they are used for the object-as-agent (`kammakattā`) as well. `bhāva` is being (`bhavana`): the bare meaning of the root, such as being or cutting, unmixed with any other `kāraka`. `kamma` is what is done (`karīyati`). ### 451 (§415): Āṇatyāsiṭṭhenuttakāle pañcamī (In command and blessing, time unstated: the `pañcamī`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§415 (451): Āṇatyā siṭṭhe’nuttakāle pañcamī" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m415" description="In command and blessing, time unstated: the `pañcamī`" />
:::

> Āṇatyatthe ca āsīsatthe anuttakāle pañcamī vibhatti hoti.

The `pañcamī` inflection is used in the sense of command and in the sense of wish, when no time is stated.

> Satipi kālādhikāre puna kāla ggahaṇena vidhinimantanājjhesanānumatipatthanāpattakālā dīsu ca pañcamī. Āṇāpanamāṇatti, āsīsanamāsiṭṭho, so ca iṭṭhassa asampattassa atthassa patthanaṃ, tasmiṃ āṇatyāsiṭṭhe. Anu samīpe uttakālo anuttakālo, paccuppannakāloti attho, na uttakāloti vā anuttakālo, tasmiṃ anuttakāle, kālamanāmasitvā hotīti attho.

Although the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) `kāle` ("in time") is in place, by the repeated mention of `kāla` the `pañcamī` is used also in injunction ([`vidhi`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), invitation (`nimantana`), entreaty (`ajjhesana`), permission (`anumati`), prayer (`patthanā`), the time having come (`pattakāla`), and the like. `āṇatti` is commanding, and `āsiṭṭha` is wishing, that is, the longing for a desired thing not yet attained; the rule applies in these two, command and wish. In `anuttakāla`, `anu` means "near": the time near (`anu`) the one spoken of (`utta`) is an `anuttakāla`, that is, the present time; or else an `anuttakāla` is a time not (`na`) stated (`utta`), and "in that unstated time" means that the inflection is used without reference to a time.

> Tattha āsīsanatthe bhūdhātuto pañcamīparassapadapaṭhamapurisekavacanaṃ tu, a ppaccayavuddhiavā desā. So sukhī bhavatu, te sukhitā bhavantu.

Of these, in the sense of a wish, the ending after the root `bhū` is `tu`, the `pañcamī` `parassapada` first person singular, with the suffix `a`, `vuddhi` and the replacement `ava`: `so sukhī bhavatu` ("may he be happy"), `te sukhitā bhavantu` ("may they be happy").

| →   | **bhavatu**                   |      |

:::

> Vipariṇāmena ‘‘akārato’’ti vattate.

With a change of inflection form, “`akārato`” ("after `a`") continues.

### 454 (§416): Anumatiparikappatthesu sattamī (In the senses of permission and supposition: the `sattamī`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§416 (454): Anumatiparikappatthesu sattamī" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m416" description="In the senses of permission and supposition: the `sattamī`" />

The `sattamī` inflection is used in the sense of permission and in the sense of supposition, when no time is stated.

> Attha ggahaṇena vidhinimantanā dīsu ca sattamī. Kattumicchato parassa anujānanaṃ anumati, parikappanaṃ parikappo, ‘‘yadi nāma bhaveyyā’’ti sallakkhaṇaṃ nirūpanaṃ, hetukriyāya sambhave phalakriyāya sambhavaparikappo ca.

By the mention of `attha` ("sense") the `sattamī` is used also in injunction, invitation and the rest. Permission (`anumati`) is consenting to another who wishes to act; supposition (`parikappa`) is supposing, that is, the considering and framing of "if only it were so", and also the supposing that, when the cause-action occurs, the result-action occurs.

> Tattha parikappe sattamīparassapadapaṭhamapurisekavacanaṃ eyya, appaccayavuddhādi purimasamaṃ, ‘‘kvaci dhātu vibhattī’’tiādinā eyya eyyāsi eyyāmi eyyaṃiccetesaṃ vikappena ekārādeso. So dāni kiṃ nu kho bhave, yadi so paṭhamavaye pabbajeyya, arahā bhaveyya, sace saṅkhārā niccā bhaveyyuṃ, na nirujjheyyuṃ. Yadi tvaṃ bhaveyyāsi, tumhe bhaveyyātha. Kathamahaṃ devo bhaveyyāmi, kiṃ nu kho mayaṃ bhaveyyāma. Tathā bhavetha, bhaveraṃ. Bhavetho, bhaveyyāvho.

Of these, in supposition the ending is `eyya`, the `sattamī` `parassapada` first person singular; the suffix `a`, `vuddhi` and the rest are as before, and by [488 (§517): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/2-vikaranavidhana#r488) `eyya`, `eyyāsi`, `eyyāmi` and `eyyaṃ` are optionally (`vikappena`) replaced by the letter `e`: `so dāni kiṃ nu kho bhave` ("what might he be now?"), `yadi so paṭhamavaye pabbajeyya, arahā bhaveyya` ("if he were to go forth in the first stage of life, he would become an arahant"), `sace saṅkhārā niccā bhaveyyuṃ, na nirujjheyyuṃ` ("if formations were permanent, they would not cease"); `yadi tvaṃ bhaveyyāsi` ("if you were"), `tumhe bhaveyyātha`; `kathamahaṃ devo bhaveyyāmi` ("how might I become a god?"), `kiṃ nu kho mayaṃ bhaveyyāma` ("what might we become?"). ### 460 (§417): Apaccakkhe parokkhātīte (In the unwitnessed past: the `parokkhā`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§417 (460): Apaccakkhe parokkhātīte" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m417" description="In the unwitnessed past: the `parokkhā`" />
:::

> Apaccakkhe vattuno indriyāvisayabhūte atīte kāle parokkhā vibhatti hoti. Atikkamma itoti atīto, hutvā atikkantoti attho.

The `parokkhā` inflection is used for past time that is unwitnessed, that is, outside the range of the speaker's senses. `atīta` (past) means gone (`ita`)[^10] beyond (`atikkamma`): what has been and has passed.

> Heṭṭhā vuttanayena parokkhāparassapadapaṭhamapurisekavacanaṃ a. ‘‘Bhū a’’itīdha –

In the way described above, the `parokkhā` `parassapada` first-person singular is `a`. Here, in `bhū` + `a` —

> Vipariṇāmena ‘‘dhātūna’’nti vattate.

By a change of inflection form, “`dhātūnaṃ`” ("of roots") continues.

### 461 (§458): Kvacādivaṇṇānamekassarānaṃ dvebhāvo (Sometimes the root's first syllable is doubled) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§458 (461): Kvacādivaṇṇānamekassarānaṃ dvebhāvo" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m458" description="Sometimes the root's first syllable is doubled" />
:::

> Dhātūnamādibhūtānaṃ vaṇṇānamekassarānaṃ kvaci dvebhāvo hoti. Vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ kvaci saddo, tena –

The initial letters of a root, with their single vowel, are sometimes (`kvaci`) doubled. This word `kvaci` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`); hence —

> Kha cha sesu parokkhāyaṃ, dvebhāvo sabbadhātunaṃ; \
> Appaccaye juhotyādi-ssapi kiccādike kvaci.

Before `kha`, `cha` and `sa`, and in the `parokkhā`, all roots are doubled; \
so too `juhoti` and the like before the suffix `a`, and sometimes before `kicca` suffixes and the like.[^11]

> ‘‘Bhū bhū a’’itīdha –

Here, in `bhū bhū` + `a` —

### 462 (§459): Pubbobbhāso (The first [of the two] is called `abbhāsa`) :badge[rūḷhī]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§459 (462): Pubbo’bbhāso" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m459" description="The first [of the two] is called `abbhāsa`" />

> Dvebhūtassa dhātussa yo pubbo avayavo, so abbhāsa sañño hotīti abbhāsasaññā.

Of a root that has been doubled, the part that stands first is called `abbhāsa`; hence [the first `bhū`] receives the name `abbhāsa`.

> Abbhāsa ggahaṇamanuvattate.

The mention of `abbhāsa` is carried over.

### 463 (§465): Antassivaṇṇākāro vā (The final [vowel of the `abbhāsa`] optionally becomes an `i`-vowel or `a`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§465 (463): Antassivaṇṇākāro vā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m465" description="The final [vowel of the `abbhāsa`] optionally becomes an `i`-vowel or `a`" />
:::

> Abbhāsassa antassa ivaṇṇo hoti vā, a kāro ca. Vavatthitavibhāsatthoyaṃ vā saddo. Tena –

The final of the `abbhāsa` optionally becomes an `i`-vowel, and the letter `a` as well. This word `vā` has the sense of a restricted option (`vavatthitavibhāsā`). Hence —

> Kha cha sesu avaṇṇassa, \
> Ikāro sagupussa ī; \
> Vāssa bhūssa parokkhāyaṃ, \
> Akāro nāparassimeti.

Before `kha`, `cha` and `sa`, for an `a`-vowel [of the `abbhāsa`] \
the letter `i`, and for that of `gupa` too; the letter `ī` \
for that of `vā`; for that of `bhū` in the `parokkhā` \
the letter `a`: these are for no other.

> Ū kārassa a kāro.

The letter `ū` becomes the letter `a`.

### 464 (§461): Dutiyacatutthānaṃ paṭhamatatiyā (Second and fourth [letters in the `abbhāsa`] become first and third) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§461 (464): Dutiyacatutthānaṃ paṭhamatatiyā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m461" description="Second and fourth [letters in the `abbhāsa`] become first and third" />

### 466 (§516): Ikārāgamo asabbadhātukamhi (the insertion of `i` in the `asabbadhātuka`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§516 (466): Ikārāgamo asabbadhātukamhi" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m516" description="the insertion of `i` in the `asabbadhātuka`" />

:::note[Analysis]

The `kvaci` of the gloss continues comprehensively ([`sīhagatiyā`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) from [488 (§517): Kvaci dhātuvibhattippaccayānaṃ dīghaviparītādesalopāgamā ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/2-vikaranavidhana#r488), a rule stated later; Kaccāyana's §516 has no `kvaci`.

:::

> Sabbasmiṃ asabbadhātukamhi pare kvaci dhātūhi paro i kārāgamo hoti.

Before any `asabbadhātuka` ending, there is sometimes (`kvaci`) an insertion ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) of the letter `i` after roots.

> Asabbadhātuke byañja-nādimhe vāyamāgamo; \
> Kvacādhikārato byañja-nādopi kvaci no siyā.

This insertion occurs only[^13] before an `asabbadhātuka` ending that begins with a consonant; \
and by the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) `kvaci`, even before one beginning with a consonant it sometimes does not.

> Ettha ca ‘‘na sabbadhātukaṃ asabbadhātuka’’miti katvā ‘‘hiyyattanī sattamī pañcamī vattamānā sabbadhātuka’’nti hiyyattanīādīnaṃ sabbadhātukasaññāya vuttattā tadaññā catasso vibhattiyo asabbadhātukanti vuccati.

And here, since `asabbadhātuka` is taken as "not `sabbadhātuka`", and the `hiyyattanī` and the rest have been named `sabbadhātuka` by [458 (§431): Hiyyattanī sattamī pañcamī vattamānā sabbadhātukaṃ](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/1-bhuvadigana-vibhatti#r458), the other four inflections are called `asabbadhātuka`.

### 469 (§419): Samīpejjatanī (In the near [past]: the `ajjatanī`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

### 474 (§430): Kālātipatti ssā ssaṃsu, sse ssatha, ssaṃssāmhā, ssatha ssisu, ssase ssavhe, ssiṃssāmhase (`kālātipatti`: the twelve endings of the unrealised) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

<LinkCard title="§430 (474): Kālātipatti ssā ssaṃsu, sse ssatha, ssaṃ ssāmhā, ssatha ssisu, ssase ssavhe, ssiṃ ssāmhase" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m430" description="`kālātipatti`: the twelve endings of the unrealised" />

> Ssā dīnaṃ dvādasannaṃ kālātipatti saññā hoti. Kālassa atipatanaṃ kālātipatti, sā pana viruddhapaccayūpanipātato, kāraṇavekallato vā kriyāya anabhinibbatti, taddīpakattā ayaṃ vibhatti kālātipattī ti vuccati.

The twelve, `ssā` and the rest,[^18] receive the name `kālātipatti`. `kālātipatti` is the lapsing (`atipatana`) of the time: it is the action's failure to come about because an opposing condition intervened or because the cause was deficient; and because it expresses that, this inflection is called `kālātipatti`.

### 475 (§422): Kriyātipannetīte kālātipatti (In the past, when the action has lapsed: the `kālātipatti`) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§422 (475): Kriyātipanne’tīte kālātipatti" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/1-pathamakanda#m422" description="In the past, when the action has lapsed: the `kālātipatti`" />

> Kriyātipannamatte atīte kāle kālātipatti vibhatti hoti. Kriyāya atipatanaṃ kriyātipannaṃ, taṃ pana sādhakasattivirahena kriyāya accantānuppatti. Ettha ca kiñcāpi na kriyā atītasaddena voharitabbā, tathāpi takkiriyuppattippaṭibandhakarakriyāya kālabhedena atītavohāro labbhatevāti daṭṭhabbaṃ.

The `kālātipatti` inflection is used for past time, merely when the action has lapsed. `kriyātipanna` is the lapsing of the action (`kriyāya atipatanaṃ`): the complete failure of the action to arise, through the absence of the power that would have accomplished it. And here, although the action cannot be designated by the word "past", nevertheless the designation "past" is indeed obtained by the distinction of time (`kālabheda`) of the action that obstructed the arising of that action — so it should be understood.

> Kālātipattiparassapadapaṭhamapurisekavacanaṃ ssā, a kārikārāgamā, vuddhiavā desā ca, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ssā ssāmhā vibhattīnaṃ kvaci rassattaṃ, sse vacanassa ca a ttaṃ.

> _Kālātipattivibhatti._

[The] `kālātipatti` inflection.

---

> Pañcamī sattamī vatta-mānā sampatināgate; \
> Bhavissantī parokkhādī, catassotītakālikā.

The `pañcamī`, the `sattamī` and the `vattamānā` are for the present, and for the future \
the `bhavissantī`; the `parokkhā` and the rest are the four of past time.

> _Chakālikavibhattividhānaṃ._

[The] prescription of the inflections of the six times.
