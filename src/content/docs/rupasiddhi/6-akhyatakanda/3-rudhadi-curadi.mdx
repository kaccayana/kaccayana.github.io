### 509 (§446): Rudhādito niggahītapubbañca ([`a`] after the `rudha` class, with a niggahīta before [the root's final]; and `i`, `ī`, `e`, `o`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§446 (509): Rudhādito niggahitapubbañca" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m446" description="[`a`] after the `rudha` class, with a niggahīta before [the root's final]; and `i`, `ī`, `e`, `o`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

`a` continues from [433 (§445): Bhūvādito a](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/1-bhuvadigana-vibhatti#r433).

:::

> Catuppadamidaṃ. Rudha iccevamādito dhātugaṇato a ppaccayo hoti kattari vibhattippaccayesu, niggahītañca tato pubbaṃ hutvā āgamo hoti, tañca niggahītaṃ pakatiyā sarānugatattā dhātussarato paraṃ hoti. Ca saddena iīeo paccayā ca, niggahītassa vaggantattaṃ. Idha saṃyogantattā na vuddhi hoti, tadāgamassa taggahaṇena gahaṇato.

This is four words. After the root-group beginning with `rudha` (to obstruct), the suffix `a` is used in the active (`kattari`) before the inflections, and a niggahīta is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) before it; since a niggahīta by its nature follows a vowel, it stands after the root's vowel. By the word `ca` the suffixes `i`, `ī`, `e` and `o` are used as well, and the niggahīta becomes the nasal of its group (`vagganta`). There is no strengthening (`vuddhi`) here, because the root ends in a conjunct, an insertion to a thing being included under the mention of that thing.[^37]

> So maggaṃ rundhati, rundhanti. Rundhasi, rundhatha. Rundhāmi, rundhāma. Rundhate, rundhante iccādi, i kārādippaccayesu pana rundhiti, rundhīti, rundheti, rundhotītipi hoti.

`so maggaṃ rundhati` ("he blocks the road"):

| parassapada | ⨀ | ⨂ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🤟 | rundhati | rundhanti |
| 🤘 | rundhasi | rundhatha |
| 👆 | rundhāmi | rundhāma |

| attanopada | ⨀ | ⨂ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🤟 | rundhate | rundhante |

and so on. With the suffixes `i` and the rest, however, there are also `rundhiti`, `rundhīti`, `rundheti` and `rundhoti`.

> Divā dito dhātugaṇato ya ppaccayo hoti kattari vihitesu vibhattippaccayesu.

> Yathā bhāvakammesu yappaccayassādeso hoti, tathā kattaripi ya ppaccayassa sadhātvantassa ca vagga ya kārava kārādeso, pubbarūpañca kātabbānīti dhātvantassa va kārattā saha tena ya kārassa va kāre kate dvibhāvo, ‘‘do dhassa cā’’ti ettha ca ggahaṇena ‘‘bo vassā’’ti vuttattā va kāradvayassa ba kāradvayaṃ, dibbati, dibbanti. Dibbasi, dibbatha. Dibbāmi, dibbāma.

Just as the suffix `ya` is replaced in the impersonal (`bhāva`) and the passive (`kamma`), so in the active too[^38] the suffix `ya` together with the root's final is to be replaced by a `c`-group letter, `y` or `v`, and is to take the form of the preceding consonant (`pubbarūpa`). So, since the root's final is `v`, once the `ya` together with it has been made `v` there is doubling, and because the word `ca` in [§20 (27): Do dhassa ca](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m20) states that `b` comes for `v`, the pair of `v` becomes a pair of `b`: `dibbati` ("he plays"), `dibbanti`; `dibbasi`, `dibbatha`; `dibbāmi`, `dibbāma`:

| parassapada, ▶️ vattamānā | ⨀ | ⨂ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🤟 | dibbati | dibbanti |
| 🤘 | dibbasi | dibbatha |
| 👆 | dibbāmi | dibbāma |

> Pada gatimhi, u pubbo dvittaṃ, ‘‘tathā kattari cā’’ti sadhātvantassa ya kārassa ja kāro, dvittañca.

`pada` is in the sense of going. With the prefix `u` there is doubling; by [§444] `Tathā kattari ca` the `ya` together with the root's final becomes `j`, and it is doubled too.

`yudha` is in the sense of fighting: `yujjhati` ("he fights"), `yujjhanti`.

> Kudha kope, kujjhati, kujjhanti.

`kudha` is in the sense of anger: `kujjhati` ("he is angry"), `kujjhanti`.

> Vidha tāḷane, vijjhati, vijjhanti iccādi.

`vidha` is in the sense of striking: `vijjhati` ("he pierces"), `vijjhanti`, and so on.

> Naha bandhane, ‘‘ha vipariyayo’’ti yogavibhāgena vipariyayo. Sannayhati, sannayhanti iccādi.

`naha` is in the sense of binding. By splitting the rule [§488 (481): Ha vipariyayo lo vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m488) to read `ha vipariyayo`, there is metathesis (`vipariyaya`): `sannayhati` ("he fastens on"), `sannayhanti`, and so on.

> Mana ñāṇe, ñādesova viseso, maññati, maññanti iccādi.

`mana` is in the sense of knowing. The only difference is the replacement by `ñ`: `maññati` ("he thinks"), `maññanti`, and so on.

> Dā ādāne, saṃā pubbo ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā ya mhi dhātvantassa i kāro, sīlaṃ samādiyati, samādiyanti iccādi.

`dā` is in the sense of taking. With the prefixes `saṃ` and `ā`, by [§517] `Kvaci dhātu…` the root's final becomes `i` before `ya`: `sīlaṃ samādiyati` ("he takes the precepts upon himself"), `samādiyanti`, and so on.

> Tusa pītimhi, yappaccayassa pubbarūpattaṃ, tussati, tussanti iccādi.

`tusa` is in the sense of satisfaction. The suffix `ya` takes the form of the preceding consonant: `tussati` ("he is pleased"), `tussanti`, and so on.

> Tathā samu upasame, sammati, sammanti.

Likewise `samu` is in the sense of calming: `sammati` ("he is calmed"), `sammanti`.

> Kupa kope, kuppati, kuppanti.

`kupa` is in the sense of anger: `kuppati` ("he is angry"), `kuppanti`.

> Jana janane, ‘‘janādīnamā timhi cā’’ti ettha ‘‘janādīnamā’’ti yogavibhāgena ya mhi dhātvantassa ā kāro. Jāyati, jāyanti. Jāyate, jāyante.

> Kamme janīyati, janīyanti. Jāyatu. Jāyeyya. Ajāyi, ajani. Jāyissati, janissati. Ajāyissa, ajanissa iccādi.

In the passive: `janīyati` ("he is begotten"), `janīyanti`.

> Su iccevamādito dhātugaṇato ṇu ṇāuṇā iccete paccayā honti kattari vihitesu vibhattippaccayesu. ‘‘Aññesu cā’’ti ettha ca ggahaṇena ṇu ppaccayassa vuddhi. Tatthevānuvattitavā ggahaṇena svādīnaṃ ṇuṇādīsu na vuddhi.

After the root-group beginning with `su` (to hear), the suffixes `ṇu`, `ṇā` and `uṇā` are used before the inflections prescribed in the active (`kattari`). > Kamme ya ppaccaye ‘‘ivaṇṇāgamo vā’’ti īkārāgamo, ya kārassa dvittaṃ, karīyyate kaṭo tena, karīyyati, karīyyanti. Karīyati, karīyanti vā. Ī kārābhāve ‘‘tassa cavagga’’iccādinā sadhātvantassa ya kārattaṃ, dvittañca. Kayyati, kayyanti. I kārāgame ‘‘kvaci dhātu’’iccādisutte ca ggahaṇena rayānaṃ vipariyayo, kayirati kaṭo tena, kayiranti iccādi.

In the passive, before the suffix `ya`, `ī` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) by [§442] `Ivaṇṇāgamo vā` and the `y` is doubled: `karīyyate kaṭo tena`[^40] ("the mat is made by him"), `karīyyati`, `karīyyanti`; or `karīyati`, `karīyanti`. Without the `ī`, the `ya` together with the root's final becomes `y` by [§441] `Tassa cavagga…`, and it is doubled: `kayyati`, `kayyanti`. With the insertion of `i`, the `r` and the `y` undergo metathesis (`vipariyaya`) by the word `ca` in the sutta [§517] `Kvaci dhātu…`: `kayirati kaṭo tena` ("the mat is made by him"), `kayiranti`, and so on.

> Tathā kusalaṃ karotu, kurutu vā, kubbantu, karontu. Karohi, karotha. Karomi, karoma. Kurutaṃ, kubbantaṃ. Kurussu, kurassu vā, kuruvho. Kubbe, kubbāmase.

> Kamme karīyatu, karīyantu, kayyataṃ, kayirataṃ, kayiratu.

> Yira ppaccaye –

With the suffix `yira` —

> Yirato āttameyyassa, ethādisseyyumādisu; \
> Eyyasaddassa lopo ca, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā.

After `yira`, `eyya` becomes `ā`, and so do `etha` and the rest; \
and in `eyyuṃ` and the rest the sound `eyya` is elided — by [§517] `Kvaci dhātu…`.

> Saralopādi, kayirā, kayiruṃ. Kayirāsi, kayirātha. Kayirāmi, kayirāma. Attanopade kayirātha dhīro, kubbetha, karetha vā, ‘‘kvaci dhātū’’tiādinā kussa kru ca, krubbetha, krubberaṃ. Krubbetho, krubbeyyāvho. Krubbeyyaṃ, krubbeyyāmhe. Kamme karīyeyya, karīyeyyuṃ.

In the `attanopada`: `kayirātha dhīro` ("the wise man should make"), `kubbetha`, or `karetha`; and `ku` becomes `kru` by [§517] `Kvaci dhātu…`: `krubbetha`, `krubberaṃ`; `krubbetho`, `krubbeyyāvho`; `krubbeyyaṃ`, `krubbeyyāmhe`. In the passive: `karīyeyya`, `karīyeyyuṃ`.

> Hiyyattaniyaṃ ‘‘karassa kā’’ti yogavibhāgena kā hoti, saralopādi.

In the `hiyyattanī`, `kara` becomes `kā` by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) [§491 (523): Karassa kāsattamajjatanimhi](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m491) as “`karassa kā`” ("`kā` for `kara`"), with the elision of the vowel and the rest.

### 523 (§491): Karassa kāsattamajjatanimhi (optionally `kāsa` for `kara` in the `ajjatanī`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§491 (523): Karassa kāsattamajjatanimhi" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m491" description="optionally `kāsa` for `kara` in the `ajjatanī`" />
:::

> Kara iccetassa dhātussa sabbasseva kāsa ttaṃ hoti vā ajjatanimhi vibhattimhi pare. ‘‘Kāsatta’’miti bhāvaniddesena aññasmāpi dhātuto sā gamo. Atha vā yadā kara ssa kā hoti, sa ttañcāgamo ajjatanimhi vāti attho, tadā ‘‘sattamajjatanimhī’’ti yogavibhāgena aññasmāpi dhātuto sā gamopi sijjhati, ‘‘yogavibhāgato iṭṭhappasiddhī’’ti yebhuyyena dīghatova hoti, ‘‘karassa kā’’ti yogavibhāgena kā bhāvo ca hiyyattaniyaṃ siddho hoti.

Optionally, the whole of the root `kara` becomes `kāsa` when an ending of the `ajjatanī` follows. By the statement in the abstract (`bhāvaniddesa`), “`kāsattaṃ`” ("`kāsa`-ness"), `s` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)) after another root too. Or else the meaning is that when `kara` becomes `kā`, `s` too is optionally inserted in the `ajjatanī`; and then, by splitting the rule (`yogavibhāga`) as “`sattam ajjatanimhi`” ("`s` in the `ajjatanī`"), the insertion of `s` after another root too is established as well, and since "by splitting a rule the desired forms are established", it comes for the most part only after a long vowel. And by splitting the rule as “`karassa kā`” ("`kā` for `kara`"), the change to `kā` in the `hiyyattanī` is established too.

> Akāsi, akāsuṃ. Akāsi, akāsittha. Akāsiṃ, akāsimha. Akāsittha. Kāsa ttābhāve akari, kari, akariṃsu, kariṃsu, akaṃsu, akaruṃ. Akari, akarittha. Akariṃ, kariṃ, akarimha, karimha. Akarittha. Akarīyittha, akarīyi vā.

`akāsi` ("he did"), `akāsuṃ`; `akāsi`, `akāsittha`; `akāsiṃ`, `akāsimha`; `akāsittha`. Without the change to `kāsa`: `akari`, `kari`, `akariṃsu`, `kariṃsu`, `akaṃsu`, `akaruṃ`; `akari`, `akarittha`; `akariṃ`, `kariṃ`, `akarimha`, `karimha`; `akarittha`; `akarīyittha` or `akarīyi`.

### 524 (§481): Karassa sappaccayassa kāho ([Optionally] `kāha` for `kara` with its `sa` [in the `bhavissantī`]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§481 (524): Karassa sappaccayassa kāho" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/3-tatiyakanda#m481" description="[Optionally] `kāha` for `kara` with its `sa` [in the `bhavissantī`]" />

:::note[Analysis]

The words “`vā`”, “`lopo`” and “`bhavissantimhi ssassa ca`”, which the lead-in of 523 carries into this rule, come from [490 (§480): Hotissare’hohe bhavissantimhi ssassa ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/2-vikaranavidhana#r490).

:::

> Kara iccetassa dhātussa sappaccayassa kāhā deso hoti vā bhavissantimhi, ssassa ca lopo hoti. Adhikabhūtasappaccaya ggahaṇena vacamucabhujā dito ssa ssa khā deso, vasa chidi labhā dito chā deso ca hoti.

In the `bhavissantī`, the root `kara` together with its suffix `sa` is optionally replaced by `kāha`, and the `ssa` is elided. > Kāhati, kāhanti. Kāhasi, kāhatha. Kāhāmi, kāhāma. Ikārāgame kāhiti, kāhinti iccādi. Kāhā bhāve karissati, karissanti. Karissasi, karissatha. Karissāmi, karissāma. Karissate, karissante. Karissase, karissavhe. Karissaṃ, karissāmhe. Karīyissati, karīyissanti. Akarissa. Akarīyissā iccādi.

> ‘‘Tathā kattari cā’’ti ito ‘‘kattarī’’ti ca sīhavilokanena bhāvakamma ggahaṇāni ca vattante, maṇḍūkagatiyā kārita ggahaṇañca.

From [511 (§444): Tathā kattari ca](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/3-rudhadi-curadi#r511), “`kattari`” continues, and so, comprehensively ([`sīhavilokana`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), do the mentions of `bhāva` and `kamma`; and selectively ([`maṇḍūkagati`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) the mention of `kārita` continues too.

<a name="r525"></a>

### 525 (§452): Curādito ṇeṇayā (`ṇe` and `ṇaya` after the `cura` class [in the active], named `kārita`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§452 (525): Curādito, ṇe ṇayā" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/2-dutiyakanda#m452" description="`ṇe` and `ṇaya` after the `cura` class [in the active], named `kārita`" />
:::

:::note[Analysis]

The class lead-in carries `bhāva` and `kamma` comprehensively ([`sīhavilokana`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) from [445 (§440): Bhāvakammesu yo](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/1-bhuvadigana-vibhatti#r445), and `kārita` selectively (`maṇḍūkagati`) from [540 (§438): Dhātūhi ṇe ṇaya ṇāpe ṇāpayā kāritāni hetvatthe](/rupasiddhi/6-akhyatakanda/4-dhatuppaccayanta#r540), which is stated only later, on the next page.

:::

> Cura iccevamādito dhātugaṇato ṇe ṇaya iccete paccayā honti kattari, bhāve ca kammani, vibhattippaccayesu. ‘‘Kāritaṃ viya ṇānubandho’’ti ṇe ṇayānaṃ kāritabyapadeso.

After the root group beginning with `cura` (to steal), the suffixes `ṇe` and `ṇaya` are used before the endings and the suffixes, in the active and also in the impersonal and the passive. By [§621 (553): Kāritaṃ viya ṇānubandho](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa/5-pancamakanda#m621), `ṇe` and `ṇaya` receive the designation (`byapadesa`) `kārita`.

<a name="r527"></a>

### 527 (§483): Asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite (`vuddhi` of a root not ending in a conjunct, before the causative) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§483 (527): Asaṃyogantassa vuddhi kārite" href="/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m483" description="`vuddhi` of a root not ending in a conjunct, before the causative" />
:::

> Asaṃyogantassa dhātussa kārite pare vuddhi hotīti ukārassokāro vuddhi.

A root that does not end in a conjunct consonant takes `vuddhi` (strengthening) when a `kārita` suffix follows; so the letter `u` becomes the letter `o`, which is the `vuddhi`.

> Dhanaṃ coreti, corenti. Coresi, coretha. Coremi, corema. Ṇaya ppaccaye – corayati, corayanti. Corayasi, corayatha. Corayāmi, corayāma. Corayate, corayante. Corayase, corayavhe. Coraye, corayāmhe.

`dhanaṃ coreti` ("he steals wealth"), `corenti`; `coresi`, `coretha`; `coremi`, `corema`. With the suffix `ṇaya`: `corayati`, `corayanti`; `corayasi`, `corayatha`; `corayāmi`, `corayāma`; `corayate`, `corayante`; `corayase`, `corayavhe`; `coraye`, `corayāmhe`.

:::tip[Derivation]

> Kamme ya ppaccaye ī kārāgamo, saralopādi ca, corīyate devadattena, corīyati, corīyanti iccādi.

In the passive, before the suffix `ya`, `ī` is inserted ([`āgama`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), with the elision of the vowel and the rest: `corīyate devadattena` ("it is stolen by Devadatta"), `corīyati`, `corīyanti`, and so on.

> Coretu, corentu. Corehi. Corayatu, corayantu. Coraya, corayāhi.

`coretu` ("let him steal"), `corentu`; `corehi`; `corayatu`, `corayantu`; `coraya`, `corayāhi`.

> Coreyya, coreyyuṃ. Coraye, corayeyyuṃ. Acoresi, coresi, acoresuṃ, coresuṃ. Acorayi, corayi, acorayiṃsu, corayiṃsu, acorayuṃ, corayuṃ. Acoresi, acoresittha. Tvaṃ acorayi, acorayittha. Acoresiṃ, acoresimha. Acorayiṃ, acorayimha. Acorayittha. Acorīyittha, acorīyi.

`coreyya` ("he should steal"), `coreyyuṃ`; `coraye`, `corayeyyuṃ`. `acoresi` ("he stole"), `coresi`, `acoresuṃ`, `coresuṃ`; `acorayi`, `corayi`, `acorayiṃsu`, `corayiṃsu`, `acorayuṃ`, `corayuṃ`; `acoresi`, `acoresittha`; `tvaṃ acorayi` ("you stole"), `acorayittha`; `acoresiṃ`, `acoresimha`; `acorayiṃ`, `acorayimha`; `acorayittha`; `acorīyittha`, `acorīyi`.

> Corissati, corissanti. Corayissati, corayissanti. Corīyissate, corīyissante. Corīyissati, corīyissanti. Acorissa, acorayissa. Acorīyissatha, acorīyissa iccādi.

`corissati` ("he will steal"), `corissanti`; `corayissati`, `corayissanti`; `corīyissate`, `corīyissante`; `corīyissati`, `corīyissanti`; `acorissa` ("he would have stolen"), `acorayissa`; `acorīyissatha`, `acorīyissa`, and so on.

> Tathā cinta cintāyaṃ, saṃyogantattā vuddhiabhāvova viseso.

Likewise `cinta` is in the sense of thinking (`cintā`); the only difference is that it has no `vuddhi`, because the root ends in a conjunct consonant.

> Cinteti, cintayati, cintenti, cintayanti. Cintetu, cintayatu. Cinteyya, cintayeyya. Acintesi, cintesi, acintayi, cintayi. Cintessati, cintayissati. Acintissa, acintayissa iccādi.

`cinteti` ("he thinks"), `cintayati`, `cintenti`, `cintayanti`; `cintetu`, `cintayatu`; `cinteyya`, `cintayeyya`; `acintesi`, `cintesi`, `acintayi`, `cintayi`; `cintessati`, `cintayissati`; `acintissa`, `acintayissa`, and so on.

> Manta guttabhāsane, manteti, mantayati i ccādi purimasamaṃ.

`manta` is in the sense of speaking in secret (`guttabhāsana`): `manteti` ("he takes counsel"), `mantayati`, and so on, the same as the preceding.

> Pāla rakkhaṇe, dhammaṃ pāleti, pālayati. Pālīyati. Pāletu, pālayatu iccādi.

`pāla` is in the sense of protecting (`rakkhaṇa`): `dhammaṃ pāleti` ("he protects the Dhamma"), `pālayati`; `pālīyati`; `pāletu`, `pālayatu`, and so on.

> Ghaṭa ghaṭane, ghāṭeti, ghāṭayati, ghaṭeti, ghaṭayati, ghaṭādittā vikappena vuddhi.

`ghaṭa` is in the sense of joining together (`ghaṭana`): `ghāṭeti` ("he joins together"), `ghāṭayati`, `ghaṭeti`, `ghaṭayati`; the `vuddhi` is optional, because the root belongs to the `ghaṭādi` group.

> Vida ñāṇe, vedeti, vedayati.

`vida` is in the sense of knowing (`ñāṇa`): `vedeti` ("he knows"), `vedayati`.

> Gaṇa saṅkhyāne, ‘‘ghaṭādīnaṃ vā’’ti na vuddhi, gaṇeti, gaṇayati iccādi, sabbattha subodhaṃ.

`gaṇa` is in the sense of counting (`saṅkhyāna`); by [§484 (542): Ghaṭādīnaṃ vā](/kaccayana/6-akhyatakappa/4-catutthakanda#m484) it has no `vuddhi`: `gaṇeti` ("he counts"), `gaṇayati`, and so on. Everywhere the forms are easily understood.

> _Curādigaṇo._

The `cura` class [is finished].

The `bhū` class, the `rudha` class, the `diva` class and the `su` class, \

The treatment of the class suffixes (`vikaraṇa`) is complete.
