:::note
Buddhappiya takes the inflection forms in order, ① to ⑦, assigns each to its meaning and defines the `kāraka` — agent, object, instrument, recipient, source, owner, location — where its form comes up, whereas Kaccāyana defined the `kāraka` first (§271–§283) and assigned the forms afterwards. His commentary adds the traditional definitions and defends them.

:::

> Atha vibhattīnamatthabhedā vuccante.

Now the different meanings of the inflections (`vibhatti`) are stated.

> Tattha ekampi atthaṃ kammādivasena, ekattādivasena ca vibhajantīti vibhattiyo, syādayo. Tā pana paṭhamādibhedena sattavidhā.

Here "inflections" (`vibhatti`) means `si` and the rest, because they divide (`vibhajanti`) even a single meaning according to its being an object (`kamma`) and so on, and according to its being one and so on. And they are of seven kinds by the division into the first form ① and the rest.

> Tattha kasmiṃ atthe paṭhamā?

Among these, in what sense is there the first form ①?

<a name="r283"></a>

### 283 (§284): Liṅgatthe paṭhamā (the first form ① for the bare meaning of the lemma) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§284 (283): Liṅgatthe paṭhamā" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m284" description="the first form ① for the bare meaning of the lemma" />
:::

> Liṅgatthābhidhānamatte paṭhamāvibhatti hoti.

The first inflection ① is used merely to express the meaning of the lemma (`liṅgattha`).

> Liṅgassa attho liṅgattho. Ettha ca līnaṃ aṅganti liṅgaṃ, apākaṭo avayavo, purisotiādīnañhi pakatippaccayādivibhāgakappanāya nipphāditānaṃ saddappatirūpakānaṃ nāmikapadānaṃ paṭhamaṃ ṭhapetabbaṃ pakatirūpaṃ apākaṭattā, avayavattā ca liṅganti vuccati. Atha vā visadāvisadobhayarahitākāravohārasaṅkhātena tividhaliṅgena sahitatthassa, tabbinimuttassupasaggādīnamatthassa ca līnassa gamanato, liṅganato vā liṅganti anvatthanāmavasena vā ‘‘dhātuppaccayavibhattivajjitamatthavaṃ liṅga’’nti vacanato parasamaññāvasena vā liṅganti idha pāṭipadikāparanāmadheyyaṃ syā divibhatyantapadapakatirūpameva vuccatīti daṭṭhabbaṃ.

`liṅgattha` is the meaning (`attha`) of the lemma (`liṅga`). And here a `liṅga` is a hidden limb (`līnaṃ aṅgaṃ`), an unmanifest component: for nominal words such as `puriso`, which are produced by positing a division into lemma, suffix and the rest and are only semblances of words (`saddappatirūpaka`), the lemma form (`pakatirūpa`) that has to be set up first is called `liṅga` because it is unmanifest and because it is a component. Or else it is called `liṅga` as a meaningful ([`anvattha`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)) name, because a hidden meaning is made known (`gamanato`) or indicated (`liṅganato`) — both a meaning accompanied by the threefold gender, which is the conventional usage of the clear, the unclear and the neither, and the meaning of prefixes and the like, which is free of that; or else it is called `liṅga` by the terminology of others (`parasamaññā`), from the statement "a `liṅga` is what has meaning apart from root, suffix and inflection". It should be understood that `liṅga` here means only the lemma form of a word ending in the inflections `si` and the rest, whose other name is `pāṭipadika` (a nominal base).

> Liṅgassattho nāma pabandhavisesākārena pavattamāne rūpādayo upādāya paññāpīyamāno tadaññānaññabhāvena anibbacanīyo samūhasantānādibhedo upādāpaññattisaṅkhāto ghaṭapaṭādivohārattho ca pathavīdhātuphassādīnaṃ sabhāvadhammānaṃ kāladesādibhedabhinnānaṃ vijātiyavinivatto sajātiyasādhāraṇo yathāsaṅketamāropasiddho tajjāpaññattisaṅkhāto kakkhaḷattaphusanādisāmaññākāro ca.

The meaning of a lemma is both the conventional meaning (`vohārattha`) of "pot", "cloth" and the like — a group, a continuum or the like, made known in dependence on material form and the rest as they occur in the form of a particular series, not to be described as either identical with them or different from them, and called a derived concept (`upādāpaññatti`) — and the general character (`sāmaññākāra`) of hardness, touching and the like that belongs to real phenomena (`sabhāvadhamma`) such as the earth element and contact, which differ by differences of time, place and so on: a character that excludes what is of another kind, is common to what is of the same kind, is established by imposition according to convention, and is called a concept of the corresponding kind (`tajjāpaññatti`).

> So pana kammādisaṃsaṭṭho, suddho cāti duvidho. Tattha kammādīsu dutiyādīnaṃ vidhīyamānattā kammādisaṃsaggarahito liṅgasaṅkhyāparimāṇayutto, tabbinimuttupasaggādipadatthabhūto ca suddho saddattho idha liṅgattho nāma.

And that meaning is of two kinds: mixed with the object (`kamma`) and the rest, and bare (`suddha`). Of these, since the second form and the rest are prescribed for the object and the rest, what is here called `liṅgattha` is the bare meaning of a word: a meaning free of connection with the object and the rest and possessed of gender, number and measure, and also the meaning of a word such as a prefix, which is free even of those.

> Yo pana ākhyātakitakataddhitasamāsehi vutto kammādisaṃsaṭṭho attho, sopi dutiyādīnaṃ puna attanā vattabbassa atthavisesassābhāvena avisayattā, liṅgatthamattassa sambhavato ca paṭhamāyeva visayo.

But a meaning mixed with the object and the rest that is expressed (`vutta`) by a verb, a `kita` word, a `taddhita` word or a compound is also the province (`visaya`) of the first form alone, since it is not the province of the second form and the rest — there being no further particular of meaning for them to express — and since only the bare meaning of the lemma is possible.

> Hoti cettha –

And here there is [the verse]:

> Paṭhamāvupasaggatthe, kesañcatthe nipātasaddānaṃ; \
> Liṅgādike ca suddhe-bhihite kammādiatthepi.

The first form is used in the sense of a prefix and in the sense of some particle words, \
in the bare [meaning] of gender and the rest, and also in the sense of the object and the rest when it is already expressed.

> Saliṅge tāva – eso puriso, ete purisā, esā kaññā, etā kaññāyo, etaṃ cittaṃ, etāni cittāni.

First, with gender: `eso puriso` ("this man"), `ete purisā` ("these men"), `esā kaññā` ("this girl"), `etā kaññāyo` ("these girls"), `etaṃ cittaṃ` ("this mind"), `etāni cittāni` ("these minds").

> Sasaṅkhye – eko dve.

With number: `eko` ("one"), `dve` ("two").

> Saparimāṇe – doṇo khārī āḷhakaṃ.

With measure: `doṇo` ("a `doṇa` measure"), `khārī` ("a `khārī` measure"), `āḷhakaṃ` ("an `āḷhaka` measure").

> Liṅgādivinimutte sattāmatte – ca vā ha ahaṃ atthi sakkālabbhā iccādi.

In mere existence (`sattāmatta`), free of gender and the rest: `ca` ("and"), `vā` ("or"), `ha` ("indeed"), `ahaṃ` ("ah!"), `atthi` ("there is"), `sakkā` ("one is able"), `labbhā` ("it is possible") and so on.

> ‘‘Liṅgatthe paṭhamā’’ti adhikicca ‘‘ālapane cā’’ti ālapanatthe ca paṭhamā, abhimukhaṃ katvā lapanaṃ ālapanaṃ, āmantanaṃ avhānanti attho.

Taking “`Liṅgatthe paṭhamā`” as the governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), by [§285 (70): Ālapane ca](/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m285) the first form ① is used in the sense of the vocative (`ālapana`) as well. The vocative (`ālapana`) is speaking (`lapana`) face to face (`abhimukhaṃ katvā`): the meaning is calling to (`āmantana`), summoning (`avhāna`).

> Ettha ca āmantanaṃ nāma pageva laddhasarūpassa saddena abhimukhīkaraṇaṃ, katābhimukho pana ‘‘gacchā’’tiādinā nayena kriyāya yojīyati, tasmā āmantanasamaye kriyāyogābhāvato idaṃ kārakavohāraṃ na labhati.

And here calling to (`āmantana`) means turning someone who already has his own identity (`sarūpa`) towards oneself by means of a word; once turned, he is joined to an action in the manner of "go" and the like. Therefore, since there is no connection with an action at the moment of calling, the vocative does not receive the designation `kāraka`.

> Vuttañca

And it is said:

> ‘‘Saddenābhimukhīkāro, vijjamānassa vatthuno; \
> Āmantanaṃ vidhātabbe, natthi ‘rājā bhave’tida’’nti.

"Calling to (`āmantana`) is turning an existing thing towards oneself by means of a word; \
where the thing is still to be brought about, as in 'may there be a king', there is none."

> Bho purisa ehi, bho purisā vā, bhavanto purisā etha.

`bho purisa ehi` ("come, man!"), or `bho purisā`; `bhavanto purisā etha` ("come, men!").

> Kasmiṃ atthe dutiyā?

In what sense is the second form ② used?

<a name="r284"></a>

### 284 (§297): Kammatthe dutiyā (② for the `kamma` [object]) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§297 (284): Kammatthe dutiyā" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m297" description="② for the `kamma` [object]" />
:::

> Kammatthe liṅgamhā dutiyāvibhatti hoti.

The second inflection ② is used after the lemma in the sense of the object (`kamma`).

> Anabhihite evāyaṃ, ‘‘kammani dutiyāyaṃ tto’’ti vacanañcettha ñāpakaṃ.

This applies only when the object is not already expressed (`anabhihita`), and here the statement [§626 (634): Kammani dutiyāya tto](/kaccayana/8-unadikappa#m626) is the indicator (`ñāpaka`) of that.

> Kiṃ kammaṃ?

What is the object (`kamma`)?

> ‘‘Yena vā kayirate taṃ karaṇa’’nti ito ‘‘vā’’ti vattate.

“`vā`” continues from [§279 (292): Yena vā kayirate taṃ karaṇaṃ](/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m279).

<a name="r285"></a>

### 285 (§280): Yaṃ karoti taṃ kammaṃ (what one does: `kamma`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§280 (285): Yaṃ karoti taṃ kammaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m280" description="what one does: `kamma`" />
:::

> Yaṃ vā karoti, yaṃ vā vikaroti, yaṃ vā pāpuṇāti, taṃ kārakaṃ kammasaññaṃ hoti.

Whatever one makes, alters or reaches — that `kāraka` is termed the object (`kamma`).

> Idha liṅgakālavacanamatantaṃ. Karīyatīti kammaṃ. Tattha kārakaṃ, sādhakaṃ kriyānipphattiyā kāraṇamuccate, taṃ pana kārakaṃ chabbidhaṃ kammaṃ kattā karaṇaṃ sampadānamapādānamokāso cāti. Tattha sabhāvato, parikappato vā kammādimhi satiyeva kriyābhāvato kammādīnaṃ channampi kārakavohāro siddhova hoti.

Here gender, tense and number are not essential (`atanta`). The object (`kamma`) is what is done (`karīyati`). There, a `kāraka` is said to be the accomplisher (`sādhaka`), the cause of bringing an action about, and that `kāraka` is of six kinds: the object (`kamma`), the agent (`kattā`), the instrument (`karaṇa`), the recipient (`sampadāna`), the point of departure (`apādāna`) and the location (`okāsa`). And since an action exists only when the object and the rest are present, whether in fact (`sabhāvato`) or in thought (`parikappato`), the designation `kāraka` is indeed established for all six of them, the object and the rest.

> Taṃ pana kammaṃ tividhaṃ nibbattanīyaṃ vikaraṇīyaṃ pāpaṇīyañcāti. Yathā – mātā puttaṃ vijāyati, āhāro sukhaṃ janayati. Ghaṭaṃ karoti devadatto, kaṭṭhamaṅgāraṃ karoti, suvaṇṇaṃ keyūraṃ, kaṭakaṃ vā karoti, vīhayo lunāti. Devadatto nivesanaṃ pavisati, ādiccaṃ passati, dhammaṃ suṇāti, paṇḍite payirupāsati.

And that object is of three kinds: what is to be produced (`nibbattanīya`), what is to be altered (`vikaraṇīya`) and what is to be reached (`pāpaṇīya`). For example, `mātā puttaṃ vijāyati` ("the mother gives birth to a son"), `āhāro sukhaṃ janayati` ("food produces pleasure"); `ghaṭaṃ karoti devadatto` ("Devadatta makes a pot"), `kaṭṭhamaṅgāraṃ karoti` ("he makes the wood into charcoal"), `suvaṇṇaṃ keyūraṃ, kaṭakaṃ vā karoti` ("he makes the gold into an armlet or a bracelet"), `vīhayo lunāti` ("he reaps the rice"); `devadatto nivesanaṃ pavisati` ("Devadatta enters the house"), `ādiccaṃ passati` ("he sees the sun"), `dhammaṃ suṇāti` ("he hears the teaching"), `paṇḍite payirupāsati` ("he waits upon the wise").

> Vuttañca

And it is said:

> ‘‘Nibbattivikatippatti-bhedena tividhaṃ mataṃ; \
> Kattu kriyābhigammaṃ taṃ, sukhaṅgāraṃ nivesana’’nti.

"By the division into production, alteration and attainment it is held to be of three kinds: \
what the agent's action reaches — pleasure, charcoal, a dwelling."

> Ettha ca icchitānicchitakathitākathitādibhedamanapekkhitvā sabbasaṅgāhakavasena ‘‘yaṃ karoti taṃ kamma’’nti vuttattā, atthantaravikappanavādhikārato ca sabbattha imināva kammasaññā hoti.

And here, because "what one does is the object" is stated all-inclusively, without regard to the divisions into wished and unwished, stated and unstated and so on, and because the `vā` that opens the option of other senses (`atthantaravikappana`) is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), it is by this rule alone that the term `kamma` is given in every case.

> Tattha anicchitakammaṃ yathā – kaṇṭakaṃ maddati, visaṃ gilati, gāmaṃ gacchanto rukkhamūlaṃ upagacchati.

Among these, an unwished object (`anicchitakamma`) is, for example, `kaṇṭakaṃ maddati` ("he treads on a thorn"), `visaṃ gilati` ("he swallows poison"), `gāmaṃ gacchanto rukkhamūlaṃ upagacchati` ("going to the village, he comes to the foot of a tree").

> Akathitakammaṃ yathā – yaññadattaṃ kambalaṃ yācate brāhmaṇo. Ettha hi ‘‘kambala’’miti kathitakammaṃ dvikammikāya yācanakriyāya pattumicchitatarattā. ‘‘Yaññadatta’’miti appadhānattā akathitakammaṃ. Tathā samiddhaṃ dhanaṃ bhikkhate, ajaṃ gāmaṃ nayati, parābhavantaṃ purisaṃ, mayaṃ pucchāma gotamaṃ, bhagavā bhikkhū etadavoca iccādi.

An unstated object (`akathitakamma`) is, for example, `yaññadattaṃ kambalaṃ yācate brāhmaṇo` ("the brahmin begs Yaññadatta for a blanket"). For here `kambalaṃ` is the stated object (`kathitakamma`), because, for the action of begging, which takes two objects, it is the one more desired to be obtained, while `yaññadattaṃ`, being subordinate (`appadhāna`), is the unstated object. Likewise `samiddhaṃ dhanaṃ bhikkhate` ("he begs the rich man for money"), `ajaṃ gāmaṃ nayati` ("he leads the goat to the village"), `parābhavantaṃ purisaṃ, mayaṃ pucchāma gotamaṃ` ("we ask Gotama about the declining man"), `bhagavā bhikkhū etadavoca` ("the Blessed One said this to the monks") and so on.

> Abhihitakamme pana na hoti, yathā – kaṭo karīyate devadattena, sugatena desito dhammo, yaññadatto kambalaṃ yācīyate brāhmaṇena iccādi.

But when the object is already expressed (`abhihitakamma`), the second form is not used; for example, `kaṭo karīyate devadattena` ("the mat is being made by Devadatta"), `sugatena desito dhammo` ("the teaching taught by the Well-gone One"), `yaññadatto kambalaṃ yācīyate brāhmaṇena` ("Yaññadatta is begged by the brahmin for a blanket") and so on.

> ‘‘Dutiyā’’ti adhikāro.

“`dutiyā`” ("the second") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

### 286 (§300): Gati buddhi bhuja paṭha hara kara sayādīnaṃ kārite vā (optionally ② for the one made to act in a causative) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§300 (286): Gati buddhi bhuja paṭha hara kara sayādīnaṃ kārite vā" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m300" description="optionally ② for the one made to act in a causative" />
:::

> Gamu sappa gatimhi, budha bodhane, budha avagamane vā, bhuja pālanabyavaharaṇesu, paṭha byattiyaṃ vācāyaṃ, hara haraṇe, kara karaṇe, si saye iccevamādīnaṃ dhātūnaṃ payoge kārite sati payojjakakattubhūte kammani liṅgamhā dutiyāvibhatti hoti vā. Niccasampatte vikappatthoyaṃ, tena tassa pakkhe tatiyā hoti.

When a causative (`kārita`) is used with the roots `gamu` and `sappa` in the sense of going, `budha` in the sense of awakening or `budha` in the sense of understanding, `bhuja` in the senses of protecting and eating, `paṭha` in the sense of distinct speech, `hara` in the sense of carrying, `kara` in the sense of doing, `si` in the sense of lying down, and others like them, the second inflection ② is optionally used after the lemma for the object that is the agent set to work (`payojjakakattā`). This `vā` has the sense of an option (`vikappa`) where the second form would apply obligatorily (`niccasampatta`), and so in the other branch of the option the third form ③ is used.

> Yo koci puriso gāmaṃ gacchati, tamañño payojayati. Puriso purisaṃ gāmaṃ gamayati, purisena vā gāmaṃ gamayati. Evaṃ sissaṃ dhammaṃ bodheti ācariyo, mātā puttaṃ bhojanaṃ bhojayati, sissaṃ dhammaṃ pāṭheti ācariyo, puriso purisaṃ bhāraṃ hāreti, tathā puriso purisaṃ kammaṃ kārayati, purisena vā kammaṃ kārāpayati, puriso purisaṃ sayāpayati. Evaṃ sabbattha kārite kattukammani dutiyā.

Some man goes to the village, and another sets him to it: `puriso purisaṃ gāmaṃ gamayati` ("the man makes the man go to the village"), `purisena vā gāmaṃ gamayati` ("or he makes the man go to the village", with the man in the third form ③). Likewise `sissaṃ dhammaṃ bodheti ācariyo` ("the teacher makes the pupil understand the teaching"), `mātā puttaṃ bhojanaṃ bhojayati` ("the mother feeds the son the meal"), `sissaṃ dhammaṃ pāṭheti ācariyo` ("the teacher makes the pupil recite the teaching"), `puriso purisaṃ bhāraṃ hāreti` ("the man makes the man carry the load"); so too `puriso purisaṃ kammaṃ kārayati` ("the man makes the man do the work"), `purisena vā kammaṃ kārāpayati` ("or he has the work done by the man"), `puriso purisaṃ sayāpayati` ("the man makes the man lie down"). So everywhere in a causative the second form is used for the agent that has become the object (`kattukamma`).

> Kārite ti kiṃ? Puriso gāmaṃ gacchati.

By saying `kārite` (in a causative) we exclude `puriso gāmaṃ gacchati` ("the man goes to the village").

> Abhihite na bhavati, purisena puriso gāmaṃ gamīyate, sisso dhammaṃ bodhīyate iccādi.

When already expressed (`abhihite`), the second form is not used: `purisena puriso gāmaṃ gamīyate` ("the man is made by the man to go to the village"), `sisso dhammaṃ bodhīyate` ("the pupil is made to understand the teaching") and so on.

<a name="r287"></a>

### 287 (§298): Kāladdhānamaccantasaṃyoge (② for time and distance in unbroken connection) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§298 (287): Kāladdhānamaccantasaṃyoge" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m298" description="② for time and distance in unbroken connection" />
:::

> Accantaṃ nirantaraṃ saṃyogoaccantasaṃyogo. Kāladdhānaṃ dabbaguṇakriyāhi accantasaṃyoge tehi kāladdhānavācīhi liṅgehi dutiyāvibhatti hoti.

Unbroken connection (`accantasaṃyoga`) is connection that is unbroken (`accanta`), that is, uninterrupted (`nirantara`). When time or distance is in unbroken connection with a thing, a quality or an action, the second inflection ② is used after the lemmas that express that time or distance.

> Kāle tāva – sattāhaṃ gavapānaṃ, māsaṃ maṃsodanaṃ, saradaṃ ramaṇīyā nadī, sabbakālaṃ ramaṇīyaṃ nandanaṃ, māsaṃ sajjhāyati, tayo māse abhidhammaṃ desesi.

First, with time: `sattāhaṃ gavapānaṃ` ("for seven days, the drink of milk-rice"), `māsaṃ maṃsodanaṃ` ("for a month, rice with meat"), `saradaṃ ramaṇīyā nadī` ("through the autumn the river is delightful"), `sabbakālaṃ ramaṇīyaṃ nandanaṃ` ("at all times the Nandana grove is delightful"), `māsaṃ sajjhāyati` ("for a month he recites"), `tayo māse abhidhammaṃ desesi` ("for three months he taught the Abhidhamma").

> Addhāne – yojanaṃ vanarāji, yojanaṃ dīgho pabbato, kosaṃ sajjhāyati.

With distance: `yojanaṃ vanarāji` ("the belt of forest [runs] for a league"), `yojanaṃ dīgho pabbato` ("the mountain is a league long"), `kosaṃ sajjhāyati` ("for a `kosa` [of the way] he recites").

> Accantasaṃyoge ti kiṃ? Māse māse bhuñjati, yojane yojane vihāraṃ patiṭṭhāpesi.

By saying `accantasaṃyoge` (in unbroken connection) we exclude `māse māse bhuñjati` ("he eats every month") and `yojane yojane vihāraṃ patiṭṭhāpesi` ("at every league he established a monastery").

<a name="r288"></a>

### 288 (§299): Kammappavacanīyayutte (② with a `kammappavacanīya` [governing particle]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§299 (288): Kammappavacanīyayutte" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m299" description="② with a `kammappavacanīya` [governing particle]" />
:::

> Kammappavacanīyehi nipātopasaggehi yutte yoge sati liṅgamhā dutiyāvibhatti hoti.

When a lemma is joined in construction with particles or prefixes that are `kammappavacanīya`, the second inflection ② is used after it.

> Kammaṃ pavacanīyaṃ yesaṃ te kammappavacanīyā, parasamaññāvasena vā anvādayo kammappavacanīyā.

`kammappavacanīya` are those by which an action (`kamma`) is to be expressed (`pavacanīya`); or else, by the terminology of others (`parasamaññā`), `anu` and the rest are the `kammappavacanīya`.

> Tattha anu saddassa lakkhaṇe, sahatthe, hīne ca kammappavacanīyasaññā vuttā. Yathā – pabbajitamanu pabbajiṃsu, nadimanvavasitā bārāṇasī, nadiyā saha avabaddhāti attho, anu sāriputtaṃ paññavā.

Among them, the word `anu` is given the term `kammappavacanīya` in the sense of a mark (`lakkhaṇa`), in the sense of "together with" (`sahattha`) and in the sense of inferiority (`hīna`). For example, `pabbajitamanu pabbajiṃsu` ("they went forth after the one who had gone forth"), `nadimanvavasitā bārāṇasī` ("Benares lies along the river"), which means `nadiyā saha avabaddhā` ("bound together with the river"), and `anu sāriputtaṃ paññavā` ("wise, second to Sāriputta").

:::tip[Examples]

> `pabbajitamanupabbajiṃsu` \
> [(7D 1.10: Mahājanakāyaanupabbajjā)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/7D/1/1.10#110) \
🚹②⨀(pabbajita) 🔼(anu) ⏮🤟⨂(pabbajati) \
the one who had gone forth | after | they went forth

:::

> Lakkhaṇādīsu ‘‘lakkhaṇi’tthambhūtakkhānabhāga vicchāsu pati pari anavo’’ti pati pari anūnaṃ kammappavacanīyasaññā vuttā.

In the senses of a mark and the rest, by the rule "`pati`, `pari` and `anu` in the senses of a mark, of the statement of someone's condition, of a share and of distribution", `pati`, `pari` and `anu` are given the term `kammappavacanīya`.

> Lakkhaṇe sūriyuggamanaṃ pati dibbā bhakkhā pātubhaveyyuṃ, rukkhaṃ pati vijjotate cando, rukkhaṃ pari, rukkhaṃ anu.

In the sense of a mark (`lakkhaṇa`): `sūriyuggamanaṃ pati dibbā bhakkhā pātubhaveyyuṃ` ("towards sunrise heavenly foods may appear"), `rukkhaṃ pati vijjotate cando` ("the moon shines towards the tree"), `rukkhaṃ pari` ("round the tree"), `rukkhaṃ anu` ("along the tree").

:::tip[Examples]

> `sūriyuggamanaṃ pati dibbā bhakkhā pātubhaveyyuṃ` \
> [(19Pv 2.9: Aṅkurapetavatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/19Pv/2/2.9#322) \
🚻②⨀(sūriyuggamana) 🔼(pati) 🚹①⨂(dibba) 🚹①⨂(bhakkha) ⏯🤟⨂(pātubhavati) \
sunrise | towards | heavenly | foods | may appear

:::

> Itthambhūtakkhāne sādhu devadatto mātaraṃ pati, mātaraṃ pari, mātaraṃ anu.

In the statement of someone's condition (`itthambhūtakkhāna`): `sādhu devadatto mātaraṃ pati` ("Devadatta is good towards his mother"), `mātaraṃ pari`, `mātaraṃ anu` ("towards his mother").

> Bhāge yadettha maṃ pati siyā, maṃ pari, maṃ anu, taṃ dīyatu.

In the sense of a share (`bhāga`): `yadettha maṃ pati siyā, taṃ dīyatu` ("whatever here would fall to me, let that be given"), or `maṃ pari`, `maṃ anu` ("to me").

> Vicchāyoge atthamatthaṃ pati saddo nivisati, rukkhaṃ rukkhaṃ pati vijjotate cando, rukkhaṃ rukkhaṃ pari, rukkhaṃ rukkhaṃ anu.

In construction with distribution (`vicchā`): `atthamatthaṃ pati saddo nivisati` ("a word settles on each meaning in turn"), `rukkhaṃ rukkhaṃ pati vijjotate cando` ("the moon shines on tree after tree"), `rukkhaṃ rukkhaṃ pari`, `rukkhaṃ rukkhaṃ anu` ("round each tree", "along each tree").

> ‘‘Abhirabhāge’’ti abhissa bhāgavajjitesu lakkhaṇādīsu kammappavacanīyasaññā vuttā. Taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato, sādhu devadatto mātaraṃ abhi.

By the rule "`abhi`, except in the sense of a share", `abhi` is given the term `kammappavacanīya` in the senses of a mark and the rest, the sense of a share excepted: `taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato` ("now about that venerable Gotama such a fair report has spread"), `sādhu devadatto mātaraṃ abhi` ("Devadatta is good towards his mother").

:::tip[Examples]

> `taṃ kho pana bhavantaṃ gotamaṃ evaṃ kalyāṇo kittisaddo abbhuggato` \
> [(1V Veranjakanda: Verañjakaṇḍa)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/Veranjakanda#2) \
🚹②⨀(ta) 🔼(kho) 🔼(pana) 🚹②⨀(bhavanta) 🚹②⨀(gotama) 🔼(evaṃ) 🚹①⨀(kalyāṇa) 🚹①⨀(kittisadda) 🚹①⨀(abbhuggata) \
that | indeed | now | the venerable | Gotama | such | a fair | report | has spread about

:::

> Nipāte dhi brāhmaṇassa hantāra miccevamādi.

With a particle (`nipāta`): `dhi brāhmaṇassa hantāraṃ` ("fie upon the killer of a brahmin") and so on.

:::tip[Example]

> `dhī brāhmaṇassa hantāraṃ` \
> [(18Dh 26.7: Sāriputtattheravatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/26/26.7#417) \
🔼(dhī) 🚹⑥⨀(brāhmaṇa) 🚹②⨀(hantu) \
fie | of a brahmin | upon the killer

:::

<a name="r292"></a>

### 292 (§279): Yena vā kayirate taṃ karaṇaṃ (that by which [the action] is done: `karaṇa`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§279 (292): Yena vā kayirate taṃ karaṇaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m279" description="that by which [the action] is done: `karaṇa`" />
:::

> Yena vā kattā upakaraṇabhūtena vatthunā kriyaṃ abyavadhānena karoti, yena vā vikaroti, yena vā pāpuṇāti, taṃ kārakaṃ karaṇasaññaṃ hoti.

The thing serving as an aid (`upakaraṇa`) by which the agent does the action without anything intervening (`abyavadhāna`), or by which he alters or reaches [it] — that `kāraka` is termed the instrument (`karaṇa`).

> Karīyate anenāti karaṇaṃ, ettha ca satipi sabbakārakānaṃ kriyāsādhakatte ‘‘yena vā kayirate’’ti visesetvā vacanaṃ kattūpakaraṇabhūtesu kārakesu sādhakatamasseva gahaṇatthaṃ.

The instrument (`karaṇa`) is that by which [the action] is done (`karīyate anena`). And here, although all the `kāraka` accomplish the action, the specific wording "that by which it is done" is there to take in only the most effective (`sādhakatama`) among the `kāraka` that serve as the agent's aids.

> Vuttañca

And it is said:

> ‘‘Yassa sabbavisesena, kriyāsaṃsiddhihetutā; \
> Sambhāvīyati taṃ vuttaṃ, karaṇaṃ nāma kāraka’’nti.

"That whose being the cause of the action's accomplishment \
is conceived as surpassing all the rest is the `kāraka` called the instrument (`karaṇa`)."

> Taṃ pana duvidhaṃ ajjhattika bāhiravasena.

And it is of two kinds, internal (`ajjhattika`) and external (`bāhira`).

> Yathā – hatthena kammaṃ karoti, cakkhunā rūpaṃ passati, manasā dhammaṃ viññāya. Dattena vīhayo lunāti, agginā kuṭiṃ jhāpeti.

For example, `hatthena kammaṃ karoti` ("he does the work with his hand"), `cakkhunā rūpaṃ passati` ("he sees a form with the eye"), `manasā dhammaṃ viññāya` ("having cognized a mental object with the mind"); `dattena vīhayo lunāti` ("he reaps the rice with a sickle"), `agginā kuṭiṃ jhāpeti` ("he burns the hut with fire").

:::tip[Examples]

> `cakkhunā rūpaṃ passati` \
> [(13S4 1.3.4.2: Khaṇasutta)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/13S4/1/1.3/1.3.4/1.3.4.2#777) \
🚻③⨀(cakkhu) 🚻②⨀(rūpa) ▶️🤟⨀(passati) \
with the eye | a form | he sees

> `manasā dhammaṃ viññāya` \
> [(6D 2.4.3.2.1: Indriyasaṃvara)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/6D/2/2.4/2.4.3/2.4.3.2/2.4.3.2.1#304) \
🚹③⨀(mana) 🚹②⨀(dhamma) 🔽(viññāya) \
with the mind | a mental object | having cognized

:::

> ‘‘Tatiyā’’ti adhikāro.

“`tatiyā`” ("the third") is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

### 293 (§288): Kattari ca (and for the agent) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§288 (293): Kattari ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m288" description="and for the agent" />
:::

> Kattari ca kārake liṅgamhā tatiyāvibhatti hoti. Caggahaṇena itthambhūtalakkhaṇe, kriyāpavagge, pubbasadisasamūnattha kalaha nipuṇa missaka sakhilatthādiyoge, kāladdhānesu, paccattakammatthapañcamiyatthādīsu ca tatiyā.

The third inflection ③ is used after the lemma for the agent (`kattā`) `kāraka` as well. By the mention of `ca`, the third form is used also for the mark of someone's condition (`itthambhūtalakkhaṇa`), for the completion of an action (`kriyāpavagga`), in construction with words in the sense of `pubba` (earlier), `sadisa` (like), `sama` (equal) and `ūna` (less), with `kalaha` (quarrel), `nipuṇa` (skilled) and `missaka` (mixed), and with words in the sense of `sakhila` (kindly) and the like, for time and distance, and in the senses of the first form (`paccatta`), of the object, of the fifth form and so on.

> Ko ca kattā?

And what is the agent (`kattā`)?

<a name="r294"></a>

### 294 (§281): Yo karoti sa kattā (the one who does: `kattā`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§281 (294): Yo karoti sa kattā" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m281" description="the one who does: `kattā`" />
:::

> Yo kriyaṃ attappadhāno hutvā karoti, so kattusañño hoti.

Whoever does the action as the principal (`attappadhāna`) is termed `kattā` (the agent).

> So tividho suddhakattā hetukattā kammakattāti. Tattha yo sayameva kriyaṃ karoti, so suddhakattā. Yo aññaṃ kātuṃ samatthaṃ akarontaṃ kammaṃ niyojeti, so hetukattā, yathā – gantuṃ samattho devadatto, tamañño payojeti ‘‘gamayati devadatta’’nti.

The agent is of three kinds: the plain agent (`suddhakattā`), the causal agent (`hetukattā`) and the object-agent (`kammakattā`). Of these, the one who does the action by himself is the plain agent. The one who sets to a task another who is able to do it but is not doing it is the causal agent; for example, `gantuṃ samattho devadatto` ("Devadatta is able to go"), and another sets him to it: `gamayati devadattaṃ` ("he makes Devadatta go").

> Yaṃ pana tattha tattha gacchati devadatto, tamañño payojayati ‘‘gamayati devadatta’’nti hetvatthanidassanaṃ, tampi sāmatthiyadassanavasena vuttanti gahetabbaṃ. Aññathā yadi sayameva gacchati, kiṃ tattha payojakabyāpārena akarontaṃ balena kārayati, pāsāṇaṃ uṭṭhāpayatītiādikañca na sijjheyya.

As for the illustration of the causative sense (`hetvattha`) given here and there — `gacchati devadatto` ("Devadatta goes"), and another sets him to it: `gamayati devadattaṃ` ("he makes Devadatta go") — it too should be taken as stated to show his ability (`sāmatthiya`). Otherwise, if he goes by himself, what would there be for the instigator's activity to do? And `akarontaṃ balena kārayati` ("he forcibly makes one who is not acting act"), `pāsāṇaṃ uṭṭhāpayati` ("he makes the stone rise") and the like could not be accounted for.

> Ettha pana

And here

> ‘‘Kattā’’ti vattate.

“`kattā`” continues.

<a name="r295"></a>

### 295 (§282): Yo kāreti sa hetu (the one who makes [the agent] act: `hetu`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§282 (295): Yo kāreti sa hetu" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m282" description="the one who makes [the agent] act: `hetu`" />
:::

> Yo kattāraṃ kāreti, so hetusañño hoti, kattā cāti hetukattusaññā.

The one who makes the agent act is termed `hetu` (the cause), and `kattā` (agent) as well: hence the term `hetukattā` (causal agent).

> Yo pana parassa kriyaṃ paṭicca kammabhūtopi sukarattā sayameva sijjhanto viya hoti, so kammakattā nāma, yathā – sayaṃ karīyate kaṭo, sayameva paccate odanoti.

But what is called the object-agent (`kammakattā`) is that which, though it is an object depending on another's action, seems to be accomplished by itself because it is easily done (`sukara`); for example, `sayaṃ karīyate kaṭo` ("the mat gets made by itself"), `sayameva paccate odano` ("the rice cooks by itself").

> Vuttañca

And it is said:

> ‘‘Attappadhāno kiriyaṃ, yo nibbatteti kārako; \
> Appayutto payutto vā, sa kattāti pavuccati.

"The `kāraka` that brings the action about as the principal, \
whether not set to it or set to it [by another], is called the agent (`kattā`).

> Hetukattāti kathito, \
> Kattuno yo payojako; \
> Kammakattāti sukaro, \
> Kammabhūto kathīyate’’ti.

The one who sets the agent to work \
is called the causal agent (`hetukattā`); \
an object that is easily done \
is called the object-agent (`kammakattā`)."

> Nanu ca ‘‘saṃyogo jāyate’’tiādīsu kathaṃ pure asato jananakriyāya kattubhāvosiyāti? Vuccate – lokasaṅketasiddho hi saddappayogo, avijjamānampi hi loko saddābhidheyyatāya vijjamānaṃ viya gahetvā voharati, vikappabuddhigahitākāroyeva hi saddenābhidhīyate, na tu vatthusabhāvo, aññathā sutamayañāṇenapi paccakkhena viya vatthusabhāvasacchikaraṇappasaṅgo ca musāvāda kudiṭṭhivādādīnamabhāvappasaṅgo ca siyā, tasmā buddhiparikappitapaññattivasenapi saddappavatti hotīti asato saṃyogādissapi hoteva jananakriyāya kattukārakatāti.

But, it may be objected, how in `saṃyogo jāyate` ("a contact arises") and the like can what did not exist before be the agent of the action of arising? The answer is that the use of words is established by worldly convention (`lokasaṅketa`): the world speaks even of what does not exist, treating it as if it existed, because it can be denoted by a word; for what a word denotes is only the form grasped by the conceiving mind (`vikappabuddhi`), not the real nature of the thing. Otherwise it would follow that the real nature of a thing could be realized by knowledge born of hearing as if by direct perception, and that false speech, wrong views and the like could not exist. Therefore, since words occur also by way of concepts constructed by the mind, a contact and the like, though non-existent, is indeed the agent `kāraka` of the action of arising.

> Yathāha

As [the verse] says:

> ‘‘Vohāravisayo saddo, nekantaparamatthiko; \
> Buddhisaṅkappito attho, tassatthoti pavuccati.

"A word has convention as its province and does not always bear on what is ultimately real; \
the meaning conceived by the mind is what is called its meaning.

> Buddhiyā gahitattā hi, saṃyogo jāyate iti; \
> Saṃyogo vijjamānova, kattā bhavati jātiyā’’ti.

For because it is grasped by the mind, in 'a contact arises' \
the contact, as though it existed, is the agent of the arising."

> Tatra tatiyā jinena desito dhammo, buddhena jito māro, ahinā daṭṭho naro, buddhena bodhito loko, saddhehi kārito vihāro.

There, the third form ③ is used: `jinena desito dhammo` ("the teaching taught by the Conqueror"), `buddhena jito māro` ("Māra conquered by the Buddha"), `ahinā daṭṭho naro` ("the man bitten by a snake"), `buddhena bodhito loko` ("the world awakened by the Buddha"), `saddhehi kārito vihāro` ("the monastery the faithful had built").

### 302 (§276): Yassa dātukāmo rocate dhārayate vā taṃ sampadānaṃ (the one to whom one wishes to give, who is pleased, or to whom one owes: `sampadāna`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§276 (302): Yassa dātukāmo rocate dhārayate vā taṃ sampadānaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m276" description="the one to whom one wishes to give, who is pleased, or to whom one owes: `sampadāna`" />
:::

> Yassa vā dātukāmo, yassa vā rocate, yassa vā dhārayate, taṃ kārakaṃ sampadānasaññaṃ hoti. Sammā padīyate assāti sampadānaṃ, paṭiggāhako.

The one to whom [someone] wishes to give, the one whom [something] pleases, or the one to whom [someone] owes — that `kāraka` is termed `sampadāna` (the recipient). It is called `sampadāna` because [something] is duly handed over (`sammā padīyate`) to it: the receiver (`paṭiggāhaka`).

> Taṃ pana tividhaṃ diyyamānassānivāraṇajjhesanānumativasena. Yathā – buddhassa pupphaṃ yajati, bodhirukkhassa jalaṃ dadāti. Ajjhesane – yācakānaṃ dhanaṃ dadāti. Anumatiyaṃ – bhikkhūnaṃ dānaṃ deti.

It is, moreover, of three kinds: by the non-refusal (`anivāraṇa`) of what is being given, by request (`ajjhesanā`) and by consent (`anumati`). For example: `buddhassa pupphaṃ yajati` ("he offers a flower to the Buddha"), `bodhirukkhassa jalaṃ dadāti` ("he gives water to the Bodhi tree"). By request: `yācakānaṃ dhanaṃ dadāti` ("he gives money to beggars"). By consent: `bhikkhūnaṃ dānaṃ deti` ("he gives the monks a gift").

> Yathāha

As it is said:

> ‘‘Anirākaraṇārādha-nābbhanuññavasena hi; \
> Sampadānaṃ tidhā vuttaṃ, rukkha yācaka bhikkhavo’’ti.

"For by way of non-refusal, of soliciting and of consent \
the recipient is said to be threefold: the tree, the beggar, the monks."

> Dātukāmo ti kiṃ? Rañño daṇḍaṃ dadāti.

By saying `dātukāmo` (wishing to give) we exclude the one to whom something is given unwillingly: `rañño daṇḍaṃ dadāti` ("he pays the king's fine").

> Rocanā dīsu pana – samaṇassa rocate saccaṃ, māyasmantānampi saṅghabhedo ruccittha, yassāyasmato khamati, devadattassa suvaṇṇacchattaṃ dhārayate yaññadatto.

But with `rocate` and the rest: `samaṇassa rocate saccaṃ` ("truth is pleasing to the ascetic"), `māyasmantānampi saṅghabhedo ruccittha` ("let not a schism be pleasing to the venerable ones too"), `yassāyasmato khamati` ("to whichever venerable one it is acceptable"), `devadattassa suvaṇṇacchattaṃ dhārayate yaññadatto` ("Yaññadatta owes Devadatta a golden parasol").

:::tip[Examples]

> `māyasmantānampi saṃghabhedo ruccittha` \
> [(1V 1.2.11: Bhedānuvattakasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.2/1.2.11#1706) \
🔼(mā) 🚹④⨂(āyasmantu) 🔼(api) 🚹①⨀(saṅghabheda) ⏮🤟⨀(ruccati) \
let not | to the venerable ones | too | a schism | be pleasing

> `yassāyasmato khamati` \
> [(1V 1.2.10: Saṃghabhedasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.2/1.2.10#1691) \
🚹④⨀(ya) 🚹④⨀(āyasmantu) ▶️🤟⨀(khamati) \

:::

> ‘‘Sampadānaṃ, vā’’ti ca vattate.

### 303 (§277): Silāgha hanu ṭhā sapa dhāra piha kudha duhi ssāsūya rādhikkha paccāsuṇa anupatigiṇapubbakattārocanatthatadattha tumatthālamattha maññānādarappāṇini gatyatthakammani āsisattha sammuti bhiyyasattamyatthesu ca (and twenty-odd further constructions that take the term `sampadāna`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§277 (303): Silāgha hanu ṭhā sapa dhāra piha kudha duhisso ssūya rādhikkha paccāsuṇa anupatigiṇa pubbakattārocanattha tadattha tumatthālamattha maññānādarappāṇini gatyatthakammaniāsisatthasammutibhiyyasattamyatthesu ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m277" description="and twenty-odd further constructions that take the term `sampadāna`" />
:::

> Catuppadamidaṃ. Silāgha katthane, hanu apanayane, ṭhā gatinivattimhi, sapa akkose, dhara dhāraṇe, piha icchāyaṃ iccetesaṃ dhātūnaṃ payoge, kudha kope, duha jighaṃsāyaṃ, issa issāyaṃ, usūya dosāvikaraṇe iccetesaṃ tadatthavācīnañca dhātūnaṃ payoge ca rādha hiṃsāsaṃrādhesu, ikkha dassanaṅkesūti imesaṃ payoge ca pati ā pubbassa su savaṇeti imassa ca anupati pubbassa ge saddeti imassa ca pubbakattā ca ārocanatthappayoge, tadatthe, tumatthe, alamatthappayoge ca maññatippayoge anādare appāṇini ca gatyatthānaṃ kammani ca āsisatthappayoge ca sammuti bhiyyappayogesu ca sattamyatthe cāti taṃ kammādikārakaṃ sampadānasaññaṃ hoti, ca saddaggahaṇena pahiṇatikappati pahoti upamāñjalikaraṇa phāsu atthaseyyappabhutiyoge ca pure viya catutthī.

This [sutta] has four lines. That `kāraka` — the object and the rest — is termed `sampadāna` in construction with the roots `silāgha` in the sense of praising, `hanu` in the sense of denying, `ṭhā` in the sense of stopping a movement, `sapa` in the sense of cursing, `dhara` in the sense of holding and `piha` in the sense of wishing; in construction with the roots `kudha` in the sense of anger, `duha` in the sense of wishing to harm, `issa` in the sense of jealousy and `usūya` in the sense of disclosing faults, and with roots that express those same senses; in construction with `rādha` in the senses of harming and satisfying, and `ikkha` in the senses of seeing and marking; for the prior agent (`pubbakattā`), with `su` in the sense of hearing preceded by `pati` and `ā`, and with `ge` in the sense of sounding preceded by `anu` and `pati`; in construction with words meaning announcing; in the sense of purpose (`tadattha`); in the sense of an infinitive (`tumattha`); in construction with `alaṃ`; in construction with `maññati`, in contempt and regarding an inanimate thing; for the object of roots meaning going; in construction with words of wishing well; in construction with `sammuti` and `bhiyya`; and in the sense of the seventh. And by the mention of the word `ca`, the fourth form ④ is used as before in construction with `pahiṇati`, `kappati`, `pahoti`, `upamā`, `añjalikaraṇa`, `phāsu`, `attha`, `seyya` and so on.

> Silāghādippayoge tāva – buddhassa silāghate. Upajjhāyassa silāghate, thometīti attho.

First, in construction with `silāgha` and the rest: `buddhassa silāghate` ("he praises the Buddha"), `upajjhāyassa silāghate` ("he praises his preceptor"); the meaning is `thometi` ("he extols").

> Hanute mayhameva, hanute tuyhameva, apalapatīti attho.

`hanute mayhameva` ("he denies [it] to me alone"), `hanute tuyhameva` ("he denies [it] to you alone"); the meaning is `apalapati` ("he disowns").

> Upatiṭṭheyya sakyaputtānaṃ vaḍḍhakī, ettha ca upaṭṭhānaṃ nāma upagamanaṃ. Bhikkhussa bhuñjamānassa pānīyena vā vidhūpanena vā upatiṭṭheyya.

`upatiṭṭheyya sakyaputtānaṃ vaḍḍhakī` ("the carpenter would attend on the sons of the Sakyans"), and here attending (`upaṭṭhāna`) means approaching (`upagamana`); `bhikkhussa bhuñjamānassa pānīyena vā vidhūpanena vā upatiṭṭheyya` ("she should attend on a monk while he eats, with drinking water or with a fan").

🚹④⨀(bhikkhu) 🚹④⨀(bhuñjanta) 🚻③⨀(pānīya) 🔼(vā) 🚻③⨀(vidhūpana) 🔼(vā) ⏯🤟⨀(upatiṭṭhati) \
on a monk | while eating | with drinking water | or | with a fan | or | should [she] attend

> Tuyhaṃ sapate, mayhaṃ sapate, ettha ca sapanaṃ nāma saccakaraṇaṃ.

`tuyhaṃ sapate` ("he swears to you"), `mayhaṃ sapate` ("he swears to me"); and here swearing (`sapana`) means taking an oath (`saccakaraṇa`).

> Dhārayatippayoge dhanikoyeva sampadānaṃ, suvaṇṇaṃ te dhārayate, iṇaṃ dhārayatīti attho. Tassa rañño mayaṃ nāgaṃ dhārayāma.

In construction with `dhārayati`, only the creditor (`dhanika`) is the `sampadāna`: `suvaṇṇaṃ te dhārayate` ("he owes you gold"), the meaning being `iṇaṃ dhārayati` ("he holds a debt"); `tassa rañño mayaṃ nāgaṃ dhārayāma` ("we owe that king an elephant").

In construction with `piha`, only the one longed for [is the `sampadāna`]: `devāpi tassa pihayanti tādino` ("even the gods long for such a one"), `devāpi tesaṃ pihayanti, sambuddhānaṃ satīmataṃ` ("even the gods long for them, the awakened and mindful"); the meaning is `patthenti` ("they long for").

> Paccāsuṇa anupatigiṇānaṃ pubbakattā ca suṇotissa dhātussa paccāyoge, giṇassa ca anupatiyoge pubbassa kammuno yo kattā, so sampadānasañño hoti. Yathā – bhagavā bhikkhū etadavoca, ettha ‘‘bhikkhū’’ti akathitakammaṃ, ‘‘eta’’nti kathitakammaṃ, pubbassa vacanakammassa kattā bhagavā. Bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ, āsuṇanti buddhassa bhikkhū, tathā bhikkhu janaṃ dhammaṃ sāveti, tassa bhikkhuno jano anugiṇāti, tassa bhikkhuno jano patigiṇāti, sādhukāradānādinā taṃ ussāhayatīti attho.

And the prior agent of `paccāsuṇa` and `anupatigiṇa`: in the construction of the root `su` with `pati` and `ā`, and of `ge` with `anu` and `pati`, the agent of the prior action is termed `sampadāna`. For example, in `bhagavā bhikkhū etadavoca` ("the Blessed One said this to the monks"), `bhikkhū` is the unstated object (`akathitakamma`), `etaṃ` the stated object (`kathitakamma`), and the agent of the prior action of speaking is the Blessed One: `bhikkhū bhagavato paccassosuṃ` ("the monks assented to the Blessed One"), `āsuṇanti buddhassa bhikkhū` ("the monks listen assenting to the Buddha"). > Ārocanatthappayoge yassa āroceti, taṃ sampadānaṃ. Ārocayāmi vo bhikkhave, paṭivedayāmi vo bhikkhave, āmantayāmi te mahārāja, āmanta kho taṃ gacchāmāti vā. Ettha ca ārocana saddassa kathanappakāratthattā desanatthādippayogepi catutthī. Dhammaṃ vo desessāmi, desetu bhante bhagavā dhammaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ, yathā no bhagavā byākareyya, niruttiṃ te pavakkhāmi iccādi.

In construction with words meaning announcing, the one to whom one announces is the `sampadāna`: `ārocayāmi vo bhikkhave` ("I announce to you, monks"), `paṭivedayāmi vo bhikkhave` ("I make known to you, monks"), `āmantayāmi te mahārāja` ("I address you, great king"), or `āmanta kho taṃ gacchāma` ("taking leave of you, we go"). And here, since the word `ārocana` (announcing) has the sense of a kind of telling, the fourth form ④ is used also in construction with words meaning teaching and the like: `dhammaṃ vo desessāmi` ("I shall teach you the Dhamma"), `desetu bhante bhagavā dhammaṃ bhikkhūnaṃ` ("venerable sir, let the Blessed One teach the Dhamma to the monks"), `yathā no bhagavā byākareyya` ("as the Blessed One would explain to us"), `niruttiṃ te pavakkhāmi` ("I shall tell you the explanation") and so on.

> Tadatthe sampadānasaññā, catutthī ca.

In the sense of purpose (`tadattha`), the term `sampadāna` applies and the fourth form ④ is used.

### 304 (§109): Āya catutthekavacanassa tu (`āya` for ④⨀ inflections) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§109 (304): Āya catutthekavacanassatu" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m109" description="`āya` for ④⨀ inflections" />

:::note[Analysis]

`ato` and `vā`, carried into this rule, come from its Kaccāyana neighbours in the Nāmakaṇḍa: `ato` from [79 (§103): Ato nena](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r79) and `vā` from [90 (§108): Smāsmiṃna vā](/rupasiddhi/2-namakanda/1-pullinga#r90).

> A kārantato liṅgamhā parassa catutthekavacanassa āyā deso hoti vā, saralopādi.

After a lemma ending in `a`, the singular inflection of the fourth form ④⨀ that follows is optionally replaced by `āya`, with the elision of the vowel and the rest.

:::

> Buddhassatthāya dhammassatthāya saṅghassatthāya jīvitaṃ pariccajāmi, piṇḍapātaṃ paṭisevāmi neva davāya na madāya na maṇḍanāya na vibhūsanāya, ūnassa pāripūriyā, atthāya hitāya sukhāya saṃvattati.

> Alaṃ saddassa atthā arahapaṭikkhepā. Arahatthe – alaṃ me rajjaṃ, alaṃ bhikkhu pattassa, akkhadhutto purisapuggalo nālaṃ dārabharaṇāya, alaṃ mallo mallassa, arahati mallo mallassa.

The meanings of the word `alaṃ` are fitness (`araha`) and refusal (`paṭikkhepa`). In the sense of fitness: `alaṃ me rajjaṃ` ("kingship is fitting for me"), `alaṃ bhikkhu pattassa` ("the monk is worthy of the bowl"), `akkhadhutto purisapuggalo nālaṃ dārabharaṇāya` ("a person who is a gambler is not fit to maintain a wife"), `alaṃ mallo mallassa` ("the wrestler is a match for the wrestler"), `arahati mallo mallassa` ("the wrestler is fit for the wrestler").

> Paṭikkhepe – alaṃ te idha vāsena, alaṃ me hiraññasuvaṇṇena, kiṃ me ekena tiṇṇena, kiṃ te jaṭāhi dummedha, kiṃ tettha catumaṭṭhassa.

In the sense of refusal: `alaṃ te idha vāsena` ("enough of your staying here"), `alaṃ me hiraññasuvaṇṇena` ("enough of unwrought and wrought gold for me"), `kiṃ me ekena tiṇṇena` ("what use to me is crossing over alone?"), `kiṃ te jaṭāhi dummedha` ("what use to you are matted locks, fool?"), `kiṃ tettha catumaṭṭhassa` ("what is there here for you, Catumaṭṭha?").

> Maññatippayoge anādare appāṇini kammaniyeva – kaṭṭhassa tuvaṃ maññe, kaḷiṅgarassa tuvaṃ maññe, jīvitaṃ tiṇāyapi na maññamāno.

> Anādare ti kiṃ? Suvaṇṇaṃ taṃ maññe. Appāṇinī ti kiṃ? Gadrabhaṃ tuvaṃ maññe.

By saying `anādare` (in contempt) we exclude regard: `suvaṇṇaṃ taṃ maññe` ("I deem that gold"). By saying `appāṇini` (inanimate) we exclude a living being: `gadrabhaṃ tuvaṃ maññe` ("I take you for a donkey").

them | to you | I make plain | asked face to face

> Ca saddaggahaṇena pahiṇādikriyāyoge, phāsuādināmapayoge ca – tassa pahiṇeyya, bhikkhūnaṃ dūtaṃ pāhesi, kappati samaṇānaṃ āyogo, ekassa dinnaṃ dvinnaṃ tiṇṇaṃ pahoti, upamaṃ te karissāmi, añjaliṃ te paggaṇhāmi. Tathā tassa phāsu hoti, lokassattho, maṇinā me attho, seyyo me attho iccādi.

By the mention of the word `ca`, in construction with the verbs `pahiṇati` and the rest, and with the nouns `phāsu` and the rest: `tassa pahiṇeyya` ("he should send to him"), `bhikkhūnaṃ dūtaṃ pāhesi` ("he sent a messenger to the monks"), `kappati samaṇānaṃ āyogo` ("the practice is allowable for ascetics"), `ekassa dinnaṃ dvinnaṃ tiṇṇaṃ pahoti` ("what is given to one suffices for two or three"), `upamaṃ te karissāmi` ("I shall make a simile for you"), `añjaliṃ te paggaṇhāmi` ("I raise my joined hands to you"). Likewise `tassa phāsu hoti` ("it is comfortable for him"), `lokassattho` ("benefit to the world"), `maṇinā me attho` ("I have need of a jewel"), `seyyo me attho` ("the better thing is my aim") and so on.

:::tip[Examples]

> `na kappati samaṇānaṃ sakyaputtiyānaṃ evarūpaṃ kātuṃ` \

🔼(na) ▶️🤟⨀(kappati) 🚹④⨂(samaṇa) 🚹④⨂(sakyaputtiya) 🚻②⨀(evarūpa) 🔼(kātuṃ) \

`kāle` and `bhavissati`, carried into this rule, come from its Kaccāyana neighbours, which Buddhappiya states in the Kibbidhānakaṇḍa: [651 (§650): Kāle vattamānātīte ṇvādayo](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/6-unadi#r651) and [647 (§651): Bhavissati gamādīhi ṇī ghiṇa](/rupasiddhi/7-kibbidhanakanda/5-vattamana-anagata#r647).

<a name="r308"></a>

### 308 (§271): Yasmādapeti bhayamādatte vā tadapādānaṃ (that from which one departs, fears or takes: `apādāna`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§271 (88, 308): Yasmā dapeti bhayamādatte vā tadapādānaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m271" description="that from which one departs, fears or takes: `apādāna`" />
:::

> Yasmā vā avadhito apeti, yasmā vā bhayahetuto bhayaṃ bhavati, yasmā vā akkhātārā vijjaṃ ādadāti, taṃ kārakaṃ apādānasaññaṃ hoti. Apanetvā ito ādadātīti apādānaṃ.

The limit (`avadhi`) from which one departs, the cause of fear from which fear arises, or the expounder (`akkhātā`) from whom one takes knowledge: that `kāraka` is termed `apādāna` (the point of departure). It is called `apādāna` because one takes [something] away from it (`apanetvā ito ādadāti`).

> Taṃ pana tividhaṃ visayabhedena niddiṭṭhavisayaṃ, upāttavisayaṃ, anumeyyavisayañcāti.

Moreover, by the difference of its province (`visaya`) it is of three kinds: of stated province (`niddiṭṭhavisaya`), of implied province (`upāttavisaya`) and of inferred province (`anumeyyavisaya`).

> Apādānasaññāvisayassa kriyāvisesassa niddiṭṭhattā niddiṭṭhavisayaṃ. Yathā – gāmā apenti munayo, nagarā niggato rājā.

It is of stated province when the particular action that is the province of the term `apādāna` is itself stated. For example: `gāmā apenti munayo` ("the sages depart from the village"), `nagarā niggato rājā` ("the king has gone out from the city").

> Ettha ca ‘‘pāpā cittaṃ nivāraye, pāpā nivārentī’’tiādīsu yadipi kāyasaṃyogapubbakāpagamanaṃ natthi, tathāpi cittasaṃyogapubbakassa apagamanassa sambhavato iminā ca apādānasaññā.

And here, in `pāpā cittaṃ nivāraye` ("one should restrain the mind from evil"), `pāpā nivārenti` ("they restrain [him] from evil") and the like, although there is no departure preceded by bodily contact (`kāyasaṃyoga`), a departure preceded by mental contact (`cittasaṃyoga`) is still possible, and so the term `apādāna` is given by this rule as well.

:::tip[Examples]

> `pāpā cittaṃ nivāraye` \
> [(18Dh 9.1: Cūḷekasāṭakabrāhmaṇavatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/9/9.1#125) \
🚻⑤⨀(pāpa) 🚻②⨀(citta) ⏯🤟⨀(nivāreti) \
from evil | the mind | one should hold back

> `pāpā nivārenti` \
> [(8D 8.13: Chaddisāpaṭicchādanakaṇḍa)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/8D/8/8.13#624) \
🚻⑤⨀(pāpa) ▶️🤟⨂(nivāreti) \
from evil | they keep [him] back

:::

> Yattha pana apagamanakriyaṃ upāttaṃ ajjhāhaṭaṃ visayaṃ katvā pavattati, taṃ upāttavisayaṃ. Yathā – valāhakā vijjotate vijju, kusūlato pacatīti. Ettha ca ‘‘valāhakā nikkhamma, kusūlato apanetvā’’ti ca pubbakriyā ajjhāharīyati.

It is of implied province where [the term] operates by making an implied (`upātta`), that is, supplied (`ajjhāhaṭa`), action of departure its province. For example: `valāhakā vijjotate vijju` ("the lightning flashes from the cloud"), `kusūlato pacati` ("he cooks from the granary"). Here the prior action `valāhakā nikkhamma` ("having come out of the cloud") or `kusūlato apanetvā` ("having taken [it] out of the granary") is supplied.

> Anumeyyavisayaṃ yathā – māthurā pāṭaliputtakehi abhirūpā. Ettha hi kenaci guṇena ukkaṃsīyantīti anumeyyova kriyāviseso. Idha pana dūrantikā disutte vibhatta ggahaṇena apādānasaññā.

Of inferred province, for example: `māthurā pāṭaliputtakehi abhirūpā` ("the Mathurans are handsomer than the Pāṭaliputrans"). For here the particular action — that they are exalted above [the others] by some quality — can only be inferred. In this case, however, the term `apādāna` comes from the mention of `vibhatta` ("distinction") in the rule [312 (§275): Dūrantikaddhakālanimmānatvālopadisāyogavibhattārappayoga suddhappamocana hetu vivittappamāṇa pubbayoga bandhana guṇavacana pañha kathanathokākattūsu ca](/rupasiddhi/3-karakakanda#r312).

> Vuttañca

And it is said:

> ‘‘Niddiṭṭhavisayaṃ kiñci, upāttavisayaṃ tathā; \
> Anumeyyavisayañcāti, apādānaṃ tidhā mata’’nti.

"Some is of stated province, some likewise of implied province, \
and some of inferred province: thus the `apādāna` is held to be threefold."

> Tadeva calācalavasena duvidhampi hoti.

The same is also twofold, as moving (`cala`) and unmoving (`acala`).

> Calaṃ yathā – dhāvatā hatthimhā patito aṅkusadhārī.

Moving, for example: `dhāvatā hatthimhā patito aṅkusadhārī` ("the goad-bearer fell from the running elephant").

> Acalaṃ yathā – pabbatā otaranti vanacarā.

Unmoving, for example: `pabbatā otaranti vanacarā` ("the foresters come down from the mountain").

> Bhayahetumhi – corā bhayaṃ jāyati, taṇhāya jāyatī bhayaṃ, pāpato uttasati, akkhātari – upajjhāyā sikkhaṃ gaṇhāti, ācariyamhā adhīte, ācariyato suṇāti.

For the cause of fear: `corā bhayaṃ jāyati` ("fear arises from a thief"), `taṇhāya jāyatī bhayaṃ` ("fear arises from craving"), `pāpato uttasati` ("he is afraid of evil"). For the expounder: `upajjhāyā sikkhaṃ gaṇhāti` ("he takes the training from his preceptor"), `ācariyamhā adhīte` ("he learns from his teacher"), `ācariyato suṇāti` ("he hears from his teacher").

:::tip[Example]

> `taṇhāya jāyatī bhayaṃ` \
> [(18Dh 16.6: Aññatarabrāhmaṇavatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/16/16.6#234) \
🚺⑤⨀(taṇhā) ▶️🤟⨀(jāyati) 🚻①⨀(bhaya) \
from craving | arises | fear

:::

> ‘‘Apādāna’’nti adhikāro.

“`apādāna`” (the point of departure) is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

<a name="r309"></a>

### 309 (§272): Dhātunāmānamupasaggayogādīsvapi ca (and [the term] with certain roots, nouns, prefixes and the like) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§272 (309): Dhātunāmānamupasaggayogādīsvapi ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m272" description="and [the term] with certain roots, nouns, prefixes and the like" />
:::

> Dhātavo ca nāmāni ca dhātunāmāni, tesaṃ avihitalakkhaṇānaṃ dhātunāmānaṃ payoge, upasaggayoge ca ādi saddena nipātayoge ca taṃyuttaṃ kārakaṃ apādānasaññaṃ hoti.

`dhātunāmāni` means roots (`dhātu`) and nouns (`nāma`). In construction with those roots and nouns for which no rule (`lakkhaṇa`) has been laid down, in construction with prefixes (`upasagga`), and, by the word `ādi`, in construction with particles (`nipāta`), the `kāraka` joined with them is termed `apādāna`.

> Dhātuppayoge tāva – parāji yoge yo asayho, pabhūyoge pabhavo, janiyoge jāyamānassa pakati ca. Yathā – buddhasmā parājenti aññatitthiyā. Himavatā pabhavanti pañca mahānadiyo, anavatattamhā mahāsarā pabhavanti, aciravatiyā pabhavanti kunnadiyo. Kāmato jāyatī soko, yasmā so jāyate gini, urasmā jāto putto, kammato jātaṃ indriyaṃ.

First, in construction with roots: with `parā` + `ji` ("to be defeated"), the one who cannot be overcome (`asayha`); with `pa` + `bhū` ("to originate"), the source (`pabhava`); and with `jana` ("to be born"), the origin (`pakati`) of what is born. For example: `buddhasmā parājenti aññatitthiyā` ("the followers of other sects lose to the Buddha"); `himavatā pabhavanti pañca mahānadiyo` ("the five great rivers originate from the Himavant"), `anavatattamhā mahāsarā pabhavanti` ("the great streams originate from Anotatta"), `aciravatiyā pabhavanti kunnadiyo` ("the small streams originate from the Aciravatī"); `kāmato jāyatī soko` ("grief arises from sensual desire"), `yasmā so jāyate gini` ("from which that fire is born"), `urasmā jāto putto` ("a son born from the breast"), `kammato jātaṃ indriyaṃ` ("a faculty born of kamma").

:::tip[Examples]

> `kāmato jāyatī soko` \
> [(18Dh 16.5: Anitthigandhakumāravatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/16/16.5#233) \
🚹⑤⨀(kāma + to) ▶️🤟⨀(jāyati) 🚹①⨀(soka) \
from sensual desire | arises | grief

> `yasmā so jāyate gini` \
> [(22J 10.1.5: Cūḷabodhijātaka)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/22J/10/10.1/10.1.5#1970) \
🚻⑤⨀(ya) 🚹①⨀(ta) ▶️🤟⨀(jāyati) 🚹①⨀(gini) \
from which | that | is born | fire

:::

> Nāmappayoge aññatthitarā dīhi yutte – nāññatra dukkhā sambhoti, nāññaṃ dukkhā nirujjhati, tato aññena kammena, tato itaraṃ, ubhato sujāto putto iccādi.

In construction with nouns, when joined with `aññatra` ("other than"), `itara` ("other") and the like: `nāññatra dukkhā sambhoti` ("nothing other than suffering comes to be"), `nāññaṃ dukkhā nirujjhati` ("nothing other than suffering ceases"), `tato aññena kammena` ("by an action other than that"), `tato itaraṃ` ("the other than that"), `ubhato sujāto putto` ("a son well born on both sides"), and so on.

:::tip[Examples]

> `nāññatra dukkhā sambhoti, nāññaṃ dukkhā nirujjhati` \
> [(12S1 5.1.10: Vajirāsutta)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/12S1/5/5.1/5.1.10#1002) \
🔼(na) 🔼(aññatra) 🚻⑤⨀(dukkha) ▶️🤟⨀(sambhoti) 🔼(na) 🚻①⨀(añña) 🚻⑤⨀(dukkha) ▶️🤟⨀(nirujjhati) \
not | other than | suffering | comes to be | not | other | than suffering | ceases

> `ubhato sujāto` \
> [(2V 1.5.9.1: Antepurasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/2V/1/1.5/1.5.9/1.5.9.1#1464) \
⚧️⑤⨀(ubha + to) 🚹①⨀(sujāta) \
on both sides | well born

:::

> Upasaggayuttesu apaparī hi vajjanatthehi yoge, mariyādābhividhiatthe āyoge pati nā patinidhipatidānatthena yoge ca. Yathā – apasālāya āyanti vāṇijā, sālaṃ vajjetvāti attho. Tathā paripabbatā devo vassati, pabbataṃ vajjetvāti attho. Mariyādāyaṃ – āpabbatā khettaṃ. Abhividhimhi – ābrahmalokā saddo abbhuggacchati, brahmalokaṃ abhibyāpetvāti attho. Patinidhimhi – buddhasmā pati sāriputto dhammadesanāya ālapati temāsaṃ. Patidāne – ghatamassa telasmā pati dadāti, kanakamassa hiraññasmā pati dadāti.

Among constructions with prefixes: in construction with `apa` and `pari` in the sense of avoiding (`vajjana`), with `ā` in the sense of a limit (`mariyādā`) or of inclusion (`abhividhi`), and with `pati` in the sense of a substitute (`patinidhi`) or of an exchange (`patidāna`). For example: `apasālāya āyanti vāṇijā` ("the merchants come, avoiding the hall"), meaning `sālaṃ vajjetvā` ("having avoided the hall"); likewise `paripabbatā devo vassati` ("it rains all round, avoiding the mountain"), meaning `pabbataṃ vajjetvā` ("having avoided the mountain"). For a limit: `āpabbatā khettaṃ` ("the field [extends] up to the mountain"). For inclusion: `ābrahmalokā saddo abbhuggacchati` ("the sound rises up as far as the Brahmā world"), meaning `brahmalokaṃ abhibyāpetvā` ("pervading the Brahmā world"). For a substitute: `buddhasmā pati sāriputto dhammadesanāya ālapati temāsaṃ` ("in place of the Buddha, Sāriputta addresses [the monks] with a Dhamma talk for three months"). For an exchange: `ghatamassa telasmā pati dadāti` ("he gives him ghee in exchange for oil"), `kanakamassa hiraññasmā pati dadāti` ("he gives him gold in exchange for bullion").

> Nipātayuttesu rite nānā vinā dīhi yoge – rite saddhammā kuto sukhaṃ labhati. Te bhikkhū nānākulā pabbajitā. Vinā saddhammā natthañño koci nātho loke vijjati. Ariyehi puthagevāyaṃ jano, yāva brahmalokā saddo abbhuggacchati.

Among constructions with particles, in construction with `rite` ("apart from"), `nānā` ("various"), `vinā` ("without") and the like: `rite saddhammā kuto sukhaṃ labhati` ("apart from the true Dhamma, whence does one get happiness?"), `te bhikkhū nānākulā pabbajitā` ("those bhikkhus have gone forth from various families"), `vinā saddhammā natthañño koci nātho loke vijjati` ("without the true Dhamma there is no other protector at all to be found in the world"), `ariyehi puthagevāyaṃ jano` ("these people are quite apart from the noble ones"), `yāva brahmalokā saddo abbhuggacchati` ("the sound rises up as far as the Brahmā world").

:::tip[Examples]

> `nānākulā pabbajitā` \
> [(1V 1.2.12: Dubbacasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.2/1.2.12#1729) \
🔼(nānā) 🚻⑤⨀(kula) 🚹①⨂(pabbajita) \
from various | families | gone forth

> `yāva brahmalokā saddo abbhuggacchati` \
> [(16A7 2.2.5: Pāricchattakasutta)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/16A7/2/2.2/2.2.5#536) \

> Api ggahaṇena kammāpādānakārakamajjhepi pañcamī kāladdhānehi, pakkhasmā vijjhati migaṃ luddako, ito pakkhasmā migaṃ vijjhatīti vuttaṃ hoti. Evaṃ māsasmā bhuñjati bhojanaṃ, kosā vijjhati kuñjaraṃ.

Likewise `māsasmā bhuñjati bhojanaṃ` ("a month from now he eats the meal"), `kosā vijjhati kuñjaraṃ` ("from a `kosa` away he shoots the elephant").

> Ca saddaggahaṇena pabhutyā diatthe, tadatthappayoge ca – yatohaṃ bhagini ariyāya jātiyā jāto, yato sarāmi attānaṃ, yato pattosmi viññutaṃ, yatvādhikaraṇamenaṃ, yato pabhuti, yato paṭṭhāya, tato paṭṭhāya iccādi.

### 310 (§273): Rakkhaṇatthānamicchitaṃ (with verbs of guarding, the thing one wants kept safe) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§273 (310): Rakkhaṇatthānamicchitaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m273" description="with verbs of guarding, the thing one wants kept safe" />

> Rakkhaṇatthānaṃ dhātūnaṃ payoge yaṃ icchitaṃ, taṃ kārakaṃ apādānasaññaṃ hoti, ca kārādhikārato anicchitañca. Rakkhaṇañcettha nivāraṇaṃ, tāyanañca. Kāke rakkhanti taṇḍulā, yavā paṭisedhenti gāvo.

In construction with roots meaning guarding (`rakkhaṇa`), what is wanted (`icchita`) — that `kāraka` is termed `apādāna`; and since the letter `ca` is a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)), so is what is not wanted (`anicchita`). Guarding here is both warding off (`nivāraṇa`) and protecting (`tāyana`): `kāke rakkhanti taṇḍulā` ("they keep the crows off the rice"), `yavā paṭisedhenti gāvo` ("they ward the cattle off the barley").

> Anicchitaṃ yathā – pāpā cittaṃ ni vāraye, pāpānivārenti, rājato vā corato vā aggito vā udakato vā nānābhayato vā nānārogato vā nānāupaddavato vā ārakkhaṃ gaṇhantu.

What is not wanted, for example: `pāpā cittaṃ nivāraye` ("one should restrain the mind from evil"), `pāpā nivārenti` ("they restrain [him] from evil"), `rājato vā corato vā aggito vā udakato vā nānābhayato vā nānārogato vā nānāupaddavato vā ārakkhaṃ gaṇhantu` ("let them take protection from the king or from thieves, from fire or from water, from the various dangers, the various diseases or the various calamities").

🚻⑤⨀(pāpa) 🚻②⨀(citta) ⏯🤟⨀(nivāreti) \
from evil | the mind | one should restrain

> `yā tā honti āpadā aggito vā udakato vā rājato vā corato vā appiyato vā dāyādato` \
> [(15A4 2.2.1: Pattakammasutta)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/15A4/2/2.2/2.2.1#452) \

### 311 (§274): Yena vā’dassanaṃ (optionally, the one from whose sight one hides) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§274 (311): Yena vā’ dassanaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m274" description="optionally, the one from whose sight one hides" />
:::

> Yena adassanamicchitaṃ antaradhāyantena, taṃ kārakaṃ apādānasaññaṃ hoti vā, antaradhānevāyaṃ. Upajjhāyā antaradhāyati sisso, nilīyatīti attho. Mātāpitūhi antaradhāyati putto.

The one by whom a person who is vanishing (`antaradhāyanta`) wishes not to be seen — that `kāraka` is optionally termed `apādāna`; and this applies only to vanishing (`antaradhāna`). `upajjhāyā antaradhāyati sisso` ("the pupil disappears from the preceptor"), meaning `nilīyati` ("he hides"); `mātāpitūhi antaradhāyati putto` ("the son disappears from his mother and father").

> Vā ti kiṃ? Jetavane antarahito. Yenā ti kiṃ? Yakkho tattheva antaradhāyati.

By saying `vā` (optionally) we include the form that takes no `apādāna` term: `jetavane antarahito` ("vanished in Jeta's Grove"). By saying `yena` (by whom) we exclude a vanishing from no one's sight: `yakkho tattheva antaradhāyati` ("the yakkha vanishes right there").

### 312 (§275): Dūrantikaddhakālanimmānatvālopadisāyogavibhattārappayoga suddhappamocana hetu vivittappamāṇa pubbayoga bandhana guṇavacana pañha kathanathokākattūsu ca (and in twenty further senses, from distance to non-agency) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§275 (312): Dūranti kaddha kāla nimmāna tvālopa disāyogavibhattārappayoga suddhappamocana hetu vivittappamāṇa pubbayogabandhana guṇavacana pañha kathana thokākattūsu ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m275" description="and in twenty further senses, from distance to non-agency" />
:::

> Dūratthe, antikatthe, addhanimmāne, kālanimmāne, tvālope, disāyoge, vibhatte, āratippayoge, suddhatthappayoge, pamocanatthappayoge, hetvatthe, vivittatthappayoge, pamāṇatthe, pubbayoge, bandhanatthappayoge, guṇavacane, pañhe, kathane, thokatthe, akattari ca yadavadhibhūtaṃ, hetukammādibhūtañca, taṃ kārakaṃ apādānasaññaṃ hoti, ca saddena yathāyogaṃ dutiyā, tatiyā, chaṭṭhī ca.

In the sense of distance, in the sense of nearness, in a measure of distance, in a measure of time, in the elision of `tvā`, in construction with a direction, in distinction, in construction with [a word of] abstaining, in construction with [a word] meaning purity, in construction with [a word] meaning release, in the sense of cause, in construction with [a word] meaning seclusion, in the sense of measure, in construction with `pubba`, in construction with [a word] meaning bondage, in the statement of a quality, in a question, in an answer, in the sense of "a little", and for a non-agent — whatever is the boundary (`avadhi`), and whatever is the cause, the object and the like — that `kāraka` is termed `apādāna`; and by the word `ca` the second ②, the third ③ and the sixth ⑥ are used as applicable.

> E ttha ca dūrantikañca dūrantikatthañcāti dūrantika nti sarūpekasesaṃ katvā vuttanti daṭṭhabbaṃ, tena dūrantikatthappayoge, tadatthe ca apādānasañño hoti.

And here it should be understood that `dūrantika` is stated with a retention of one of like forms (`sarūpekasesa`) of `dūrantika` and `dūrantikattha`; so the term `apādāna` applies both in construction with [a word] meaning far or near and in that sense itself.

> Dūratthappayoge tāva – kīvadūro ito naḷakāragāmo, tato have dūrataraṃ vadanti, gāmato nātidūre. Ārakā te moghapurisā imasmā dhammavinayā, ārakā tehi bhagavā. Dūratthe – dūratova namassanti, addasa dūratova āgacchantaṃ.

First, in construction with a word meaning far: `kīvadūro ito naḷakāragāmo` ("how far from here is Naḷakāragāma?"), `tato have dūrataraṃ vadanti` ("farther than that indeed, they say"), `gāmato nātidūre` ("not too far from the village"), `ārakā te moghapurisā imasmā dhammavinayā` ("far are those foolish men from this Dhamma and discipline"), `ārakā tehi bhagavā` ("far from them is the Blessed One"). In the sense of far: `dūratova namassanti` ("from afar they pay homage"), `addasa dūratova āgacchantaṃ` ("he saw [him] coming from afar").

> `tato have dūrataraṃ vadanti, satañca dhammaṃ asatañca dhammaṃ` \

⚧️⑤⨀(ta + to) 🔼(have) 🚻②⨀(dūratara) ▶️🤟⨂(vadati) 🚹⑥⨂(santa) 🔼(ca) 🚹②⨀(dhamma) 🚹⑥⨂(asanta) 🔼(ca) 🚹②⨀(dhamma) \
than that | indeed | farther | they say | of the good | and | the Dhamma | of the bad | and | the Dhamma

> `dūratova namassanti, mahantaṃ vītasāradaṃ` \

⚧️⑤⨀(dūra + to) 🔼(eva) ▶️🤟⨂(namassati) 🚹②⨀(mahanta) 🚹②⨀(vītasārada) \
from afar | indeed | they pay homage | to the great one | free of diffidence

> `dūratova āgacchantaṃ` \
> [(1V 1.2.6: Kuṭikārasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.2/1.2.6#1423) \
⚧️⑤⨀(dūra + to) 🔼(eva) 🚹②⨀(āgacchanta) \
from afar | indeed | coming

:::

> Antikatthappayoge – antikaṃ gāmā, āsannaṃ gāmā, samīpaṃ gāmā, gāmassa samīpanti attho.

In construction with a word meaning near: `antikaṃ gāmā` ("near the village"), `āsannaṃ gāmā` ("close to the village"), `samīpaṃ gāmā` ("in the vicinity of the village"), meaning `gāmassa samīpaṃ` ("the vicinity of the village").

> Dutiyā, tatiyā ca, dūraṃ gāmaṃ āgato, dūrena gāmena āgato, dūrato gāmā āgatoti attho. Dūraṃ gāmena vā. Antikaṃ gāmaṃ āgato, antikaṃ gāmena vā, āsannaṃ gāmaṃ, āsannaṃ gāmena vā iccādi.

The second ② and the third ③ as well: `dūraṃ gāmaṃ āgato`, `dūrena gāmena āgato` ("come from the far village"), meaning `dūrato gāmā āgato` ("come from the village far away"); or `dūraṃ gāmena` ("far from the village"); `antikaṃ gāmaṃ āgato` ("come from near the village"), or `antikaṃ gāmena` ("near the village"); `āsannaṃ gāmaṃ` ("close to the village"), or `āsannaṃ gāmena`; and so on.

> Addhakālanimmāne nimmānaṃ nāma parimāṇaṃ, tasmiṃ gamyamāne – ito mathurāya catūsu yojanesu saṅkassaṃ, rājagahato pañcacattālīsayojanamatthake sāvatthi.

In a measure of distance or of time — `nimmāna` means measure (`parimāṇa`) — when that is understood: `ito mathurāya catūsu yojanesu saṅkassaṃ` ("four leagues from here, from Mathurā, is Saṅkassa"), `rājagahato pañcacattālīsayojanamatthake sāvatthi` ("Sāvatthī lies forty-five leagues from Rājagaha").

> Kālanimmāne – ito ekanavutikappamatthake vipassī bhagavā loke udapādi, ito vassasahassaccayena buddho loke uppajjissati.

In a measure of time: `ito ekanavutikappamatthake vipassī bhagavā loke udapādi` ("ninety-one aeons from now the Blessed One Vipassī arose in the world"), `ito vassasahassaccayena buddho loke uppajjissati` ("a thousand years from now a Buddha will arise in the world").

:::tip[Example]

> `ito so, bhikkhave, ekanavutikappe yaṃ vipassī bhagavā arahaṃ sammāsambuddho loke udapādi` \
> [(7D 1.1: Pubbenivāsapaṭisaṃyuttakathā)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/7D/1/1.1#6) \
⚧️⑤⨀(ima + to) ⚧️①⨀(ta) 🚹⓪⨂(bhikkhu) 🚹⑦⨀(ekanavutikappa) ⚧️①⨀(ya) 🚹①⨀(vipassī) 🚹①⨀(bhagavantu) 🚹①⨀(arahanta) 🚹①⨀(sammāsambuddha) 🚹⑦⨀(loka) ⏮🤟⨀(uppajjati) \
from now | that | monks | in the ninety-first aeon | when | Vipassī | the Blessed One | the Arahant | the fully awakened | in the world | arose

:::

> Tvāpaccayantassa lopo nāma tadatthasambhavepi avijjamānatā, tasmiṃ tvālope kammādhikaraṇesu – pāsādā saṅkameyya, pāsādaṃ abhiruhitvā saṅkameyyāti attho. Tathā hatthikkhandhā saṅkameyya, abhidhammā pucchanti, abhidhammaṃ sutvā vā, abhidhammā kathayanti, abhidhammaṃ paṭhitvā vā, āsanā vuṭṭhaheyya, āsane nisīditvā vā.

The elision of [a word] ending in the suffix `tvā` means that the word is absent although its sense is present. In that elision of `tvā`, for the object (`kamma`) and the locus (`adhikaraṇa`): `pāsādā saṅkameyya` ("he may step across from the palace"), meaning `pāsādaṃ abhiruhitvā saṅkameyya` ("having climbed the palace, he may step across"). Likewise `hatthikkhandhā saṅkameyya` ("he may step across from the elephant's back"); `abhidhammā pucchanti` ("they ask from the Abhidhamma"), or `abhidhammaṃ sutvā` ("having heard the Abhidhamma"); `abhidhammā kathayanti` ("they speak from the Abhidhamma"), or `abhidhammaṃ paṭhitvā` ("having recited the Abhidhamma"); `āsanā vuṭṭhaheyya` ("he may rise from the seat"), or `āsane nisīditvā` ("having sat on the seat").

> Disatthavācīhi yoge, disatthe ca – ito sā purimā disā, ito sā dakkhiṇā disā, ito sā pacchimā disā, ito sā uttarā disā, avīcito uparibhavaggā, uddhaṃ pādatalā, adho kesamatthakā iccādi. Disatthe – puratthimato dakkhiṇatotiādi. Ettha pana sattamiyatthe to paccayopi bhavissati.

In construction with words that express a direction, and in the sense of a direction: `ito sā purimā disā` ("from here, that is the eastern quarter"), `ito sā dakkhiṇā disā` ("from here, that is the southern quarter"), `ito sā pacchimā disā` ("… the western quarter"), `ito sā uttarā disā` ("… the northern quarter"), `avīcito uparibhavaggā` ("from Avīci up to the peak of existence"), `uddhaṃ pādatalā` ("upward from the soles of the feet"), `adho kesamatthakā` ("downward from the top of the hair"), and so on. In the sense of a direction: `puratthimato`, `dakkhiṇato` ("from the east", "from the south"), and the like. Here, however, the suffix `to` may also be in the sense of the seventh ⑦.

> [(7D 9.1.4: Kāyānupassanāpaṭikūlamanasikārapabba)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/7D/9/9.1/9.1.4#959) \
🔼(uddhaṃ) 🚻⑤⨀(pādatala) 🔼(adho) 🚹⑤⨀(kesamatthaka) \
upward | from the soles of the feet | downward | from the top of the hair

> Vibhattaṃ nāma sayaṃ vibhattasseva tadaññato guṇena vibhajanaṃ, tasmiṃ vibhatte – yato paṇītataro vā visiṭṭhataro vā natthi, attadanto tato varaṃ. Kiñcāpi dānato sīlameva varaṃ, tato mayā sutā assutameva bahutaraṃ, sīlameva sutā seyyo. Chaṭṭhī ca, channavutīnaṃ pāsaṇḍānaṃ pavaraṃ yadidaṃ sugatavinayo.

Distinction (`vibhatta`) means the separating (`vibhajana`), by a quality, of something that is itself already distinct from another. In that distinction: `yato paṇītataro vā visiṭṭhataro vā natthi` ("than which there is nothing more excellent or more distinguished"), `attadanto tato varaṃ` ("one who has tamed himself is better than those"), `kiñcāpi dānato sīlameva varaṃ` ("although virtue indeed is better than giving"), `tato mayā sutā assutameva bahutaraṃ` ("compared with what I have heard, what I have not heard is far more"), `sīlameva sutā seyyo` ("virtue alone is better than learning"). The sixth ⑥ as well: `channavutīnaṃ pāsaṇḍānaṃ pavaraṃ yadidaṃ sugatavinayo` ("of the ninety-six sectarian doctrines the foremost is this, the discipline of the Sugata").

:::tip[Examples]

elephants | and | great tuskers | one who has tamed himself | than those | is better

> `sīlameva sutā seyyo, iti me natthi saṃsayo` \
> [(22J 5.2.2: Sīlavīmaṃsajātaka)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/22J/5/5.2/5.2.2#1223) \
🚻①⨀(sīla) 🔼(eva) 🚻⑤⨀(suta) 🔼(seyyo) 🔼(iti) ⚧️⑥⨀(amha) 🔼(na) ▶️🤟⨀(atthi) 🚹①⨀(saṃsaya) \
virtue | alone | than learning | is better | thus | for me | not | there is | doubt

> Āratippayogo nāma viramaṇatthasaddappayogo. Tattha – asaddhammā ārati, virati pāpā, pāṇātipātā veramaṇī, adinnādānā paṭivirato, appaṭivirato musāvādā.

Construction with [a word of] abstaining (`ārati`) means construction with a word meaning to desist (`viramaṇa`). There: `asaddhammā ārati` ("abstaining from wrong practice"), `virati pāpā` ("refraining from evil"), `pāṇātipātā veramaṇī` ("abstention from killing living beings"), `adinnādānā paṭivirato` ("refraining from taking what is not given"), `appaṭivirato musāvādā` ("not refraining from false speech").

> `pāṇātipātā veramaṇī` \

🚹⑤⨀(pāṇātipāta) 🚺①⨀(veramaṇī) \
from killing living beings | abstention

### 313 (§106): Dīghorehi (With `dīgha`, `ora`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§106 (313): Dīghorehi" href="/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m106" description="With `dīgha`, `ora`" />

:::note[Analysis]

A Nāma rule of Kaccāyana (§106), stated here for the `dīghaso` of 312; the `so` and `vā` of its gloss continue from [§105 (0): So vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa/1-pathamakanda#m105), a rule the Rūpasiddhi does not state.

> Dīgha ora icceteti smā vacanassa so ādeso hoti vā.

After `dīgha` ("long") and `ora` ("this side"), the inflection `smā` is optionally replaced by `so`.

> Dīghaso, dīghamhā vā, [oraso, oramhā vā] tatiyā ca, yojanaṃ āyāmena, yojanaṃ vitthārena, yojanaṃ ubbedhena sāsaparāsi.

`dīghaso` or `dīghamhā` ("in length"), [`oraso` or `oramhā` ("from this side")]. The third ③ as well: `yojanaṃ āyāmena, yojanaṃ vitthārena, yojanaṃ ubbedhena sāsaparāsi` ("a heap of mustard seed a league in length, a league in breadth, a league in height").

|     | dīgha + smā (⑤⨀)       | rule |

> Paṭhamatthavācakena pubba saddena yogo pubbayogo, ettha ca pubba ggahaṇaṃ adisatthavuttino pubbā diggahaṇassupalakkhaṇanti daṭṭhabbaṃ, tena parā diyogepi. Construction with `pubba` (`pubbayoga`) is construction with the word `pubba` in the sense of "first"; and here it should be understood that the mention of `pubba` is an indication (`upalakkhaṇa`) standing for the mention of `pubba` and the like when they do not have the sense of a direction; hence also in construction with `para` ("after") and the like. For example: `pubbeva me bhikkhave sambodhā` ("even before my awakening, monks"), `ito pubbenāhosi` ("before this it was not [so]"), `tato paraṃ paccantimā janapadā` ("beyond that are the border countries"), `dhātuliṅgehi parā paccayā` ("suffixes come after roots and lemmas"), `tato aparena samayena` ("at a time later than that"), `tato uttarimpi` ("even beyond that"), and so on.

> Phalasādhanahetubhūtassa guṇassa vacanaṃ guṇavacanaṃ, tasmiṃ guṇavacane pañcamī, tatiyā ca, issariyā janaṃ rakkhati rājā, issariyena vā, sīlato naṃ pasaṃsanti, sīlena vā, paññāya vimuttimano iccādi.

The statement of a quality (`guṇavacana`) is the statement of a quality that is the cause producing a result; in that statement of a quality the fifth ⑤, and the third ③ as well, is used: `issariyā janaṃ rakkhati rājā` ("by his lordship the king protects the people"), or `issariyena`; `sīlato naṃ pasaṃsanti` ("they praise him for his virtue"), or `sīlena`; `paññāya vimuttimano` ("by wisdom, a mind released"); and so on.

> Pañhakathanesu – kutosi tvaṃ, kuto bhavaṃ, pāṭaliputtato. Ettha ca kathanaṃ nāma vissajjanaṃ.

In a question and in an answer: `kutosi tvaṃ` ("where are you from?"), `kuto bhavaṃ` ("where is your honour from?"), `pāṭaliputtato` ("from Pāṭaliputta"). And here `kathana` ("telling") means answering (`vissajjana`).

> Thokatthe asatvavacane karaṇe tatiyā ca, thokā muccati, thokena muccati vā, appamattakā muccati, appamattakena vā, kicchā muccati, kicchena vā.

In the sense of "a little", when the instrument (`karaṇa`) expressed is not a substance (`asatvavacana`), the third ③ as well: `thokā muccati` ("he escapes by a little"), or `thokena muccati`; `appamattakā muccati` ("he escapes by a trifle"), or `appamattakena`; `kicchā muccati` ("he escapes with difficulty"), or `kicchena`.

> Akattari akārake ñāpakahetumhi – kammassa katattā upacitattā ussannattā vipulattā uppannaṃ hoti cakkhuviññāṇaṃ[^2], na tāvidaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ahetukaṃ sabbattha sabbadā sabbesañca ekasadisabhāvāpattito.

For a non-agent (`akattā`), a cause that is an indicator (`ñāpakahetu`) and not a `kāraka`: `kammassa katattā upacitattā ussannattā vipulattā uppannaṃ hoti cakkhuviññāṇaṃ` ("because the deed has been done, accumulated, become abundant and extensive, eye-consciousness has arisen"), `na tāvidaṃ nāmarūpaṃ ahetukaṃ sabbattha sabbadā sabbesañca ekasadisabhāvāpattito` ("to begin with, this mind-and-matter is not without a cause, since [otherwise] it would turn out alike everywhere, always and for all").

> `kāmāvacarassa kusalassa kammassa katattā upacitattā vipākaṃ cakkhuviññāṇaṃ uppannaṃ hoti` \
> [(29Dhs 2.1.7.1: Kusalavipākapañcaviññāṇa)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/29Dhs/2/2.1/2.1.7/2.1.7.1#905) \
🚻⑥⨀(kāmāvacara) 🚻⑥⨀(kusala) 🚻⑥⨀(kamma) 🚻⑤⨀(katatta) 🚻⑤⨀(upacitatta) 🚻①⨀(vipāka) 🚻①⨀(cakkhuviññāṇa) 🚻①⨀(uppanna) ▶️🤟⨀(hoti) \
of the sense-sphere | wholesome | deed | because of having been done | because of having been accumulated | as result | eye-consciousness | arisen | is

:::

> Hutvā abhāvato ’niccā[^3], udayabbayapīḷanā; \
> Dukkhā avasavattittā, anattāti tilakkhaṇaṃ.

[Things are] impermanent, since they cease to be after having been; from the oppression of rise and fall \
they are painful; and not-self, since they do not submit to one's will: such are the three characteristics.

[^3]: CST4 prints `niccā`; the sense and the metre require `aniccā`, its initial `a` elided after `abhāvato`, as written here.

“`pañcamī`”, which the end of 313 says continues into this rule, comes from [307 (§295): Apādāne pañcamī](/rupasiddhi/3-karakakanda#r307).

:::

> Karoti attano phalanti kāraṇaṃ, kārakahetu [janakahetu], tasmiṃ kāraṇatthe ca pañcamīvibhatti hoti, vikappenāyaṃ, hetvatthe tatiyāya ca vihitattā, ananubodhā appaṭivedhā catunnaṃ ariyasaccānaṃ yathābhūtaṃ adassanā evamidaṃ dīghamaddhānaṃ sandhāvitaṃ saṃsaritaṃ, avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā, saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṃ, avijjāya tveva asesavirāganirodhā saṅkhāranirodho, saṅkhāranirodhā viññāṇanirodho, viññāṇanirodhā iccādi.

A cause (`kāraṇa`) is what produces its own result: the productive cause (`kārakahetu`) [the generating cause (`janakahetu`)]. In that sense of cause too the fifth inflection ⑤ is used, and this optionally (`vikappena`), because the third ③ has also been prescribed in the sense of cause: `ananubodhā appaṭivedhā catunnaṃ ariyasaccānaṃ yathābhūtaṃ adassanā evamidaṃ dīghamaddhānaṃ sandhāvitaṃ saṃsaritaṃ` ("through not understanding, not penetrating and not seeing the four noble truths as they really are, this long course has been run and wandered"), `avijjāpaccayā saṅkhārā` ("with ignorance as condition, formations"), `saṅkhārapaccayā viññāṇaṃ` ("with formations as condition, consciousness"), `avijjāya tveva asesavirāganirodhā saṅkhāranirodho` ("but with the utter fading away and cessation of ignorance, the cessation of formations"), `saṅkhāranirodhā viññāṇanirodho` ("with the cessation of formations, the cessation of consciousness"), `viññāṇanirodhā …` ("with the cessation of consciousness …"), and so on.

<a name="r316"></a>

### 316 (§283): Yassa vā pariggaho taṃ sāmī (the one whose possession [a thing] is: `sāmī`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§283 (316): Yassa vā pariggaho taṃ sāmī" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m283" description="the one whose possession [a thing] is: `sāmī`" />
:::

> Pariggayhatīti pariggaho, yo yassa pariggaho āyatto sambandhī, taṃ pati so attho sāmisañño hoti. Vā ggahaṇena sāmitabba rujādiyogepi.

A possession (`pariggaha`) is what is taken as one's own (`pariggayhati`). When something is someone's possession — dependent on him (`āyatta`), related to him (`sambandhī`) — then, with respect to it, that meaning (`attha`) is termed `sāmī` (the owner). By the mention of `vā`, the term applies also in construction with `sāmī`, `tabba`, `ruja` ("to pain") and the like.

> Ettha ca kriyābhisambandhābhāvā na kārakatā sambhavati. Sāmibhāvo hi kriyākārakabhāvassa phalabhāvena gahito, tathā hi ‘‘rañño puriso’’ti vutte yasmā rājā dadāti, puriso ca patiggaṇhāti, tasmā ‘‘rājapuriso’’ti viññāyati. Evaṃ yo yassa āyatto sevakādibhāvena vā bhaṇḍabhāvena vā samīpa samūhāvayavavikāra kāriyaavatthā jāti guṇa kriyādivasena vā, tassa sabbassāpi so sambandhādhārabhūto visesanaṭṭhānī āgamīvasena tividhopi attho sāmī nāmāti gahetabbo.

And here, since there is no direct connection with an action, being a `kāraka` is not possible. For ownership (`sāmibhāva`) is taken as the fruit of the relation between an action and its `kāraka`s: when `rañño puriso` ("the king's man") is said, it is because the king gives and the man receives that he is understood as `rājapuriso` ("the king's man"). So whatever is dependent on someone — by being his servant (`sevaka`) and the like, or by being his property (`bhaṇḍa`), or by way of proximity (`samīpa`), group (`samūha`), part (`avayava`), transformation (`vikāra`), effect (`kāriya`), state (`avatthā`), kind (`jāti`), quality (`guṇa`), action (`kriyā`) and the like — for all of these too, the meaning that is the bearer of the relation (`sambandha`), though threefold as qualifier (`visesana`), substituend (`ṭhānī`) and receiver of an insertion ([`āgamī`](/introduction#types-of-vidhi-operation)), is to be taken as what is called `sāmī`.

> Vuttañca

And it is said:

> ‘‘Kriyākārakasañjāto, \
> Assedaṃ bhāvahetuko; \
> Sambandho nāma so attho, \
> Tattha chaṭṭhī vidhīyate.

Born of an action and its `kāraka`s, \
grounded in the notion "this is his", \
that meaning is called relation (`sambandha`); \
there the sixth ⑥ is prescribed.

> Pāratantyañhi sambandho, \
> Tattha chaṭṭhī bhavetito; \
> Upādhiṭṭhānā gamito, \
> Na visesyādito tito’’ti.

For relation is dependence (`pāratantya`); \
there the sixth ⑥ arises from the three: \
from the qualifier, the substituend and the receiver of an insertion, \
not from the qualified and the rest — [only] from those three.

> Visesanato tāva – rañño purisoti, ettha ca rājā purisaṃ aññasāmito viseseti nivattetīti visesanaṃ, puriso tena visesīyatīti visesitabbo, evaṃ sabbattha visesitabbayoge visesanatova chaṭṭhī.

First, from the qualifier: `rañño puriso` ("the king's man"). Here the king distinguishes (`viseseti`) the man — sets him apart (`nivatteti`) — from [those who have] other masters, and so is the qualifier (`visesana`); the man is distinguished by him, and so is the thing qualified (`visesitabba`). In this way, wherever there is construction with a thing qualified, the sixth ⑥ comes from the qualifier alone.

> Bhaṇḍena sambandhe – pahūtaṃ me dhanaṃ sakka, etassa paṭivīso, bhikkhussa pattacīvaraṃ.

In the relation through property: `pahūtaṃ me dhanaṃ sakka` ("abundant is my wealth, Sakka"), `etassa paṭivīso` ("his share"), `bhikkhussa pattacīvaraṃ` ("the bhikkhu's bowl and robe").

> Samūhasambandhe – suvaṇṇassa rāsi, bhikkhūnaṃ samūho.

In the relation of a group [to its members]: `suvaṇṇassa rāsi` ("a heap of gold"), `bhikkhūnaṃ samūho` ("a group of bhikkhus").

> Avayavasambandhe – manussasseva te sīsaṃ, rukkhassa sākhā.

In the relation of a part [to its whole]: `manussasseva te sīsaṃ` ("your head is indeed a man's"), `rukkhassa sākhā` ("the branch of the tree").

> Vikārasambandhe – suvaṇṇassa vikati, bhaṭṭhadhaññānaṃ sattu.

In the relation of a transformation [to its material]: `suvaṇṇassa vikati` ("an article wrought of gold"), `bhaṭṭhadhaññānaṃ sattu` ("meal of parched grain").

> Kāriyasambandhe – yavassa aṅkuro, meghassa saddo, puttāpi tassa bahavo, kammānaṃ phalaṃ vipāko.

> Avatthāsambandhe – khandhānaṃ pātubhāvo, khandhānaṃ jarā, khandhānaṃ bhedo.

> Jātisambandhe – manussassa bhāvo, manussānaṃ jāti.

In the relation of a kind [to its members]: `manussassa bhāvo` ("the state of a human being"), `manussānaṃ jāti` ("the kind [that is the birth] of human beings").

> Guṇasambandhe – suvaṇṇassa vaṇṇo, vaṇṇo na khīyetha tathāgatassa, buddhassa guṇaghoso, pupphānaṃ gandho, phalānaṃ raso, cittassa phusanā, sippikānaṃ sataṃ natthi, tilānaṃ muṭṭhi, tesaṃ samāyogo, sandhino vimokkho, tathāgatassa paññāpāramiṃ ārabbha, pubbacariyaṃ vā, sukhaṃ te, dukkhaṃ te, cetaso parivitakko udapādi, paññāya paṭubhāvo, rūpassa lahutā, rūpassa mudutā, rūpassa upacayo.

In the relation of a quality [to its bearer]: `suvaṇṇassa vaṇṇo` ("the colour of gold"), `vaṇṇo na khīyetha tathāgatassa` ("the praise of the Tathāgata could not be exhausted"), `buddhassa guṇaghoso` ("the renown of the Buddha's virtues"), `pupphānaṃ gandho` ("the scent of flowers"), `phalānaṃ raso` ("the taste of fruits"), `cittassa phusanā` ("the touch of the mind"), `sippikānaṃ sataṃ natthi` ("there is not a hundred shells"), `tilānaṃ muṭṭhi` ("a handful of sesame"), `tesaṃ samāyogo` ("their combination"), `sandhino vimokkho` ("the loosening of the joint"), `tathāgatassa paññāpāramiṃ ārabbha` ("concerning the Tathāgata's perfection of wisdom"), `pubbacariyaṃ vā` ("or [his] former conduct"), `sukhaṃ te` ("happiness is yours"), `dukkhaṃ te` ("suffering is yours"), `cetaso parivitakko udapādi` ("a reflection of [his] mind arose"), `paññāya paṭubhāvo` ("the skill of wisdom"), `rūpassa lahutā` ("the lightness of matter"), `rūpassa mudutā` ("the malleability of matter"), `rūpassa upacayo` ("the growth of matter").

> Kriyāsambandhe – pādassa ukkhipanaṃ, pādassa avakkhepanaṃ vā, hatthassa samiñjanaṃ, pādānaṃ pasāraṇaṃ, dhātūnaṃ gamanaṃ, dhātūnaṃyeva ṭhānaṃ, nisajjā, sayanaṃ vā. ### 317 (§308): Chaṭṭhī ca (and [sometimes] ⑥ in the sense of the third and seventh) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§308 (317): Chaṭṭhī ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m308" description="and [sometimes] ⑥ in the sense of the third and seventh" />

:::note[Analysis]

The words `kvaci` and `tatiyāsattamīnaṃ`, which the end of 316 carries into this rule, come from [289 (§306): Kvaci dutiyā chaṭṭhīnamatthe](/rupasiddhi/3-karakakanda#r289) and [290 (§307): Tatiyāsattamīnañca](/rupasiddhi/3-karakakanda#r290).

> Tatiyāsattamīnamatthe kvaci chaṭṭhīvibhatti hoti.

In the sense of the third ③ and the seventh ⑦, the sixth inflection ⑥ is used in some places.

> Yajassa karaṇe – pupphassa buddhaṃ yajati, pupphena vā, ghatassa aggiṃ juhoti.

### 320 (§278): Yodhāro tamokāsaṃ (the support of the action: `okāsa`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§278 (320): Yodhāro tamokāsaṃ" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m278" description="the support of the action: `okāsa`" />
:::

> Ādhārīyati asminti ādhāro, adhikaraṇaṃ. Kattukammasamavetānaṃ nisajjapacanādikriyānaṃ patiṭṭhānaṭṭhena yo ādhāro, taṃ kārakaṃ okāsasaññaṃ hoti.

A support (`ādhāra`) is that in which something is held (`ādhārīyati`): the `adhikaraṇa`. The `kāraka` that is the support, in the sense of a footing, of actions such as sitting and cooking, which inhere in the agent and the object, is termed `okāsa` (the location).

> Kaṭe nisīdati devadatto, thāliyaṃ odanaṃ pacati. Ettha hi devadattataṇḍulānaṃ kattukammānaṃ dhāraṇato taṃsamavetaṃ āsanapacanasaṅkhātaṃ kriyaṃ dhāreti nāma.

`kaṭe nisīdati devadatto` ("Devadatta sits on the mat"); `thāliyaṃ odanaṃ pacati` ("he cooks rice in the pot"). For here, since they hold the agent and the object, Devadatta and the rice grains, they are said to hold the action that inheres in those, namely the sitting and the cooking.

> So panāyamokāso catubbidho byāpiko opasilesiko sāmīpiko vesayikoti.

And this `okāsa` is fourfold: pervading (`byāpika`), by contact (`opasilesika`), by proximity (`sāmīpika`) and by domain (`vesayika`).

> Tattha byāpiko nāma yattha sakalopi ādhārabhūto attho ādheyyena patthaṭo hoti, yasmiñca ādheyyabhūtaṃ kiñci byāpetvā tiṭṭhati, taṃ yathā – tilesu telaṃ atthi, khīresu[^4] jalaṃ, dadhimhi sappīti.

Among these, the pervading (`byāpika`) is one where even the whole of the thing that is the support is spread through by the thing supported, and in which whatever is supported stands pervading it: for example `tilesu telaṃ atthi` ("there is oil in sesame seeds"); `khīresu jalaṃ` ("water in milk"); `dadhimhi sappi` ("ghee in curds").

> Opasilesiko nāma paccekasiddhānaṃ bhāvānaṃ yattha upasilesena upagamo hoti, yasmiñca ādheyyo upasilissati allīyitvā tiṭṭhati, taṃ yathā – āsane nisinno saṅgho, thāliyaṃ odanaṃ pacati, ghaṭesu udakaṃ atthi, dūre ṭhito, samīpe ṭhitoti.

The one by contact (`opasilesika`) is where things that exist each on their own come together by contact, and in which the thing supported clings, standing attached to it: for example `āsane nisinno saṅgho` ("the Saṅgha seated on the seat"); `thāliyaṃ odanaṃ pacati` ("he cooks rice in the pot"); `ghaṭesu udakaṃ atthi` ("there is water in the pots"); `dūre ṭhito` ("standing far off"); `samīpe ṭhito` ("standing near").

> Sāmīpiko nāma yattha samīpe samīpivohāraṃ katvā tadāyattavuttitādīpanatthaṃ ādhārabhāvo vikappīyati, taṃ yathā – gaṅgāyaṃ ghoso vasati, gaṅgāya samīpe vajo vasatīti attho. Bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane, sāvatthiyā samīpeti attho.

The one by proximity (`sāmīpika`) is where the vicinity is spoken of by the name of what it is near, and so is supposed to be the support, in order to show that the thing supported occurs in dependence on it: for example `gaṅgāyaṃ ghoso vasati` ("the cowherds' village lies on the Ganges"), which means `gaṅgāya samīpe vajo vasati` ("the cattle pen lies near the Ganges"); `bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane` ("the Blessed One dwells at Sāvatthī, in Jeta's Grove"), which means `sāvatthiyā samīpe` ("near Sāvatthī").

:::tip[Examples]

> `buddho bhagavā sāvatthiyaṃ viharati jetavane anāthapiṇḍikassa ārāme` \
> [(1V 1.2.1: Sukkavissaṭṭhisikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.2/1.2.1#775) \
🚹①⨀(buddha) 🚹①⨀(bhagavantu) 🚺⑦⨀(sāvatthī) ▶️🤟⨀(viharati) 🚻⑦⨀(jetavana) 🚹⑥⨀(anāthapiṇḍika) 🚹⑦⨀(ārāma) \
the Buddha | the Blessed One | at Sāvatthī | dwells | in Jeta's Grove | of Anāthapiṇḍika | in the park

:::

> Vesayiko nāma yattha aññatthābhāvavasena, desantarāvacchedavasena vā ādhārabhāvo parikappo, taṃ yathā – ākāse sakuṇā pakkhandanti[^5], bhūmīsu manussā caranti, jalesu macchā, pādesu patito, pāpasmiṃ ramatī mano, pasanno buddhasāsane, paññāya sādhu, vinaye nipuṇo, mātari sādhu, pitari nipuṇo iccādi.

The one by domain (`vesayika`) is where the state of being a support is assumed from absence elsewhere, or from the exclusion of other places: for example `ākāse sakuṇā pakkhandanti` ("birds leap forth in the sky"); `bhūmīsu manussā caranti` ("men walk on the earth"); `jalesu macchā` ("fish in the waters"); `pādesu patito` ("fallen at his feet"); `pāpasmiṃ ramatī mano` ("the mind delights in evil"); `pasanno buddhasāsane` ("confident in the Buddha's teaching"); `paññāya sādhu` ("good in wisdom"); `vinaye nipuṇo` ("skilled in the Vinaya"); `mātari sādhu` ("good towards his mother"); `pitari nipuṇo` ("skilled towards his father"); and so on.

:::tip[Examples]

> `dandhañhi karoto puññaṃ, pāpasmiṃ ramatī mano` \
> [(18Dh 9.1: Cūḷekasāṭakabrāhmaṇavatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/9/9.1#125) \
🔼(dandhaṃ) 🔼(hi) 🚹⑥⨀(karonta) 🚻②⨀(puñña) 🚻⑦⨀(pāpa) ▶️🤟⨀(ramati) 🚹①⨀(mana) \
slowly | for | of one doing | merit | in evil | delights | the mind

> `pāmojjabahulo bhikkhu, pasanno buddhasāsane` \
> [(18Dh 25.11: Vakkalittheravatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Dh/25/25.11#408) \
🚹①⨀(pāmojjabahula) 🚹①⨀(bhikkhu) 🚹①⨀(pasanna) 🚻⑦⨀(buddhasāsana) \
full of gladness | a bhikkhu | confident | in the Buddha's teaching

:::

> Sabbopi cāyamādhāro padhānavasena vā parikappitavasena vā kriyāya patiṭṭhā bhavatīti okāso tveva vuttoti veditabbo.

And it should be understood that every one of these supports, whether primarily or by supposition, is a footing for the action, and so is called simply `okāsa`.

> Vuttañcetaṃ

And this has been said:

> ‘‘Kiriyā kattukammānaṃ, \
> Yattha hoti patiṭṭhitā; \
> ‘Okāso’ti pavutto so, \
> Catudhā byāpikādito.

That in which the action of agent and object \
stands established \
is called `okāsa`; \
it is of four kinds, the pervading and the rest.

> Byāpiko tilakhīrādi, \
> Kaṭo opasilesiko; \
> Sāmīpiko tu gaṅgādi, \
> Ākāso visayo mato’’ti.

The pervading is sesame, milk and the like; \
the mat is the one by contact; \
but the one by proximity is the Ganges and the like; \
the sky is held to be a domain.

> ‘‘Chaṭṭhī, sattamī’’ti ca adhikāro.

And “`chaṭṭhī`” (the sixth) and “`sattamī`” (the seventh) are a governing rule ([`adhikāra`](/introduction#types-of-suttas)).

> Bhāvenabhāvalakkhaṇe – bhikkhusaṅghesu bhojīyamānesu gato, bhuttesu āgato, gosu duyhamānāsu gato, duddhāsu āgato, jāyamāne kho sāriputta bodhisatte ayaṃ dasasahassilokadhātu saṃkampi sampakampi sampavedhi.

In the sense of a state marking a state (`bhāvenabhāvalakkhaṇa`): `bhikkhusaṅghesu bhojīyamānesu gato` ("while the communities of bhikkhus were being fed, he went"); `bhuttesu āgato` ("when they had eaten, he came back"); `gosu duyhamānāsu gato` ("while the cows were being milked, he went"); `duddhāsu āgato` ("when they had been milked, he came back"); `jāyamāne kho sāriputta bodhisatte ayaṃ dasasahassilokadhātu saṃkampi sampakampi sampavedhi` ("when indeed the Bodhisatta was being born, Sāriputta, this ten-thousandfold world system shook, quaked and trembled").

<a name="r328"></a>

### 328 (§314): Upājhādhikissaravacane (⑦ with `upa`, `adhi` in the senses "more than" and "lord over") :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§314 (328): Upa’dhyādhikissaravacane" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m314" description="⑦ with `upa`, `adhi` in the senses 'more than' and 'lord over'" />
:::

> Dvipadamidaṃ. Adhikatthe, issaratthe ca vattamānehi upaadhi iccetehi yoge adhikissaravacane gamyamāne liṅgamhā sattamīvibhatti hoti.

This is two words. In construction with `upa` and `adhi` occurring in the sense of exceeding (`adhika`) or of lordship (`issara`), when an expression of exceeding or of lordship is understood, the seventh inflection ⑦ is used after the lemma.

> Adhikavacane – upa khāriyaṃ doṇo, khāriyā doṇo adhikoti attho. Tathā upa nikkhe kahāpaṇaṃ, adhi devesu buddho, sammutiupapattivisuddhidevasaṅkhātehi tividhehipi devehi sabbaññū buddhova adhikoti attho.

In an expression of exceeding: `upa khāriyaṃ doṇo` ("a `doṇa` over a `khārī`"), which means `khāriyā doṇo adhiko` ("a `doṇa` in excess of a `khārī`"); likewise `upa nikkhe kahāpaṇaṃ` ("a `kahāpaṇa` over a `nikkha`"); `adhi devesu buddho` ("the Buddha is above the gods"), which means that the all-knowing Buddha alone exceeds even the gods of all three kinds, those called gods by convention, gods by birth and gods by purity.

> Issaravacane – adhi brahmadatte pañcālā, brahmadattissarā pañcālāti attho.

In an expression of lordship: `adhi brahmadatte pañcālā` ("the Pañcālas are under Brahmadatta"), which means `brahmadattissarā pañcālā` ("the Pañcālas have Brahmadatta as their lord").

### 329 (§315): Maṇḍitussukkesu tatiyā ca (③, and ⑦, in the senses of `maṇḍita` and `ussukka`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::tip[Kaccāyana]
<LinkCard title="§315 (329): Maṇḍitussukkesu tatiyā ca" href="/kaccayana/3-karakakappa#m315" description="③, and ⑦, in the senses of `maṇḍita` and `ussukka`" />
:::

> Maṇḍitaussukkaiccetesvatthesu gamyamānesu liṅgamhā tatiyāvibhatti hoti, sattamī ca. Maṇḍita saddo panettha pasannatthavācako, ussukka saddo saīhattho. Ñāṇena pasanno, ñāṇasmiṃ pasanno, ñāṇena ussukko, ñāṇasmiṃ ussukko sappuriso.

When the senses of `maṇḍita` and `ussukka` are understood, the third inflection ③ is used after the lemma, and the seventh ⑦ as well. And here the word `maṇḍita` expresses the sense of gladdened (`pasanna`), and the word `ussukka` has the sense of striving (`sa-īha`): `ñāṇena pasanno` ("gladdened through knowledge") or `ñāṇasmiṃ pasanno` ("gladdened in knowledge"); `ñāṇena ussukko` ("zealous through knowledge") or `ñāṇasmiṃ ussukko sappuriso` ("a good man zealous in knowledge").

> Kārakaṃ chabbidhaṃ saññā-vasā chabbīsatīvidhaṃ; \
> Pabhedā sattadhā kammaṃ, kattā pañcavidho bhave.

The `kāraka` is sixfold, and by way of its names twenty-sixfold; \
by division the object is sevenfold, and the agent would be fivefold.

> Karaṇaṃ duvidhaṃ hoti, sampadānaṃ tidhā mataṃ; \
> Apādānaṃ pañcavidhaṃ, ādhāro tu catubbidho.

The instrument is twofold, the recipient is held to be threefold; \
the point of departure fivefold, and the support fourfold.

> Vibhattiyo pana paccattavacanādivasena aṭṭhavidhā bhavanti. Yathāha

The inflections, however, are eightfold by way of the `paccattavacana` and the rest. As it is said:

> ‘‘Paccattamupayogañca, karaṇaṃ sampadāniyaṃ; \
> Nissakkaṃ sāmivacanaṃ, bhummālapanamaṭṭhama’’nti.

`paccatta` and `upayoga`, `karaṇa`, `sampadāniya`, \
`nissakka`, `sāmivacana`, `bhumma`, and `ālapana` the eighth.
