:::note
A `samāsa` (compound) joins two or more nouns whose meanings belong together into one word, dropping the inflections of the members. §316–§318 define the compound and the treatment of its members; §319–§329 name the six kinds by the relation of their members; §330–§343 give the changes at the junction and at the end of the compound.

The Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana edition counts this chapter as the seventh section of the `nāma` chapter. Every rule here has a parallel in the [Bālāvatāra's `samāsa` section](/balavatara/3-samasa).

:::

## Sattamakaṇḍa (Seventh Section)

<a name="m316"></a>

### §316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho (a `samāsa` is [a joining of] nouns whose meanings are connected) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | nāmānaṃ (payujjamānapadatthānaṃ) yuttattho | (saññā) | samāso | | |
|        |        | nouns (word-meanings used together) whose meanings are connected | (is named) | `samāsa` (compound) | | |

The sutta is `nāmānaṃ yuttattho [attho] samāso`, "the connected meaning of nouns is a `samāsa`". `nāma` is the noun, the word with a stem (`liṅga`, [§53 (61): Liṅgañca nippajjate](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m53)) and an inflection; `yuttattho` is `yutto attho`, "a meaning that is joined": the vutti's `payujjamānapadatthānaṃ` ("of the word-meanings that are being employed [together]") makes the point that what is compounded is not two words that happen to stand side by side but two meanings that bear on one another — the Bālāvatāra glosses `aññamaññasambandhattho`, "a meaning related to the other's" (¶202). `samāsa` is `saṃ` + `√asa` ("to throw"): what is "thrown together", a shortened expression (the Rūpasiddhi: `samassate ti samāso, saṅkhipiyatī ti attho`). The name says what the thing is, so the definition is `anvattha`. It lays down no operation; it gives the term that the operating rules of the chapter presuppose — [§317 (332): Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca](#m317) elides the inflections of "those" nouns, and the vutti's purpose question points to [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337) — and its two `kimatthaṃ` questions mark its two conditions: only nouns are compounded, and only nouns whose meanings connect. The Bālāvatāra reads the sutta as an `adhikāra`, a heading that governs the whole compound section, and adds that `vibhāsā` ("optionally") is to be understood with it throughout: a compound is always an alternative to the phrase it stands for (¶202; cf. [§323 (360): Vibhāsā rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapadā dīnañca](#m323)). The Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana counts the Samāsakappa as the seventh section of the `nāma` chapter; the Padarūpasiddhi, whose numbers stand in parentheses, opens its own compound section with this rule as 331.

:::

> Tesaṃ nāmānaṃ payujjamānapadatthānaṃ yo yuttattho, so samāsasañño hoti.

> Kathinadussaṃ, āgantukabhattaṃ, jīvitindriyaṃ, samaṇabrāhmaṇā, sāriputtamoggallānā, brāhmaṇa gahapatikā.

> Nāmānamiti kimatthaṃ? Devadatto pacati, yaññadatto pacati.

> Yuttatthoti kimatthaṃ? Bhato rañño putto devadattassa.

> Samāsaiccanena kvattho? Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto.

Of those nouns — the word-meanings that are being employed [together] — that [joining] whose meaning is connected is named `samāsa` (compound).

:::tip[Examples]

The six examples show three of the kinds the chapter will define: two `tappurisa` compounds of [§327 (351): Amādayo parapadebhi](#m327), a `kammadhāraya` of [§324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo](#m324), and three `dvanda` compounds of [§329 (357): Nāmānaṃ samuccayo dvando](#m329).

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(kathina + dussa) | kathinadussaṃ | cloth for the `kathina` [frame] — `kathinassa dussaṃ`, "cloth for the kathina" |
| 🚻①⨀(āgantuka + bhatta) | āgantukabhattaṃ | food for newcomers — `āgantukassa bhattaṃ` |
| 🚻①⨀(jīvita + indriya) | jīvitindriyaṃ | the life faculty — `jīvitaṃ eva indriyaṃ`, "the faculty that is life" |
| 🚹①⨂(samaṇa + brāhmaṇa) | samaṇabrāhmaṇā | ascetics and brahmins — `samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca` |
| 🚹①⨂(sāriputta + moggallāna) | sāriputtamoggallānā | Sāriputta and Moggallāna |
| 🚹①⨂(brāhmaṇa + gahapatika) | brāhmaṇagahapatikā | brahmins and householders |

|     | kathinassa + dussaṃ (kathina + sa ⑥⨀, dussa + si ①⨀) | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | kathina + ~~sa~~ + dussa + ~~si~~                         | §317 |
| →   | kathina + dussa                                           | §318 |
| →   | kathinadussa + si                                         | §601 |
| →   | kathinadussa + (si→aṃ)                                    | §219 |
| →   | **kathinadussaṃ**                                         |      |

The compound, once formed, is itself a noun and takes the inflections afresh by [§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa#m601); the neuter `dussa` gives its `si` the shape `aṃ` by [§219 (195): Siṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m219).

🚻①⨀(ima) 🚹⑥⨀(saṃgha) 🚻①⨀(kathina + dussa) 🚻①⨀(uppanna) \
this | the Saṅgha's | kathina cloth | has arisen

> `jīvitindriyaṃ upacchindati` \
> [(1V 1.1.3: Tatiyapārājikasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.1/1.1.3#401) \
🚻②⨀(jīvita + indriya) ▶️🤟⨀(upacchindati) \
the life faculty | he cuts off

> `santi, bhante, samaṇabrāhmaṇā lajjino kukkuccakā sikkhākāmā` \
> [(1V 1.1.2: Dutiyapārājikasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.1/1.1.2#201) \
▶️🤟⨂(atthi) 🚹⓪⨀(bhadanta) 🚹①⨂(samaṇa + brāhmaṇa) 🚹①⨂(lajjī) 🚹①⨂(kukkuccaka) 🚹①⨂(sikkhākāma) \
there are | venerable sir | ascetics and brahmins | scrupulous | conscientious | desirous of training

:::

:::danger[Counter-examples]

By saying `nāmānaṃ` (of nouns) the rule reaches nouns only: a noun and a verb (`ākhyāta`) are not compounded, though their meanings connect.

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(devadatta) ▶️🤟⨀(pacati) | devadatto pacati | Devadatta cooks |
| 🚹①⨀(yaññadatta) ▶️🤟⨀(pacati) | yaññadatto pacati | Yaññadatta cooks |

By saying `yuttattho` (connected in meaning) words that merely stand side by side are not compounded. In `bhato rañño putto devadattassa`, "the king's hireling, Devadatta's son", `rañño` goes with `bhato` and `devadattassa` with `putto`: `rañño` and `putto` are adjacent but unconnected, so there is no `rājaputto` here (the Rūpasiddhi adds `devadattassa kaṇhā dantā`, "Devadatta's black teeth", where `devadattassa` looks to `dantā`, not to `kaṇhā`).

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(bhata) 🚹⑥⨀(rāja) 🚹①⨀(putta) 🚹⑥⨀(devadatta) | bhato rañño putto devadattassa | the king's hireling, Devadatta's son |

:::

:::tip[Purpose]

`Samāsa iccanena kvattho?` — what is the use of this term `samāsa`? It is used in [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337), which changes the final of a word standing at the end of a compound; the elision of [§317 (332): Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca](#m317) and the rules that name the kinds of compound depend on it as well.

:::

<a name="m317"></a>

### §317 (332): Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca (and the inflections of the members are elided) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     |        | tesaṃ (yuttatthānaṃ samāsānaṃ) vibhattiyo | lopā | ~~vibhattiyo~~ | | |
| and    |        | the inflections of those [nouns compounded] | elision | ~~inflections~~ | | |

`tesaṃ` ("of those") picks up the `nāmānaṃ … yuttattho` of [§316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho](#m316): the nouns whose connected meaning the compound expresses. `vibhattiyo` are the inflections of [§55 (63): Si yo, aṃ yo, nā hi, sa naṃ, smā hi, sa naṃ, smiṃ su](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m55); `lopā` (plural, agreeing with `vibhattiyo`) "are elided". The rule states the basic operation of compounding without any restricting condition: the words enter the compound as bare stems, and the compound as a whole then takes an inflection again ([§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa#m601)). Hence `utsarga`. The `ca` does not here extend the rule to a new case, its usual work: the vutti says it is `avadhāraṇattha`, for restriction — the elision is not without exception, and `pabhaṅkaro`, `amatandado`, `medhaṅkaro`, `dīpaṅkaro` keep the `aṃ` of their first member, which sandhi then turns into `ṅ` or `n` ([§31 (49): Vaggantaṃ vā vagge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m31)). The Bālāvatāra glosses `cakārena kvaci na`, "by `ca`, sometimes not", and the Rūpasiddhi `lopanivattanatthaṃ`, "to bar the elision". The vutti also reads `tesaṃ` widely (`Tesaṃgahaṇena …`): the elision it names covers not only inflections in compounds but the letters that fall in `taddhita`, `ākhyāta` and `kitaka` formations too — the `ṇ` of the suffixes `ṇa` and `ṇeyya` in `vāsiṭṭho`, `venateyyo`, which [§396 (363): Tesaṃ ṇo lopaṃ](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m396) states in its own place.

:::

> Tesaṃ yuttatthānaṃ samāsānaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca honti.

> Kathinadussaṃ, āgantukabhattaṃ.

The inflections of those [nouns] that are compounded with connected meaning are elided.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(kathina + dussa) | kathinadussaṃ | cloth for the `kathina` — the `sa` of `kathinassa` and the `si` of `dussaṃ` elided |
| 🚻①⨀(āgantuka + bhatta) | āgantukabhattaṃ | food for newcomers — the `sa` of `āgantukassa` and the `si` of `bhattaṃ` elided |

|     | āgantukassa + bhattaṃ (āgantuka + sa ⑥⨀, bhatta + si ①⨀) | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | āgantuka + ~~sa~~ + bhatta + ~~si~~                          | §317 |
| →   | āgantuka + bhatta                                            | §318 |
| →   | āgantukabhatta + si                                          | §601 |
| →   | āgantukabhatta + (si→aṃ)                                     | §219 |
| →   | **āgantukabhattaṃ**                                          |      |

> `āgantukabhattaṃ dātuṃ, gamikabhattaṃ dātuṃ` \
> [(3V 8.18: Visākhāvatthu)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/3V/8/8.18#1655) \
🚻②⨀(āgantuka + bhatta) 🔼(dātuṃ) 🚻②⨀(gamika + bhatta) 🔼(dātuṃ) \
food for newcomers | to give | food for travellers | to give

:::

> Tesaṃgahaṇena samāsataddhitākhyātakitakānaṃ vibhattipaccayapadakkharāgamā ca lopā honti. Vāsiṭṭho, venateyyo.

By the mention of `tesaṃ` ("of those") the inflections, suffixes, words, letters and augments of compounds, `taddhita` derivatives, verbs and `kitaka` derivatives are elided as well.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(vasiṭṭha + ṇa) | vāsiṭṭho | a descendant of Vasiṭṭha — the `ṇ` of the suffix `ṇa` elided |
| 🚹①⨀(vinatā + ṇeyya) | venateyyo | Vinatā's son (the garuḷa) — the `ṇ` of `ṇeyya` elided |

|     | vasiṭṭhassa + apaccaṃ → vasiṭṭha + sa + ṇa | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | vasiṭṭha + ~~sa~~ + ṇa                      | §344, §317 |
| →   | vasiṭṭha + (~~ṇ~~ + a)                      | §396 |
| →   | v + (a→ā) + siṭṭha + a                      | §400 |
| →   | vāsiṭṭh + (~~a~~ + a)                       | §83  |
| →   | vāsiṭṭha + (si→o)                           | §104 |
| →   | **vāsiṭṭho**                                |      |

The suffix `ṇa` is added in the sense "his offspring" by [§344 (361): Vāṇa’pacce](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m344), its `ṇ` is elided by [§396 (363): Tesaṃ ṇo lopaṃ](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m396), and the first vowel is strengthened (`vuddhi`) by [§400 (364): Vuddhādisarassa vā’saṃyogantassa saṇe ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m400); `venateyyo` takes `ṇeyya` by [§346 (367): Ṇeyyo kattikādīhi](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m346) and strengthens `i` to `e` by the same rule and [§405 (365): Ayuvaṇṇānañcāyo vuddhi](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m405).

> `vāsiṭṭho mayā diṭṭho` \
> [(1V 1.2.9: Dutiyaduṭṭhadosasikkhāpada)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/1V/1/1.2/1.2.9#1644) \
🚹①⨀(vasiṭṭha + ṇa) ⚧️③⨀(amha) 🚹①⨀(diṭṭha) \
Vāsiṭṭha | by me | was seen

:::

> Caggahaṇa mavadhāraṇatthaṃ, pabhaṅkaro, amatandado[^1], medhaṅkaro, dīpaṅkaro.

The word `ca` is for restriction: [the elision does not hold in every compound —] `pabhaṅkaro`, `amatandado`, `medhaṅkaro`, `dīpaṅkaro`.

:::tip[Examples]

| 🚹①⨀(dīpaṃ + kara) | dīpaṅkaro | the maker of light (the Buddha Dīpaṅkara) |

<a name="m318"></a>

### §318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa (and a member ending in a vowel returns to its original form) :badge[utsarga]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     |        | assa sarantassa (liṅgassa) | pakati | (pakatirūpāni) | | (luttāsu vibhattīsu) |
| and    |        | of that [stem] ending in a vowel | original form | (its original shape) | | (when the inflections have been elided) |

The sutta is `pakati ca assa sarantassa`: "and of that [stem] ending in a vowel, the original form". `assa` ("of that") is the stem (`liṅga`, which the vutti supplies) of each noun of [§316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho](#m316); `sarantassa` "ending in a vowel"; `pakati` the natural form, the shape a stem has before any inflection is added ([§53 (61): Liṅgañca nippajjate](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m53)), the sense in which [§83 (67): Saralopo’ mādesa paccayādimhi saralope tu pakati](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m83) uses the word. `luttāsu vibhattīsu` ("when the inflections have been elided") continues from [§317 (332): Tesaṃ vibhattiyo lopā ca](#m317), and the `ca` joins this second consequence of the elision to the first: the inflection goes, and whatever the inflection had done to the stem is undone — `rañño` gives back `rāja`, `sotaṃ` and `mukhaṃ` give back `sota` and `mukha`. A general rule without a restricting condition, so `utsarga`; `sarantassa` names the class it applies to, which in Pāli is every stem. The Rūpasiddhi (333) reads the `ca` as extending the restoration to the few words that end in a `niggahīta` (`kiṃ` in `kiṃsamudaya`, `idaṃ` in `idappaccayatā`), and remarks that the restored stem is not a bare stem for good: the compound takes inflections again as a noun by [§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa#m601).

:::

> Luttāsu vibhattīsu assa sarantassa liṅgassa pakatirūpāni honti.

> Cakkhusotaṃ, mukhanāsikaṃ, rājaputto, rājapuriso.

When the inflections have been elided, that stem which ends in a vowel has its original forms.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(cakkhu + sota) | cakkhusotaṃ | eye and ear — `cakkhu ca sotañca`; `sotaṃ` gives back `sota` |
| 🚻①⨀(mukha + nāsikā) | mukhanāsikaṃ | mouth and nose — `mukhañca nāsikā ca`; `mukhaṃ` gives back `mukha` |
| 🚹①⨀(rāja + putta) | rājaputto | the king's son — `rañño putto`; `rañño` gives back `rāja` |
| 🚹①⨀(rāja + purisa) | rājapuriso | the king's man — `rañño puriso` |

|     | rañño + putto (rāja + sa ⑥⨀, putta + si ①⨀) | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | rāja + ~~sa~~ + putta + ~~si~~                | §317 |
| →   | rāja + putta                                  | §318 |
| →   | rājaputta + si                                | §601 |
| →   | rājaputta + (si→o)                            | §104 |
| →   | **rājaputto**                                 |      |

The phrase `rañño putto` had the whole of `rāja` + `sa` replaced by `rañño` ([§135 (118): Rājassa rañño rājino se](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m135)); once the `sa` is elided, nothing of that replacement remains and the stem `rāja` stands in the compound. The first two examples are `dvanda` compounds made neuter singular by [§322 (359): Tathā dvande pāṇi tūriya yoggasenaṅgakhuddajantuka vividhaviruddha visabhāgatthādīnañca](#m322); in `mukhanāsikaṃ` the restored `nāsikā` is then shortened by [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342).

<a name="m319"></a>

### §319 (330): Upasagganipātapubbako abyayībhāvo (an `abyayībhāva` begins with a prefix or a particle) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | upasagganipātapubbako (samāso) | (saññā) | abyayībhāvo | | |
|        |        | [a compound] whose first member is a prefix or a particle | (is named) | `abyayībhāva` | | |

`upasagga` is a prefix (`upa`, `ni`, `adhi`, `pati`, `anu`), `nipāta` a particle (`yathā`, `yāva`, `tiro`, `anto`), `pubbaka` "having … as its first [member]"; `samāso` is supplied from [§316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho](#m316). `abyayībhāva` is `a` + `byaya` ("change", "passing away" — the inflectional variation a noun undergoes) + `ī` + `bhāva` ("becoming"): "becoming an invariable". The name says what the compound does: whatever its sense, it keeps one shape, like an indeclinable — neuter singular in `aṃ` ([§320 (335): So napuṃsakaliṅgo](#m320), [§341 (336): Aṃ vibhattīnamakārantā abyayībhāvā](#m341)) or the bare stem ([§343 (338): Aññasmā lopo ca](#m343)); hence `anvattha`. Its first member carries the principal meaning (the Bālāvatāra's verse: `pubbapadatthappadhāno abyayībhāvo`), which is why the vutti's analyses (`viggaha`) explain each example from the sense of the prefix or particle: nearness (`upa`), absence (`ni`), order or distribution (`yathā`), limit (`yāva`), reference (`adhi`), "beyond" (`tiro`), "against" (`pati`), "inside" (`anto`). Because such a compound has no phrase it alternates with (it is a `niccasamāsa`, a compulsory compound), the analysis brings in a word from outside — `kathā` ("talk") in `nagarassa samīpe pavattati kathā`, `nāvā` in `sotassa pati pavattati nāvā` (Bālāvatāra ¶230). The definition has no operation of its own; the rules that act on the term are §320, §341, [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342) and §343. The Rūpasiddhi numbers this rule 330, before the definition of the compound (331).

:::

> Upasagganipātapubbako samāso abyayībhāvasañño hoti.

> Nagarassa samīpe pavattati kathā iti upanagaraṃ, darathānaṃ abhāvo niddarathaṃ, makasānaṃ abhāvo nimmakasaṃ, vuḍḍhānaṃ paṭipāṭi yathāvuḍḍhaṃ, ye ye vuḍḍhā vā yathāvuḍḍhaṃ, jīvassa yattako paricchedo yāvajīvaṃ, cittamadhikicca pavattanti te dhammāti adhicittaṃ, pabbatassa tiro tiropabbataṃ, sotassa pati pavattati nāvā iti patisotaṃ, pāsādassa anto antopāsādaṃ.

> Abyayībhāvamiccanena kvattho? Aṃvibhattīna makārantā abyayībhāvā.

<a name="m320"></a>

### §320 (335): So napuṃsakaliṅgo (it is neuter) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | so (abyayībhāvasamāso) | (daṭṭhabbo) | napuṃsakaliṅgo | | |
|        |        | that [`abyayībhāva` compound] | (is to be regarded as) | neuter in gender | | |

`so` ("that") is the `abyayībhāva` just defined in [§319 (330): Upasagganipātapubbako abyayībhāvo](#m319). A compound ordinarily takes the gender of its last member (the Bālāvatāra's verse ¶204: `tayo parapade liṅgaṃ`, "these three take their gender from the last member"); this rule overrides that for the `abyayībhāva`, which is neuter whatever the gender of its members — `kumārī`, `vadhū`, `gaṅgā`, `maṇikā` are all feminine, yet `adhikumāri`, `upavadhu`, `upagaṅgaṃ`, `upamaṇikaṃ` are neuter. Hence `apavada`. The vutti's `va` (= `eva`, "only") says the neuter is the only gender; the Bālāvatāra adds that the compound is also singular, by the general licence of [§391 (423): Yadanupapannā nipātanā sijjhanti](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m391) (¶230: `yadādinā ekavacano ca`). The neuter gender has two consequences stated below: the final vowel is shortened ([§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342): `kumārī` → `kumāri`, `gaṅgā` → `gaṅga`), and the inflections become `aṃ` after a final `a` ([§341 (336): Aṃ vibhattīnamakārantā abyayībhāvā](#m341)) or are elided after any other final ([§343 (338): Aññasmā lopo ca](#m343)). The Rūpasiddhi (335) remarks that this is a statement of gender, not an extension by analogy (`atidesa`), since the sutta has no `iva`; the same four examples return under §342 and §343.

:::

> So abyayībhāvasamāso napuṃsakaliṅgova daṭṭhabbo.

> Kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā iti adhikumāri, vadhuyā samīpe pavattati kathā iti upavadhu, gaṅgāya samīpe pavattati kathā iti upagaṅgaṃ, maṇikāya samīpe pavattati kathā iti upamaṇikaṃ.

That `abyayībhāva` compound is to be regarded as neuter in gender only.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(adhi + kumārī) | adhikumāri | concerning girls — `kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā`, "talk that goes on with reference to girls" |
| 🚻①⨀(upa + vadhū) | upavadhu | near the young wife — `vadhuyā samīpe pavattati kathā` |
| 🚻①⨀(upa + gaṅgā) | upagaṅgaṃ | near the Ganges — `gaṅgāya samīpe pavattati kathā` |
| 🚻①⨀(upa + maṇikā) | upamaṇikaṃ | near the water-jar — `maṇikāya samīpe pavattati kathā` |

|     | adhi + kumārīsu (kumārī + su ⑦⨂)  | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | adhi + kumārī + ~~su~~            | §317 |
| →   | adhikumār + (ī→i)                 | §320, §342 |
| →   | adhikumāri + ~~si~~               | §601, §343 |
| →   | **adhikumāri**                    |      |

|     | upa + gaṅgāya (gaṅgā + sa ⑥⨀)     | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | upa + gaṅgā + ~~sa~~              | §317 |
| →   | upagaṅg + (ā→a)                   | §320, §342 |
| →   | upagaṅga + (si→aṃ)                | §601, §341 |
| →   | **upagaṅgaṃ**                     |      |

The neuter gender is what lets §342 shorten the final and §341 or §343 dispose of the inflection; a feminine `kumārī` or `gaṅgā` would have kept its long vowel and its own inflections. None of the four is attested in the Tipiṭaka; they are the grammarians' stock examples (Bālāvatāra ¶230–¶231 derives `adhitthi` in the same way).

:::

### §321 (349): Digussekattaṃ (a `digu` is singular [and neuter]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | digussa (samāsassa) | (hoti) | ekattaṃ (napuṃsakaliṅgattañca) | | |
|        |        | of a `digu` [compound] | (there is) | singular number (and neuter gender) | | |

The sutta is `digussa ekattaṃ`, "of a `digu`, oneness". `digu` is the compound that begins with a numeral, defined only below in [§325 (348): Saṅkhyāpubbo digu](#m325) — Kaccāyana states the property before the definition, and the vutti of §325 points back here for the term's use. `ekatta` ("the state of being one") is the singular number: the several things the phrase names (`tayo lokā`, "three worlds") are gathered into one whole (`samāhāra`), and the compound is inflected in the singular. The vutti adds `napuṃsakaliṅgattañca`, "and neuter gender", which is not in the sutta: it is carried over from [§320 (335): So napuṃsakaliṅgo](#m320) (the Rūpasiddhi: `napuṃsakaliṅgo ti vattate`). Both settings override what the compound would otherwise take from its last member — the plural and the gender of `lokā`, `disā`, `indriyāni` — so the rule is `apavada`. There is no option marker; the Rūpasiddhi (349) limits the rule to the collective (`samāhāra`) `digu`, and notes that a `digu` which is not collective keeps the number and gender of its last member: `catuddisā`, "the four directions", `tibhavā`, "the three existences", `ekapuggalo`. The neuter singular brings with it the shortening of a final vowel by [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342), which the Rūpasiddhi applies to `catuddisaṃ`, and the `a` for the final of `godāvarī` in §325's `sattagodāvaraṃ` by [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337). By [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326) the `digu` also counts as a `tappurisa`.

### §322 (359): Tathā dvande pāṇi tūriya yoggasenaṅgakhuddajantuka vividhaviruddha visabhāgatthādīnañca (likewise in a `dvanda` of limbs, instruments, gear, army parts, small creatures, natural enemies, unlike pairs and the rest) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     |        | pāṇi tūriya yogga senaṅga khuddajantuka vividhaviruddha visabhāgatthādīnaṃ | (hoti) | (ekattaṃ napuṃsakaliṅgattaṃ) | | tathā dvande |
| and    |        | of [`dvanda` compounds] in the sense of limbs, instruments, gear, army parts, small creatures, natural enemies, unlike pairs and the rest | (there is) | (singular number and neuter gender) | | likewise in a `dvanda` |

`tathā` ("likewise") carries the `ekattaṃ` and `napuṃsakaliṅgattaṃ` of [§321 (349): Digussekattaṃ](#m321) and [§320 (335): So napuṃsakaliṅgo](#m320) into the `dvanda`, the compound of co-ordinate nouns defined in [§329 (357): Nāmānaṃ samuccayo dvando](#m329), and the `ca` at the end of the long compound extends the neuter singular to it: a `dvanda` of these classes is a collective (`samāhāra`) like the `digu`, "eye and ear" taken as one pair. The list names the classes by their sense (`-attha`, "in the sense of"): `pāṇi` — the vutti reads `pāṇyaṅga`, limbs of a living being (`cakkhusotaṃ`); `tūriya` — musical instruments (`saṅkhapaṇavaṃ`); `yogga` — gear, the tackle of ploughing (`yuganaṅgalaṃ`); `senaṅga` — the parts of an army (`hatthiassaṃ`); `khuddajantuka` — small creatures (`ḍaṃsamakasaṃ`); `vividhaviruddha` — things opposed in various ways, natural enemies (`ahinakulaṃ`); `visabhāga` — things unlike yet paired (the Bālāvatāra: `vividhā ca te sabhāgā ca`, "different, yet alike", ¶228): `samathavipassanaṃ`. `ādi` ("and the rest") is answered by the vutti's own question, `Ādiggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ?`: it admits pairs of different gender (`dāsidāsaṃ`, `itthipumaṃ`), the requisites (`pattacīvaraṃ`, `chattupāhanaṃ`), numbers (`tikacatukkaṃ`), craftsmen (`venarathakāraṃ`), hunters (`sākuṇikamāgavikaṃ`) and sizes (`dīghamajjhimaṃ`). Outside these classes a `dvanda` keeps the plural and the gender of its last member (`candimasūriyā`, §329), so the rule is `apavada`. No option marker: within the classes the neuter singular is the form; [§323 (360): Vibhāsā rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapadā dīnañca](#m323) will name the classes where it is optional. The feminine finals `nāsikā`, `upāhanā` are shortened in the neuter by [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342) (which the Rūpasiddhi, 359, applies here) or take `a` by [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337), whose vutti lists `chattupāhanaṃ`.

:::

### §323 (360): Vibhāsā rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapadā dīnañca (optionally for trees, grasses, animals, wealth, grain, countries and the rest) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| vibhāsā |       | rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapadādīnaṃ | (hoti) | (ekattaṃ napuṃsakaliṅgattaṃ) | | (dvande samāse) |
| optionally |    | of [`dvanda` compounds] of trees, grasses, animals, wealth, grain, countries and the rest | (there is) | (singular number and neuter gender) | | (in a `dvanda` compound) |

`dvande samāse`, `ekattaṃ` and `napuṃsakaliṅgattaṃ` all continue from [§322 (359): Tathā dvande pāṇi tūriya yoggasenaṅgakhuddajantuka vividhaviruddha visabhāgatthādīnañca](#m322), and the `ca` again extends them, now to `dvanda` compounds of trees (`rukkha`), grasses (`tiṇa`), animals (`pasu`), kinds of wealth (`dhana`), grains (`dhañña`), countries (`janapada`) "and the rest" (`ādi`, which the vutti's `Ādiggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ?` fills with pairs of opposites). The option marker `vibhāsā` opens an alternative (`vikappa`): in these classes the neuter singular of §322 stands beside the ordinary treatment of a `dvanda` under [§329 (357): Nāmānaṃ samuccayo dvando](#m329) — plural, in the gender of the last member — and both are good Pāli: `assatthakapītanaṃ` and `assatthakapītanā`, `kāsikosalaṃ` and `kāsikosalā`, both of which the canon has. Ruiz-Falqués (reference chapter, §3.2) notes that `vibhāsā`, which Vimalabuddhi treats as equivalent to `vā`, occurs only twice in Kaccāyana, both times in the derivation of compounds: here and in [§154 (0): Samāse ca vibhāsā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m154). The Rūpasiddhi carries this `vibhāsā` forward into every compound rule (`ito paraṃ … vibhāsā ti samāsavidhāne sabbattha vattate`), and the Bālāvatāra reads it into the definition itself (¶202): every compound is an option beside its phrase. The heading follows the CST's sutta list and the Rūpasiddhi, `pasu dhana dhañña`; the CST's vutti section prints the sutta as `pasukha na dhañña`, against its own gloss `rukkhatiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapada…`.

:::

### §324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo (two words with the same referent make a `kammadhāraya`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe (samāso) | (saññā) | kammadhārayo | | |
|        |        | [a compound] of two words with the same referent | (is named) | `kammadhāraya` | | |

`dvipada` is "two words" and `tulyādhikaraṇa` is `tulya` ("equal") + `adhikaraṇa` ("substrate", the thing a word rests on — its referent): two words that, on different grounds, denote one and the same thing, as `mahanto` ("great") and `puriso` ("man") both denote the one man. One of the pair qualifies (`visesana`), the other is qualified (`visesitabba`), and the qualifier stands first (Bālāvatāra ¶203). The vutti's analyses show the relation with `ca … ca` and a pronoun: `mahanto ca so puriso ca`, "he is great and he is a man"; the compound drops both, since it now says what they said (`vuttatthānam appayogo`). The name is explained by the Rūpasiddhi (339) as `kammam iva dvayaṃ dhārayatī ti kammadhārayo`, "it bears two like an action": as an action (`kamma`) carries both a doing and its purpose, so this compound carries two names for one thing — the name says what the compound is, and the definition is `anvattha`. The rule lays down no operation; the rules that operate on the term are [§330 (340): Mahataṃ mahā tulyādhikaraṇe pade](#m330), which applies to the first member of such a pair, [§332 (343): Kammadhārayasaññe ca](#m332), which the purpose question names, [§325 (348): Saṅkhyāpubbo digu](#m325), which carves the `digu` out of it, and [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326), which counts it as a `tappurisa`. The compound takes the gender and number of its last member (`nīluppalaṃ` neuter, `brāhmaṇadārikā` feminine); the last two examples show a feminine first member (`brāhmaṇī`, `khattiyā`) put into the masculine form by §332. The Rūpasiddhi (339) distinguishes nine sub-kinds — qualifier first, qualifier last (`sāriputtatthero`), both qualifiers (`sītuṇhaṃ`), comparison last (`munisīho`), `iti`-sense (`dhammasaññā`), `eva`-sense (`cakkhundriyaṃ`), and those beginning with `na`, `ku` and a prefix (`abrāhmaṇo`, `kadannaṃ`, `pāvacanaṃ`) — which §333–§336 below treat.

:::

> Dve padāni tulyādhikaraṇāni yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso kammadhārayasañño hoti.

> Mahanto ca so puriso cāti mahāpuriso, kaṇho ca so sappo cāti kaṇhasappo, nīlañca taṃ uppalañcāti nīluppalaṃ, lohitañca taṃ candanañcāti lohitacandanaṃ, brāhmaṇī ca sā dārikā cāti brāhmaṇadārikā, khattiyā ca sā kaññā cāti khattiyakaññā.

> Kammadhārayaiccanena kvattho? Kammadhārayasaññe ca.

When two words having the same referent are compounded, that compound is named `kammadhāraya`.

### §325 (348): Saṅkhyāpubbo digu (a `digu` [is a `kammadhāraya`] beginning with a numeral) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | saṅkhyāpubbo (kammadhārayasamāso) | (saññā) | digu | | |
|        |        | [a `kammadhāraya` compound] whose first member is a numeral | (is named) | `digu` | | |

`saṅkhyā` is a numeral and `pubba` "first": a compound whose first member is a numeral. `kammadhārayasamāso` continues from [§324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo](#m324) — the numeral and its noun have the same referent (`tayo lokā`, "three worlds", where "three" is said of the worlds), so the `digu` is a `kammadhāraya` with a numeral in front, and by [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326) it is a `tappurisa` too. The name `digu` is `dvi` + `go`, "two cows" — itself a `digu`; the kind is named after a specimen of itself, as Pāṇini's `dvigu` is (the Rūpasiddhi, 348: `dve gāvo digu, digusadisattā ayam pi samāso digū ti vutto`), and the name thereby says what the thing is: `anvattha`. What the name is for is stated in [§321 (349): Digussekattaṃ](#m321), to which the vutti's purpose question points: the collective `digu` is singular and neuter, and its members undergo the neuter changes — `godāvarī` takes a final `a` by [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337), `disā` is shortened by [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342). Four of the seven examples are those of §321.

:::

> Saṅkhyāpubbo kammadhārayasamāso digusañño hoti.

### §326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā (both [`digu` and `kammadhāraya`] are `tappurisa`) :badge[saññaṅga]{variant=tip}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | ubhe (digukammadhārayasamāsā) | (saññā) | tappurisā | | |
|        |        | both [the `digu` and the `kammadhāraya` compounds] | (are named) | `tappurisa` | | |

`ubhe` ("both") are the two kinds just defined, the `kammadhāraya` of [§324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo](#m324) and the `digu` of [§325 (348): Saṅkhyāpubbo digu](#m325). `tappurisa` is `tassa puriso`, "his man" — a compound of a word in the sixth form with the noun that follows it, whose last member carries the principal meaning (`uttarapadatthappadhāno`); it is defined in the next rule, [§327 (351): Amādayo parapadebhi](#m327). This rule defines nothing: it extends a name already in use to two further classes that share the feature the name marks — in `mahāpuriso` and `tilokaṃ` too the last member is what the compound denotes — so that the rules stated for the `tappurisa` reach them. That is what the vutti's purpose question shows: `Attaṃ nassa tappurise` ([§333 (344): Attaṃ nassa tappurise](#m333)) turns `na` into `a` "in a `tappurisa`", and every one of the vutti's examples is a `na`-compound that is a `kammadhāraya` (`na brāhmaṇo`) or a `digu` (`na pañcavassaṃ`) — without this rule §333 could not apply to them. A rule that governs the reach of a term rather than defining one is `saññaṅga`, like [§9 (11): Parasamaññā payoge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m9). It is not `rūḷhī`: the transfer is not arbitrary but rests on a stated likeness (the Rūpasiddhi, 341: `tappurisasadisattā ayam pi samāso anvatthasaññāya tappuriso ti vutto`), and not `anvattha`, since the name `tappurisa` itself is defined elsewhere. The Bālāvatāra keeps the rule as a bare statement between its `kammadhāraya` and `digu` sections (¶207) and glosses `ubhe` as "hence 'both'".

:::

> Ubhe digukammadhārayasamāsā tappurisasaññā honti.

> Na brāhmaṇo abrāhmaṇo, na vasalo avasalo, na bhikkhu abhikkhu, na pañcavassaṃ apañcavassaṃ, na pañcapūlī apañcapūlī, na sattagodāvaraṃasattagodāvaraṃ, na dasagavaṃ adasagavaṃ, na pañcagavaṃ apañcagavaṃ.

### §327 (351): Amādayo parapadebhi ([a word in] `aṃ` or a later inflection with the word that follows: `tappurisa`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | amādayo (nāmehi) parapadebhi (samasyante) | (saññā) | (tappuriso) | | |
|        |        | [words ending in] `aṃ` and the following [inflections], compounded with the nouns that follow them | (is named) | (`tappurisa`) | | |

`amādayo` is `aṃ` + `ādayo`, "`aṃ` and those that follow it": the inflections from `aṃ` onwards in the list of [§55 (63): Si yo, aṃ yo, nā hi, sa naṃ, smā hi, sa naṃ, smiṃ su](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m55), that is, the second to the seventh forms ②…⑦; the first form is excluded, since a word in the first form with another word of the same referent makes the `kammadhāraya` of [§324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo](#m324). `parapadebhi` is `parapadehi` with the `bhi` of [§99 (81): Smāhismiṃnaṃ mhābhimhi vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m99), "with the following words" — the nouns (`nāmehi`, supplied) that the inflected word depends on: `bhūmiṃ gato`, "gone to the ground", `rañño putto`, "the king's son". The name `tappuriso` is supplied from [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326), which has already used it; the vutti's `tadā so samāso tappurisasañño hoti` completes the sutta. `tappurisa` is `tassa puriso`, "his man" — itself a sixth-form `tappurisa`: the kind is named after a specimen of itself (the Rūpasiddhi, 341), in which, as in every `tappurisa`, the last member carries the principal meaning and gives the compound its gender; hence `anvattha`. The Rūpasiddhi (351) sorts the kind by the inflection of the first member into six (`dutiyātappurisa` … `sattamītappurisa`), which the vutti's examples run through in order — two or three for each form — and adds the `alopatappurisa` of §317's `pabhaṅkaro`, where the inflection stays, and the `amādiparatappurisa`, where the inflected word comes last (`rājahaṃso` beside `haṃsarājā`). The Bālāvatāra (¶210) gives the counter-example: in `passa vāsiṭṭha gāmaṃ, gato tisso sāvatthiṃ` ("look at the village, Vāsiṭṭha; Tissa has gone to Sāvatthī") `gāmaṃ` and `gato` stand together but are not connected, so no `gāmagato` is formed — the `yuttattha` of [§316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho](#m316) still governs.

:::

> Tā amādayo nāmehi parapadebhi yadā samasyante, tadā so samāso tappurisasañño hoti.

<a name="m328"></a>

### §328 (352): Aññapadatthesu bahubbīhi ([nouns compounded] in the sense of another word: `bahubbīhi`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | aññapadatthesu (dve nāmāni bahūni nāmāni samasyante) | (saññā) | bahubbīhi | | |
|        |        | [two or many nouns compounded] in the senses of other words | (is named) | `bahubbīhi` | | |

`aññapadatthesu` is `añña` ("other") + `pada` ("word") + `attha` ("meaning"), in the seventh form plural: "in the senses of other words". The compound denotes not what its members denote but something else to which they point — `dasabalo` is not "ten powers" but "the one who has ten powers" — and that other thing gives it its gender and number (`abhidheyyaliṅgavacano`, the Rūpasiddhi, 352): the same stem is `nigrodhaparimaṇḍalo` of a prince and would be feminine of a princess. The vutti supplies the subject, `dve nāmāni bahūni nāmāni`, "two nouns or many nouns": a `bahubbīhi` may have any number of members, and the vutti's long examples show compounds of other kinds (`dvanda`, `kammadhāraya`, `tappurisa`) nested inside one — it calls them `-gabbha`, "having … in the womb". The name `bahubbīhi` is `bahavo vīhayo yassa so`, "he who has much rice" — itself a `bahubbīhi`; the kind is named after a specimen of itself (the Rūpasiddhi: `anvatthasaññāvasena`), and the name thereby says what the kind is: `anvattha`. Two things in the vutti's analyses need a word. First, `yo … so ayaṃ` and `yassa … so ayaṃ`: the pronoun shows in which form the "other word" stands — the vutti runs through the second to seventh forms (`imaṃ saṅghārāmaṃ` ②, `anena samaṇena` ③, `yassa rañño` ④, `asmā gāmā` ⑤, `yassa purisassa` ⑥, `yasmiṃ janapade` ⑦), and the Bālāvatāra (¶218) reads `aññesaṃ padānaṃ` as "words not in the first form", though it lets the plural `atthesu` admit a first-form case as well (¶225, `sahetuko`). Second, `tulyādhikaraṇa` and `bhinnādhikaraṇa`: a `bahubbīhi` is `tulyādhikaraṇa` when its members share a referent within the phrase (`sayaṃpatitapaṇṇapupphaphalavāyutoyāni āhārā`, "the fallen leaves … are the food", both in the first form) and `bhinnādhikaraṇa` when they do not (`…vāyutoyehi āhārā`, "food [made] from the fallen leaves …", the first member in the third form); several examples are analysed both ways (`atha vā`). The remark `sāpekkhatte sati pi gamakattā samāso` says that a compound such as `vāsitasānu` is allowed even though `vāsita` still looks to a word outside it (`pupphehi`), because the sense is understood (`gamakatta`) — the principle the Bālāvatāra states at ¶216. The vutti's purpose question points to [§167 (210): Bahubbīhimhi ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m167), where a pronoun at the end of a `bahubbīhi` loses its pronominal inflection. The rules that touch the members of a `bahubbīhi` are [§331 (353): Itthiyaṃ bhāsitapumitthī pumāva ce](#m331) (a feminine member takes the masculine form), [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337) and [§338 (356): Nadimhā ca](#m338) (the final of the last member), and [§340 (355): Dhanumhā ca](#m340). The CST's text of this long vutti has several misprints, corrected and footnoted below.

:::

`Bahubbīhi iccanena kvattho?` — what is the use of this term `bahubbīhi`? :::

:::note[Additional]

The vutti's `Yamettha vattabbaṃ, taṃ heṭṭhā vuttameva` ("what is to be said here has been said below", that is, in the analyses that follow) and `sāpekkhatte satipi gamakattā samāso` ("though it still looks to [another word], the compound is made, because the sense is understood") state the licence under which `vāsitasānu`, `cumbitakūṭo`, `saṅghaṭṭitacaraṇā` and `upasobhitakandarāni` are compounded before the instrument (`pupphehi`, `bindūhi`) is added: a member may keep a dependence outside the compound if the meaning remains clear (`gamakatta`). The Bālāvatāra states the same for `rañño garuputto` (¶216). The Rūpasiddhi (352) sorts the `bahubbīhi` into nine kinds — two-word with the same or with different referents, three-word, with `na`, with `saha`, with a comparison first, with two numerals (`dvattipattā`), of an intermediate direction (`pubbadakkhiṇā`) and of reciprocal action (`kesākesi`) — and notes that its qualifier stands first, as in the word `bahubbīhi` itself.

### §329 (357): Nāmānaṃ samuccayo dvando (a collection of nouns is a `dvanda`) :badge[anvattha]{variant=note}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | nāmānaṃ (ekavibhattikānaṃ) samuccayo | (saññā) | dvando | | |
|        |        | a collection of nouns (that have the same inflection) | (is named) | `dvanda` | | |

`samuccaya` ("gathering together", from `saṃ` + `ud` + `√ci`) is the collection of two or more nouns that stand on an equal footing, joined in the phrase by `ca` ("and"): `candimā ca sūriyo ca`. The vutti supplies `ekavibhattikānaṃ`, "having one [and the same] inflection": words co-ordinated in a sentence stand in the same form, and only such words make a `dvanda` — the Rūpasiddhi (357) excludes a bare enumeration whose members depend on the verb separately (`cīvaraṃ piṇḍapātañca … adāsi`), since there the nouns are not connected with one another (`yuttattha`, [§316 (331): Nāmānaṃ samāso yuttattho](#m316)). In a `dvanda` both members carry the meaning (`ubhayapadatthappadhāno`, the Bālāvatāra's verse at ¶231), unlike the `tappurisa`, where the last does, and the `bahubbīhi`, where a word outside does. `dvanda` is "pair" (`dve dve padāni dvandā`), and the Rūpasiddhi again calls the name `anvattha`: the kind is named after what it is. The examples are the ordinary (`itarītarayoga`) `dvanda`: plural, since it names several things, and in the gender of its last member (`candimasūriyā` masculine after `sūriyo`). The collective (`samāhāra`) `dvanda` is neuter singular by [§322 (359): Tathā dvande pāṇi tūriya yoggasenaṅgakhuddajantuka vividhaviruddha visabhāgatthādīnañca](#m322), optionally so by [§323 (360): Vibhāsā rukkha tiṇa pasu dhana dhañña janapadā dīnañca](#m323). On the order of the members the Rūpasiddhi gives the rule of thumb that the more honoured, the shorter, and a word in `i` or `u` stand first (`sāriputtamoggallānā`, `candasūriyā`, `aggidhūmā`); the Bālāvatāra's parallel is ¶227. The purpose question points to the Nāmakappa's [§165 (208): Dvandaṭṭhā vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m165), and [§339 (358): Jāyāya tudaṃ jāni patimhi](#m339) below acts on a `dvanda` (`jāyā ca patī ca`).

:::

> Nāmānaṃ ekavibhattikānaṃ yo samuccayo, so dvandasañño hoti.

> Candimā ca sūriyo ca candimasūriyā, samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca samaṇabrāhmaṇā, sāriputto ca moggallāno ca sāriputtamoggallānā, brāhmaṇo ca gahapatiko ca brāhmaṇagahapatikā, yamo ca varuṇo ca yamavaruṇā, kuvero ca vāsavo ca kuveravāsavā.

> Dvandaiccanena kvattho? Dvandaṭṭhā vā.

That which is a collection of nouns having the same inflection is named `dvanda`.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨂(candima + sūriya) | candimasūriyā | moon and sun — `candimā ca sūriyo ca` |
| 🚹①⨂(samaṇa + brāhmaṇa) | samaṇabrāhmaṇā | ascetics and brahmins — `samaṇo ca brāhmaṇo ca` |
| 🚹①⨂(sāriputta + moggallāna) | sāriputtamoggallānā | Sāriputta and Moggallāna |
| 🚹①⨂(brāhmaṇa + gahapatika) | brāhmaṇagahapatikā | brahmins and householders |
| 🚹①⨂(yama + varuṇa) | yamavaruṇā | Yama and Varuṇa |
| 🚹①⨂(kuvera + vāsava) | kuveravāsavā | Kuvera and Vāsava (Sakka) |

|     | candimā ca + sūriyo ca (candima + si, sūriya + si) | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | candima + ~~si~~ + sūriya + ~~si~~                  | §317 |
| →   | candima + sūriya                                    | §318 |
| →   | candimasūriya + yo (①⨂, two referents)              | §601 |
| →   | candimasūriy + (~~a~~ + yo → ā)                     | §107 |
| →   | **candimasūriyā**                                   |      |

The compound takes the plural `yo` because it names two things, and the masculine of its last member; the `yo` becomes `ā` by [§107 (69): Sabbayonīnamāe](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m107). The `ca` of the phrase is not used, the compound itself expressing the conjunction.

### §330 (340): Mahataṃ mahā tulyādhikaraṇe pade (`mahā` for `mahanta` before a word with the same referent) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | (tesaṃ) mahataṃ (mahantasaddānaṃ) | (ādeso) | mahā | tulyādhikaraṇe pade | |
|        |        | of the words `mahanta` | (replaced by) | `mahā` | before a word with the same referent | |

`mahataṃ` is the sixth form plural of `mahanta` ("great"): its `nta` is treated like the suffix `ntu` by [§187 (108): Sesesu ntuva](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m187), so that before `naṃ` it becomes `taṃ` by [§128 (104): Naṃmhi taṃ vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m128) — `mahataṃ`, "of the great [words]". `tulyādhikaraṇe pade`, "before a word with the same referent" (⑦, the following member), is the setting of the `kammadhāraya` of [§324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo](#m324) — `mahanto ca so puriso ca` — and of the `bahubbīhi` whose members share a referent (`mahantī paññā yassa so`, `mahāpañño`, Bālāvatāra ¶223); it does not reach a `tappurisa` such as `mahantassa putto`, where `mahanta` and `putta` denote different persons. The whole word `mahanta`, whatever its gender in the phrase (`mahanto`, `mahantī`, `mahantaṃ`), is replaced by the one form `mahā`; hence `apavada` to the general restoration of the stem by [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318). There is no option marker for `mahā` itself. The vutti then reads a second replacement out of the plural (`bahuvacanaggahaṇena`): had the sutta meant `mahā` alone it could have said `mahato`; the plural signals that in some words (`kvaci`) the replacement is the short `maha`, with the following consonant doubled — `mahapphalaṃ`, `mahabbalaṃ`, `mahaddhanaṃ`, `mahabbhayaṃ` ([§28 (40): Para dvebhāvo ṭhāne](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m28) and, for the aspirate, [§29 (42): Vagge ghosāghosānaṃ tatiyapaṭhamā](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m29)). Both shapes are canonical: `mahābalaṃ` beside `mahabbalaṃ`, `mahādhanaṃ` beside `mahaddhanaṃ`. `tulyādhikaraṇe pade` is carried over into [§331 (353): Itthiyaṃ bhāsitapumitthī pumāva ce](#m331) and [§332 (343): Kammadhārayasaññe ca](#m332). The Bālāvatāra applies the rule in its derivation of `mahāvīro` (¶203).

:::

> Tesaṃ mahantasaddānaṃ mahā ādeso hoti tulyādhikaraṇe pade.

> Mahanto ca so puriso cāti mahāpuriso, mahantī ca sā devī cāti mahādevī, mahantañca taṃ balañcāti mahābalaṃ, mahanto ca so nāgo cāti mahānāgo, mahanto ca so yaso cāti mahāyaso, mahantañca taṃ padumavanañcāti mahāpadumavanaṃ, mahantī ca sā nadī cāti mahānadī, mahanto ca so maṇi cāti mahāmaṇi, mahanto ca so gahapatiko cāti mahāgahapatiko, mahantañca taṃ dhanañcāti mahādhanaṃ, mahanto ca so puñño cāti mahāpuñño.

Those words `mahanta` ("great") are replaced by `mahā` before a word having the same referent.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |

<a name="m331"></a>

### §331 (353): Itthiyaṃ bhāsitapumitthī pumāva ce (a feminine word also spoken as masculine counts as masculine before a feminine word with the same referent) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ce     |        | bhāsitapumitthī | (daṭṭhabbā) | pumāva | itthiyaṃ (tulyādhikaraṇe pade) | |
| if     |        | a feminine word that has [also] been spoken as masculine | (is to be regarded as) | just as masculine | when a feminine word with the same referent [follows] | |

`bhāsitapumitthī` is `bhāsita` ("spoken") + `puma` ("masculine") + `itthī` (here "a feminine [word]"): a feminine word whose stem has also been used in the masculine — an adjective or participle that takes all three genders, `dīghā` beside `dīgho`, `kalyāṇī` beside `kalyāṇo`, `pahūtā` beside `pahūto`. `itthiyaṃ` is "when [the word with the same referent] is feminine": in the phrase the adjective agrees with a feminine noun, `dīghā jaṅghā`, "long shins". `tulyādhikaraṇe pade` continues from [§330 (340): Mahataṃ mahā tulyādhikaraṇe pade](#m330). `pumāva` is `pumā iva`, "just as [if] masculine": inside the compound the feminine word gives up the feminine suffix `ā` or `ī` of [§237 (176): Itthīyamato āpaccayo](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m237) and [§238 (187): Nadādito vā ī](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m238) and stands as the bare stem — `dīghajaṅgho`, never `dīghājaṅgho`. `ce` ("if") states the condition; there is no option marker, and the treatment is obligatory where the condition holds, so the rule is an `apavada` to [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318), which would have restored `dīghā`. The vutti's examples are all `bahubbīhi` compounds ([§328 (352): Aññapadatthesu bahubbīhi](#m328)) referring to a man, and in them the last member too has become masculine (`jaṅgho`, `bhariyo`, `pañño`): that is the `bahubbīhi` taking the gender of the thing it denotes, not the work of this rule — `jaṅghā`, `bhariyā` and `paññā` have no masculine and are not `bhāsitapuma`. The Rūpasiddhi (354) puts that shortening of the last member under [§403 (354): Kvacādimajjhuttarānaṃ dīgharassā paccayesu ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m403), while the Bālāvatāra (¶223) reads this rule as removing the feminine suffix "from earlier and later member alike". The Rūpasiddhi (353) also observes that this rule concerns the `bahubbīhi`, because the next, [§332 (343): Kammadhārayasaññe ca](#m332), adds the `kammadhāraya` separately. The CST's vutti section prints the question as `Bhāsitapupeti`, corrected below.

:::

> Itthiyaṃ tulyādhikaraṇe pade ce bhāsitapumitthī pumāva daṭṭhabbā.

> Dīghā jaṅghā yassa soyaṃ dīghajaṅgho, kalyāṇabhariyo, pahūtapañño.

> Bhāsitapumeti[^10] kimatthaṃ? Brāhmaṇabandhu ca sā bhariyā cātibrāhmaṇabandhubhariyā.

If, before a word with the same referent that is feminine, there is a feminine word that has [also] been spoken as masculine, it is to be regarded just as masculine.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(dīgha + jaṅghā) | dīghajaṅgho | one whose shins are long — `dīghā jaṅghā yassa so` |
| 🚹①⨀(kalyāṇa + bhariyā) | kalyāṇabhariyo | one whose wife is good — `kalyāṇī bhariyā yassa so` |
| 🚹①⨀(pahūta + paññā) | pahūtapañño | one whose wisdom is abundant — `pahūtā paññā yassa so` |

|     | dīghā + jaṅghā (dīghā + si, jaṅghā + si) → yassa so | rule |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | dīghā + ~~si~~ + jaṅghā + ~~si~~                     | §317 |
| →   | (dīghā→dīgha) + jaṅghā                               | §331 |
| →   | dīgha + jaṅgh + (ā→a)                                | §403 |
| →   | dīghajaṅgha + (si→o)                                 | §601, §104 |
| →   | **dīghajaṅgho**                                      |      |

|     | kalyāṇī + bhariyā (kalyāṇī + si, bhariyā + si) → yassa so | rule |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | kalyāṇī + ~~si~~ + bhariyā + ~~si~~                        | §317 |
| →   | (kalyāṇī→kalyāṇa) + bhariyā                                | §331 |
| →   | kalyāṇa + bhariy + (ā→a)                                   | §403 |
| →   | kalyāṇabhariya + (si→o)                                    | §601, §104 |
| →   | **kalyāṇabhariyo**                                         |      |

`dīghā` had taken `ā` by §237 and `kalyāṇī` `ī` by §238 to agree with their feminine nouns; this rule strips them. The final of the last member is shortened because the compound is masculine, denoting the man (§403, or on the Bālāvatāra's reading this rule); the compound then takes `si` → `o` by [§104 (66): So](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m104).

> `jutimā mutimā pahūtapañño` \
> [(18Sn 3.6: Sabhiyasutta)](https://tipitaka2500.github.io/tipitaka/18Sn/3/3.6#639) \
🚹①⨀(jutimantu) 🚹①⨀(mutimantu) 🚹①⨀(pahūta + paññā) \
splendid | intelligent | of abundant wisdom

:::

:::danger[Counter-example]

By saying `bhāsitapuma` (spoken as masculine) the rule does not reach a feminine word that is feminine in its own right and has no masculine form for the same thing. `brāhmaṇabandhu`, "a brahmin's kinswoman", is such a noun — a feminine in `u`, like `dhenu`, not made with a feminine suffix from a masculine adjective — so in `brāhmaṇabandhubhariyā`, "a wife who is a brahmin's kinswoman", it is not treated as masculine. The Rūpasiddhi's counter-examples make the same point with `saddhādhuro`, `paññāpakatiko`: `saddhā` and `paññā` are never spoken as masculine and keep their `ā`.

| ----- | --------- | ------- |

:::

### §332 (343): Kammadhārayasaññe ca (and in a compound named `kammadhāraya`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     |        | (pubbe bhāsitapumitthī) | (daṭṭhabbā) | (pumāva) | (itthiyaṃ tulyādhikaraṇe pade) | kammadhārayasaññe (samāse) |
| and    |        | (a feminine word also spoken as masculine, standing first) | (is to be regarded as) | (just as masculine) | (when a feminine word with the same referent follows) | in a compound named `kammadhāraya` |

Everything but the setting is carried over from [§331 (353): Itthiyaṃ bhāsitapumitthī pumāva ce](#m331): `itthiyaṃ`, `tulyādhikaraṇe pade`, `bhāsitapumitthī`, `pumāva` and the condition `ce`. `kammadhārayasaññe` (⑦) is "in [a compound] that bears the name `kammadhāraya`", the term of [§324 (339): Dvipade tulyādhikaraṇe kammadhārayo](#m324), to whose purpose question this rule is the answer. By saying `ca` the rule is an extension of §331: the masculine treatment of a feminine first member, stated there for the `bahubbīhi`, holds in the `kammadhāraya` as well — `brāhmaṇī ca sā dārikā ca`, "she is a brahmin and she is a girl", gives `brāhmaṇadārikā`, not `brāhmaṇīdārikā`. The vutti adds `pubbe`, "standing first": in a `kammadhāraya` only the first member is affected, and the compound itself remains feminine with its last member (`dārikā`, `kaññā`), unlike the `bahubbīhi` of §331, which turned masculine with its referent. The Rūpasiddhi (343) draws the consequence: where the feminine word stands last it keeps its suffix — `khattiyakumārī`, `kumārasamaṇī`, `taruṇabrāhmaṇī` — and it gives as counter-examples to `itthiyaṃ` the neuter `kumārīratanaṃ`, `samaṇīpadumaṃ` (the following word is not feminine), and to `bhāsitapuma` `gaṅgānadī`, `taṇhānadī`, `pathavīdhātu` (the first word has no masculine). Kaccāyana's own counter-examples, `khattiyabandhudārikā`, `brāhmaṇabandhudārikā`, are of the second kind. The Bālāvatāra applies the rule to `rattapaṭī` and `mahāsaddhā` (¶205).

:::

> Kammadhārayasaññe ca samāse itthiyaṃ tulyādhikaraṇe pade pubbe bhāsitapumitthī ce, pumāva daṭṭhabbā. Brāhmaṇadārikā, khattiyakaññā, khattiyakumārikā.

> Bhāsitapumeti kimatthaṃ? Khattiyabandhudārikā, brāhmaṇabandhudārikā.

And in a compound named `kammadhāraya`, if before a word with the same referent that is feminine there stands first a feminine word that has [also] been spoken as masculine, it is to be regarded just as masculine.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚺①⨀(brāhmaṇī + dārikā) | brāhmaṇadārikā | a brahmin girl — `brāhmaṇī ca sā dārikā ca` |
| 🚺①⨀(khattiyā + kaññā) | khattiyakaññā | a khattiya maiden — `khattiyā ca sā kaññā ca` |
| 🚺①⨀(khattiyā + kumārikā) | khattiyakumārikā | a khattiya girl — `khattiyā ca sā kumārikā ca` |

|     | khattiyā ca sā + kaññā ca (khattiyā + si, kaññā + si) | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | khattiyā + ~~si~~ + kaññā + ~~si~~                     | §317 |
| →   | (khattiyā→khattiya) + kaññā                            | §332 |
| →   | khattiyakaññā + ~~si~~                                 | §601, §220 |
| →   | **khattiyakaññā**                                      |      |

The `ā` that `khattiya` took by [§237 (176): Itthīyamato āpaccayo](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m237) and the `ī` that `brāhmaṇa` took by [§238 (187): Nadādito vā ī](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m238) are given up; the compound keeps the feminine of `kaññā` and `dārikā`, and its `si` is elided by [§220 (74): Sesato lopaṃ gasipi](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m220).

### §333 (344): Attaṃ nassa tappurise (`a` for `na` in a `tappurisa`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | nassa (padassa) | attaṃ (ādeso) | a | (uttarapade) | tappurise |
|        |        | of the word `na` | the state of being `a` (replacement) | `a` | (before the following member) | in a `tappurisa` |

`nassa` is "of `na`", the negative particle; `attaṃ` is `a` + `tta`, "the state of being `a`" — the whole particle is replaced by the letter `a`; `tappurise` "in a `tappurisa`", and the vutti supplies `uttarapade`, "when a following member comes". A compound beginning with `na` is a `kammadhāraya` (`na brāhmaṇo`, "not a brahmin", the `nanipātapubbapada` kind of the Rūpasiddhi, 343) or a `digu` (`na pañcavassaṃ`), and it is only because [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326) counts both as `tappurisa` that this rule reaches them — the vutti of §326 said so. There is no option marker: before a consonant `na` always becomes `a`; before a vowel the next rule, [§334 (345): Sare ana](#m334), gives `an`. The rule overrides [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318), which would have kept `na`; hence `apavada`. The Bālāvatāra (¶223) extends the rule by `yogavibhāga` (reading `Attaṃ nassa` as a rule on its own, without `tappurise`) to the `bahubbīhi` `asamo`, "one who has no equal", and Kaccāyana's own vutti of [§328 (352): Aññapadatthesu bahubbīhi](#m328) derives `anantañāṇo` with `nassa anto anantaṃ`. The Rūpasiddhi (344) discusses the sense of such negatives: `abrāhmaṇo` is not "nobody" but "one like a brahmin who is not one" (`pariyudāsa`), as against the plain denial `akatvā` (`pasajjapaṭisedha`).

:::

> Nassa padassa tappurise uttarapade attaṃ hoti.

> Nabrāhmaṇo abrāhmaṇo, avasalo, abhikkhu, apañcavassaṃ, apañcagavaṃ.

In a `tappurisa`, before the following member, the word `na` becomes `a`.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(na + brāhmaṇa) | abrāhmaṇo | a non-brahmin — `na brāhmaṇo` |
| 🚹①⨀(na + vasala) | avasalo | not an outcaste |
| 🚹①⨀(na + bhikkhu) | abhikkhu | not a bhikkhu |
| 🚻①⨀(na + pañca + vassa) | apañcavassaṃ | not five years (of seniority) |
| 🚻①⨀(na + pañca + go) | apañcagavaṃ | not five cows |

|     | na + vasalo (vasala + si)              | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | na + vasala + ~~si~~                   | §317 |
| →   | (na→a) + vasala                        | §326, §333 |
| →   | avasala + (si→o)                       | §601, §104 |
| →   | **avasalo**                            |      |

The derivations of `abrāhmaṇo` and `apañcagavaṃ` are given under [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326).

### §334 (345): Sare ana (`ana` before a vowel) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | (nassa padassa) | (ādeso) | ana | sare | (tappurise) |
|        |        | (the word `na`) | (replaced by) | `ana` | before a vowel | (in a `tappurisa`) |

`sare` is "before a vowel" (⑦); `nassa padassa` and `tappurise` continue from [§333 (344): Attaṃ nassa tappurise](#m333), whose `a` this rule replaces in one environment: when the following member begins with a vowel, `na` becomes `ana`. The replacement is stated as `ana` with a final `a` that the following vowel then elides by [§12 (13): Sarā sare lopaṃ](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m12): `na` + `asso` → `ana` + `asso` → `anasso`, so that the audible result is `an`. There is no option marker, and before a vowel the form is always `an`, never `a` — the rule is an `apavada` to §333. The Rūpasiddhi (345) adds `aniṭṭho`, `anupavādo`, `anādāya`, `anoloketvā`, and in its `bahubbīhi` section derives `anuttaro`, `anantaṃ`, `anāsavā` the same way, remarking that `tappurise` is there taken loosely (`upalakkhaṇa`).

:::

> Nassa padassa tappurise ana ādeso hoti sare pare.

> Na asso anasso, anissaro, anariyo.

In a `tappurisa`, the word `na` is replaced by `ana` when a vowel follows.

| →   | **anasso**                             |      |

### §335 (346): Kada kussa (`kada` for `ku` [before a vowel]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | kussa  | (ādeso)   | kada       | (sare) | (tappurise) |
|        |        | of `ku` | (replaced by) | `kada` | (before a vowel) | (in a `tappurisa`) |

`kussa` is "of `ku`", the particle meaning "bad", "despised" (`kucchita`), which the vutti glosses with `kucchitaṃ annaṃ`, "despicable food"; `kada` is its replacement. `sare pare` ("when a vowel follows") continues from [§334 (345): Sare ana](#m334), and `tappurise` from [§333 (344): Attaṃ nassa tappurise](#m333) — a compound beginning with `ku` is a `kammadhāraya` (the Rūpasiddhi's `kupubbapada` kind), counted as `tappurisa` by [§326 (341): Ubhe tappurisā](#m326). The final `a` of `kada` is elided before the vowel by [§12 (13): Sarā sare lopaṃ](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m12): `kada` + `annaṃ` → `kadannaṃ`. No option marker: before a vowel `ku` is always `kad`; before a consonant it stays `ku` (`kudārā`), unless [§336 (347): Kā’ppatthesu ca](#m336) gives `kā`. `apavada` to [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318). The heading follows the CST's vutti section, `Kada kussa`, which its own gloss (`kada hoti`) and the Rūpasiddhi's vutti (346, `kada hoti sare pare`) support; the CST's sutta list prints `Kadi kussa`, and the Rūpasiddhi's heading is corrupt (`Kadada kussa`). The vutti's `(apuññakārānaṃ)`, "of evil-doers", is the CST's own parenthesis, glossing whose wives are meant.

:::

> Ku iccetassa kada hoti sare pare.

> Kucchitaṃ annaṃ kadannaṃ, kucchitaṃ asanaṃ kadasanaṃ[^11].

> Sareti kimatthaṃ? Kucchitā dārā yesaṃ (apuññakārānaṃ) te hontīti kudārā, kujanā. Evaṃ kuputtā, kugehā, kuvatthā, kudāsā.

This [particle] `ku` becomes `kada` when a vowel follows.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(ku + anna) | kadannaṃ | bad food — `kucchitaṃ annaṃ` |
| 🚻①⨀(ku + asana) | kadasanaṃ | bad eating, bad fare — `kucchitaṃ asanaṃ` |

|     | ku + annaṃ (anna + si)                 | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | ku + anna + ~~si~~                     | §317 |
| →   | (ku→kada) + anna                       | §335 |
| →   | kad + (~~a~~ + a) + nna                | §12  |
| →   | kadanna + (si→aṃ)                      | §601, §219 |
| →   | **kadannaṃ**                           |      |

Neither word is attested in the Tipiṭaka (`kadanna` belongs to the commentaries); the Bālāvatāra repeats `kadannaṃ` (¶231).

:::

:::danger[Counter-examples]

By saying `sare` (before a vowel) the rule does not apply when the following member begins with a consonant: `ku` stands unchanged. The vutti's examples are `bahubbīhi` compounds — `kucchitā dārā yesaṃ te`, "those whose wives are bad" — and `ku` keeps its shape in them, as it does in the `kammadhāraya` `kupuriso` of the Rūpasiddhi (347).

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨂(ku + dāra) | kudārā | [men] whose wives are bad |
| 🚹①⨂(ku + jana) | kujanā | bad people |

| 🚹①⨂(ku + geha) | kugehā | [those] whose houses are bad |
| 🚹①⨂(ku + vattha) | kuvatthā | [those] whose clothes are bad |
| 🚹①⨂(ku + dāsa) | kudāsā | [those] whose slaves are bad |

:::

### §336 (347): Kā’ppatthesu ca (and `kā` in the sense of "little") :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     |        | (kussa) | (ādeso)  | kā         |        | appatthesu |
| and    |        | (of `ku`) | (replaced by) | `kā` |     | in the senses of "little" |

The sutta is `kā appatthesu ca`: `appa` ("little", "few") + `attha` ("sense"), in the seventh form plural, "in the senses of little"; `kussa` continues from [§335 (346): Kada kussa](#m335). By saying `ca` the rule is an extension of §335: a second replacement of the particle `ku`, `kā`, conditioned now by meaning rather than by the following sound — `kā` appears when `ku` means "a little", `appakaṃ lavaṇaṃ` → `kālavaṇaṃ`, and, since the examples begin with consonants, the `sare` of §334–§335 is not carried over. The vutti asks why the plural (`Bahuvacanaggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ?`): had the sutta said `appatthe`, `kā` would be confined to the sense "little"; the plural signals that `kā` also occurs, in some places (`kvaci`), in the other sense of `ku`, contempt — `kāpurisā`, "wretched men". So the rule as a whole is restricted: `kā` for the sense "little", `kā` sometimes elsewhere, and otherwise `ku` unchanged (`kupuriso`, which the Rūpasiddhi, 347, gives beside `kāpuriso`). The CST's `anappatatthesupi` in the vutti is corrected to `anappatthesupi`, "also in senses that are not 'little'".

:::

> Ku iccetassa kā hoti appatthesu ca.

> Kālavaṇaṃ, kāpupphaṃ.

> Bahuvacanaggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ? Ku iccetassa anappatthesupi[^12] kvaci kā hoti, kāpurisā.

And this [particle] `ku` becomes `kā` in the senses of "little".

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(ku + lavaṇa) | kālavaṇaṃ | a little salt — `appakaṃ lavaṇaṃ` |
| 🚻①⨀(ku + puppha) | kāpupphaṃ | a few flowers — `appakaṃ pupphaṃ` |

|     | ku + lavaṇaṃ (lavaṇa + si)             | rule |
| --- | -------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | ku + lavaṇa + ~~si~~                   | §317 |
| →   | (ku→kā) + lavaṇa                       | §336 |
| →   | kālavaṇa + (si→aṃ)                     | §601, §219 |
| →   | **kālavaṇaṃ**                          |      |

Neither is attested in the Tipiṭaka; the Bālāvatāra has `kālavaṇaṃ` (¶231).

:::

:::tip[Purpose]

`Bahuvacanaggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ?` — what is the purpose of using the plural [`appatthesu`]? So that `ku` becomes `kā` in some places also in senses other than "little": `kāpurisā`, "wretched men", where `ku` has its sense of contempt.

| ----- | --------- | ------- |

<a name="m337"></a>

### §337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto (sometimes `a` for the final of a word at the end of a compound) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| kvaci  |        | samāsantagatānaṃ (nāmānaṃ) anto (saro) | (hoti) | akāro | | (samāsante) |
| sometimes |     | the final [vowel] of [nouns] that have come to the end of a compound | (becomes) | the letter `a` | | (at the end of a compound) |

`samāsantagatānaṃ` is `samāsa` + `anta` + `gata`, in the sixth form plural: "of [nouns] that have gone to the end of a compound", the vutti supplying `nāmānaṃ`; `akāranto` is `akāra` + `anto`, "the final [is] the letter `a`". The option marker `kvaci` restricts: the general rule for the last member is [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318), which keeps its own stem, and only in certain words does the final become `a` instead — even then not always, as the vutti's pairs show: `devarājo` and `devarājā`, `devasakho` and `devasakhā` are both good Pāli. The Rūpasiddhi (350) calls the words affected the `rāja` group (`rājādigaṇa`): `rāja` and `sakha`, which by the change become ordinary `a` stems and lose the special inflection of [§189 (113): Syā ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m189) (`devarājo`, `devarājaṃ`, `devarājena` like `purisa`; without it `devarājā`, `devarājāno` like `rāja`); `aha`, "day", whose stem otherwise appears as `aho` (`ahorattaṃ`), in `pañcāhaṃ`, `sattāhaṃ`; `go`, whose `o` becomes `av` before the `a` by [§78 (31): O sare ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m78), in `pañcagavaṃ`; the feminines `upāhanā` and `godāvarī` (`chattupāhanaṃ`, §325's `sattagodāvaraṃ`); `akkhi` in `visālakkho`; and `mukha` in the `bahubbīhi` `vimukho`, masculine after the man it denotes. Because the sutta says `akāra`, "the letter `a`", and not simply `a`, the vutti asks what the word `kāra` is for (`Kāraggahaṇaṃ kimatthaṃ?`) and answers that the finals also become `ā` and `i`: `paccakkhadhammā`, whose final `ā` swallows the `si` ([§220 (74): Sesato lopaṃ gasipi](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m220)), and the `gandha` compounds `surabhigandhi`, `sugandhi`, `pūtigandhi`, `kugandhi`, `dugandhi`, where `gandha` ends in `i`. The Rūpasiddhi reads the same `kāra` as licensing the suffix `ka` at the end of a `bahubbīhi` (or parses `akāranto` as `a ca ko ca`, the suffixes `a` and `ka`); Kaccāyana's vutti states that as a separate rule sentence at the end — `Nadīantā ca kattuantā ca kapaccayo hoti samāsante` — which the next sutta, [§338 (356): Nadimhā ca](#m338), restates for the `nadī` finals, and [§340 (355): Dhanumhā ca](#m340) gives the suffix `ā` after `dhanu`. The `kvaci` of this rule is carried on: the vutti of [§341 (336): Aṃ vibhattīnamakārantā abyayībhāvā](#m341) supplies it and asks `Kvacīti kimatthaṃ?`, and the Rūpasiddhi reads it into [§339 (358): Jāyāya tudaṃ jāni patimhi](#m339) and §340 as well. The Bālāvatāra's parallel is ¶224.

:::

> Samāsantagatānaṃ nāmānamanto saro kvaci akāro hoti.

> Devānaṃ rājā devarājo, devarājā, devānaṃ sakhā devasakho, devasakhā, pañca ahāni pañcāhaṃ, sattāhaṃ, pañcagavaṃ, chattupāhanaṃ, upasaradaṃ, visālakkho, vimukho.

Sometimes the final vowel of nouns that have come to the end of a compound becomes the letter `a`.

### §338 (356): Nadimhā ca (and `ka` after a `nadī` [feminine `ī`, `ū`] final) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     | nadimhā |       | (kapaccayo hoti) | ka   |        | (samāsante) |
| and    | after a `nadī` [final] | | (the suffix is added) | `ka` | | (at the end of a compound) |

`nadimhā` is the fifth form of `nadī`, "after `nadī`", with the `mhā` of [§99 (81): Smāhismiṃnaṃ mhābhimhi vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m99); `kapaccayo` ("the suffix `ka`") and `samāsante` ("at the end of a compound") are supplied from the closing sentence of [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337), which had already stated this rule together with the `kattu` finals. `nadī` here is not only the word for "river": it is the term of Sanskrit grammar (Pāṇini 1.4.3 `yū stryākhyau nadī`) for feminine stems in `ī` and `ū`, admitted into Kaccāyana's usage by [§9 (11): Parasamaññā payoge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m9); the Rūpasiddhi (356) says so — `nadī ti c'ettha itthivācakānaṃ īkārūkārānaṃ parasamaññā` — and the Bālāvatāra paraphrases it as `itthiyaṃ ivaṇṇantā`, "feminines ending in `i` or `ī`" (¶224), which lets the vutti's `bahukantiko` (from `kanti`, in short `i`) stand beside `bahunadiko` and `bahunāriko`. By saying `ca` the rule is an extension of §337: a suffix added to the compound's end, beside the changed final of §337. Its being stated as a sutta of its own, after §337 has already said it, takes it out from under §337's `kvaci`: after a `nadī` final the `ka` is regular (the Rūpasiddhi: `niccatthaṃ vacanaṃ`). The long vowel is shortened before the suffix by [§403 (354): Kvacādimajjhuttarānaṃ dīgharassā paccayesu ca](/kaccayana/5-taddhitakappa#m403), and the compound, a `bahubbīhi`, takes the gender of its referent (`janapado`, `puriso`).

:::

> Nadimhā ca kapaccayo hoti samāsante.

> Bahū nadiyo yasmiṃ soyanti bahunadiko. Bahū kantiyo yassa soyanti bahukantiko. Bahunāriko.

And after a `nadī` [final] there is the suffix `ka` at the end of a compound.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(bahu + nadī + ka) | bahunadiko | [a country] in which there are many rivers — `bahū nadiyo yasmiṃ so` |
| 🚹①⨀(bahu + kanti + ka) | bahukantiko | [one] who has many desires, or much beauty — `bahū kantiyo yassa so` |
| 🚹①⨀(bahu + nārī + ka) | bahunāriko | [one] who has many women — `bahū nāriyo yassa so` |

|     | bahū + nāriyo (bahu + yo, nārī + yo) → yassa so | rule |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | bahu + ~~yo~~ + nārī + ~~yo~~                    | §317 |
| →   | bahu + nārī + ka                                 | §338 |
| →   | bahu + nār + (ī→i) + ka                          | §403 |
| →   | bahunārika + (si→o)                              | §601, §104 |
| →   | **bahunāriko**                                   |      |

None of the three is attested in the Tipiṭaka; the Bālāvatāra repeats `bahukantiko`, `bahunadiko` (¶224), and the Rūpasiddhi adds `bahujambukaṃ vanaṃ`, "a forest with many rose-apple trees".

### §339 (358): Jāyāya tudaṃ jāni patimhi (`tudaṃ`, `jāni` for `jāyā` before `pati`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | jāyāya | (ādesā)   | tudaṃ\|jāni | patimhi | |
|        |        | of `jāyā` ("wife") | (replaced by) | `tudaṃ`\|`jāni` | before `pati` ("husband") | |

`jāyāya` is the sixth form of `jāyā`, "wife"; `patimhi` "before `pati`", "husband" (⑦, with the `mhi` of [§99 (81): Smāhismiṃnaṃ mhābhimhi vā](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m99)). In the `dvanda` `jāyā ca patī ca`, "wife and husband" ([§329 (357): Nāmānaṃ samuccayo dvando](#m329)), the word `jāyā` is replaced by `tudaṃ` or by `jāni`: `tudampatī`, `jānipatī` — the Pāli forms of the Sanskrit `dampatī` and `jampatī`, the old word for the married pair. Two replacements are given with no marker between them: both are simply available, as `tvaṃ` and `tuvaṃ` are in [§140 (232): Tvamahaṃ simhi ca](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m140). The rule is an `apavada` to [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318), which would have left `jāyā`. The Rūpasiddhi (358) carries the `kvaci` of [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337) into the rule and so admits `jāyāpati` unchanged as a third form; it also adds `jayampatikā`. The `ṃ` of `tudaṃ` before `p` becomes `m` by [§31 (49): Vaggantaṃ vā vagge](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m31), whose `vā` lets the CST print the unassimilated `tudaṃpatī`. The final `ī` is the plural of the pair: the `yo` of the first form is elided after the `i` stem by [§118 (146): Ghapato ca yonaṃ lopo](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m118) and the `i` lengthened by [§88 (147): Yosu katanikāralopesu dīghaṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m88), as in `aggī`.

:::

> Jāyā iccetāya tudaṃ jāni iccete ādesā honti patimhi pare.

> Tudaṃpatī, jānipatī.

This [word] `jāyā` is replaced by `tudaṃ` and `jāni` when `pati` follows.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨂(jāyā + pati) | tudaṃpatī | wife and husband — `jāyā ca patī ca`, with `tudaṃ` |
| 🚹①⨂(jāyā + pati) | jānipatī | wife and husband, with `jāni` |

|     | jāyā ca + patī ca (jāyā + si, pati + si) | rule |
| --- | ----------------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | jāyā + ~~si~~ + pati + ~~si~~             | §317 |
| →   | (jāyā→tudaṃ) + pati                       | §339 |

| →   | tudampat + (i→ī) + ~~yo~~                 | §118, §88 |

| --- | ----------------------------------------- | ---- |

Neither form is attested in the Tipiṭaka; they belong to the grammarians' inheritance from Sanskrit. `jāni` on its own is a Pāli word for "wife", which is why the second replacement looks less strange than the first.

### §340 (355): Dhanumhā ca (and `ā` after `dhanu`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     | dhanumhā |      | (āpaccayo hoti) | ā    |        | (samāsante) |
| and    | after `dhanu` ("bow") | | (the suffix is added) | `ā` | | (at the end of a compound) |

`dhanumhā` is the fifth form of `dhanu`, "bow" — "after `dhanu`"; `samāsante` continues from [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337) and [§338 (356): Nadimhā ca](#m338), and the vutti supplies `āpaccayo`, the suffix `ā`. By saying `ca` the rule is an extension of §338: as `ka` is added after a `nadī` final, so `ā` is added after `dhanu` at the end of a compound — a third treatment of a compound's final beside the changed vowel of §337 and the `ka` of §338. Before the vowel of the suffix the `u` of `dhanu` becomes `v` by [§18 (20): Vamodudantānaṃ](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m18): `dhanu` + `ā` → `dhanvā`; the compound is then an `ā`-final masculine, `gāṇḍīvadhanvā`, "whose bow is Gāṇḍīva" — Arjuna — whose `si` is elided by [§220 (74): Sesato lopaṃ gasipi](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m220). The Rūpasiddhi (355) reads §337's `kvaci` into the rule (giving `sahassathāmadhanu` without the suffix) and lets its `ca` extend the `ā` to `dhamma` (`paccakkhadhammā`), which Kaccāyana's vutti of §337 obtains from the word `kāra` instead; it spells the bow `gāṇṭhiva`.

:::

> Dhanumhā ca āpaccayo hoti samāsante.

> Gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa soyaṃ gāṇḍīvadhanvā.

And after `dhanu` there is the suffix `ā` at the end of a compound.

:::tip[Example]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚹①⨀(gāṇḍīva + dhanu + ā) | gāṇḍīvadhanvā | he whose bow is Gāṇḍīva (Arjuna) — `gāṇḍīvo dhanu yassa so` |

| --- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ---- |
| →   | gāṇḍīva + ~~si~~ + dhanu + ~~si~~                      | §317 |
| →   | gāṇḍīva + dhanu + ā                                    | §340 |
| →   | gāṇḍīva + dhan + (u→v) + ā                             | §18  |
| →   | gāṇḍīvadhanvā + ~~si~~                                 | §601, §220 |
| →   | **gāṇḍīvadhanvā**                                      |      |

### §341 (336): Aṃ vibhattīnamakārantā abyayībhāvā (`aṃ` for the inflections after an `abyayībhāva` in `a`) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| (kvaci) | akārantā abyayībhāvā (parāsaṃ) | vibhattīnaṃ | (ādeso) | aṃ | | |
| (sometimes) | after an `abyayībhāva` ending in `a` | of the inflections | (replaced by) | `aṃ` | | |

`vibhattīnaṃ … aṃ` is "for the inflections, `aṃ`"; `akārantā abyayībhāvā` is the fifth form, "after an `abyayībhāva` ending in `a`", the compound of [§319 (330): Upasagganipātapubbako abyayībhāvo](#m319), and the vutti's `tasmā … parāsaṃ` ("of those that follow it") spells the construction out. The vutti's `kvaci` is not in the sutta: it is carried over from [§337 (350): Kvaci samāsantagatānamakāranto](#m337), and the vutti's own question `Kvacīti kimatthaṃ?` confirms it is in force — `adhicittassa bhikkhuno`, "of a bhikkhu of higher mind", keeps the sixth-form inflection, and the canon's `adhicitte ca āyogo` keeps the seventh. So the rule restricts: after these compounds every inflection may appear as `aṃ` — `upanagaraṃ` serves as nominative, accusative and locative alike — but the ordinary inflections are not excluded. The Rūpasiddhi (336) gives the scope in a verse: the `aṃ` never displaces the fifth form (`upanagarā`, `upanagaramhā`), and for the third, sixth and seventh it is optional (`upanagaraṃ` or `upanagarena`, `upanagarassa`, `upanagare`); the Bālāvatāra's counter-example is `upanagare` (¶230). The `aṃ` is what makes the `abyayībhāva` "become invariable", the sense of its name; for compounds ending in another vowel [§343 (338): Aññasmā lopo ca](#m343) elides the inflections instead, after [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342) has shortened the final. `apavada` to the regular inflection of a neuter `a` stem ([§219 (195): Siṃ](/kaccayana/2-namakappa#m219) and the rest). The Rūpasiddhi numbers this rule 336, directly after `So napuṃsakaliṅgo` (335).

:::

> Tasmā akārantā abyayībhāvasamāsā parāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ kvaci aṃ hoti.

### §342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake (the final vowel is shortened in the neuter) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
|        |        | (abyayībhāvasamāsassa liṅgassa) saro | rasso | (rasso saro) | | napuṃsake (vattamānassa) |
|        |        | the [final] vowel (of the stem of an `abyayībhāva` compound) | short | (a short vowel) | | in the neuter |

`saro` is the vowel — the final vowel of the compound's stem, as the vutti's `liṅgassa saro` makes clear; `rasso` "short", the term of [§4 (4): Lahumattā tayo rassā](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m4); `napuṃsake` "in the neuter", the gender the `abyayībhāva` has by [§320 (335): So napuṃsakaliṅgo](#m320). `abyayībhāvasamāsassa` continues from [§341 (336): Aṃ vibhattīnamakārantā abyayībhāvā](#m341). A feminine last member in `ī`, `ū` or `ā` — `kumārī`, `vadhū`, `gaṅgā`, `maṇikā` — has its long final shortened when the compound has become neuter: `adhikumāri`, `upavadhu`, `upagaṅgaṃ`, `upamaṇikaṃ`, the four examples of §320. No option marker: in the neuter the shortening is regular, overriding the restored stem of [§318 (333): Pakati cassa sarantassa](#m318); hence `apavada`. It prepares for the disposal of the inflections: after the shortened `a` they become `aṃ` by §341, after `i` and `u` they are elided by [§343 (338): Aññasmā lopo ca](#m343). The vutti confines the rule to the `abyayībhāva`, but the Rūpasiddhi (337) declines to carry that word over — `abyayībhāvaggahaṇaṃ nānuvattetabbaṃ` — and applies the shortening to every neuter compound, the `digu` (`catuddisaṃ`), the `dvanda` (`mukhanāsikaṃ`, `biḷāramūsikaṃ`) and the neuter `bahubbīhi` alike; Kaccāyana's own examples in [§321 (349): Digussekattaṃ](#m321) and [§322 (359): Tathā dvande pāṇi tūriya yoggasenaṅgakhuddajantuka vividhaviruddha visabhāgatthādīnañca](#m322) presuppose it (`disā` → `disa`, `nāsikā` → `nāsika`). The Bālāvatāra cites the rule for `adhitthi` and `nimmakkhikaṃ` (¶230).

:::

> Napuṃsake vattamānassa abyayībhāvasamāsassa liṅgassa saro rasso hoti.

> Kumārīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā iti adhikumāri. Upavadhu, upagaṅgaṃ, upamaṇikaṃ.

The [final] vowel of the stem of an `abyayībhāva` compound used in the neuter becomes short.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |

<a name="m343"></a>

### §343 (338): Aññasmā lopo ca (and elision [of the inflections] after any other [`abyayībhāva`]) :badge[apavada]{variant=success}

:::note[Analysis]

| option | before | from   | operation | to         | after  | context |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ---------- | ------ | ------- |
| ca     | aññasmā (abyayībhāvasamāsā anakārantā parāsaṃ) | (vibhattīnaṃ) | lopo | ~~vibhattiyo~~ | | |
| and    | after another [`abyayībhāva` compound, one not ending in `a`] | (of the inflections) | elision | ~~inflections~~ | | |

`aññasmā` is "after another" (⑤): an `abyayībhāva` other than the `a`-final one of [§341 (336): Aṃ vibhattīnamakārantā abyayībhāvā](#m341) — the vutti glosses `anakārantā`, "not ending in `a`", that is, ending in the `i` or `u` that [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342) has just produced. `abyayībhāvasamāsā` and `vibhattīnaṃ` continue from §341; `lopo` is elision. By saying `ca` the rule is an extension of §341: as the inflections become `aṃ` after an `a`-final `abyayībhāva`, so after any other they are elided altogether, and the compound stands as its bare stem in every use — `adhitthi` is "concerning women" whether the sense is nominative, accusative or locative (the Bālāvatāra: `adhitthi passa`, `adhitthi kataṃ`, ¶231). Their sense is carried by the prefix (`vibhattīnam attho ādhārādi`). No option marker of its own; the Rūpasiddhi (338) does not carry §337's `kvaci` into this rule, and the elision is regular. `apavada` to the regular inflection of a neuter stem. The Rūpasiddhi adds `upagu`, "near the cows", where the `o` of `go` is shortened to `u`. This is the last sutta of the chapter; the Chaṭṭha Saṅgāyana closes it as the seventh section of the `nāma` chapter (`Iti nāmakappe samāsakappo sattamo kaṇḍo`).

:::

> Aññasmā abyayībhāvasamāsā anakārantā parāsaṃ vibhattīnaṃ lopo ca hoti.

> Adhitthi, adhikumāri, upavadhu.

And after another `abyayībhāva` compound, one not ending in `a`, the inflections that follow are elided.

:::tip[Examples]

| lemma | inflected | meaning |
| ----- | --------- | ------- |
| 🚻①⨀(adhi + itthī) | adhitthi | concerning women — `itthīsu adhikicca pavattati kathā` |
| 🚻①⨀(adhi + kumārī) | adhikumāri | concerning girls |
| 🚻①⨀(upa + vadhū) | upavadhu | near the young wife |

|     | adhi + itthīsu (itthī + su ⑦⨂)    | rule |
| --- | --------------------------------- | ---- |
| →   | adhi + itthī + ~~su~~             | §317 |
| →   | adh + (~~i~~ + i) + tthī          | §12  |
| →   | adhitth + (ī→i)                   | §320, §342 |
| →   | adhitthi + ~~si~~                 | §601, §343 |
| →   | **adhitthi**                      |      |

The `i` of `adhi` is elided before the `i` of `itthī` by [§12 (13): Sarā sare lopaṃ](/kaccayana/1-sandhikappa#m12); the final is shortened by §342, and the `si` — or any other inflection — that the compound would take as a noun ([§601 (334): Taddhitasamāsakitakā nāmaṃ vā’ tave tunādīsu ca](/kaccayana/7-kibbidhanakappa#m601)) is elided by this rule. The Bālāvatāra derives `adhitthi` in the same steps (¶230–¶231). The derivations of `adhikumāri` and `upavadhu` are given under [§320 (335): So napuṃsakaliṅgo](#m320) and [§342 (337): Saro rasso napuṃsake](#m342). None of the three is attested in the Tipiṭaka.

:::
